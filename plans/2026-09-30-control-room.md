playlist. Written 30 September 2026 after reading every marketing document
in Google Drive. Every link on this page is the live file. If a thing is not
on this page it is not part of the daily job. The Google Doc copy lives in
Drive under 00 CONTROL ROOM; this repo copy is for sessions to re read after
compaction. No dashes anywhere.

## 0. How this page works, in thirty seconds

- **Morning, 08:05, twenty minutes.** Open the tracker row for today. Do the one Must. Reply to two real conversations.
- **Evening, 21:00, fifteen minutes.** Reply to anything real. Numbers into the row. Type DONE. One voice note if something happened.
- **Friday, 12:30, forty five minutes.** Write next week's unit (one script, one founder chapter, one research brick). Then the review: one decision.
- **Saturday, 14:00, one hour.** Film four clips, one per strand.
- **Sunday, 20:30, one hour.** The founder post, then load Monday to Friday.

All five are on your personal Google Calendar as recurring reminders from
1 October, with the launch dates (kick off, thirty day review, six week
review, Safer Internet Day registration and the day itself) as all day
events.

## 3. The daily routine

**Morning, twenty minutes.**

1. Open the morning brief if one landed. Open the tracker. Today's Must is in column D.
2. Do the Must. If it is a post: check the claim is true, the link works on your phone, and it sounds like you out loud. Post it. On LinkedIn the link goes in the first comment, never the body.
3. Reply to two real conversations. A comment from a parent or a teacher beats a new post.

**Evening, fifteen minutes.**

1. Reply to anything real from the day.
2. Write the numbers in the row: stage checks started, founders, school replies, playlist accounts. Missing is missing, never zero.
3. Type DONE at the start of the Notes cell. DONE means the Must happened, not everything.
4. If something happened, a sixty second voice note: what did I notice, what do I think, what follows. That is raw material for Sunday.

**The five minute day.** Approve one thing, answer one enquiry, stop. A missed day is not a debt. Five DONEs in a week is a full week.

## 6. Filming: what to record and how

**The rules.** Phone, natural light, one take is fine. Camera on you and your
phone, never a child's face. Backs of heads, hands and objects are fine.
Warm and plain, no dashes in captions, no fear, never allow or deny. Every
clip that goes public ends on the same line: what stage is your child, three
questions, no sign up.

**The format.** Sixty to ninety seconds. The first three seconds carry the
hook. One idea. Captions on, because most people watch with the sound off.
Vertical for Reels and TikTok, the same file works for both. A screen
recording with your voice over it counts as a clip.

**The four strands, one clip each on Saturday.**

| Strand | What it is for | Where it goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder | Why you care, a decision you made, what you got wrong | LinkedIn Sunday, Founder Monday on the family account |
| Research | One finding and the tonight line | Research Wednesday, a Reel |
| Product | A real screen, your voice, one job | Service Friday, the silent version for TikTok |
| Real life | The kitchen, the paddock, the fridge, the school gate | Happy News Saturday, Stories |

**The prompt bank.** Pick one per strand each Saturday. Cross it off when used.

Founder:

1. The question I could not get a straight answer to as a parent.
2. The decision I made this week and why.
3. What a founder gets for £7.99, and why the cap of 50 is real.
4. The thing I got wrong and changed.
5. What the first families told me (only their real words, with permission).
6. This week's numbers, said plainly, with what they mean.

Research:

7. Ofcom found 35 percent of children met harm in a feed and 1 percent searched for it. What that means at your house tonight.
8. That number is not zero. The beta 0.061 finding in sixty seconds.
9. What a ban does, and what it does not do.
10. In 2012 a journal published one sentence about this. The panic pattern.
11. The question Year 6 parents ask me most.
12. Worse mornings predict more passive phone use, not the other way round. What to ask instead.

Product:

13. The stage check, three questions on my phone, my voice over it.
14. The star quest: the child pitches a job, the minutes land. Screen only.
15. The first check in and what the number does over a week.
16. The scripts fridge card: print, cut, stick.
17. The school lesson: the teacher's prep page, nothing to prepare.
18. The child's app: the five a day, the child's screen, no face.

Real life:

19. The five o'clock fight, at the kitchen table, camera on you.
20. The school gate line: a bit like Duolingo, but for getting your kid ready for a phone.
21. The paddock: why we moved, and why the questions did not go away.
22. The chart on the fridge, hands only.
23. Cooking while the timer runs: the deal, not the nag.
24. Sunday evening, loading the week. Build in public.

## 12. The Friday review and the decision rules

Twenty minutes with the tracker. One decision: KEEP, CHANGE or HOLD one
thing, with the evidence. Then:

- Visits do not become stage checks: fix the page, not the traffic.
- Stage checks do not finish: fix the reveal.
- Finished checks do not start trials: fix the day two and day three emails.
- Trials do not pay: stop adding traffic, fix day zero in the product.
- A post gets impressions and no stage checks twice: drop the format.
- A post moves stage checks twice: it is the format, and the only candidate for £100 after day thirty.
