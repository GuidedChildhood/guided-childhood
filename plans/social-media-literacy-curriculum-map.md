## The thesis (what every version carries)

Preparation over fear, in Justin's voice. Sixteen is a ramp, not a cliff. It is the activity and the child, not the device. We teach teens to see how the machine works, to run the settings, to read their own mood, and to handle the real scenarios, calmly and without a lecture. The evidence backs a calm, staged, agency first approach, and the classroom research is blunt that scare tactics and abstinence messaging backfire with this age group.

## The evidence base (named experts, disciplined)

- **Candice Odgers**, preparation over fear. Effects are small on average and causation is unproven, so prepare and support rather than ban. Our strongest anchor. Do not overstate her into "social media is harmless".
- **Amy Orben**, small effects but real windows of sensitivity (roughly girls 11 to 13, boys 14 to 15, both around 19). Blanket age rules are crude because sensitivity is developmental. The "wearing glasses" line is Orben and Przybylski 2019, not Odgers.
- **Andrew Przybylski**, the Goldilocks amount. Moderate use beats both zero and heavy. Target a sensible middle, not abstinence.
- **Sonia Livingstone**, the 4 Cs (content, contact, conduct, contract). The safest, most institutionally accepted spine, already used in 092. It becomes our risk scaffold.
- **Catherine Knibbs**, the nervous system and disclosure. Do not confiscate the device when a child discloses, it teaches them telling makes things worse. Verify the exact phrase "the telling plan" against her book before we brand it as hers.
- **Sue Atkins**, practitioner. Family digital agreement the child helps write, conversation scripts, connection over control. Use for parent tone and scripts, never for statistics.
- **danah boyd**, networked publics. The digital native myth is false, literacy must be taught explicitly. Cite for the social and developmental framing.
- **Haidt and Twenge**, the concern side held honestly. Teen mental health did worsen from the early 2010s (agreed), social media is plausibly one contributor especially for some girls (defensible), social media is the proven primary cause (not established). Never state more than the middle claim.

## The pedagogy (how all three versions are taught)

- **Seven beat Rosenshine**, which we already use. Recall, hook in small steps, worked example, check for understanding, guided practice, independent practice, review. The research confirms this maps cleanly onto Rosenshine's ten principles.
- **Non judgemental, scenario first, agency building.** Ground rules, distancing techniques (teach through characters not "raise your hand if this happened to you"), real dilemmas, discussion over transmission, help routes signposted every time. This is the PSHE Association standard and it is the opposite of scare tactics.
- **Same content, re cut three ways.** One idea (say, filtered images distort comparison) becomes: a parent co view prompt plus a conversation starter, a child facing interactive scenario they resolve themselves, and a full seven beat schools lesson with baseline and endpoint assessment.

## UK statutory alignment (the schools version's selling point)

- **RSHE is statutory.** The updated statutory guidance comes into force **1 September 2026** and specifically adds AI and chatbots, deepfakes, spotting fake and AI generated accounts, misogyny and incel culture, and sextortion. Building the schools version to the 2026 guidance means schools can adopt it to meet a duty that lands this September. That is the commercial hook.
- **Map to UKCIS Education for a Connected World** (eight strands: self image and identity, online relationships, online reputation, online bullying, managing online information, health wellbeing and lifestyle, privacy and security, copyright and ownership). Stage not age.
- **PSHE Association Programme of Study** and Computing national curriculum e safety at KS3, inside the school's KCSIE safeguarding duty.

## Age staging within 13+

- **13 (early adolescence).** Most peer sensitive and identity forming. Concrete, scenario based, social norm correction, strong scaffolding and support signposting. Modules 1 to 4, 7, 12.
- **14 to 15 (mid).** Deeper critical analysis, algorithms, misinformation, influencer and misogyny content, data trade offs. More debate and independent practice. Modules 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, and the start of 10.
- **16+ (late).** Rights and citizen lens, data rights, digital footprint and employment, consent law, supporting others. Least scaffolding. Modules 10 (full), 13, 14.
