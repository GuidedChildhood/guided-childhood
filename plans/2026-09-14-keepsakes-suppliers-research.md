# Keepsakes suppliers research

Date of research: 14 September 2026. Every URL below was searched on that date.

How to read the tags on every number:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| SNIPPET | The figure was quoted by the search engine from the supplier's own page (the URL given). The page itself could not be opened from this session because the sandbox proxy blocked every supplier domain, so the figure needs one click to confirm before it goes in an email or a spreadsheet. |
| UNVERIFIED | The figure comes from a secondary source (a blog, a guide, a third party), or no primary figure exists and only a range is known. Do not quote it to a supplier. |
| ARITHMETIC | Worked out from a tagged figure, with the sum shown. Only as good as the figure it came from. |

No number in this document was invented. Where a supplier only shows a calculator, the table says "calculator, no public price" and the next step is to run the calculator.

## Summary

The passport is the only product that must be printed one copy at a time, and three services already fit that shape: Peecho (Amsterdam, free API, A6 stapled brochure on the menu, prints in the nearest country to the family), Lulu (free API, saddle stitch 4 to 48 pages, prints in the UK, ships to the buyer), and Mixam UK (public API, from one copy, A6 stapled, plain packaging option). Doxzoo and Doxdirect print from one copy with no minimum but show no API, so they suit a manual founder run order rather than an automated one. Gelato does saddle stitch brochures but I could not confirm A6. Prodigi and Printful do photo books and notebooks, not stapled booklets, and Blurb has no format small enough, so those four are out for the passport.

The sticker sheet does not need to be one at a time. A bulk kiss cut run in the UK lands at about £1.10 a sheet ex VAT at 250 sheets (Mission Print, snippet), which supports the £4 shop price at over 3x before postage. Print on demand per sheet is possible (Printful, Avery) but costs several times more per sheet, so bulk is the right call and Justin posts them himself.

Plush is the long pole. UK makers quote 500 minimum per style (Brazier, Magic Kingdom) with a 5 to 6 month lead, Best Years wants a £250 refundable sample and 90 to 100 days, and Maua Gang in the UK goes as low as 50 pieces with a £180 to £300 sample. China direct is $7 to $12 a unit at 100 to 300 pieces plus $400 to $1,400 in set up and about $700 to $1,150 for EN71 testing per design. Five characters at MOQ is a real capital outlay, so a Makeship style pre order campaign (200 backers, 21 days, no upfront cost) matches the "campaign run" wording already in the catalogue and is the lowest risk first move.

## Safety and compliance

Applies to the plush only. The passport (paper) and sticker sheet (paper or vinyl) are not toys under the regulations, though stickers aimed at under threes would attract a small parts question; at 35 to 45 mm they are above the small parts cylinder and are sold to parents, so no toy testing is planned for them.

| Point | What applies | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Law | The Toys (Safety) Regulations 2011 for Great Britain. Whoever puts their own brand on the toy is the manufacturer in law and carries the duties below. | https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/toys-safety-regulations-2011/toys-safety-regulations-2011-great-britain (SNIPPET) |
| Marking | UKCA or CE marking, visibly and indelibly on the toy, its label or its packaging; where the toy is too small the mark can go on a label or leaflet. The UK government extended recognition of CE marking indefinitely for these goods from 1 October 2024, so a CE marked plush made for the EU market can be sold in Great Britain. To sell into the EU or Northern Ireland, CE is required. | Same GOV.UK page and https://www.gov.uk/guidance/using-the-ukca-marking (SNIPPET) |
| Paperwork | A Declaration of Conformity (UK DoC or EU DoC) plus a technical file, both kept for 10 years. Manufacturer name and address and a batch or serial reference on the toy or packaging. | GOV.UK page above (SNIPPET) |
