Justin, from the parent lesson player with a misspelled board in the video

## What is there today (read from the live database)
- `lessons`: 102 live lessons, all written for parents ("what every parent
  should know"), 25 to 40 per stage. The child app shows these same lessons to
  the child, with a `child_key_message`. The passport counts them ("0 of 25").
- `schools.school_lessons`: 32 pupil facing modules (EYFS 1, KS1 2, KS2 10,
  KS3 10, KS4 7, KS5 2), Oak structured, every one with an assessment and a
  parent note, many with a home code. Only the school app uses them.
- Video: one parent lesson has a clip (the algorithm one). The same clip is a
  school video beat. It has AI lettering on the board, misspelled. Seven more
  school clips carry board lettering and have not been checked by eye (the
  CDN is blocked from the build sandbox).
- 17 lesson completions ever. The parent side is not being used much.

## What proven apps do
- Khan Academy Kids, Duolingo: the child plays short lessons themselves on a
  path, with stars, and the adult sees progress.
- Be Internet Awesome, Common Sense Digital Citizenship: a school lesson with
  a family take home, so class and kitchen table say the same thing.
- Joint media engagement research: under about 7, learning sticks when a
  grown up watches with them and talks about it.

## Recommendation: the child learns it, the parent closes it
1. Child app lessons come from the school modules for their key stage, not
   the parent library. One a week, in the five a day, stars on the check pass.
2. The pass ticks the passport, and the parent gets that module's parent note
   as a one line "ask them this at tea" card.
3. Under 7 (EYFS and KS1) it opens on the parent's phone as a sit together
   lesson, because they cannot read it alone.
4. The parent library stays as the parent's own learning, and stops being
   the thing the passport counts.
5. Never trust words baked into a generated clip: regenerate with a blank
   board and put the words on as real text.
