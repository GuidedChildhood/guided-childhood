# "Ready": the Guided Childhood brand advert

9 October 2026. Justin asked for the music and feel of the John Lewis
Christmas adverts, made into the best marketing advert for Guided Childhood,
built on what the platform actually does and why, at the length that works.
Two researchers ran on 9 October: the John Lewis craft (music, structure,
effectiveness, what flopped) and the length that works by platform.

## The one idea

The John Lewis formula is one child, one small longing, years or days
compressed into ninety seconds, no dialogue, a song that lifts at the turn,
and a reveal that reframes everything you just watched, with the brand held
back to the last few seconds.

Our longing writes itself, and it is the one in THE-STORY.md section 1: a
child waiting for the day she gets a phone. Every parent has watched that
wait. The advert's twist is the John Lewis "Long Wait" twist turned round:
you think the film is about the day she finally gets the phone. It is about
the ten years of small evenings that made her ready for it.

The line it lands on is Justin's own (founder context, GREEN):
"Eventually the controls come off. What I want left behind is judgement."

## What the John Lewis research changed

- **Make the giver the hero** (The Long Wait, Man on the Moon, the
  "thoughtful giving" line the IPA papers credit). So the reveal is not the
  parent handing over a phone. It is the sixteen year old giving something
  back.
- **A loveable recurring character wins** (Monty, Edgar; System1 2025: John
  Lewis would have gained from owning a recurring character again). We have
  one: DiGi. The gold star runs through the whole film, first as the glow on
  the parent's phone, then as the gold star stickers on the chart.
- **Show the product** (2024 recovered from 3.6 to 4.6 stars by putting the
  store and the products back in, the highest brand fluency in a decade). The
  star chart, the passport and the phone with the star are real things the
  platform makes, so they are on screen, not hinted at.
- **No words, the song talks** (Long Wait, Bear and the Hare, Monty, Man on
  the Moon, Buster).
- **What flopped:** stories where the gift and the brand were faint (2021,
  2022), and a song with nothing to hold on to (2023).

## Rules this has to keep

- **Never a child presented as real, never a real child's face**
  (`brand/GDC_SOCIAL_VISUAL_SYSTEM.md` section 15). The family is the same
  original cartoon toy world as the two brains film, clearly animated, the
  way John Lewis did the Bear and the Hare and Monty the Penguin.
- **DiGi only from the approved art** (`public/digi-squad/DiGi-star.png` as
  the video reference), and here only as the glow on the parent's phone.
- **No invented screen.** The phone shows the star and a warm glow, never a
  fake interface.
- **No outcome claimed for a child.** The film shows a family's choices,
  not a promise.
- **No dashes anywhere.**
- **No paid generation without Justin's yes on the credit cost first**
  (section 15, rule 6).

## The music

The John Lewis sound (2010 to 2015 peak): a well known song slowed to a
piano or acoustic cover, a soft close vocal, usually a young British woman,
roughly 60 to 80 beats a minute. Verse one is voice and one instrument;
strings and a low pulse arrive at the turn, about two thirds in; the
arrangement drops near silence for the reveal, then resolves under the end
card. The lift is instruments swelling, not a key change.

A cover is out: it needs the publisher's sync licence (reported at tens to
hundreds of thousands for national use), and even copying a cover's
arrangement carries risk (the 2021 Portraits row). The originals John Lewis
tried (2020, 2023) charted worst. So the track must be an original in that
style, and it needs a hummable melody, the thing 2023 lacked.

| Time | The music |
|---|---|
| 0 to 26 | Solo felt piano, close and slightly unsure, about 70 beats a minute |
| 26 to 52 | A soft wordless vocal or hum joins the melody as the years pass |
| 52 to 64 | The lift: strings and a low pulse swell as she grows up |
| 64 to 72 | Drops almost to silence, one piano note at a time, as the box opens |
| 72 to 82 | The melody returns, full and warm, as the parent opens the card |
| 82 to 90 | Resolves on a single held chord under the end card |

How we get it is the open question (see the cost section). The music
generator in our toolset is restricted to game use, the HyperFrames
catalogue route is closed on this Mac, and a piano composed in code will
sound synthetic in a film that lives on its music.

## Length

From the length research (9 October, sources at the end of this file).

- **Hero, 90 seconds.** John Lewis runs about two minutes, but John Lewis
  buys TV, where nobody can skip. We are on owned and organic channels, where
  finishing rates fall after a minute (Vidyard: 66 per cent finish under a
  minute, 53 per cent across all lengths). Google's Honey Maid test: the
  longer cuts lifted brand favourability more than the 15. Apple's "Saving
  Simon" is the model: one long film, then a 60, then short cuts.
- **Hook, even on the hero.** Google found an emotional or suspenseful first
  five seconds raised recall, and a calm opening increased skips. So the
  film opens on the doorway at 9pm, not on a slow establishing shot.
- **Binet and Field.** No prescribed length, but emotional campaigns are
  nearly twice as likely to drive top profit growth, and the split is roughly
  60 brand, 40 activation. The hero is the brand half; the short cuts are
  the activation half.

| Cut | Where | Job | Hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90 s hero | YouTube, the website, launch post on Facebook and LinkedIn | Brand, the story people share | The doorway at 9pm inside five seconds |
| 60 s | Facebook feed, LinkedIn native, YouTube paid | Brand and consideration | The tension inside five seconds |
| 30 s | Reels and feed paid, TikTok, LinkedIn paid | Consideration, the paid workhorse | Open on the twelve year old at the kitchen door, the middle of the story |
| 15 s | Reels, Stories, TikTok, retargeting | Sign up | One moment (the charger at sixteen), then the stage check |
| 6 s | YouTube bumper, retargeting | Reminder | The star on the phone, the line, the logo |
| 20 to 45 s vertical | Instagram, Facebook Reels, TikTok organic | Reach and shares | Silent with captions, opens on the peak |

Every cut in 16:9, 1:1 and 9:16, captions burned in on the social ones,
because most people watch with the sound off.


## Cost and the route

Higgsfield credits on 9 October: 817. A Seedance shot of eight seconds costs
24 as a 480p draft, 56 at 720p, 96 at 1080p. The film is thirteen shots.

| Route | Credits | Result |
|---|---|---|
| A. All thirteen at 720p, a check after the first three for character consistency | about 730 | Sharp enough for social and YouTube; leaves about 90 for retries |
| B. Thirteen drafts, then the six hero shots at 1080p | about 744 | Sharpest where it matters; the other seven soft |
| C. Thirteen drafts only, to judge the story first | about 312 | A watchable rough cut; finals bought later |

Recommendation: C first. A rough cut tells us if the story lands before the
bulk of the credits are spent, and the drafts can be finalised one by one.

The music, in order of quality: a licensed library track (Artlist, Musicbed
or Epidemic Sound, roughly £10 to £20 a month, search "emotional piano
ballad, soft female vocal, 70 bpm, builds") for the film now; a commissioned
original from a composer for the paid campaign, so the brand owns its song
the way John Lewis owns its sound.
