# Common Sense's new curriculum, a review and how we use it

Written 19 August 2026. A competitive review of Common Sense Education's relaunched curriculum, mapped
against our own 15 module 13+ social media curriculum, with what we borrow, where we are
differentiated, and the legal line. No dashes.

**Source caveat, read first.** commonsense.org and commonsensemedia.org are blocked from our research
environment, so every fact here is from Common Sense's own press releases and independent reviews as
surfaced in search, not first hand page reads. Anything marked VERIFY needs a human to confirm on the
live site or from a downloaded lesson.

## The one line

Common Sense just rebuilt itself into a free, teacher facing, slide based curriculum and retired the
games children actually played. We are the opposite on every one of those axes, parent facing, child
interactive, UK anchored, and we lead with how the machine works rather than how the child should
behave. That is not a weakness to close, it is the moat to press.

## 1. What they launched, dated

- **August 2025, the flagship.** They relaunched the fifteen year old Digital Citizenship Curriculum
  as the **Digital Literacy and Well-Being Curriculum**, K to 8. About **140 lessons**, roughly 13 to
  18 per grade, each a **20 minute** slide based mini lesson. Built with Harvard Project Zero,
  Georgetown and Cornell. (Common Sense press release, Aug 2025.)
- **August 2026, now.** The updated **grades 9 to 12** lessons are landing this month. The legacy K
  to 12 Digital Citizenship Curriculum is **archived December 2026**. So the thing that "just
  launched" is the top end of the same relaunch. (Press release.)
- **The six themes are retained** as the spine, reframed around human skills, kindness, focus, self
  regulation: Media Balance and Well-Being, Privacy and Security, Digital Footprint and Identity,
  Relationships and Communication, Cyberbullying Digital Drama and Hate Speech, News and Media
  Literacy. **AI literacy is now woven through** rather than bolted on.
- **They killed the games.** Digital Passport (grades 3 to 5) retired June 2025, Digital Compass
  (grades 6 to 8) retires June 2026. The new product is slides and lesson plans, not a thing a child
  plays. (digitalpassport.org, digitalcompass.org.)
- **AI, separately.** AI Literacy collections for grades 6 to 12 (US) and Years 7 to 13 (UK), plus a
  parent facing family toolkit, Using AI Wisely for School Success, funded in the US by a $1M
  Salesforce grant.

## 2. Access, the honest version

- **Free, but educator gated.** No paywall, but the lesson materials (slides, handouts, plans) unlock
  only behind a free Common Sense Education account. Landing pages are public, the assets are not.
- **Parents do not get the classroom curriculum as is.** The family path is separate and lighter, the
  FACE program, Family Media Agreements, Family Tips in many languages, and the AI family toolkit.
- **US first, UK thin but real.** UK Digital Citizenship and a UK AI Literacy Years 7 to 13 exist,
  informed by UK educators and clinical psychologists, independently evaluated by the LSE in June
  2024. There is no UK equivalent of the US K to 8 flagship.
- **How we would legally see the gated material if needed.** Justin creates the free educator account
  himself, downloads the specific lesson PDFs, and drops them in the Guided Childhood Drive folder or
  the repo for review. We do not log in as him and we do not scrape the site. See the legal line at
  the end.

## 3. Their pedagogy and theory, the parts worth learning from

- **Rings of Responsibility.** Their core scaffold, three rings, Self, Community, World. A clean,
  memorable mental model that pushes a child from personal safety out to ripple effects. This is
  genuinely good and we have no younger age equivalent.
- **Digital Dilemmas plus thinking routines.** Middle and high lessons open on an authentic tension
  and use named routines (for example Feelings and Options) to structure the talk. Straight from
  Project Zero.
- **Implementation Guide.** A whole school roll out and scope and sequence resource for leaders. Table
  stakes for a serious schools offer.
- **Family Media Agreement (FACE).** Grade banded family contracts, printable, multilingual, for
  parent evenings and sending home.
- **Theory base.** Project Zero at Harvard, Howard Gardner as senior advisor, the Good Play Project,
  and Educating with Digital Dilemmas led by Carrie James and Emily Weinstein. SEL mapped to CASEL.

## 4. The critiques of Common Sense, and why they matter to us

The consistent academic critique is the most useful thing in this whole review, because our product
already answers it.

- **It individualises.** Using Westheimer and Kahne's citizen typology, scholars place Common Sense
  almost entirely in the personally responsible box, safety, etiquette, self regulation, with a
  tenuous link to actual citizenship, and argue it is more online safety than citizenship. (What Kind
  of Digital Citizen, ERIC; Estelles and Doyle, Review of Education, 2025.)
- **It responsibilises.** It shifts the burden onto the child and parent, and stays largely silent on
  platform business models, attention design, data extraction and regulation as the place to
  intervene. (LSE Media blog.)
- **Thin behaviour change evidence.** Knowledge and attitude gains are shown, durable behaviour change
  is not well evidenced. The nearest independent trial of a similar programme, Google's Be Internet
  Awesome, found gains in knowledge and self efficacy but no impact on privacy behaviour, civility,
  cyberbullying or talking to parents. The definitive independent test of Common Sense's own
  curriculum (UNH CCRC) is not yet published.
- **Classroom bound.** Effectiveness depends on trained teachers and whole school buy in, and the LSE
  found under resourced schools least likely to teach it, which widens the divide.

**Why this matters.** Our module 1 is literally How the machine works, the attention economy, the
business model, dark patterns and the ICO Children's Code. We open on the structure the critics say
Common Sense omits. And we are a parent product used at home, ten minutes a day, which is the exact
gap left by a classroom bound programme that under resourced schools do not reliably teach. The
critique of the market leader is a description of our differentiation.

## 5. Head to head against our 15 modules

Our curriculum is social media 13+, fifteen modules, Rosenshine seven beat, mapped to statutory RSHE
and UKCIS, three versions each (schools in depth, and shorter home and child versions).

| Where we are clearly ahead | Where they are clearly ahead |

## 6. What we borrow, ranked and actionable

At the level of ideas only, never their copy. See the legal line.

1. **A young age scaffold like Rings of Responsibility.** We are strong 13+, weak below. Build a
   foundation stage mental model for ages 4 to 11, our own, in the Planet Friends and DiGi world, Self
   then family then the wider world, so the pathway has real substance at the young end.
2. **The family agreement, refined.** They validate our agreement you write together. Borrow the idea
   of age banded versions and a printable, and consider a second language given Natalia's Spanish and
   our bilingual family story.
3. **The 20 minute drop in format.** Our schools modules are about 55 minutes. Add a 20 minute drop in
   version of each, which is what a busy teacher or a home evening will actually use, and matches the
   format the market now expects.
4. **The dilemma opener.** Open more scripts and lessons on an authentic dilemma, the Project Zero
   move, it lifts discussion and it suits our scripts engine.
5. **AI woven through, not bolted on.** They put AI across the curriculum. We have module 15 on AI
   companions. Thread AI touches into more modules, the algorithm one, the media literacy one, the
   inner life one.
6. **A schools Implementation Guide.** For schools/ we need a scope and sequence and a roll out plan,
   this is table stakes and we should have our own.
7. **SEL mapping alongside RSHE.** For the schools sell, map our modules to SEL competencies too, not
   only RSHE and UKCIS, it widens the buyer.

## 7. Where we press the advantage, marketing and product

- **Founder and schools angle, honest.** The biggest name in this space just retired the games
  children actually played and doubled down on teacher slides. We built the opposite, the thing a
  child plays, for the parent, at home, that starts with how the machine works, not just how to
  behave. This is a strong LinkedIn post and a strong schools one liner. It is true and it is on
  thesis.
- **The structural lens is our moat.** Keep leading with the machine, the business model and the
  design, because that is precisely the axis the market leader is criticised for missing. Do not drift
  into pure personal responsibility content, that is their box.
- **Parent at home is the open field.** Their own weakness, classroom bound and unevenly taught, is
  our whole delivery model. Say so.

## 8. The gap we should own, honestly

We are thin below age 13. Common Sense's strongest, most researched work is K to 5. Our biggest
curriculum opportunity is a proper foundation stage set for ages 4 to 11, built in our world, with the
machine first lens from the start. That is the single most valuable thing this review points at.
