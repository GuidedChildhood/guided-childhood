# The launch plan: parents, schools and the playlist, day by day

Written 30 September 2026 for Justin. Covers Thursday 1 October to Friday 13
November 2026, then the horizon to Safer Internet Day on 9 February 2027.
Built from four research sweeps run today (parent competitors, school
competitors, the safe playlist market, and a council of fourteen marketers),
the distribution playbook of 11 July, the launch strategy of 18 August, the
founding beta kit, the schools launch pack of 23 September and the Social
Billboard parent plan written earlier today. Every claim carries a grade:
VERIFIED (primary page read today), LIKELY (secondary), UNVERIFIED (never
publish). No dashes anywhere. The Google Doc copy lives in Drive under Guided
Childhood Research, App Launch Marketing, with the tracker sheet beside it.

## 1. The recommendation

Run one warm launch, not three cold ones. The parent app leads, because it is
the product with a paywall, a proven hook and a proven post format. Schools
run underneath it as distribution, opened by the free lesson and by parents
who already use the app. The playlist runs on its own pages in its own voice,
as a record of checked channels, and touches Justin's LinkedIn once in six
weeks.

The whole first thirty days cost nothing. Every action points at one number,
stage checks started at guidedchildhood.com/starter-pack, and the week's work
is one unit: one script, one founder chapter, one research brick, cut for
each platform. Paid spending starts only for a post that has already moved
stage checks, and then at £100, not £1,000.

Targets by Friday 13 November, stated as targets and not forecasts: the 50
founder seats full, 1,000 stage checks in the window, five pilot schools
signed, and 100 playlist accounts with 20 parents who have opened a playlist
and told us something useful.

### The playlist

Parents' complaint has moved from scary videos in 2017 to the feed and the
slop in 2025 to 2026. Ofcom's May 2026 report: 35 percent of 11 to 17 year
olds met harmful content in a feed against 1 percent who searched for it,
and YouTube declined to change its feed when Ofcom asked in March. VERIFIED
via two summaries. The whitelist apps (WhitelistVideo at $6.99 a child a
month, Safe Vision) give control without judgement. The lists (Common Sense,
The Dad Vibes on Substack) give judgement without a method or a date. Nobody
sits where The Social Billboard says it sits: a stated, repeatable check,
dated, applied to the channels a child actually watches. The honest risk is
decay: a curated product dies the week it stops being re checked. The
lightest version that proves demand is a public page of checked channels
with the score and the date, three public YouTube playlists by age band, and
a weekly re check email.

## 4. The council's verdict

Fourteen marketers were put to the same brief and checked against their own
sources. Where at least four agreed:

1. Lead with the free script, not the app (Godin, Berger, Fishkin, Kennedy,
   Handley).
2. The stage check is the right lead magnet and belongs one tap from
   everything: every bio, footer, printable and first comment (Hormozi,
   Sharp, Fishkin, Chen, Duolingo).
3. Win small and dense before wide: one class, one school, one regional
   group (Godin, Chen, Greenwell and Fernyhough, Weiss).
4. Warm outreach before content, content before anything paid (Hormozi,
   Weiss, Fishkin).
5. Measure new stage checks, not impressions (Duolingo, Fishkin, Chen,
   Hormozi).

Two disagreements, settled: reach versus depth, depth wins for the first
month because reach needs money the budget rule forbids until the funnel is
proven; bold versus warm on social, warm wins, keeping Duolingo's one week
turnaround and DiGi as a character with a point of view.

Five risks they named, and the guard for each: the funnel leaks before paid
(instrument in week one, fix the worst drop each week); ban tribalism (ban
neutral in every sentence, companion never critic); the founder runs out of
evenings (one script a week is the unit, everything else is a cut of it);
scarcity reads as fake (the cap is in code, show the live count, never add a
deadline); social rises while stage checks do not (judge every post by stage
checks in the following 48 hours, drop the formats that only earn
impressions).

## 9. Measurement and the decision rules

One sheet, one row a day, in Drive beside this document. Columns: date, lane,
what went out, stage checks started, stage checks completed, trials, paid,
founder seats left, school emails, school replies, pilots, playlist accounts,
playlist activated, notes. Weekly on Sunday, monthly on the 30th.

The funnel and its rules, from the council and the Social Billboard plan:

- Visits do not become stage checks: fix the page, not the traffic.
- Stage checks do not finish: watch the drop between question one and the
  email, fix the reveal.
- Finished checks do not start trials: fix the day two and day three emails.
- Trials do not pay: stop adding traffic, fix day zero in the product.
- A post gets impressions and no stage checks twice: drop the format.
- A post moves stage checks twice: it is the format, and the only candidate
  for £100 of amplification after day thirty.
