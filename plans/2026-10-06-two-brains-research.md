## The timeline and what the evidence supports

1. **The cue.** Cuts, edits, sound onsets and movement pull attention
   automatically (orienting). Lang 2000, Journal of Communication 50(1).
   Anderson and Levin 1976, Child Development 47(3), ages 1 to 4. Grade:
   LIKELY, both paywalled, Lang's base is adults. Christakis 2004 is not used:
   McBee 2021 reran it 848 ways and only 19.6 per cent were significant.
2. **Anticipation.** Dopamine neurons code reward prediction error (Schultz,
   Dayan and Montague 1997, Science; Schultz 2016, Dialogues in Clinical
   Neuroscience). Dopamine mediates wanting, not liking (Berridge and
   Robinson 2016, American Psychologist 71(8)). Nobody has measured dopamine
   in a child watching video. VERIFIED.
3. **Autoplay.** Hiniker, Heung, Hong and Kientz 2018, CHI: three week trial,
   24 homes, preschool video player; the autoplay version extended viewing
   past the agreed limit and reduced self regulation. Hiniker 2016: 11 of 27
   parents named autoplay as the thing they fight. VERIFIED (direction, not
   minutes; paper paywalled).
4. **The brake.** Diamond 2013, Annual Review of Psychology 64: inhibitory
   control is disproportionately difficult for young children, maturing into
   adolescence. Casey, Tottenham, Liston and Durston 2005, TICS 9(3):
   association areas for top down control mature last. VERIFIED.
5. **Light.** Hartstein and others 2022, Journal of Pineal Research 72(2):
   36 children aged 3 to 5, one hour of light before bed; 5 to 40 lux
   suppressed melatonin 77.5 per cent. Higuchi 2014 and Lee 2018: children
   suppress far more than adults under the same light. Carter 2016, JAMA
   Pediatrics meta analysis: bedtime device use roughly doubles the odds of
   inadequate sleep. VERIFIED. Light from a TV across a room has never been
   measured, so the film says "evening light", never "the TV's blue light".
6. **Next morning.** Kelly, Kelly and Sacker 2013, Pediatrics 132(5): UK
   Millennium Cohort, 10,230 children, non regular bedtimes linked to worse
   behaviour at seven in a dose response pattern. Sadeh 2003: one hour less
   sleep over three nights changed attention at ages 9 to 12. VERIFIED.
7. **What helps.** Mindell, Li, Sadeh, Kwon and Goh 2015, Sleep 38(5):
   10,085 mothers, 13 regions, a nightly routine gave earlier bedtimes and
   longer sleep, dose dependent. AAP 2016, Pediatrics 138(5): no screens in
   the hour before bed. Hiniker 2016 diary: children were less upset when
   screen time was routine (2.84 vs 3.10) and when the technology ended it
   rather than a parent (2.98 vs 3.47). Warned transitions were more upset,
   so a two minute warning alone is not the fix. VERIFIED.

## Lines refused

A dopamine hit like a drug. Addicted. Rewires her brain or causes ADHD. The
TV's blue light switches off her melatonin. A two minute warning makes the
tantrum disappear.

## Age caveat

Every child sample is 0 to 5; Lang is adults. Six is the nearest age the
evidence reaches. The film shows mechanisms, never an outcome for this child.
