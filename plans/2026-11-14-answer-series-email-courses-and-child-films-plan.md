# The answer series, email courses and child films

Written 14 November 2026, from Justin's brief of 13 November. A plan and the
prompts to run it. Nothing here is built yet.

## What Justin asked for, in five lanes

1. **The answer series.** Twice a week, take a post from Family IT Guy, Dr
   Becky Kennedy or Catherine Knibbs and make our own version: the hook, the
   research told so it does not scare, then the solution.
2. **Email courses, not downloads.** Each topic ends in a short sequence of
   emails that teaches the parent how to solve the problem, swapped for their
   email address.
3. **The parent problem sweep.** Find what parents are actually stuck on, from
   Reddit, Mumsnet and Facebook: Roblox, Messenger, WhatsApp, the first phone,
   homework online, Google and so on.
4. **Child lesson films.** Start releasing the lesson videos and animations we
   have made, find the skills and routines in them, and make better ones that
   teach the child and the parent together.
5. **The research pop Reel.** A Reel where the piece of research floats in
   over the headline with its details, and every story the three creators use
   is checked closely first.

I have read "bi weekly" as twice a week. If it means once a fortnight, the
plan is the same at a quarter of the pace.

## The one idea that makes this manageable

**One verified topic pack feeds everything.** The Roblox pilot proved the
shape: six lenses, every citation checked, one cleared figure list, then every
format written from that list and nothing else. So the unit of work is a
topic, not a post. Each topic produces:

| From one topic pack | Goes into |
| --- | --- |
| The answer post (LinkedIn) | The daily pipeline, with a GDC number as normal |
| The research pop Reel and a Short | The family account and Shorts, same GDC number, own asset id |
| The carousel | The ladder session |
| A seven day email course, one short email a day | The email programme (Mailchimp journey by tag) |
| One child lesson film with a parent note | The lesson player, and released publicly once chosen |
| A refreshed product guide or script | The app, on Justin's approval only |

Twice a week is two posts, not two new topics. A topic takes a full research
and checking pass, so the honest pace is **one new topic a week**, giving two
posts a week from it (the answer post and a second angle), plus the Reel.

## Where I disagree or need a decision

Advice from outside, including James Smith's, goes through the feedback
filter. It is input, not instruction.

1. **Email course over download: adopt, with one change.** It fits us. We
   already send a sequence and a sequence teaches better than a PDF. The
   change: the funnel today is one hook everywhere, "What stage is your
   child? Three questions, no sign up." My recommendation is to keep that as
   the call to action on every post, and offer the topic course on the result
   page, matched to the child's stage. One funnel, one number to count. The
   alternative is to send posts straight to a course sign up, which is a
   second funnel and breaks the "no sign up" promise in the hook. Justin's
   call. I also do not hold what James Smith actually said, so please send
   the link.
2. **Naming.** Family IT Guy may be named (13 November). Dr Becky Kennedy and
   Catherine Knibbs are practising clinicians, and the house rule since July
   is that no living clinician is named beside our advice. So their posts are
   answered by topic and argument, with their work cited only in the research
   file. If Justin wants them named too, that is a change to the rule and his
   to make.
3. **"Our own version" means our own.** Their topic and the parent question
   underneath, never their words, footage, look or structure copied across.
   A short quote with a link is the limit.
4. **Not every post of theirs is ours to answer.** Dr Becky's work is mostly
   general parenting. We only answer a post where the product has a proof
   path. A topic with no proof path does not become content.
5. **Collecting their Instagram.** Justin allowed collection from Family IT
   Guy's account for analysis on 13 November. This brief extends that to the
   other two accounts, same limits: analysis only, nothing republished, slow,
   stop at any warning. Instagram's terms forbid automated collection, so the
   risk is to the logged in account.
6. **Facebook groups.** Most parent groups are private. We read public pages
   only. Mumsnet and Reddit are open and are where the sweep starts.
7. **Child films.** I have not yet checked which films are rendered and
   finished as opposed to scripted. The first job in that lane is an honest
   inventory. No child film claims an outcome, and none goes out with a
   synthetic person presented as real.
8. **Where child films are released.** YouTube, the family account, or inside
   the product only. Not decided.

## The prompts

Each is written to be pasted into a fresh session. They share the same rules
block, given once here and referred to as THE RULES.

### THE RULES

```
1. Read THE-STORY.md sections 1 to 3 first. Every piece must relate to what
   the product actually does and end at the stage check: "What stage is your
   child? Three questions, no sign up."
2. Research is told so a parent finishes more capable, not more afraid. Never
   scare a parent or a child. Never claim an outcome for a child. We claim
   clarity, a calmer conversation and a pathway.
3. Never allow or deny. Always a calibrated pathway.
4. Every figure carries what it counts and its source. Only figures from the
   topic's cleared list. A finding with no proof path in the product never
   becomes content.
5. No dashes in any copy. UK English. Justin's voice: warm, plain, direct.
   Run `npm run ai-tells` on every pack.
6. Family IT Guy may be named. No clinician is named beside our advice. Never
   attribute words to anyone without the link and the exact quote.
7. Never a synthetic person presented as a real parent or child. Never an
   invented app screen. Real brand assets only.
8. Never post, schedule, send or publish. Justin posts. A GDC number is
   claimed only through the register.
9. Report in three parts: what changed, whether it worked, what is needed
   from Justin.
10. Every piece must pass this test: could a parent get this from a chatbot,
    shorter and just as good? If yes, it is the wrong piece. What we have
    that a chatbot does not is a checked figure list, a real product screen
    and Justin's own vantage point.
```

### Prompt A, the creator breakdown (run per account)

```
CREATOR BREAKDOWN

Account: [family_it_guy | the Dr Becky account | the Catherine Knibbs account]
Follow THE RULES in plans/2026-11-14-answer-series-email-courses-and-child-films-plan.md.

Justin has Instagram open and logged in. He has approved collecting from this
one account for analysis only. Go at a human pace. If Instagram shows a
warning or a block, stop and tell him. Collect from this account only.
Nothing collected is republished or reused in any asset. A post you could not
see is logged NOT SEEN. Keep a count of posts seen against posts on the
account and never claim full coverage without it. Do not post, like, follow,
comment or message.

For each post record: link, date, format, the hook in the first three
seconds, every factual claim word for word, the advice given, and any
commercial interest shown.

For each claim give a verdict against our verified packs: SUPPORTED,
UNSUPPORTED, NO DENOMINATOR, OUT OF DATE or NOT CHECKED. A claim no pack
covers goes to a citation check before it gets a verdict.

For each post give our response class: AGREE, AGREE WITH CONTEXT, PRACTICAL
EXTENSION, EVIDENCE CHALLENGE, ALTERNATIVE INTERPRETATION, FACTUAL CORRECTION
or INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE. Never force a disagreement. If they are right, say
so.

Then say whether the product has a proof path for the topic. If it has none,
mark it NOT OURS and move on.

Output: a register of every post seen, and a ranked list of the ten posts
most worth answering, each with the parent question underneath it.
```

### Prompt B, the parent problem sweep

```
PARENT PROBLEM SWEEP

Follow THE RULES in plans/2026-11-14-answer-series-email-courses-and-child-films-plan.md.

Find what UK parents of children aged 4 to 16 are actually stuck on with
phones, games and apps. Read open pages only: Mumsnet, Reddit (for example
the parenting and UK parenting communities), public Facebook pages, and the
questions under popular posts. Do not log in and do not enter private groups.
Store no usernames and no personal details. Quote no more than a short
phrase, to show the wording parents use.

Start from these and add what you find: Roblox, WhatsApp and group chats,
Messenger, the first phone, homework online, Google and AI for schoolwork,
YouTube, bedtime and sleep, gaming spend.

For each problem give: how often it came up in what you read (a count of
threads, with the dates, never a claim about all parents), the parents' own
words for it, the age it bites, what parents have already tried, whether we
hold a verified pack on it, and the proof path in the product.

Rank them by how often they come up and how well we can answer. Mark any with
no proof path as NOT OURS. Output one ranked log, and name the next three
topics to research.
```

### Prompt C, one topic into every format (the production script)

```
TOPIC PRODUCTION

Topic: [for example, the first WhatsApp group]
Follow THE RULES in plans/2026-11-14-answer-series-email-courses-and-child-films-plan.md.
The model to copy is the Roblox pilot:
briefings/2026-11-13-roblox-predators-ban-v2.html and
content/packs/2026-11-13-roblox-pilot/pack.md.

Step 1. Research. Run the kids-research skill on the topic: six lenses, the
contradiction map, then every citation checked against its primary source.
Nothing is written until the checked version is saved. If a verified briefing
already exists, re check its figures for anything that has changed since.

Step 2. The cleared list. One table of the only figures and quotes allowed,
each with exactly what it counts and its source, and a do not quote list.

Step 3. Write every format from that list and nothing else:
  a. The answer post for LinkedIn: hook on a real number, the research told
     calmly, the honest concession, the solution, a real question to close.
     Sources go in the first comment.
  b. A second angle for the week's other post.
  c. The research pop Reel: silent, vertical, the headline first, then the
     figure floating in over it with what it counts and its source, then the
     one thing to do, ending at the stage check. Time every line to the
     reading rule.
  d. A 45 second Short to camera for Justin.
  e. A seven day email course, one email a day. Each one reads in 60 to 90
     seconds, teaches one thing, gives one thing to do that evening, and
     stands alone with at most one link, to the product. Day 1 what is
     actually going on. Day 2 what the evidence says, calmly. Day 3 the words
     to say. Day 4 the rule to agree together. Day 5 what to do when it goes
     wrong, and the first plain mention of what the product adds. Day 6 the
     child's side of it. Day 7 what comes next, and the offer once more. No
     invented urgency and no pressure lines.
  f. A child lesson film brief with a parent note: what the child learns to
     recognise, the routine they practise, and the line the parent says.
  g. A parent checklist, marked NOT READY until any menu path in it has been
     checked by a person in the app.

Step 4. Checks. Run `npm run ai-tells`. Put the story through the research
register and claim its GDC number the normal way. Say plainly what is
unverified and what is still open.

Do not publish, schedule or send. Deliver the briefing and the pack.
```
