# Simon Squibb's ideas on school and work, weighed against our objectives

Written 24 September 2026. Justin asked whether we could build a separate
module on preparing children for work in the ways school does not, one Simon
Squibb would value, wrapped in AI and preparing for the future. This runs his
ideas through `.claude/skills/feedback-filter` first, as CLAUDE.md requires.
Nothing is built from it yet.

Research: his own words, with a link and a date, gathered from his posts,
interviews and publisher pages. Where only a journalist's summary exists, the
table says so. His unsourced statistics are listed and are not reused.

## What he says, in one paragraph

School trains obedient employees who memorise, when it should teach children
to solve problems, sell, handle money and ask for help. He left school at 15,
is dyslexic, and home educates his son. On phones he is close to us. He uses
social media *with* his son, teaches him to create rather than consume, and
says the answer is education, not a ban. On AI he thinks jobs will go to "the
person using the robot", and he tells children to build with it.

## What already exists, so nothing is built twice

- **ks5-21** "Digital identity and the future of work", Years 12 to 13. It covers how AI changes tasks, the skills that last, and the online record as a portfolio. This is the AI and future of work half, but only for sixth form.
- **ks5-20** "AI mastery and data rights": use it, check it, own it.
- **ks3-24** "Is it doing my thinking?": use AI without handing over the thinking.
- **ks2-25** "Stay the maker": the child keeps the making.
- **ks2-06** covers how algorithms work. **ks4-28** covers money and the odds. **ks3-13** covers scams.

**The gap is real.** Nothing below Year 12 has a young person make something for real people, test it, and learn what AI can and cannot do for them while they do it.

## The module this points to (a sketch, not a build)

**Working title:** What problem would you solve?

**Who:** KS3, Years 8 and 9, before GCSE options. This is also the age his Puffin workbook leads into: it is aimed at the move to secondary school, per the publisher.

**The tool they leave with.** Three moves:
1. Name a problem from your own life.
2. Ask three real people you know.
3. Build the smallest version that helps one of them.

**Where AI sits:**
- A helper at each step, under the ks3-24 rule: struggle first, then use it to check or improve.
- ks5-20's "use it, check it, own it" applies throughout.
- It never does the finished thing.
- The honest future of work line, sourced when the module is built: AI changes tasks inside jobs, and the person who can direct it and check it has the edge.

**At home.** A rose and thorn at dinner, and the parent as first customer. Any money goes through the parent.

**What it will not do:**
- tell anyone to ignore their parents;
- rank school against business;
- use a statistic without a source;
- send children to strangers online for customers;
- use his name on slides.

**Evidence to gather before it ships:**
- the platforms' current age rules for earning money;
- a sourced account of how AI changes tasks, such as the World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs report, read in the original;
- where it could sit in the PSHE and careers frameworks, as a note for a teacher. This is optional now that it stands alone (decision 4 below), because it no longer widens the scheme.

**Size:** large. It is one standalone lesson, about 30 slides, built the same way as ks3-24.

## How it stands alone

- **The same lesson format and the same player** as the scheme: a row in
  `schools.school_lessons` like any lesson. Nothing new is built to teach it,
  and it can join the scheme later with one change if Justin decides to add it.
- **Its own free link**, opened the way the ks3-12 taster is, so Simon Squibb
  can open it without a school code. It is unlisted: no scheme page changes.
- **Kept off the scheme.** It is not in the curriculum manifest
  (`shared/schools-curriculum.ts`), which already keeps it out of the module
  count, the curriculum map, the tracker, the RSHE mapping, the DSL hub and the
  passport. The print room lists every live row, so it needs a filter there,
  and every count of `school_lessons` rows in the database gets the same check.
- **Its mirror sits outside `content/modules`**, so the scheme guards do not
  count it. The module contract runs on it directly, without the passport
  and RSHE rules.
- **Its own bar, not the taster's.** The taster bar sells the scheme, and this
  lesson is not a sample of the scheme.
