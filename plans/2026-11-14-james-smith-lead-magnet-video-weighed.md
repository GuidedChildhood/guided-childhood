# James Smith on lead magnets, weighed

14 November 2026. Justin pasted the transcript of the James Smith video he
meant: stop sending a PDF, send a short email a day instead. This is the
right video. (The earlier one, weighed in
`2026-11-14-seven-rules-video-weighed.md`, was a different speaker.)

Weighed through the feedback filter. Advice is an input, never an
instruction. His audience is fitness coaches selling coaching. Ours is a
parent with a child and a problem tonight. His sales figures, including "day
19 is the best day", are his own and unchecked. We repeat none of them.

## Point by point

| His point | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find the bottleneck in the funnel before anything else. Usually it is leads | Adopt | We already count stage checks by source. The honest next step is to name our own narrowest point with real numbers before building more content |
| Aim for 20 percent of your following in a list you can email | Park as a yardstick | A rule of thumb, not evidence. Useful once we know Justin's follower and list numbers |
| Never send a PDF as the lead magnet. Nobody goes back to it | Adopt | It is why the plan chose a course over a download. Our printables are different: they are things a family uses on the fridge, not the price of an email address, and they stay |
| A free programme is either bad, so they will not buy, or complete, so they need not | Adapt | The course solves one problem this week. It must show plainly what the product adds after that: the daily plan, the star quest, DiGi for the next argument. It must not pretend to be the whole answer |
| One short email a day, 60 to 90 seconds, one thing to do, easy to find again | Adapt, and it changes the plan | The plan had five emails spread over ten days. Changed to seven, one a day for a week, each readable in about a minute with one thing to do that evening. A week, not thirty days: a parenting problem is not a training block, and a month of daily email to a tired parent is how you get unsubscribed |
| Make the offer after day five | Adopt | Day five and day seven, said once each, plainly |
| Do not send them to Instagram or YouTube from the email | Adopt | Each email stands alone. One link at most, to the product |
| Close them quickly because the wish to change fades | Adapt | Start the course the moment they ask for it. No invented urgency. The founder rate cap of 50 is real and may be stated as it is |
| "I will not email you tomorrow unless you buy" | Decline | A pressure line. Not our voice and not how we treat a parent |
| Leave them wanting more. Brief, regular | Adopt | Already the voice rule |

## What changes

1. The email course in the plan becomes **seven daily emails**, not five over
   ten days. Prompt C, step e, is rewritten.
2. Before more content is built, **name the bottleneck with real numbers**:
   people reached, stage checks started, stage checks finished, emails given,
   paid. Justin's follower count and list size are needed for this.
3. Nothing changes in the funnel Justin chose. The stage check stays the call
   to action. The course is the reason to leave an email on the result page.
