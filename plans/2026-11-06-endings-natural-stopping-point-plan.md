# 6 November 2026: endings at a natural stopping point, not a warning

Evidence map C1 (Research Pack 09): Hiniker, Suh, Cao and Kientz, CHI 2016
(EV-130, children aged 1 to 5) found warned endings were followed by more upset;
endings went better at a natural stopping point or as part of a routine. Justin
asked for every line that still advises a warning to say instead: "End at a
natural stopping point, like the end of an episode, and make it part of the
routine. Tell them what comes next, not how long is left." Agreement clauses:
"We stop at the end of the episode or the level, then it's tea."
