# New service launch, research briefing, August 2026

Six web research streams, run in parallel and synthesised here: device and screen
use, mental health evidence, policy and regulation, platform and industry moves,
the benefit and demand side, and the competitor landscape. Prioritised 2026 then
2025. Every figure below traces to a primary source in the source list; verify the
exact wording and decimals against the linked originals before any goes into public
copy. No dashes in copy. Evidence held to the Odgers test throughout.

---

## 1. The moment, in one paragraph

Three waves have crested at once. The data turned (harm now tracks the pattern of
use, not the hours). The law turned (the UK committed to an under 16 ban for early
2027, made school phone bans statutory this September, and Ofcom moved to hard
enforcement, while France passed an under 15 ban and the EU shipped an age check
app). And a new fear arrived (AI companions, with child deaths, settlements and the
first usage caps on Meta). Parents are more anxious than at any point we have
tracked, and the market offers them exactly two answers: block it, or spy on it.
The prepare and teach corner, staged, trust based, parent and child together, school
and home as one journey, is genuinely open. That is our lane, and the window is now.

## 2. What is genuinely new since mid 2025

### The usage picture: it starts younger, and AI is inside it
- **Digital childhood now starts at age two.** Common Sense 2025 census: 40% of two
  year olds own a tablet, nearly 1 in 4 own a phone by age 8. Ofcom 2026: almost 1
  in 5 UK 3 to 5s own their own phone; 65% of parents of 6 month to 2 year olds say
  their child already goes online.
- **The first phone tips at the secondary move.** Ownership jumps from 56% of ten
  year olds to 83% of eleven year olds. This is exactly the Year 6 to 7 bridge we
  already built for.
- **AI companions went mainstream in a year.** Two thirds of US teens use AI
  chatbots (Pew, Dec 2025); 72% of teens have used an AI companion and 52% use one
  regularly (Common Sense). 1 in 10 UK 8 to 17s have used AI "as a friend or someone
  to talk to" (Ofcom 2026).
- **Rules exist, follow through does not.** 86% of parents set screen rules, only
  19% always stick to them (Pew). 64% of 8 to 14s are online between 11pm and 5am at
  least once a month (Ofcom Online Nation 2025). Sleep and night use are the sharpest
  felt pain.

### The evidence turn: pattern, not hours
- **Total screen time keeps failing to predict harm.** Xiao et al, JAMA June 2025,
  ABCD cohort of 4,285 children: baseline total screen time was not associated with
  later mental health outcomes; the addictive (compulsive, distress) trajectory was,
  carrying a risk ratio of 2.14 for suicidal behaviour. Roughly 1 in 3 children were
  on an increasing addiction path from age 11.
- **The one causal handle points the same way.** A 2025 meta analysis of 10 RCTs
  (N=1,491) found reducing social media use cut depressive symptoms, g=0.25, a modest
  effect, and reduction worked about as well as full abstinence.
- **It is who, and what kind.** Nature Human Behaviour, June 2025 (3,340 UK
  adolescents with clinical assessment): teens with mental health conditions use
  more, compare more, and feel feedback harder, so use is often a marker of prior
  vulnerability. Body image harm is driven by idealised video and appearance focused
  engagement, not total time (Body Image meta analysis, 2025).
- **Adversity sits upstream.** Kovacic and Orso (Rev Econ Household 2025): childhood
  adversity predicts intensive adult social media use, mediated by loneliness. The
  screen is where an older wound shows.

### AI companions: the freshest, least contested harm frontier
- Three independent 2026 studies converge: Stanford (companions worsen loneliness
  for isolated users), Drexel (teen overreliance on Character.AI), Aalto (2,000
  Replika users, rising distress and pullback from human connection over two years).
- The counter literature here is thin, unlike the older social media question. This
  is where fresh harm evidence and low contrarian pushback meet, so a service can act
  without overclaiming.

### Regulation: the wave, and its cautionary tale
- **UK.** Under 16 ban announced 15 June 2026 (in force targeted early 2027, going
  further than Australia on livestreaming and stranger contact); school phone bans
  statutory from 1 September 2026, inspected by Ofsted; RSHE makes AI literacy,
  deepfakes and AI imagery mandatory from 1 September 2026; Ofcom in hard enforcement
  (90+ investigations, six fines).
- **International.** Australia's under 16 ban in force since 10 Dec 2025 but widely
  evaded (8 in 10 under 16s still on, via VPNs, fake and borrowed accounts). France
  passed an under 15 ban (21 July 2026). EU shipped a privacy preserving age check
  app (April 2026). 58% of countries now ban phones in schools (UNESCO).
- **The lesson, and our thesis, in the data.** Australia is the sector wide proof
  that a ban is a start line, not a finish line. Kids stay online, so the job is to
  guide them, not just gate them.

### Platforms and the courts
- First jury verdict holding Meta and YouTube liable for addictive design (LA, Mar
  2026). New Mexico court imposed a 90 hour a month usage cap on Meta (Aug 2026).
  Character.AI and Google settled five teen harm suits (Jan 2026).
- An independent June 2026 audit found only 40% of platform youth safety features
  actually work as advertised and are findable by a child. This is the single most
  quotable fact for our positioning: the tools parents are told to trust mostly do
  not work.

## 3. The evidence balance, held honestly

The survivable position, defensible to a hostile expert:
- Associations between social media and youth mental health are real but small on
  average (r roughly 0.08 to 0.22), not the clear cause and effect a sweeping ban
  assumes. Odgers, Orben, Przybylski, Ferguson and Livingstone hold this line, and it
  is the mainstream expert reading.
- Bans are genuinely contested. The English SMART Schools study (Lancet Regional
  Health, Feb 2025) found school phone bans moved nothing on wellbeing, because total
  use was unchanged. The Norwegian quasi experiment found real benefit for girls.
  Both are true; do not cite one and hide the other.
- The defensible, current position is: harm concentrates in specific patterns
  (compulsive, appearance focused, sleep displacing) and specific vulnerable groups,
  benefits are real for many, and the biggest drivers (family stress, CAMHS
  shortage, economic insecurity, adverse childhood experiences) sit outside the
  phone. This is exactly our hidden thread, now better evidenced than a year ago.

## 4. The market: where everyone is, and the empty quarter

The market just consolidated toward surveillance. Aura (a 1.6bn identity firm)
bought Qoria (Qustodio, Linewize, Smoothwall, Family Zone), welding the home app to
a school filter in 32,000 schools. Capital is following the same bet: security and
identity (Aura), general AI parenting (Joy, 14m; Good Inside at 34m revenue and 100k
paid subscribers), and kid safe hardware comparatively starved.

Everyone clusters in two corners:
- **Monitor and block for the parent.** Bark, Aura, Qustodio, Canopy, BrightCanary,
  Bosco, Jiminy, and every kid safe phone (Gabb, Pinwheel, Troomi). All remove
  capability or watch the child. Teens increasingly experience it as spying and route
  around it, which quietly breaks the model as children age.
- **General emotional parenting for the parent.** Good Inside (Dr Becky), Joy. Proven
  that parents pay a subscription for an expert branded AI coach, but no screens
  pathway, no child facing product, no school arm.

The corners nobody owns:
- **Age staged preparation.** The industry is binary: block now, or hand over a full
  phone later. Nobody has productised the 4 to 16 ramp. Smartphone Free Childhood
  (85,000+ UK parents) created a nation that has delayed and now has no map for after
  the delay. That readiness cliff is unclaimed.
- **Education over eradication.** Almost everything removes or watches. Angel AI
  teaches, but only young kids inside its own app.
- **Parent and child together.** Good Inside coaches the parent, Angel occupies the
  child. Nothing does both sides of the same moment.
- **Non surveillance / trust.** No brand of scale wins on "we watch less and prepare
  more."
- **School plus home as one spine.** Aura owns both halves but bolted together. No
  one has a single staged journey from classroom to kitchen.
- **The AI companion moment.** The sharpest gap: peak fear, fresh evidence, empty
  answer shelf.

We already sit in the empty quarter. The staged pathway, the passport, the family
agreement, the balance report, the scripts, DiGi as a calibrated pathway not a
verdict, and the schools curriculum are the exact assets nobody else has assembled.

## 5. Service options for launch, ranked

Each fits education not eradication, preparation over fear, and builds on what we
already have rather than starting cold.

**Lead option. The AI companion strand (ride the sharpest fear, launch fast).**
A short, age staged module that teaches a child what an AI companion actually is,
why it feels like a friend, where the parasocial pull and the danger sit, and gives
the parent a script for the "my chatbot said" conversation. Timely (peak fear, fresh
2026 evidence, RSHE now mandates AI literacy from 1 September, thin contrarian
pushback), defensible, and almost entirely unserved. Can ship as a standalone that
pulls families into the wider pathway. We already started module 15 (AI companions)
in the curriculum, so this is a fast, credible first move.

**Option 2. The Readiness Pathway, aimed straight at the delay movement.**
Position the staged 4 to 16 pathway explicitly at the 85,000 Smartphone Free
Childhood parents: you delayed brilliantly, here is the ramp for what comes next.
Each stage unlocks a real capability only after the lesson is done and the family has
agreed the terms. Owns the readiness cliff nobody else claims. This is less a new
build than a repositioning of the core product to the warmest available market.

**Option 3. The trust based, shared balance report (the anti surveillance play).**
We just built the family wall balance report and made it a signal parent, passport,
child and DiGi all share. Productise and market that as the explicit opposite of the
monitoring dashboard: a weekly "how is our family doing" the child sees too, not a
covert log of what the child typed. Nobody at scale can copy it, because their whole
model is watching. Positioning we can own outright.

**Option 4. The living family agreement.**
Turn the family agreement (already built, written together, reviewed each term) into
the marketed spine: a living document that renegotiates at each stage, the child
earning new terms by completing lessons. Good Inside proved parents pay for
structured guidance through hard moments; this is the digital specific version they
do not offer.

**Option 5. One pathway, classroom to kitchen.**
Sell schools the staged curriculum and give every family the matching home strand for
the same child, same characters, same stages, so Tuesday's lesson has a script
waiting at home that night. Aura has both halves but bolted together; a single
continuous journey is a moat none of them can assemble without rebuilding. This is
the biggest prize and the slowest to stand up, so it is the twelve month goal, with
the AI companion strand as the wedge that opens the door.

## 6. Positioning and timing

- **Timing.** Launch into late 2026. The UK ban is announced but not yet biting,
  school bans land this September, RSHE lands this September. Parents are told change
  is coming but have no product to act on. That announcement to enforcement gap is the
  adoption runway.
- **Positioning line.** The ban will not parent for you. Or: the ban delays the
  apps, we build the judgement. Australia is the proof: 8 in 10 kids stayed online.
- **Ban resilience.** The age line is drifting (UK 16, France 15, Denmark 15, Spain
  16, EU coalition forming) and enforcement is contested. Key the product to the
  durable need, readiness and staged capability and families waiting together, not to
  a platform or a specific age, so the offer stays true whether the line lands at 15,
  16, in 2027 or later. The social_media_law flag we already have is the right
  instinct: make the wave a tailwind we can re point, not a date we bet on.
- **Proof stat to lead content with.** Only 40% of platform youth safety features
  actually work (June 2026 audit). The tools parents are told to trust mostly do not.
