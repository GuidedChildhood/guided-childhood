60 Second Summary

Nobody has published a rate. Every headline number about Roblox and predators is a raw count with nothing underneath it, and the one party that could publish the real rate, Roblox, has not. So "full of predators" and "safe" are both unproven.

What is proven is narrower and it is enough to act on. Adults and older teenagers have reached and harmed children through Roblox, including UK children. The route usually runs from the game to a private app such as Discord, Snapchat or WhatsApp, and Roblox's own lawyers say that move is the step they cannot see or report.

Roblox in November 2026 is not the Roblox that was tested in 2025. Chat now needs an age check, accounts are split into age tiers, and chat is off by default for the youngest. Those changes are real. They were also forced by regulators, they rest on a face estimate with a stated error of 1.4 years, and Ofcom's own panel found a 9 year old registered as 16 to 17.

Playing with people they have never met is the normal condition of children's gaming in the UK. More than half of children who game online do it. Fewer than half of parents set any rule about who their child plays with. That gap, not the app, is where a parent's plan does its work.

A ban lowers exposure. It also has a cost the evidence keeps pointing at: children say they stay quiet because they fear losing the device, and a child who is somewhere they are not meant to be is the child least likely to tell. No study has tested a staged, supervised path against a ban, so our position rests on reasoning and on what children say, not on a trial.

The honest answer for a parent is not yes or no. It is: who can message my child, where does that conversation go next, and will my child tell me when it goes wrong. That is a pathway question, and it is the one the product already answers.

Key Findings

1. The platform changed in 2026, and most public advice still describes the old one

Reliability 8/10

Roblox now splits accounts by checked age: Roblox Kids for ages 5 to 8, Roblox Select for 9 to 15, standard from 16. On Kids accounts all communication is off by default. On Select accounts direct chat is off by default. Chat needs an age check by face estimate or ID, voluntary from 19 November 2025 and enforced worldwide from early January 2026. If chat is on, a child aged 5 to 8 can chat with under 13s, a child aged 9 to 12 with under 16s, and a child aged 13 to 15 with ages 9 to 17. Note what that means: a 9 year old's band reaches 15 year olds. Roblox says every setting varies by region and does not state the UK position by name.

Supported: Economist, Clinician, Skeptic·Challenged: Child (beaten in practice)
Sources: S2, S3, S4, S5, S6

2. The route is game first, private app second, and Roblox says it cannot see the second step

Reliability 8/10

CEOP tells parents that gifts and trades in games can be used to build trust and to move a child to another platform, and that once chat moves off a monitored platform nobody is moderating it. Roblox's own letter to a US senator in April 2026 says a suggestion to move off platform "would likely not be reportable by Roblox". A UK case shows the path: in June 2026 an 18 year old was sentenced at Sheffield Crown Court after first contact on Roblox and a move to Discord. This is case evidence and guidance, not a measured rate.

Supported: Clinician, Historian, Skeptic, Academic, Child·Challenged: none
Sources: S7, S16, S17

3. Nobody has published a rate, so neither "full of predators" nor "safe" is supported

Reliability 8/10

The numbers in circulation are counts: 24,522 reports from Roblox to the US clearing house in 2024, 664 UK police reports mentioning the word Roblox in 2025, around 170 lawsuits. They sit on a base of 138 million daily accounts. Roblox's reassurance that its reports are 0.12 percent of the total is equally empty, because that total is dominated by automated image matches on services that allow image sharing, which Roblox chat does not. No source gives a like for like rate for Roblox against Snapchat, Discord or Fortnite.

Supported: Skeptic, Academic, Clinician, Historian·Challenged: none
Sources: S1, S8, S15

4. Safety and growth now pull in opposite directions inside Roblox's own accounts

Reliability 8/10

In the third quarter of 2026 Roblox reported 138 million daily active users, down 9 percent on the year, and 30 million monthly payers, down 16 percent. It booked 140 million dollars in nine months for settlements with US states over youth safety matters. The same letter says users who chat grew 24 percent in the quarter, that adults in the US spend more than 50 percent above under 18s, and that marketing aimed at adults rose nearly three times. Of users who had passed an age check, 34 percent were under 13. Only 64 percent of daily users worldwide had been checked, about 75 percent in the UK.

Supported: Economist, Skeptic·Challenged: none
Sources: S1

5. Playing with people they have never met is the norm, and it is the rule parents set least

Reliability 7/10

Ofcom reported in October 2026 that 55 percent of children who game online play at least sometimes with people they have never met offline, and 20 percent do so all or most of the time. 64 percent of parents of gaming children worry about strangers, the top worry. Only 43 percent set rules about who their child can play with, against 56 percent who set time limits. In the Children's Commissioner's April 2026 polling, 51 percent of 13 to 17 year olds who game had been sent a message by someone they did not know. Most of those strangers are other children. This is exposure, not grooming.

Supported: Academic, Clinician, Skeptic, Child·Challenged: none
Sources: S9, S20

6. UK police data neither convicts nor clears Roblox

Reliability 7/10

The NSPCC's request to police found 7,263 Sexual Communication with a Child offences recorded in 2024/25 by 44 UK forces. Police knew the platform in only 2,111 of them. Of those, 40 percent were Snapchat, 9 percent WhatsApp, 9 percent Facebook and Instagram. Roblox is not named. That does not clear it: the platform recorded is where the messages were sent, not where the child was found. The one Roblox figure, from ITV News in July 2026, is a keyword count: 664 reports mentioning Roblox in 2025 against 355 in 2024, from 31 of 43 forces, with 46 percent of the sampled logs describing sexual offences against a child and alleged victims from age 5.

Supported: Clinician, Skeptic, Academic, Historian·Challenged: none
Sources: S14, S15, S23

7. Children beat the age checks and the controls, and friends show them how

Reliability 7/10

In Ofcom's Children's Media Lives 2026, a panel of 17 children, a 9 year old had his Roblox age set to 16 to 17 after someone else did the face scan, and had over 150 Roblox friends, many unknown offline. He said he friended people "so I don't feel that lonely". A 13 year old held an account registered as 21 to unlock voice chat. In a government survey of 2,299 children aged 11 to 17, 39 percent said they had got around an age check at least once, and 59 percent of those learned how from friends their own age. The panel shows how it happens. It cannot show how often.

Supported: Child, Academic, Skeptic·Challenged: Economist (rules as written)
Sources: S10, S21

8. The stranger is not always a stranger, and not always an adult

Reliability 7/10

In the ONS survey of adults in England and Wales, 33.2 percent of those sexually abused as children named a stranger, rising to 44.2 percent for non contact abuse by adults and falling to 12.2 percent for rape or assault by penetration. For online offences recorded by police in 2024, a stranger was the suspect in 35 percent where the relationship was known. A US national study found perpetrators of online child sexual abuse were under 18 in 29.9 percent of cases and known offline in 62.0 percent. So "it is always someone they know" is wrong, and so is the middle aged stranger as the only picture.

Supported: Historian, Academic, Clinician·Challenged: none
Sources: S23, S24, S25

9. Roblox is outside the UK social media ban and inside a second, separate restriction

Reliability 7/10

The government's progress statement says social media companies will be banned from offering services to under 16s and names six, none of them Roblox. Separately it says under 16s "will not have access to risky functions on services like gaming services", meaning communication with strangers, and that this will not stop children playing multiplayer games. First restrictions are expected in spring 2027 and the regulations are not yet laid. Ofcom sent Roblox a binding information request in March 2026 and in May reported that Roblox had committed to let parents switch off direct chat entirely for under 16s. We found no formal Ofcom investigation or fine.

Supported: Economist, Clinician·Challenged: none
Sources: S12, S30

10. Children say fear of losing the device keeps them quiet, but nobody has counted it

Reliability 6/10

The Children's Commissioner's December 2025 guide, built from focus groups with 38 children aged 13 to 18, reports that children "worry about losing their phones or having them taken away as punishment, and that stops them asking for help". CEOP's advice to a parent after disclosure is to lead with "I don't blame you in any way; I blame the abuser". The only behavioural figure we found is from a Roblox funded study of neurodivergent young people: 39 percent of those who had a problem while gaming told a parent. There is no UK percentage for children staying silent for fear of losing the game, and no study of disclosure after a device is removed. Do not publish a number for this.

Supported: Clinician, Child, Skeptic·Challenged: Academic (qualitative, teenagers only)
Sources: S18, S19, S31

11. Restriction lowers exposure and costs something, and the staged path has never been trialled against a ban

Reliability 6/10

A survey of 6,400 parents in eight European countries found restrictive mediation went with fewer online risks and fewer opportunities. A 2023 review of 40 studies found little support for parental controls as a stand alone strategy. History adds case studies, not measurements: when MSN closed its chat rooms in 2003 the contact moved to private messaging, and when a default was changed, as the US regulator required of Fortnite in 2022, the specific fault was fixed. Where meeting strangers was the whole product, as with Omegle, closure was right. Anyone who says bans do not work is overclaiming as much as anyone who says they solve it.

Supported: Historian, Academic·Challenged: Skeptic (case studies only)
Sources: S27, S28, S36

12. Asked what would make them feel safer, children put a ban last

Reliability 6/10

In the Children's Commissioner's April 2026 polling children chose: an adult to talk to if I see something bad 35 percent, not being able to talk to strangers 34 percent, child only apps and websites 32 percent, clear rules about what I can watch and play 27 percent, no access to social media 10 percent. Children are not saying it is fine. A third want stranger chat gone. They are saying they want an adult and a rule before they want the door shut.

Supported: Child·Challenged: Clinician (children underrate risk)
Sources: S20

13. What the three public voices have actually said about Roblox

Reliability 7/10

Family IT Guy has a sourced position: "I'd skip Roblox", with the fallback that if a child plays anyway "the only way to keep them safe on Roblox is to supervise 100% of their time on the platform". He discloses commission on a parental control product he also cites as a source. For Dr Catherine Knibbs and Dr Becky Kennedy we found NO SOURCED ROBLOX POSITION. Knibbs has spoken about gaming and disclosure in general, on a June 2024 podcast: "shame is the reason why they can be exploited further". Kennedy's nearest material is a December 2025 collaboration with Xbox about boundaries before play, with no direct quote and no mention of strangers. Do not attribute a Roblox view to either.

Supported: Skeptic·Challenged: none
Sources: S33, S34, S35

Six perspectives

Lens Panels

The Clinician

The variable a parent controls is whether the child will tell them

Children say they stay silent because they fear the phone being taken. CEOP says lead with no blame. The police chiefs say support children who report, do not punish them. A ban imposed after an incident removes the place the parent can see and teaches the child not to disclose next time. I found no study measuring that, only children and practitioners saying it.

The Academic

Real, smaller and differently shaped than the headlines

About one in eight young people report online sexual solicitation in a year across global studies, and that includes requests from peers. No peer reviewed study measures grooming on Roblox, the effect of its 2026 controls, or the age at which a child is ready for multiplayer chat. Any age threshold we give is a judgement call and should be labelled as one.

The Skeptic

The same number is misused in both directions

Critics quote 24,522 reports as an epidemic. Roblox quotes it as 0.12 percent of the total. Both are wrong uses of one figure. Everyone in this debate has an interest: a short seller, law firms, elected attorneys general, a parental control company, creators on commission, and Roblox. That is a reason to check each number, not to dismiss any of them.

The Economist

The controls are real and they were extracted, not offered

Chat is the growth engine and adults are the profit target, on a platform where a third of checked users are under 13. Age banded chat is what keeps those facts apart. It protects a child whose age has been checked and whose parent has linked an account. There is no published safety budget: the line is blended with servers, and its recent growth is put down to AI.

The Historian

Past panics were wrong about the monster and right about the mechanism

MSN chat rooms, Habbo, Fortnite, Omegle. Each time the thing that mattered was whether an unknown adult could open a private, unmoderated channel to a child by default. Where that was the whole product, closing it was right. Where it was a setting inside a play space, changing the default fixed most of it. Count defaults, not features.

The Child

They are not saying it is fine. They are saying it is theirs.

Adults say strangers and mean predators. Children say people I do not know and mostly mean other players. A 13 year old said roleplay would be "completely pointless if you weren't doing it with anyone else". The child adding 150 strangers was the lonely one. So friendship is a protective lever as much as settings are, and a ban lands hardest on the child with the fewest friends offline.

So what do we do

What This Means for Guided Childhood

  • The position Not ban, not shrug: who can message my child, where does it go next, and will they tell me. This is the never allow or deny rule applied to the loudest question in the category. It is supported by findings 2, 5, 10 and 11, and it is honest about finding 11: we cannot claim a trial behind it.
  • Stages The Roblox guide in the product needs a refresh, for your approval, not an automatic change. The live guide still speaks of a Parental PIN and of settings for under 13s. Roblox now runs Kids accounts (5 to 8), Select accounts (9 to 15), Trusted Friends and age banded chat. The first setting to check for a child aged 13 to 15 is Trusted Friends, because that is where the text filter comes off. Menu paths must be checked by hand in the app before anything ships, because Roblox's help pages blocked us.
  • DiGi DiGi's Roblox answer should ask three things before it advises: the child's checked age tier, whether a parent account is linked, and which room the child plays in. For primary age the CEOP line is to play in a shared space. DiGi should never say a setting makes Roblox safe, only what it switches off.
  • Scripts One new script, the telling promise. Said before anything goes wrong: "If anyone asks you to move to another app, or asks for a photo, or gives you something for free, come and tell me. You will not lose the game for telling me." Finding 2 gives the trigger and finding 10 gives the promise. It claims a calmer conversation, never an outcome.
  • Scripts One rule most parents have not set: who you play with. 43 percent of parents have this rule against 56 percent with a time limit (finding 5). This is the star quest candidate for the story, because the family can agree it in one evening.
  • Schools For Year 5 and 6: the move off the game is the moment to tell an adult. It is the one behaviour that every source agrees on and that a 10 year old can recognise.
  • Marketing Safe to say: more than half of children who game online play with people they have never met (Ofcom, October 2026). Fewer than half of parents set a rule about who their child plays with. Roblox is not in the under 16 social media ban but stranger chat in games is in a separate restriction due from spring 2027. Roblox chat now needs an age check, and children get around it.
  • Do not quote Never in any asset: the 18.7 percent "unsafe chat" figure (a classifier that was right about one time in three). "Ofcom is investigating Roblox". Any "safety spend" figure. "40 million under 13s". The 47 percent and 29 percent parent statistics on the Family IT Guy page, which have no source. "Hellscape" or any attorney general's phrase as a finding. Any number for children who stay silent for fear of losing the device. The 25 percent Ofcom harmful content figure until the Ofcom table is read. Any Roblox position for Knibbs or Kennedy.
  • Response classes How the engine should class each voice on this story. Family IT Guy, "I'd skip Roblox": AGREE WITH CONTEXT on the worry, EVIDENCE CHALLENGE on "the only way is to supervise 100 percent", ALTERNATIVE INTERPRETATION on reports "accelerating", INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE on his unsourced parent statistics. Knibbs on shame and silence: AGREE, as a general point, with no Roblox position attributed. Kennedy: INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE, nothing on Roblox to respond to. No disagreement is being forced here: on Knibbs and Kennedy there is nothing to disagree with.
  • Ban readiness Parents will assume the 2027 ban covers Roblox. It does not, as described. That is a clear, checkable thing to tell them, and it routes to the stage check: the plan for games is still theirs to make.
Honest limits

Assumptions and the Missing Lens

This briefing rests on

Roblox's own pages for every setting, undated and marked "vary by region", with the UK never named. Roblox's help centre, Ofcom and the NSPCC all refused automated reading, so several UK figures were first read through trade press or mirrors and then sent for checking. The disclosure argument rests on focus groups with teenagers, not on primary age children, who are Roblox's core. No independent test of Roblox has been published since the January 2026 age checks. And the central recommendation, a staged path with the conversation kept open, has never been tested against a ban on any abuse outcome.

The missing seventh lens

A serving UK police online investigator or CEOP officer. They would know what share of cases begin in a game, whether the January 2026 changes moved offenders elsewhere, and what a parent should do with the device after a disclosure: keep it safe for the police, or leave it with the child. We found no official guidance on that last point, and it changes the script we would write.
