## The reconciliation, read this first

Our last series (Louder Than The Evidence) argued the average population effect of social media on
wellbeing is small, and warned against panic. This series leans into children being harmed. Left
unhandled that looks like a flip into the panic camp. It is not, and the series must make that
plain, or it damages the brand.

- **Different question.** Louder Than The Evidence was the population social media and wellbeing
  question, where the average is small. Knibbs is about acute trauma from specific violent, gory or
  developmentally wrong content in young children, the concentrated tail, and the mechanism, not
  the prevalence.
- **Same thesis.** We always said the average is small and harm concentrates in the vulnerable, by
  content and pattern, not by hours. Knibbs is clinical evidence for exactly that tail. She is not a
  panic merchant, her own report says "precision is the opposite of panic" and "this research is
  not anti-technology."
- **Same answer.** Her fix is an askable adult, co-regulation, connection, teaching before
  exposure, never making the child the problem. That is our pathway, word for word. And she frames
  cybertrauma as a form of childhood adversity, like ACEs, which is our hidden thread.

So the series reinforces the last one. The average is small, and the tail is real, and both point
at preparation and an askable adult, not a ban.

## The research, held honestly

Knibbs gave children aged 7 to 10 (school Years 3 to 5) a way to show, through trauma informed
drawing and conversation, what surveys cannot safely ask and parents cannot see. What she reports:
biological stress responses and dissociation concordant with post traumatic stress criteria in the
DSM-5, arising from Squid Game (rated 15+), YouTube, experiences inside Roblox, 18+ games played at
home, targeting by older gamers in game, sexual content below the Online Safety Act priority
threshold, and violent content on the family TV chosen by parents. One child drew the physiological
cascade of polyvagal theory with the precision of an adult clinical recollection. Ten of sixteen
children affected.

The honest frame: a small, qualitative, hypothesis generating study with no control group and a
clinician as interpreter, so it can show that and how some young children carry trauma responses to
online content, but it cannot establish prevalence or population effect. That is its strength, not
its weakness, it reaches the age group and the mechanism the big surveys miss. Cite it as clinical
evidence of a real harm pathway in some children, and stop there.

Four findings that carry the series:
1. **Harm beneath the threshold.** The content traumatising these children sits below the Online
   Safety Act's priority line, and some of it (the family TV) sits outside any platform duty.
2. **The silent child.** They do not tell. Parents "don't get it," and one Year 5 group had ruled
   out teachers, or feared a teacher would just tell their parents. An absence of complaints is a
   measurement failure, not an absence of harm.
3. **The seeking loop.** After exposure, children go back for more. The child shapes the technology
   and the technology shapes the child. Recommendation engines meet trauma physiology here.
4. **The one askable adult.** Engaged parents changed what was inside a child. Co regulation, a
   calm adult who does not flinch, is the protection. Never make the child the problem.

## News hooks, with the caveats

- **4chan fine:** Ofcom's first OSA financial penalty, £450,000 for the child protection failure
  (age checks on pornography), plus risk assessment fines, ~March 2026. Cite £450,000 as the core.
  Source: SCL and multiple outlets. Shows the Act bites on illegal, priority content and leaves the
  everyday harm to young children untouched.
- **Internet Matters silent harm:** 7 in 10 UK children (survey of 1,000, ages 9 to 17) experienced
  online harm, only about 36 percent took any action, fewer than 1 in 5 told a trusted adult, 27
  May 2025. Dated 2025, pair with KCSIE 2026 if strict recency is needed.
- **Children's Commissioner:** 1,048,965 children with an active mental health referral in England
  2024 to 25, nearly double 2018 to 19, over 60,000 waiting more than two years, ~June 2026. The
  authoritative upstream spine.
- **Roblox:** worldwide mandatory facial age estimation from January 2026, age banded chat, after a
  BBC test found a 27 year old and a 15 year old could still message. Also 170+ child safety
  lawsuits, $35m+ in US state settlements by Aug 2026. Verify the BBC test detail before quoting.
- **Australia ban evasion:** ~8 in 10 under 16s still on restricted platforms three months in, over
  half reporting no age check, eSafety Commissioner, Aug 2026.
