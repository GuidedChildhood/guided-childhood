The SEN parent, and the first run of the build lenses

The seventh lens the last sweep asked for, plus the first outing of the three standing lenses that convert research into decisions. Companion to the ten problems briefing, not a replacement for it.

60 Second Summary

Three build lenses ran independently on pricing, demand and schools, and all three came back saying the same thing by different routes: the product is not the problem, the packaging is. The SaaS lens found that £29.99 a month buys four reports while £9.99 buys one with a credit that never expires, so the cheap product is a substitute for the expensive one rather than a wedge into it, in the worst retaining price shape there is. The demand lens found the price is on the wrong shelf: against parental controls at £4 to £8 a month it is unbuyable, against tutoring at £140 a month it is the cheapest thing on the page, and six words of positioning decide which. The schools lens found the pricing model reads as a penalty for being big, and something worse underneath it.

That worse thing is the most important finding here. Oak National Academy, the DfE's own curriculum body, has published a complete, free, statutory aligned RSHE curriculum including online safety. Project Evolve is free and in a third of UK schools. Natterhub went free in 2025. A head asking why they would pay for lessons is being correct, and there is no honest answer at the level of lesson content. There are exactly three things a school cannot get for nothing this term, and none of them is a lesson.

The SEN lens is the one that touches the code. For a large share of neurodivergent children the screen is not a want, it is the regulation tool, which means an economy that prices screen time as earned has made regulation contingent on compliance. That is one child in five in an English classroom. Its verdict is not to fix it this week but to stop implying we serve that family until we have, and it offers a version of that which costs nothing to ship.

What the four lenses found

Key Findings

1. The pricing packaging makes the cheap product a substitute for the expensive one

Act on this

£29.99 a month buys up to four reports. £9.99 buys one, with a credit that never expires. The subscription therefore breaks even at three reports a month and no parent needs three reports a month, so the rational purchase for anyone who does the arithmetic is the one off, forever. The never expiring credit removes the only urgency it had. Separately, high priced monthly plans retain 6.7 percent of subscribers at twelve months against 36 percent for low priced annual, and £4,000 MRR at £29.99 is 134 paying parents needing roughly 17 replacements every month at category churn.

Supported: SaaS Operator, Parent Demand·Challenged: none
Sources: B1, B2

2. The shelf decides whether the price is unbuyable or cheap, and six words choose the shelf

Act on this

On the parental controls shelf the anchor is £4 to £8 a month, Qustodio is £39.95 to £69.95 a year and Apple and Google are free, so £29.99 a month loses on comparison before anyone reads the second sentence. On the preparation and tutoring shelf, 30 percent of the target families already pay around £35 an hour, roughly £140 a month, and £29.99 is the cheapest thing there. UK parental controls is a market of roughly £60m; UK private tutoring is £2bn. Good Inside, the best funded player in the emotional territory, sits at about £18 to £22 a month equivalent with a famous clinician attached, which puts £29.99 above the ceiling that competitor found.

Supported: Parent Demand, SaaS Operator·Challenged: none
Sources: B2, B3

3. The purchase trigger is a dated calendar event, and this year's window has passed

Plan around this

Smartphone ownership jumps from 56 percent of ten year olds to 83 percent of eleven year olds, and 46 percent of parents plan to hand over the first phone before the school year starts, with £452 per child already leaving the wallet in that six week window. The ask moment is not a mood, it is a date, and the parent inside it has one question with an answer: he has asked, is he ready, what do I say. That is a one off purchase a parent can make in ninety seconds without consulting a co parent, which matters because the co parent is the most likely veto. Selling season is June and July. It is September.

Supported: Parent Demand·Challenged: none
Sources: B3, B4

4. The free schools competitor is the government, and it is good

Changes the pitch

Oak National Academy, the DfE's own arm's length curriculum body, publishes a complete, free, adaptable RSHE curriculum for KS1 to KS4 built with subject specialists, aligned to the guidance that became statutory on 1 September 2026, with downloadable resources and online safety units running from primary. Project Evolve, free and built on the same Education for a Connected World framework we build on, reports 15,500 schools and 75,000 educators, about a third of UK schools. Twinkl took Natterhub free for UK primaries in 2025. Any pitch that says we map to the guidance and we are ready to teach describes something a head can already get for nothing from the department that wrote the mandate.

Supported: Schools Digital Learning·Challenged: none
Sources: B5, B6

5. Three things a school cannot get free this term, and none of them is a lesson

This is the pitch

First, a document for the inspector. Ofsted began inspecting under the renewed framework on 10 November 2025, with personal development and wellbeing now a separately graded evaluation area on a five point scale, and free lesson banks do not produce sequence, progression, coverage and impact evidence a subject lead can hand over. Second, confidence for a teacher who never trained for this: roughly 30 percent deliver RSE with no training, 46 percent of secondary teachers do not feel confident, 86 percent want better training, and fidelity research finds teacher comfort predicts delivery better than experience does. Third, a purchase with no data protection assessment attached: the ICO's June 2026 edtech audit found over 40 percent of providers had done no DPIA at all, the DfE updated its procurement guidance in July, and a product holding no personal data removes that work from the purchase entirely.

Supported: Schools Digital Learning·Challenged: none
Sources: B7, B8, B9

6. The distribution claim is real but the mechanism is a legal duty, not goodwill

Build this

We have said a school is worth 250 families with the head's endorsement. Schools are institutionally cautious about naming commercial services to parents, and a head who fronts a paid product owns the complaint. But the 2026 RSHE guidance requires schools to engage parents proactively, be transparent about all materials, and share teaching content on request. Every school in England now has that obligation and most will meet it with a PDF nobody opens. If the parent facing pathway is the thing the school links to in order to comply, distribution stops being a favour and becomes the head discharging a statutory duty using our page. That is a far stronger claim, and it currently has no product surface at all.

7. For one child in five, our central mechanic makes regulation contingent on compliance

The product finding

1.7 million pupils in England have SEND, 482,640 hold an EHCP, and autism is the largest primary need at 33.5 percent of those. Across qualitative work, autistic children's screen use is described as serving regulation: predictability, control, absence of social demand, escape. The most product relevant figure is that high sensory curators, about 40 percent of neurodivergent children, experience conflict over digital media up to four times as often as low sensory curators, and reducing their use is described as very difficult without other sensory supports, which we do not provide. An economy that prices screen time as earned through jobs has, for these families, made the regulation tool contingent on compliance.

Supported: SEN Parent·Challenged: see finding 8, which is the honest other half
Sources: B11, B12, B13

8. And the same children are the highest risk group in the literature, so there is no comfortable answer

Hold both

Raising the free core does not escape the problem, because this is also the population with the highest problematic use rates anywhere: clinical comparisons put 44 to 72 percent of ADHD samples and about 45 percent of autism samples above gaming disorder screening cut offs, against 9.5 percent of controls, and a meta analysis of 46 studies finds autistic traits associated with problematic internet use at around r = 0.26. Worse for our thesis, the strongest harm signal in the Radesky calming study sat in high reactivity boys, the subgroup neurodivergent children most resemble, and nobody has ever run that study in a neurodivergent sample. The same behaviour is doing two opposite jobs in the same child and no product can tell a parent which without knowing the child.

Supported: SEN Parent·Challenged: none, and this is deliberately the uncomfortable half
Sources: B14, B15

9. PDA is the direct threat, and two of our design choices already answer it

Mixed news

Practitioner guidance on demand avoidant profiles is consistent that reward charts, token economies and structured behavioural plans are themselves demands, and that compliance is what the nervous system is fighting. A star rate, a job list, an earned balance and a protected window are four demands stacked together. What is recommended instead is declarative language, genuine rather than disguised choices, and collaborative framing. Two things we already do land squarely on that: never allow or deny is structurally the low demand principle, and a request inside a protected window going to the parent as an ask rather than a refusal preserves the negotiation the guidance says to preserve. A third does too: the ladder toward making the system unnecessary is the token fading protocol the behavioural literature asks for, and we never claim it.

Supported: SEN Parent·Note: no controlled trial exists for rewards in demand avoidant profiles, in either direction
Sources: B16, B17

10. The transition evidence does not diverge for SEN children, it sharpens into a spec

Build this

The behaviour analytic literature on autism does not contradict the finding that warnings make screen transitions worse. It says something more useful: advance notice is a bet whose payoff depends on what the warning predicts. Where it reliably signals that the good thing is about to be taken, it becomes a cue for loss and makes things worse, which is exactly the screen case. Where it is paired with a visible, predictable, reinforced next step, it helps. Visual schedules alone were ineffective for preferred to non preferred transitions and worked only in combination. That is a design specification: default to natural endpoints rather than clock minutes, let the device deliver the ending rather than the parent, always show what comes next and make it concretely good.

Supported: SEN Parent, and the six lens sweep independently·Challenged: none
Sources: B18, B19

So what do we do

What This Means for Guided Childhood

  • Pricing · this month Make the founder plan annual and turn the report into a deposit rather than a substitute. An annual founder plan against the 50 seats already enforced in code, monthly still available but no longer the headline, the £9.99 coming off the plan if upgraded within 30 days, and the never expiring credit becoming a 30 day one so the offer has an edge. Stop describing the subscription in units of reports.
  • Positioning · costs nothing Rename the report to the question parents actually type. Digital Health Report shelves it under mental health, a category parents browse and do not buy in. Is my child ready for a phone is the same product answering the question at the moment it gets asked.
  • SEN · ship this week Add the branch question at onboarding and let the economy be switched off. One question: is your child autistic or ADHD, or does the screen do a regulation job here. If yes, route to the paper chart and DiGi's scripts with the star economy off. It needs no new mechanic, it is defensible today, and it buys the right to talk to a segment of 1.7 million pupils while the rest gets built.
  • SEN · say it out loud Claim the three things we already do right for this family. Never allow or deny is the low demand principle. The ask inside a protected window preserves the negotiation. The ladder toward making the system unnecessary is the token fading protocol the literature asks for. All three are genuine strengths we have never once mentioned.
  • Transitions Make natural endpoints the default and let the device deliver the ending. End of episode, end of level, end of round rather than clock minutes, with the next thing visible and concretely good. Two independent literatures now point at the same spec, and nobody else in the market has built it.
  • Schools · before half term Give the lessons away and sell the three things that are not free. The inspector document, the twenty minute confidence track for a non specialist, and a dated one page statement saying no logins, no pupil accounts, no personal data, no DPIA required. Keep the open catalogue permanently free so we sit beside Oak rather than against it.
  • Schools · pricing Drop per pupil pricing for flat bands. Kapow charges £300 flat with unlimited users, so a 281 pupil primary at £2 per pupil pays £562 and reads it as a penalty for being big. Every band already sits below a head's delegated limit, which means one person can say yes without a procurement process, so the whole sale should be designed for one conversation.
  • Schools · the missing surface Build the parent materials page schools use to discharge the transparency duty. Every school in England must now share RSHE materials with parents. If our parent pathway is what they link to, distribution is a legal obligation being met with our page rather than a favour we asked for. This is the whole business model and it has no product surface.
  • Timing Two windows, and one of them is open now. The schools autumn window closes at half term in late October and the next does not open until January, by which point most schools will have defaulted to free. The parent window is June and July, on the school calendar rather than the content calendar, which means this year's peak has already passed.
  • Measurement Instrument one activation event, named now. Something like a parent opening a script and marking it used within 24 hours of signup, measured against day seven return. Without it every retention conversation for the next six months is opinion.
Honest limits

Assumptions and the Missing Lens

This briefing rests on

No agent opened a primary source. The egress proxy blocked every publisher, vendor and regulator domain across all four lenses, so every figure came through the search index and none was read on the page. Prices in particular are accurate to within a tier at best and several should be re read before they enter a deck: Qustodio UK, Bark, Good Inside, the PSHE Association bundle, and the Sutton Trust tutoring figures.

No UK search volume was verifiable. Not one. Those figures sit behind Keyword Planner, Ahrefs and Semrush and no reachable published source quotes them. Anyone offering a search volume for this category without a screenshot is guessing, including us.

The SEN regulation thesis is well evidenced as a parental account and not as a measured mechanism. The qualitative work is consistent and the direction is clear, but there is no large UK quantitative study establishing it, and the one strong longitudinal study on devices used for calming was in typically developing children, with its strongest harm signal in the subgroup neurodivergent children most resemble. That gap is the biggest hole in the argument and the briefing states it rather than papering over it.

Nobody could establish who holds the purse. No credible UK figure exists on who decides or vetoes a family purchase of this kind. Every lens will assert it anyway. Fifty of our own customers would answer it properly and it changes the checkout page.

The missing lens

The churned customer. Every lens here studies people who might buy or people who are suffering. Nobody has studied the parent who bought, used it for three weeks and stopped, which is the population that decides whether any of this becomes a business. That lens cannot be run from a search engine. It needs ten phone calls to real cancellations, and until those exist the retention half of this briefing is inference.
