LESSON 2.3 · SCREENS AND SLEEP
PARENT AND CHILD VIDEO v3
Stage 2 Builder (ages 8 to 10) · Strand: Health and wellbeing · Journey step 3 of 5

KNOWLEDGE INTENTION: I can explain that my brain reads light to work out what time of day it is. I can name the pulls that are built to make stopping hard. I can say why the phone sleeping in the kitchen makes the last hour easier for everybody in the house, including my grown up.

EMOTIONAL INTENTION: I feel clever and in charge of my own evening, not punished and not watched. I know that finding it hard to stop is not a fault in me, and I know I will never be in trouble for telling.

KEYWORD: LANDING (taught as "the slow bit at the end")

CATCHPHRASE (x3): "Phone sleeps first. Brain lands after."

MISCONCEPTION (for parent notes): children often think stopping is purely willpower. You either have it or you do not, so "one more and then I will stop" is a promise you can keep if you simply mean it hard enough, and a person who cannot stop is being weak or being naughty. Tonight's Stop Clock game exists to break exactly that: if you are happy to be timed, your child times YOU trying to put your own evening scroll down, and if you would rather not, you time them instead. Then the two of you go round the house counting the features built to keep you both there. Once stopping is a design problem rather than a character problem, moving the phone stops feeling like a punishment and starts feeling like common sense.

PARENT NOTE (safeguarding): tonight's promise is "you will never be in trouble for telling me you cannot sleep, or that something you saw is going round in your head, or that you took the device up with you". The last one is the hard one to keep. A child who expects a row for taking the tablet upstairs will lie in the dark with it instead, and a child lying awake with something frightening on a screen is the exact situation this film exists to prevent. Thank them for telling you, sort it out together, and say out loud that telling was the right move. One cross reaction voids the promise. And name your second person out loud tonight, so the route is not only you. A grandparent, an auntie, their teacher. If what comes down the stairs one night turns out to be bigger than sleep, that second name is the one that matters. Childline is free on 0800 1111, it is open all night, and it does not show up on the bill. Three rules make the rest of it work: the charging spot holds your phone too, screens going downstairs is never used as a consequence for something else, and if your child has a real reason to need a way of reaching somebody at night, a young carer, a child who rings their other parent, a child with a health condition, then the routine bends round that. Leave them the way to call and move everything else. The moment it becomes a punishment it stops being a routine, and every bedtime turns back into a negotiation.

One more thing worth knowing, because it will come up at the school gate: the evidence on blue light specifically is a good deal weaker than the headlines suggest, and a child told a scary thing that later turns out to be shaky stops believing the rest of it too. What IS solid is that the content and the designed pull keep children up, so that is what this film is built on. Light gets its honest share and no more.

RETRIEVAL: Lesson 2.2, Spot the trick, catchphrase "Rushing, promising, asking. Stop and check." The REMEMBER WHEN beat calls back the Clock, and the film then turns it over: there, the clock was built to rush a decision out of you. Tonight's clock is built so the decision never gets asked at all.

SEGMENT A

[1. HELLO RITUAL · ON SCREEN: DiGi's workshop at night. One lamp, a dark window, and a small model aeroplane going round and round above the bench on a wire, never coming down.]
DiGi: Hello, it's DiGi. Grown up, stay for this one, because tonight the rule lands on you too. If nobody is sitting with you tonight, stay anyway. This is about sleep, and it is not a telling off. By the end you will know why stopping at night is so hard. Somebody built it that way, and once you can see how, you get to be the one deciding.

[2. CALM ME · ON SCREEN: the lamp dims one notch. The plane slows. DiGi's eyes go heavy.]
DiGi: Quick reset. Sit back, shoulders down, let your eyes go heavy, like when you are nearly asleep in the car. Breathe in, two, three. Out, slow, two, three, four. That heavy feeling in your arms is your body coming down.

[3. REMEMBER WHEN · ON SCREEN: the Clock from lesson 2.2 back on the workshop wall, ticking.]
DiGi: Remember the three tells? Rushing, promising, asking. Stop and check. The Clock was anything built to rush you. Tonight you meet its twin. A clock built so that time never runs out at all.

[4. TODAY'S ONE THING · ON SCREEN: the word LANDING, huge, on the blackboard. The model plane finally drops onto the bench, slowly, wheels first.]
DiGi: Today's word is LANDING. A plane cannot just stop in the sky. It comes down slowly, and the slow bit at the end is what makes it a landing instead of a crash. Your brain is the same. It needs the last hour to come down.

[5. TEACH, STORY · ON SCREEN: a cutaway of a head with a tiny light reader behind the eyes, wired to a dial. Outside the window the sun sinks and the dial swings towards NIGHT. Then a bright phone rises close to the face and the dial swings straight back to DAY.]
DiGi: Here is the machinery. Your body has a clock, and it does not run on numbers. It runs on light. As the light drops, your brain makes melatonin, the chemical that makes you feel sleepy. And your brain trusts the light over the clock on the wall, because the sky is far older than any clock. So a bright screen up close at half past nine tells your body, still daytime, stay up.

[ON SCREEN: the plane lifts off the bench again. Around it: an episode stopping on a cliffhanger, a circle counting NEXT EPISODE IN 5, a game chest with a streak counter beside it reading 6, and a feed scrolling on with no bottom to it.]
DiGi: Now the bigger half. Your show, your game and your feed are built so that stopping is hard. On purpose. Episodes do not end, they stop in the middle of the exciting part and the next one starts by itself in five seconds. Games hold a streak you lose if you do not come back tomorrow. Feeds have no bottom, so nothing is ever finished. Rooms full of grown ups are paid to make putting it down hard. At nine at night you are not losing to a show, you are losing to a team. Nobody wins that one, so we skip the fight. The phone sleeps in the kitchen. So does your grown up's.

DiGi: Pause me here. Grown up, ask them the winter question on the screen. I'll wait.

SEGMENT B

[6. TEACH, APPLY · ON SCREEN: an oven clock reading 21:04. On the tablet the episode stops mid chase, and a small circle begins filling. NEXT EPISODE IN 5. 4. 3.]
DiGi: Nine o'clock. The episode stops, and a little circle appears. Next episode in five, four, three. Last time, a clock rushed you into pressing something. This one is the other way round. It counts down so you never have to decide at all. The hard part was never saying no. The hard part is that nobody asked you a question.

[ON SCREEN: a child on the sofa saying "I'm not even tired", a brain above them lit up and busy. The screen goes dark, the brain dims, and a few minutes later an enormous yawn arrives.]
DiGi: And the sentence everybody says. I'm not even tired. Honestly? You probably are. A busy brain cannot feel tired while it is busy. The excitement sits on top of the tiredness like a trampoline, so you never feel the floor. Turn the screen off and sit there doing nothing, and somewhere in the next few minutes the first yawn turns up. That gap, between the screen going off and your body catching up, is your runway.

[ON SCREEN: two arrows pointing into one pile marked STILL AWAKE AT ELEVEN. A small arrow labelled LIGHT. A much bigger one labelled WHAT IT IS DOING TO YOU.]
DiGi: One honest thing, because you will hear grown ups say it. It's all the blue light. The light is real, but it is the smaller half. What keeps you up is mostly what the screen is doing to you. That is the half you can outsmart.

▐ PAUSE AND SAY #2  [ON SCREEN: the filling circle again, NEXT EPISODE IN 3 · "PAUSE. Say it out loud: what is that five second countdown actually for?" · Older child variant: "That countdown is already switched on before anybody gets there, and you have to go and find the setting to turn it off. Why build it that way round?"]
DiGi: Pause me, and say it out loud. What is that little countdown actually for?

[7. SAY IT TOGETHER · ON SCREEN: the catchphrase in chunky letters. A phone asleep on a kitchen worktop, and above it the model plane coming gently down.]
DiGi: Both of you, out loud, three times. Listen to your grown up, they always mumble the first one. Phone sleeps first. Brain lands after. Again. Phone sleeps first. Brain lands after. Last one, like you mean it. Phone sleeps first. Brain lands after.

◆ platform slide: SAY THIS TONIGHT
[Carries the catchphrase for the house, plus the one line for the grown up: "Mine goes down there too, and you are allowed to call landing on me."]


SEGMENT C

[8. TRY IT TONIGHT · ON SCREEN: "STOP CLOCK". A grown up on the sofa mid scroll, a child beside them counting out loud, eyebrows up.]
DiGi: Tonight, play Stop Clock. Grown up, you go first, if you are happy to be timed. If not, swap it round. Start whatever you really watch in the evening, and let them get into it. Then they shout stop and count out loud until you put it down. Then say what nearly kept you there. A big number is not a telling off, it is the proof. Then count the pulls round your house. Then pick the charging spot together, somewhere nobody sleeps, and tonight the grown up's phone goes down there first. And ask for your power for the week. If they say yes, once this week you can call landing on the whole house, them included.

[9. REFLECT AND CLOSE · ON SCREEN: the charging spot, two phones asleep side by side, the hall light going warm, the plane parked on the bench.]
DiGi: One last thing, and it matters most. If you cannot sleep, or something you saw is going round and round in your head, or you took a screen up with you when you were not meant to, go and tell somebody. Your grown up first. If you cannot tell them, tell another grown up you trust, and a teacher counts. You will never be in trouble for telling. You were never bad at stopping. You were up against a team. Move the phone, and the fight is over before it starts. Sleep well. Let your brain land.


WHAT WOULD DiGi DO?
ON SCREEN: It is nine o'clock. DiGi has one episode left of the thing everybody at school is talking about, the little circle is already counting down to the next one, and the charging spot is downstairs in the kitchen.
Question: What would DiGi do?

A) Watch this last one and properly mean it this time. One episode is not going to change anything. [SEEDED MISCONCEPTION]
   Feedback: Have a think about who you are up against. Meaning it harder does not beat a countdown built by a team of grown ups, and that countdown is not waiting for you to mean anything. Change the room, not the promise. Try again.

B) Stop it there and take the phone down to the kitchen. [CORRECT]
   Feedback: That's it. You moved the phone instead of fighting the phone, and that is the only version of this that works on a tired brain. Your grown up's goes down there too, remember. This is a house rule, not a you rule.

C) Turn the brightness right down and watch in the dark, then put it on the charging spot when the episode finishes.
   Feedback: Good thinking, and the light really is part of it. Just the smaller part. Dim screen, same cliffhanger, same countdown, same brain wide awake at eleven. Go after the bigger half. Try again.
