LESSON 2.1 · WHAT WE KEEP PRIVATE ONLINE
PARENT AND CHILD VIDEO v3
Stage 2 Builder (ages 8 to 10) · Strand: Privacy and security · Journey step 1 of 5

KNOWLEDGE INTENTION: I can explain that private information is collected in small pieces and joined up, I can run the piece test on anything that asks me for something, and I know the words to use in a chat when I am not handing a piece over.

EMOTIONAL INTENTION: I feel clever and in charge of this, not watched and not told off, and I know that telling my grown up about something I have already typed in is good news, never trouble.

KEYWORD: JIGSAW (taught as "small pieces that join into a picture of you")

CATCHPHRASE (x3): "One piece is never just one piece!"

RUN TIME: 841 words of recorded narration, about six and a quarter minutes at Mabel's read rate of 131 words a minute. Segment split A 358 · B 282 · C 201.

MISCONCEPTION (for parent notes): children often think information is either dangerous or harmless, so the big things (an address, a password) need guarding and the small things (a first name, a birthday, which school) do not count. Tonight's Five Pieces Game exists to break exactly that: nobody collecting information ever asks for the whole picture, because you would notice. They ask for one piece, in one place, on one day, and the smallness is precisely what makes it work.

PARENT NOTE (safeguarding): the promise tonight is "you will never be in trouble for telling me what you have already put in". At this age most children have already typed something on the list somewhere, and the closing beat asks them to tell you this evening. Whatever comes out, respond to the telling, not to the thing. A sigh, a "why would you do that", a device taken away, any one of those voids the promise and you will not hear about the next one. Say thank you for telling me, then fix it together: change what can be changed, take down what can be taken down, and say out loud that they sorted it. If what comes out is bigger than a piece of information, an adult who has been asking your child things, someone who has asked to meet them, anything sexual, or images, then stop and do not question them about it further. Write down what they said in their own words, report it to CEOP, the police's child protection service, at ceop.police.uk, and ring 101, or 999 if your child is in danger right now. Keep your voice exactly as it was while you do all of that, because they are watching to work out whether telling you was safe. One more thing to back them on: this lesson teaches your child to say "nah, not that one" to another player. A friend may call that rude. You are the one who tells them it is not.

RETRIEVAL: Lesson 1.7, My privacy shield, catchphrase "Check with your grown up first!" The step up: the shield kept things in, and tonight things are asking to come out.

NARRATOR: Mabel voices DiGi throughout.

   Feedback: Yes. You did not accuse anybody of anything, you did not make it awkward, you simply did not hand the piece over. And mentioning it to your grown up is not telling on your teammate, that is you checking. One piece is never just one piece.
