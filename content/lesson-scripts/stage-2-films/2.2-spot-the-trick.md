LESSON 2.2 · SPOT THE TRICK
PARENT AND CHILD VIDEO v1
Stage 2 Builder (ages 8 to 10) · Strand: Online risks · Journey step 2 of 5

KNOWLEDGE INTENTION: I can name the three tells of a trick, the Clock, the Prize and the Ask, I know one tell is enough to stop, and I know why every trick has to rush me.
EMOTIONAL INTENTION: I feel quick and clever, not watched. Spotting is my job, deciding is a job we do together, and I will never be in trouble for telling.
KEYWORD: TELL (taught as "the bit a trick cannot hide")
CATCHPHRASE (x3): "Rushing, promising, asking. Stop and check."
MISCONCEPTION (for parent notes): children often think you spot a trick by looking at it. Bad spelling, ugly graphics, something obviously fake. So a message with the proper logo, neat spelling and a real game name gets trusted. Tonight's Catch the Grown Up game exists to break exactly that: you build tricks that look perfect, on a real phone screen, and your child still finds the tell, because a tell is what a message DOES to you, not how it looks.
PARENT NOTE (safeguarding): "You will never be in trouble for telling" is the promise, and at this age it has to stretch one step further than it did at five. It covers telling you AFTER they already tapped, already typed the password, already spent the coins. A child who thinks they will be told off for the tap will hide the tap, and hiding is the only thing that turns a small mess into a big one. So respond calmly every single time, including the false alarms, especially the false alarms, and including the times they got it wrong first. One cross reaction voids the lesson and buys you months of silence. The same warning now sits on the SAY THIS TONIGHT slide, next to the words you actually say out loud, because that is where it has to be read.
ROUTE (safeguarding): the film names three doors, not one. The grown up at home first, then a teacher or a club leader, then Childline on 0800 1111. It also gives the child the half of the move they can always make alone, stop and leave it, for the evenings when there is nobody to fetch.
RETRIEVAL: lesson 2.1, the private list, catchphrase "If it's on the list, it stays out." The REMEMBER WHEN beat calls it back, and the film then reuses it inside the third tell: the Ask is a message going after the list.
NARRATOR: Mabel voices DiGi throughout.

[5. TEACH, STORY · ON SCREEN: sheet one whips off a CLOCK. Sheet two off a glittering PRIZE CHEST. Sheet three off a small brass KEY, chalk labelled THE ASK. The three objects line up on the bench and stay on screen for the rest of the beat.]
DiGi: The Clock. The Prize. The Ask. The Clock is anything that rushes you. Your account will be deleted in ten minutes. The Prize is anything promising far too much. Free Robux. You've won, and you never even entered. The Ask is anything that wants something it should never need. Your password. Your address. A code that just landed on your grown up's phone. Now here is the bit almost nobody explains. Why is there always a clock? Because the trick maker has a problem. Their trick is not very good. Give you one quiet minute with it and you would see straight through it. So they do not make the trick cleverer. They can't. They make your thinking time shorter instead. The countdown is not protecting the prize. The countdown IS the trick. Which means that rushed feeling in your chest is not something that happens just before you notice. That feeling IS the noticing.

WHAT WOULD DiGi DO?
ON SCREEN: DiGi is halfway through a build in a game when a message arrives from an account called Game Support, with the game's real logo on it: "Your account will be deleted in 10 minutes. Confirm your password here to keep it."
Question: What would DiGi do?

A) The logo is right and the spelling is perfect, so it is real support. Type the password in before the ten minutes run out. [SEEDED MISCONCEPTION]
   Feedback: Careful. Anyone can copy a logo in about a minute, so looks are the one thing a trick can fake for free. Look at what it is DOING instead. A countdown and a password box. That is two tells. Have another go.

B) Ask in the group chat whether anybody else got it, since three of your mates are in the same game.
   Feedback: Not daft, but everyone in that chat got the same countdown, so they are all rushing too, and a rushed group agrees rather than checks. Take it to somebody who is not on the clock. Try again.

C) Hands off, go and get somebody in the house, and look at it together before a single letter gets typed. [CORRECT]
   Feedback: That's it. Clock and Ask, spotted, and the deciding happens with somebody who is calm. And the real thing worth knowing: a proper games company never asks for your password in a message. Not once. Not ever.
