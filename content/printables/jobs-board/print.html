Two columns that never mix

At eleven to fifteen the screen argument stops being about bedtime stories and starts being about fairness. This board makes the rules visible, so the deal does the arguing and you get to be on their side of it. There are two columns, and the line between them matters most.

1
Family jobs: no stars, ever

Emptying the dishwasher, their own washing, laying the table. Everyone in the house does these, grown ups included. They get ticked, never paid, because contribution is belonging, not payment.

2
Earning jobs: stars for screen time

Bigger or extra jobs: washing the car, a full clean of the bathroom, helping a younger sibling with reading. Each has a star value you agree. One star is five minutes, or ten or fifteen if you choose.

3
A weekly most

Guided Childhood's guide for a healthy day is about an hour and a half of screen time at 11 to 13 and two hours at 13 to 15. At five minutes a star that is at most 126 stars a week for 11 to 13 and 168 for 13 to 15. The week starts on Monday.

4
Protected time: no star buys it

The hour before bed, mealtimes and school hours. If they want a screen inside one, it comes to you as an ask, and it is your call every time. It is never for sale.

5
They pitch the jobs

The pitch slips let them propose a new earning job, or swap one they are tired of. You set the stars when you say yes.

The aim is a board they stop needing. You run it, then you run it together, then they run it and you check in.
