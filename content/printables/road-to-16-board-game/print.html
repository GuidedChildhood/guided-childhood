How to play

You need: the board, a dice, a counter each, the star tokens, and the choice cards in five piles, one for each Friend.

1
Start on square 1, with Pebble

The youngest player goes first.

2
Roll the dice and move

Follow the gold road up the board.

3
See where you landed
?
Choice card

Someone reads you a card from your Friend's pile. Right answer? Take a star. Not sure? Read the answer out loud. Nobody loses anything.

Job

Take the stars shown on the square.

Family job

Pick another player and move them forward 2. No stars: helping at home is part of being a family.

Stripy square

Screens rest here. You cannot spend stars while you are on it.

4
Star time

Before you roll, you can pay 2 stars to roll twice and add them up. Not on a stripy square.

5
The first to 16 wins

You do not need an exact roll. Keep going until everyone is home. The most stars picks the next game.

Making the board

The board is four A4 sheets, pages 4 to 7, that tape into one big board twice the size of A3. Or print the separate A3 file in the download on one sheet, if your printer takes A3.

1
Print pages 4 to 7

Full size, no scaling. Thick paper holds up best.

2
Trim the inside edges

Each sheet has a dashed trim line on the edges that meet the others. Cut along it to lose the white printer border.

3
Line up and tape

Sheet 1 top left, 2 top right, 3 bottom left, 4 bottom right. Follow the gold road across the joins and tape on the back.

Make it last. Glue the taped board onto a flattened cereal box or a sheet of card, and it folds in half to store.

The rest. Page 8 is the counters and star tokens. Pages 9 to 16 are the 48 choice cards, six to a page, and page 17 is their backs, to print on the reverse if you want. Keep the cards in five piles, one per Friend.

Younger players can be read their cards. Older players read for them. Everyone plays the same board.

For the grown up · After the game

The real road to 16

The five zones on the board are the five stages Guided Childhood uses to get a child to sixteen ready for a phone and social media, ten minutes a day, with you beside them. Every choice card is a real question from one of its lessons.

The rules are the app's rules

Jobs earn stars. Family jobs earn none, because helping at home is part of living there. Stripy squares are protected time that no star can buy: bedtime, meals and school. A wrong answer costs nothing, because telling and trying should never be punished. If the game starts a question at home, that is the conversation worth having.

Where is your child on the road?

What stage is your child? Three questions, no sign up. The stage check is free at guidedchildhood.com.
