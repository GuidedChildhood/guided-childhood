For the grown up · Read this first

Ten minutes, then stop

These cards are questions for your child, not a lesson for them. The aim is one good conversation, and the best ones are short. Pick a card or two at tea, or in the car, and stop while it is still fun. Ten minutes is plenty.

Talk at tea · 40 cards

Everyday questions to get everyone talking. Everybody answers, the person who read it goes last, and grown ups answer too.

Online · 34 cards

Real situations: a group chat that turns unkind, a game that asks for your school, a pop up with a timer. Talk it through first. Then turn the card round: the foot has the strong move from the lesson it comes from.

What would DiGi say? · 26 cards

Something a child might ask: can I have a phone, can I stay up, can I post this. Everyone guesses what DiGi would say. DiGi never says yes or no. It says where you are, the next step, and the words. Turn the card round to check.

The one rule at the table

Nobody gets in trouble for an answer. If an online card brings up something real, thank them for telling you, and come back to it later, just the two of you.

Printing. Card stock works best. Cut on the dashed lines. Want backs? Print page 28 on the reverse of each card sheet, either way up. Punch the small circle in the corner and the cards go on a ring, one suit per ring.

Keep the deck by the table. The cards work best when they turn up on an ordinary Tuesday.
