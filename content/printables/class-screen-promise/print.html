For the teacher · One lesson to make it

Making the promise with your class

The promise belongs to the class, so the class makes it. You read it together, talk each line through, vote on what goes on the wall, and everyone signs. It fits in one lesson, around forty minutes, and works from Year 1 to Year 6. Younger classes need more talk and less writing.

1

Talk

Read each promise aloud. Ask what it would look like in real life, at school and at home. The questions below help. Let children put it in their own words.

2

Vote

Hands up, thumbs or a sticky dot for each promise. Keep five or six. If the class wants different words, use the blank poster on page 4 and write it in together.

3

Sign

Each child writes their own promise on a slip, draws it and signs it. Scribe for anyone who wants you to. Pin the slips around the poster.

One question for each promise
Kind. What does kind look like in a game chat or on a video call?
Tell. Who are the grown ups you could tell, at school and at home?
Real or pretend. Could a computer make a picture of something that never happened?
Private. What stays private, like a toothbrush? Your address, your school, your password.
The other side. Who might be there: someone you know, someone you do not, or a machine?
Our stop. When one video ends, what starts next? So who picks the stop?
Where to put it

At children's eye height, near where screens get used: by the class computers, the tablet trolley or the board. By the door works too, as the last thing they see before home time.

Printing

The poster prints at A4 or A3. The PDF is vector, so it scales cleanly to A3 on any printer: choose A3 paper and fit to page. One slip sheet covers six children.

Come back to it each half term

Five minutes. Read it aloud together. Ask which promise was easy to keep and which was hard. Change a word if the class has grown out of it. Tick the box when you have.

The promises follow habits taught in the Guided Childhood primary lessons: Pebble's feel it, name it, tell a grown up; stop, look, ask; private like a toothbrush; who is on the other side; and picking a stop. You do not need the lessons to use this set. Promise two asks children to tell a grown up when something on a screen feels bad or scary. If a child tells you something that worries you, follow your school's safeguarding policy, as you would in any lesson.

A letter home · Our class screen promise
Dear families in
class, Date

This week our class made a screen promise together. We talked through each promise, voted on the ones we wanted, and everyone signed their own. It is now up on our classroom wall. Here is what we promised:

We are kind on screens, just like in class.
If something on a screen feels bad or scary, we tell a grown up.
Real or pretend? We stop, look and ask before we believe it.
We keep private things private, and ask before we share.
We notice who is on the other side of the screen.
The screen never says stop, so we pick our stop.

If our class wrote its own words, they are here instead:

One conversation to have at home

Ask your child which promise they wrote on their own slip, and what it would look like at home. Then choose one to keep as a family this week. Picking the stop is a good first one: agree it out loud before the screen goes on, one episode or one game, then the next thing.

No need for a big talk. A few minutes at teatime or on the walk home is plenty.

If you would like more ideas for screens at home, you can find more at guidedchildhood.com.
