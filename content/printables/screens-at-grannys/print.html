Why your screen deal should travel with you

At home there is a rhythm. Your child knows when screens are on and when they rest. Then you arrive somewhere else, and every bit of it goes up for grabs. A different telly, cousins with tablets, a grandparent who loves saying yes, and you, feeling awkward about saying no in somebody else's front room. Your child spots the gap in about four minutes.

Without a deal

Granny guesses. Your child asks whichever grown up is most likely to say yes. The host ends up as the referee in their own house, and you spend the visit either nagging or feeling guilty for not nagging.

With a deal that travels

The rules are the ones your child already knows, written on a card. Your host never has to invent anything or be the baddie. Fewer arguments for them, and a lot less guilt for everyone, you included.

The golden rule

Same deal, different house. And the host is always the boss of their own house.

Ten minutes together, the week before you go

1. Sit down with your child, this pack and a pen. Read page three out loud together.
2. Fill in My deal at Granny's with your child. Let them write the jobs in their own words.
3. Fill in the card for the grown ups: your child's own time, your star rate, the times screens rest, and your phone number.
4. Cut out both copies of the grown ups card. One for your host, one for the fridge at the other house, or the second grandparent.
5. Hand it over on arrival, with a cup of tea, before anyone has asked for anything.
6. Pack the visit tally and a pen. Count the stars together at bedtime.

The three kinds of time on the next page are the same three the Guided Childhood app uses: your child's own time, time earned with stars, and protected time that no stars can buy. If you already set them in the app, copy the same numbers onto the cards. This pack is a parenting tool for your family. It does not promise any result for your child.

When Granny does it differently

She lets them watch the film past bedtime. That is fine: her house, her treat. Say it out loud so it stays a gift and never turns into a gap: "Granny's house, Granny's treat. Our deal picks up again at home."

The three kinds of time

Our time
Kind one · it is yours

A little every day, no strings

A small amount of screen time each day that is simply yours. You do not earn it and nobody takes it away for a grumpy moment. It does not save up, so today's is for today, and it is the time you use first.

Earned time
Kind two · you earn it

Do a job, earn a star

Extra jobs earn stars. One star is five minutes of screen time, on any screen, unless your family agrees a different rate. Stars you have earned are never taken back. Clearing your own plate does not count: that is just what family does.

Protected time
Kind three · screens rest

Times no stars can buy

Mealtimes, bedtime and the hour before it. At Granny's, maybe the Christmas dinner table too. If you really want a screen then, you can ask. The grown up hears it, and you always get an answer and a next step.

Our time first, then stars.
