Ten minutes, every evening, together

Thirty days, one small change a day. Nothing gets taken away for good and nobody is on trial. You try things as a family, notice how the days feel, and on day thirty you sit down and decide together what stays. The grown ups do every change too, which is the bit that makes it fair.

Our reset team

Up to four of you on each day. Grown ups write their names here too.

Our deal for thirty days

1. A missed day is just a missed day. Leave the tick empty and carry on tomorrow.
2. Nobody uses this journal to tell anybody off.
3. Every face is allowed. We ask about each other's faces and never argue with them.
4. The grown ups do every change too, phones included.
5. If a change does not fit our family, we swap it for one that does and write it in.

DiGi says: keep the journal and a pen where you all walk past every evening. The fridge, the kitchen table, next to wherever the phones charge.

Children under 8 can join in by drawing: their own face, or a picture of what they noticed. This journal is a family activity for trying small changes together. It is not a treatment for anything.

Circling them

Each person gets a row on every day: write your name, then circle your face. Grown ups circle too. A younger child can point while a grown up circles, or draw their own face beside the row.

Reading them on day thirty

Never add them up. Look along each person's faces instead. Which days were going great? Which were hard going? What was the change on those days? That is the conversation for the last page.

Where these faces come from

The Guided Childhood app asks parents the same question every day with these same five faces and words, and the very first answer becomes the starting point every later one is read against. Your Where we start page does that job here: day zero is the baseline, and day thirty is the second look.
