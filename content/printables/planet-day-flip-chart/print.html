Three kinds of time, on one chart

A routine chart tells a child what comes next, so you are not the one saying it ten times. This one also settles the question that starts most screen arguments: is it screen time now? The picture cards come in kinds, and each kind looks different, so a child of four can read the answer off the chart.

My time: theirs, every day

A small amount of screen time you set, with no stars needed, so the screen never becomes the prize. It is spent first. Fifteen or twenty minutes is plenty to start. Gold cards.

Star time: earned with jobs

Extra time comes from the job cards with a star space. One star is five minutes unless you choose ten or fifteen. Routine cards earn nothing: getting dressed is just what we do.

Protected time: no star can buy it

Breakfast, lunch, tea, school time and the hour before bed. Striped cards, and no star space on them, so the chart itself shows what stars cannot buy. If they ask for a screen inside one, the ask comes to you, and it is your call every time.

Family jobs: no stars, ever

Some jobs are just part of living together. Every job card has a twin marked family job. Choose which kind each job is in your house. Contribution is belonging, not payment.

How many stars in a week?

Guided Childhood's guide for a healthy day is about an hour of screen time for ages 4 to 7 and an hour and a quarter for 8 to 10. The app turns that into the most stars a child can hold in a week: at five minutes a star, 84 for Pebble's age and 105 for Bloop's. The tally strip on page 24 has a line for your number. The week starts on Monday.

The aim is a chart they stop needing. Start with you moving the cards, then do it together, then let them run it.

Making the flip chart

Each flip chart is four sheets stacked and stapled at the top. Each sheet is cut a little shorter than the one under it, so every label shows at the bottom like a staircase. Flip a sheet up to see what comes next.

1 · Print one set

Pebble's set is pages 4 to 7, Bloop's is 8 to 11. Thick paper or card stock.

2 · Cut on the line

Each sheet shows a dashed cut line over a striped strip. Cut the strip off. The bedtime sheet stays whole.

3 · Stack and staple

Bedtime at the bottom, then after school, morning, and the name sheet on top. Line up the top edges and staple at the two marks.

4 · The cards

Cut the picture cards on their dashed lines. A sticky dot or a hook and loop dot on each slot lets them move.

Laminate it if you can. Then the tick in each slot wipes clean with a dry wipe pen, and the chart lasts the year. No laminator? Clear sticky back plastic does the same job.

No time to cut? Pages 12 and 13 are ready made week charts, one for each age, with the routine already printed in and a tick box for every day from Monday.

Typing names in. The PDF is fillable. Open it in a free PDF reader to type your child's name on the name sheets and the blank cards, then print.
