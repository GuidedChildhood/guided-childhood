Setting up the hunt

A phone handed over on the sofa arrives with no plan. This one arrives at the end of a hunt, and on the way your child reads out ten things that are true before a phone: where it sleeps, who they tell, what they never spend. By the box, the plan has been said out loud, in their own voice, and it is fun.

1
Print and cut

Pages 4 to 8 are the ten clue cards, two to a page. Cut along the dashed lines. Card stock holds up best.

2
Walk the route

Each card's riddle leads to the place the next card hides. Use the hiding map on page 3. If your home has no bookshelf or no clock, write your own clue on the card, or use a blank card from pages 11 and 12.

3
Hide them in order

Card 1 stays with you. Card 2 goes where card 1's riddle points, card 3 where card 2's points, and so on. Card 10 hides at the charging spot and points to the box.

4
Pack the box

The phone, the box card from page 9 that fits your family, and the promises page folded on top. Wrap it and put it under the tree, or wherever your last clue says.

5
Hand over card 1

Christmas morning, or the day it arrives. Stay close: the lines are meant to be heard. Then open the box together and sign the promises before the phone is switched on.

Not a phone this Christmas?

The hunt still works. Use the second box card on page 9: the box holds the plan, a date you will sit down together, and the promises page. Each card's line becomes something to practise first.

Got the First Phone Kit too? Do its readiness check and setup night after the hunt. The hunt is the door; the kit is the plan behind it.
