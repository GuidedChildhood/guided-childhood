# Worked example: ks3-14 bodies, image and pressure

The perfect lesson standard, applied to one module all the way through.
Companion to `plans/2026-09-08-the-perfect-lesson-standard.md`.

**Why this module.** It is DSL flagged, it carries the pornography topic, DiGi
hosts it in the calm register, and it is the lesson where a non specialist is
most likely to be out of their depth. If the standard holds here it holds
everywhere.

## Part 2: the four new fields, written out

### 2a. Subject knowledge, what the teacher needs to understand first

*Read this before you teach. It is not the lesson, it is the ground under it.*

**Retouching is invisible by design.** The edit that matters is not the obvious
filter, it is the small one: a jawline nudged, skin smoothed, a waist narrowed
by two percent. Retouching that announces itself has failed commercially. This
is why "you can tell when a photo is edited" is the misconception the lesson
attacks: pupils believe they can spot it, and they cannot, and neither can you.

**Selection does more work than editing.** A creator shoots two hundred frames
and posts one. Before any software touches it, the image is already the best of
two hundred moments. Pupils find this more persuasive than editing, because it
requires no villain.

**Why insecurity has a market.** Attention is sold. A feed is optimised for
time on app, and content that makes a viewer feel slightly worse about
themselves reliably increases time on app and purchase intent. Nobody has to be
evil for this to happen: the system selects for it. Teach the mechanism, not a
conspiracy, because the mechanism is defensible and the conspiracy is not.

**Social comparison, and which direction hurts.** Comparing upward, against
someone perceived as better off, is the move that lowers mood. Feeds are almost
entirely upward comparison by construction. The lesson's framing, "your ordinary
day against someone's chosen highlight", is this idea in pupil language.

**What the evidence actually supports, and what it does not.** Be careful here,
because this is the claim a sceptical parent or a hostile expert will test.
The honest position: the association between heavy social media use and body
dissatisfaction is real and repeatedly observed, the effect sizes are modest,
and the direction of causation is genuinely contested. Do NOT say social media
causes eating disorders. Say that feeds are built in a way that makes unfair
comparison constant, and that pupils can name and interrupt that. That claim
survives scrutiny. The stronger one does not.

**Why pornography is named at all.** RSHE 2026 names the harms of pornography
as statutory content. The teaching move is a brief, calm, non graphic
acknowledgement that it exists and that it distorts what real bodies and real
relationships look like, followed immediately by a route to a trusted adult.
The evidence that silence protects children is absent; the evidence that
distortion goes unchallenged in silence is not. Brief is the whole skill: one
sentence, no detail, move on.

**Disordered eating is not yours to diagnose.** You are watching for a
disclosure, not making an assessment. Restriction talk, body checking, a pupil
who cannot let the topic go, or a written answer that goes somewhere private
are all DSL referrals, not conversations to have alone at the end of the lesson.

### 2b. Hard questions a pupil may ask, with an answer

**"What if I have already seen it?"**
Nothing is wrong with you and you are not in trouble. Lots of people see things
they did not go looking for. If it is still in your head, that is worth telling
someone, and I can help you do that. *Then follow up privately, and tell the
DSL that day.*

**"My sister uses filters. Is she lying?"**
No. Using a filter is normal and it is not a lie. What the lesson is about is
what happens when we forget that everyone is doing it, and start measuring
ourselves against pictures that had help.

**"Do you use filters, Miss?"**
Answer honestly and briefly, then hand it back to the class. A teacher who
deflects this loses the room. A teacher who says "sometimes, yes, and I still
compare myself to people" wins it.

**"Is it true that social media causes eating disorders?"**
It is more complicated than that, and I am not going to tell you something
that is not true. What we know is that feeds are built so you are always
comparing yourself with someone's best moment, and that constant comparison
makes a lot of people feel worse. Whether it causes an illness is something
researchers still disagree about.

**"So should I just delete everything?"**
That is one option and it works for some people. It is not the only one, and it
is not what this lesson is asking. The check is smaller than that: notice the
feeling, ask who profits from it, and decide what you want to do about that one
account. *Never allow or deny. Always the calibrated pathway.*

### 2c. Parent questions, and how a teacher answers them

**"Why are you discussing pornography with my twelve year old?"**
Because the revised RSHE guidance makes the harms of pornography statutory
content, and because most children encounter it before anyone chooses to talk
to them about it. What happened in the lesson was one sentence, no detail: that
it exists, that it does not show what real bodies or real relationships look
like, and that a trusted adult is the right place to take it. We can send you
exactly what was said.

**"Will this not make her more self conscious than she already is?"**
It is a fair worry and it is why the lesson is built the way it is. We do not
show pupils idealised images and critique them. We teach the mechanism behind
the images, and the finding pupils take away is that the bad feeling is
manufactured and is not about them. That reframe tends to reduce self
consciousness rather than raise it.

**"My son is fine. Is this not scaremongering?"**
Most children are fine, and we say so. There is no claim in this lesson that
screens are damaging your son. What we teach is a check he can run when
something online makes him feel worse, which is a skill whether or not he ever
needs it.

**"What should I do at home?"**
The note that went home has it: scroll a feed together for two minutes, each
pick one image, and run the check. Then ask the family question, which is
whether there is an account that reliably makes you feel worse.

### 2d. The evidence base

*Every figure below must pass the citation verifier before it appears anywhere
public. Named here are the bodies whose work anchors each claim, deliberately
without numbers I have not verified in this session.*

| Claim in the lesson | Anchored in | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Children encounter pornography younger than parents expect, often unsought | Children's Commissioner for England, research on children and pornography | **Verify before publishing:** exact figure and year |
| Heavy social media use is associated with body dissatisfaction, effect sizes modest, causation contested | The published literature on social media and body image, and the critiques of it | **Verify:** state as association, never causation |
| Feeds optimise for time on app | Platform design and disclosure | Defensible as mechanism, no figure needed |
| Upward social comparison lowers mood | Social comparison research | Defensible, no figure needed |
| Retouching is usually undetectable to a viewer | Editing practice | Defensible as craft, no figure needed |

The rule this table enforces: **a claim with no verified source does not go on a
slide.** Where we cannot verify, we teach the mechanism instead of the number,
which is stronger anyway.

## Part 3: what the panel found

The ten lenses run over this module. The disagreements are the useful part.

| Lens | Finding | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cybertrauma | A pupil who has experienced image based abuse could be in this room. The lesson opens on comparison, which is safe, but the pornography sentence must never invite response in the moment | Keep the sentence one way. No discussion beat attached to it |
| 2 Practical family tech | The image check is good but pupils cannot act on it without knowing where the unfollow and mute controls are this term | Add a device agnostic "where the controls live" line to the tool |
| 3 Safeguarding | Strong. DSL note is required and names the record honestly | No change |
| 4 PSHE pedagogy | Pupil voice is present in the discussion beats. The exit is a commitment, not a test | No change |
| 5 Explicit instruction | Retrieval, small steps and checks all present, and prior knowledge names its source modules | No change |
| 6 Frameworks | EfCW 1 and 6 correct. RSHE 2026 hook correct | No change |
| 7 SEND and access | Paper fallback present. The feeling based check may be hard for a pupil with alexithymia | Add a body cue alternative: "notice where you feel it" |
| 8 Adolescent development | Good. It does not lecture and it does not moralise about appearance | No change |
| 9 Evidence | **The only red.** `evidence_anchor` is a single string. A parent challenging the pornography content gets nothing | The evidence base above |
| 10 Near future | AI generated bodies are the near term version of this lesson and it is only tagged, not taught | Add one beat: an idealised body can now be generated whole, with nobody in it |

**Where lenses 1 and 10 disagree:** lens 10 wants the AI generated body beat,
lens 1 warns that generated bodies sit next to deepfake abuse and could land
badly. Resolution: teach generated bodies as an advertising and beauty
phenomenon only, and leave image based abuse to ks4-16, where it is properly
supported. That disagreement is exactly why the panel is worth running.

## Part 4: what this changes for the module

1. Four new teacher fields: subject knowledge, hard questions, parent
   questions, evidence base.
2. One new teach beat on AI generated bodies, advertising framing only.
3. One line in the tool on where the controls live.
4. One SEND alternative on the image check.
5. `ailit_domains` stays "Engage with AI" and gains "Understand AI".

Nothing in the existing deck is removed. This is a module that was already
good becoming one a nervous non specialist can teach without wobbling.
