### Caption

Two teenagers, the same hour, the same app, and only one of them is having a bad time. That is the bit a screen time number can never show you.

Researchers followed 197 teenagers for a year, recorded what their phones actually did rather than asking them to remember, and asked how they were feeling four times a day. 17,580 moments.

For entertainment apps the order surprised them. Feeling better came first, and less phone time followed. For social apps there was a small real cost, and we are saying that out loud rather than skipping it.

But when they looked at each teenager individually, about nine in ten showed no measurable link between mood and picking the phone up. How they felt an hour earlier mattered far more than what the phone did.

One study, 197 teenagers in one country, measuring how they felt in the moment. It cannot tell you about sleep or about next year, and we would rather say so.

## Single image · The one line version

**On the card**

The best predictor of a
teenager's mood
was their mood.

Not their screen time.

### Caption

197 teenagers. A year. Phones logged rather than remembered, feelings recorded four times a day.

How a teenager felt an hour ago predicted how they feel now several times more strongly than anything their phone did.

Their mood was mostly about their day.

There was a small real effect from social apps and we are not pretending otherwise. But if you are trying to read your child in a weekly screen time report, that report is not where they are.

Ask about the day first.
