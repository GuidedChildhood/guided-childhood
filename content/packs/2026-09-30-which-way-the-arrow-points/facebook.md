## Post · Your child is probably not in the statistic

Something worth knowing, if you have spent this year feeling like the phone is doing something to your child.

Researchers followed 197 teenagers for a year. Rather than asking them at the end how much they thought they had used their phone, they recorded what the phone actually did, and asked them how they were feeling four times a day.

Then they looked at each teenager one at a time instead of lumping them together.

For around nine in ten of them, there was no detectable link between how they were feeling and whether they picked the phone up.

Not a small link. Nothing they could measure.

There was one real finding, and I will not skip it, because it is the one that goes against the comforting version. Time on social apps was followed by feeling slightly worse. Small, but there, and the researchers had expected the opposite.

But the thing that stayed with me is this. How a teenager felt an hour ago predicted how they feel now far more strongly than anything their phone did. Several times more strongly.

Their mood was mostly about their day. Not their screen.

I am not telling you phones are fine. This was 197 teenagers in one country, measuring how they felt in the moment, and it cannot tell you about sleep or about next year.

What it does say is that if you are staring at a screen time number and trying to read your child in it, you are reading the wrong page.

Ask them how their day went first. The number will still be there afterwards.

## Notes

**No numbers in the body on purpose.** The LinkedIn audience wants b values. A
parent on Facebook wants to know whether to worry about their own child. The
proof lives in the comment if someone asks, and the plan file holds it.

**The concession stays in.** "The one that goes against the comforting version"
is what stops this reading as reassurance for its own sake. A parent can smell
that, and the whole brand is that we do not do it.

**It ends on an action that costs nothing and needs no product.** Ask about the
day before you look at the number. That is the argument made small enough to do
tonight.
