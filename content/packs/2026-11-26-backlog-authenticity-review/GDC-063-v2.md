## LinkedIn

Parents often ask who their child follows. A new study suggests that may be the wrong first question.

Researchers asked 147 young people in the Netherlands, aged 16 to 25, to donate their real TikTok feed histories. Together those histories covered about 6.7 million public videos, mostly from October 2023 to May 2026.

For the typical person in the study, news, politics and societal issues made up 5.34 percent of the feed, or about 126 videos a month.

A median of 98.4 percent of that content came from accounts the person did not follow, based on the accounts they followed when they donated their data.

That doesn't mean 98.4 percent of every young person's feed came from strangers. The figure describes the median person in a small volunteer sample, and some participants saw much less from unfollowed accounts. These were older teenagers and young adults in one country, not children in the UK. The paper is a preprint, and it doesn't show any effect on beliefs, mood, politics or wellbeing.

It does show a gap in the usual family conversation. We ask who they follow. The feed has another question built into it: why did the system choose to show you this?

That's worth teaching before a child has to work it out alone. In the Guided Childhood primary curriculum, a lesson on how algorithms work gives children a simple starting point: you don't choose the feed, the algorithm does.

The study doesn't prove that the lesson changes what a child later sees or believes. It's a reason to teach how recommendation works before the feed becomes ordinary.

The preprint is in the comments. Next time you look at a feed together, try asking why it showed you that one.

## First comment

Source: a PsyArXiv preprint, November 2026:

https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/j73bg

The big limit is the sample: 147 volunteers in the Netherlands, aged 16 to 25, with an average age of 20.4. It tells us nothing about children under 16 or young people in the UK. The 98.4 percent figure is the median for news, politics and societal content, not the share of anyone's whole feed, and follows were measured when the data were donated, not when each video was seen.
