## LinkedIn

77 percent of parents told YouGov they back a ban on social media for under 16s, and they split 45 to 46 on whether it will work.

YouGov published the figures on 15 June, the day the government confirmed the ban. Among British parents with a child under 18, 77 percent were in support and 14 percent against. Asked whether it would be effective, 45 percent said yes and 46 percent said no.

I think those parents are being realistic. You can want a rule and still know your own child, and your child's friends, and the older cousin with a spare phone.

A poll measures what people expect, and expecting something to fail is no evidence that it will. The parents were asked a few days before the announcement, so nobody was reacting to the detail. YouGov hasn't published how the two answers overlap, so I can't tell you how many parents hold both views at once, only that on those numbers it's unlikely to be a handful. And the support comes from somewhere real: around four in ten parents in the same research said social media had affected their own children badly.

Put the two numbers side by side and you can see who is answering. A parent who wants the law and expects to be doing the work anyway.

The government says the protections should take effect in spring 2027. For a ten year old that leaves six years on the far side of the rule, and one birthday at the end of it when the rule stops.

Nothing in the law says what happens in those six years. That was never its job.

We built Guided Childhood for those years: ten minutes a day, parent and child together, working through what a child should be able to do before the account arrives, with the words for the arguments along the way.
