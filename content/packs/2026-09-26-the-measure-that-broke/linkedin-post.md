# LinkedIn post · The measure that quietly stopped working

Drafted 26 September 2026, reacting to Meta Connect (23 to 25 September) and the
settlement approved 26 August. News reactive, so it decays. Post same day or not
at all.

1,960 characters. Under the 3,000 limit with room for the tag.

## The post

On 26 August a court approved a settlement requiring Meta to put default time limits on every user under 18.

Four weeks later, Meta launched a device with no screen to limit.

The settlement was approved by Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers and covers 29 state attorneys general. Just over $17bn, rising to roughly $18bn once the remaining states and territories are counted. Among the things Meta agreed to were default time limits and blocks for under 18s on Facebook and Instagram.

At Meta Connect last week the company announced more than a hundred styles of AI glasses arriving by the end of the year, including the Ray Ban Meta Audio at $349. Its AI agent, Muse, reaches the glasses in the coming months, woken by saying its name.

Then there is the age split. The glasses need a Meta account from 13. The Muse app is 18 and over.

One thing Meta got right, and it matters. The Audio glasses have no camera. They chose to ship a pair without one. That is a better decision than most of this week's commentary has allowed for and I am not going to pretend otherwise.

But a screenless, always available assistant in a child's ear does something to our favourite measure. You cannot count minutes of screen on a device that has no screen. Every parental control I know of, and every clause in that settlement, counts sessions on a feed. There is no feed here.

Two thirteen year olds, same house, same pair of glasses. One asks it to translate a menu. One asks it what to say to the girl who stopped replying. Same device, same minutes, nothing in common.

That is the trouble with counting hours, and this is the device that finally makes it obvious.

What bothers me is not whether the glasses are dangerous. It is that the measure we all agreed to use has quietly stopped working, and nothing has replaced it.

If your child asked for a pair at 13, what would you actually want to know before you said yes?

## Prepared first comment

Post this the second the post goes live. It carries the sourcing and gets ahead
of the one objection that would otherwise define the thread.

Sources, since the dates are doing the work here. The settlement was approved 26 August 2026 by Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers, 29 state attorneys general, and the under 18 default time limits are in the agreed terms. The glasses and Muse were announced at Meta Connect, 23 to 25 September. Age requirements are Meta's own: 13 for a Meta account, 18 for the Muse app, and they vary by country.

And to be clear about what I am not saying. I have no evidence Meta built glasses to get around a settlement, and I do not think that. The two things are almost certainly unrelated. That is rather the point. Nobody had to plan this for our only measure to stop working.

## Why it is shaped this way

**The hook is two dates and no adjective.** The whole argument is the gap between
26 August and 23 September, so the first two lines are only those two facts. No
claim to argue with, which is what makes it hard to dismiss.

**The honest pivot is the camera credit.** Meta shipped a pair with no camera.
That is genuinely the better decision and most of the week's commentary missed
it. Conceding it before anyone raises it is what earns the rest, and it is the
move the best performing post already rested on.

**The contrast is two children, not a statistic.** Same house, same glasses, same
minutes, nothing in common. It is the screen time argument in one image, and it
is the argument the product exists to answer.

**No claim about the glasses being harmful.** There is no evidence for one and it
is not the point. The point is the measurement, which is checkable.

## What was left out, on purpose

There is a story circulating that the Muse mascot is designed to appeal to young
children on an 18 plus product. It is a strong angle and it could not be stood
up: the only source found was a partisan site. It fails the Odgers test, so it is
not in the post. If a mainstream outlet runs it, it earns its own piece.
