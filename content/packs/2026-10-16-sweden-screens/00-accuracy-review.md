# The Sweden post, checked claim by claim

Justin, 16 October 2026: a viral LinkedIn post about Sweden going back to
printed textbooks. He asked how embedded screens actually are in UK and US
schools, what the stats say, whether there is proven data on it working,
whether the post is accurate, and then a comment and a post of our own.

**The verdict in one line.** The policy is real and the post describes it
accurately. What the post does is leave out the only question that matters,
which is whether it worked, and the answer to that is that nobody knows yet.

## There is no proven data on it working, and one number went the wrong way

This is the hole in every version of this story we could find.

- **No evaluation exists.** We looked for a Skolverket or OECD assessment of the
  textbook reforms against reading outcomes and found none. The closest thing is
  a study of the earlier Reading Lift programme, which examined whether the
  government's instruction was followed, not whether children read better.
- **PISA 2025, published 8 September 2026, has Sweden's reading at 466, down 21
  points since 2022.** The OECD average also fell, by 14 points to 463, so
  Sweden slipped to about average rather than below it. Maths fell 18 points to
  464 and science 8 points to 485. Roughly one in three Swedish 15 year olds can
  handle only the simplest texts. Anna Castberg, head of analysis at Skolverket:
  "It is very serious that we see such a large group that does not reach a basic
  level."
- **That number does not judge the policy, and we will not pretend it does.**
  Those students were born in 2009. Almost all of their schooling happened
  before 2023. The honest reading is that the reversal has not been tested yet,
  and anyone claiming Sweden proves books beat screens is claiming something
  nobody has measured.

## What the evidence does and does not support

**Paper over screen for reading is real and small.** Delgado and colleagues
(2018) pooled 54 studies and 171,055 students and found an advantage for paper
of g = 0.21, stronger for expository text than for narrative, and growing since
2000.

**For children specifically it is weaker than that.** The child focused
meta analysis (Furenes, Kucirkova and Bus, 2021) put story comprehension at
about 0.10 in favour of paper books with a confidence interval that still
includes zero. So for children, no clear advantage either way on that measure.

**The handwriting study everyone quotes is not about children.** The EEG paper
on handwriting and brain connectivity (Van der Weel and Van der Meer, 2024,
Frontiers in Psychology) tested 36 university students, not school pupils, and a
2025 commentary by Pinet and Longcamp disputes its claim about learning. It is
an interesting finding. It is not a basis for a primary school policy.

**Distraction is the better evidenced harm, and it is a different claim.** In
PISA 2022, about 30 percent of students across OECD countries said they get
distracted using digital devices in most or every maths lesson, and 25 percent
said they get distracted by other students using them. Those reporting
distraction in most or every lesson scored 15 points lower in maths after
accounting for the socio economic profile of students and schools.

**Removing the device is not a reliable fix either.** The University of
Birmingham study published in February 2025 looked at 1,227 students across 30
schools, 20 of them with restrictive phone policies, and found no difference in
wellbeing, sleep, physical activity or English and maths attainment. Bans cut
phone use in school by around 40 minutes but did not reduce overall use. It is
cross sectional, so it cannot settle cause. Norwegian work by Abrahamsson across
roughly 400 middle schools points the other way, with bullying down about 43 to
46 percent and better grades for girls, and no notable effect on boys. A scoping
review of 22 before and after studies concluded the evidence is weak and
inconclusive. Both of those things are true at once and a careful person says so.

## How embedded this actually is

**The United States, deeply.** CoSN's 2026 survey of district technology leaders
reports one to one device access in 90 percent of middle school grades, 88
percent of high school, 84 percent of grades 3 to 5 and 78 percent of
kindergarten to grade 2. In 2020 the comparable elementary figure was 43
percent. It is also where the first retreat is visible: Los Angeles Unified, the
second largest district in the country, is removing digital devices from early
education through grade 1 from autumn 2026, and 38 percent of districts expect
reduced device funding.

**England, real but patchier, and nobody is measuring the use.** The DfE
Technology in Schools Survey found 95 percent of primaries had tablets for
pupils against 43 percent of secondaries, and across device types relatively few
IT leads reported devices available for more than a quarter of pupils. So
England is not a one to one country. The DfE's own June 2026 market assessment
puts 1,092 edtech companies at £5.93 billion of annual turnover and calls for
clearer usage data, which is the admission worth noticing: the spend is
measured, the effect is not. Every school is expected to meet six digital and
technology standards by 2030.

*We could not open the 2024 to 2025 survey's device tables. The PDF would not
extract in this container and no page tool was available, so the device figures
above come from the published survey summary and the earlier wave. Worth
confirming before any of them goes in a post.*

## What this means for Guided Childhood

The whole Sweden story is about **which tools a school puts in front of a
child**. Our customer's problem is **the hours at home and the argument at nine
at night**, which no textbook policy touches.

The useful crossover is the distraction finding, because it says the harm is in
the interruption rather than the medium. That is the same shape as our own
thesis, that it is not the quantity of screen time but what the design does with
a particular child's attention. The proof path already exists in the product:
protected time that cannot be bought at any price, covering bedtime, mealtimes
and school hours, and the staged pathway and stage check at /starter-pack.

## What never goes in

No claim that Sweden proves anything about outcomes. No use of the handwriting
EEG study as if it tested children. No rounding the paper advantage up into
"screens harm reading". No claim that our product protects a child. The two
statutory figures stay as stated, and the textbook funding is described as
reported rather than confirmed.
