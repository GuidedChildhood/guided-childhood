## The motive, which is the new material

Reimers (University of Gothenburg), Martin Bylund and Stenliden (Linköping
University), *Constructions of a reading crisis: representation of PIRLS 2021 in
public discourses*, published online 17 August 2025 in **Discourse: Studies in
the Cultural Politics of Education**, 47(2), 2026, pages 265 to 273.

Their finding, in their own framing:

- **The National Agency for Education emphasised the positives** of PIRLS 2021.
  **The government presented the same results as a reading crisis.**
- The results were slightly lower than the previous round, but **Sweden had moved
  up the league table and declined less than most countries did.** The authors
  ask why potentially positive results were framed negatively.
- **The agency attributes the main problem in Swedish education to segregation.
  The government emphasises digitalisation, migration, and insufficient time
  allocated for reading.**

That is not a conspiracy theory and we do not present it as one. It is a
documented divergence between what a country's own education agency said about
its data and what its government said about the same data, published in a peer
reviewed journal by three Swedish academics.

**The symmetry that finishes the argument.** The 2023 digitisation plan was
scrapped for not being sufficiently evidence based. What replaced it is also not
evidence based. Same move, opposite direction.

**Context worth holding.** Lotta Edholm, the schools minister who drove this, is
a Liberal in the centre right coalition that took office after the 2022
election. The reversal arrived with a change of government rather than with new
evidence. We state the timing and let the reader draw the inference, because we
cannot read anyone's mind and will not claim to.

**What we will not say.** That Sweden acted in bad faith. That publishers drove
it, because we found no evidence for that and looked. That the reading decline
is fictional, because reading difficulties among 10 year olds are reported to
have risen from 12 to 19 percent in five years and the pandemic sits inside that
window. The claim is narrower and survives: the crisis framing was a political
choice that its own agency did not share, and the villain it chose is the one
you can fix with a book order.

Five years ago I would have shared the Sweden story without checking it. Screens out of classrooms, printed textbooks back in.

I thought screens were mostly bad for children and that school should stay close to paper and pen, so a country as digital as Sweden turning round would have gone straight onto my feed. I changed my mind since, and not into the opposite camp, which is the part that takes longer.

The policy is real. In 2023 Sweden threw out its own national digitisation plan. In 2024 it funded printed textbooks. From July 2025, under twos in preschool use analogue materials only. All of that is accurate.

Here is what the viral version leaves out.

Sweden's own education agency never called it a crisis.

Three Swedish researchers went back through how the 2021 reading results were presented. The National Agency for Education emphasised the positives. The government called it a reading crisis. Sweden had in fact moved up the league table and declined less than most countries did.

And the two of them named different culprits. The agency said the main problem in Swedish schooling was segregation. The government said digitalisation, migration, and not enough time spent reading.

Only one of those is a villain you can fix with a book order.

Nobody has shown the books worked either. There is no evaluation, and PISA in September had Swedish reading down 21 points. I am not using that against the policy, because those students were born in 2009 and were schooled before the reform. It judges nothing.

The research is thinner than the story too. Paper does beat screen for reading, by about a fifth of a standard deviation across 54 studies. Look only at children and it halves, with a confidence interval that still includes zero. The handwriting brain study everyone quotes tested 36 university students.

So a government threw out one plan for not being evidence based, and replaced it with another that is not evidence based. Same move, opposite direction, and far better press.

The fight in my house was never about which book my son was reading. It was about the thing in his hand that kept asking for him back. No book order reaches that.

A school can change its tools. A parent cannot. So my bet, and I will call it a bet, is on teaching a child to notice it.

What actually changed in your house, the device or the rules around it?

### First comment

Sources. The crisis framing: Reimers, Martin Bylund and Stenliden, Constructions of a reading crisis, Discourse: Studies in the Cultural Politics of Education, 2025, which documents that the National Agency for Education emphasised the positives of PIRLS 2021 while the government called it a crisis, that Sweden moved up the league table and declined less than most countries, and that the agency named segregation where the government named digitalisation and migration. Policy: the digitisation plan was scrapped in May 2023 for not being sufficiently evidence based, and an ordinance in force from 1 July 2025 restricts non analogue tools in preschool. ## What was deliberately not said

No bad faith. No publisher lobby. No claim the reading decline is invented. No
claim Sweden proves anything about outcomes in either direction. The handwriting
study is not presented as if it tested children. The paper advantage is not
rounded up. No product, no stage check, no price, because the argument has to
stand on its own. The original poster is not named.

## What would make this post wrong

An evaluation of the Swedish reforms, or a randomised trial of classroom device
removal. The nearest is the Bradford IRL Trial, reporting 2028. If either lands,
say so in public.
