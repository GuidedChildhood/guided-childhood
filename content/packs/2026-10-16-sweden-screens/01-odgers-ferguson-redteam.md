# What Odgers and Ferguson would say

Justin, 16 October 2026: "can we check what odgers and ferguson would say."

Candice Odgers (UC Irvine) and Christopher Ferguson (Stetson) are the two names
behind our own internal standard, the Odgers test, and both appear in the Wrong
Villain post that remains Justin's best performer. This is GDC-043 and the
accuracy review put through their actual published positions rather than a
caricature of them.

## Their positions, as they state them

**Odgers.** In Nature, April 2025: "Science to date does not support the
widespread panic around social media and adolescent mental health." Her
longitudinal work following thousands of 10 to 14 year olds since 2008 does not
find social media emerging as a major predictor of mental health. She argues no
study has yet tested whether removing social media improves it, and that bans
are evaded anyway, citing a BMJ study in which over 85 percent of Australians
under 16 still use social media after the account ban. Her alternative focus is
economic insecurity, family stress, caregiver mental health and access to care.

**Ferguson.** His 2022 meta analysis of 37 effect sizes across 33 studies found
screen media plays little role in mental health concerns, and the same when
restricted to smartphones or social media. His 2025 follow up, narrowed to
under 18s, reports no correlation between time on social media and adolescent
mental health problems. And the one that matters most here, also 2025, is titled
**Smartphone bans in schools remain unproven**: the available research does not
ratify the case for school phone bans.

## What they would approve of in our draft

Quite a lot, and it is worth saying so rather than only collecting the hits.

- Refusing to use the PISA 2025 fall against the policy, on the grounds that
  those students were schooled before the reform. Both of them spend their
  careers objecting to exactly that move in the other direction.
- Saying the paper advantage is small rather than decisive, and giving the
  child specific figure where the confidence interval still includes zero.
- Naming the handwriting study as 36 university students rather than letting it
  stand as evidence about children.
- Refusing to claim the reform worked when nobody has measured it.

## Where they would wince, and both hits are fair

**1. Ferguson would turn our own argument back on us.** The draft says the
distraction finding is "well evidenced" and then pivots the whole post onto it.
But the PISA figure is cross sectional self report, and the man who has
published most on this has a 2025 paper saying school phone bans remain
unproven. If bans are unproven, then using self reported distraction to imply
interruption is the lever commits the same error we are accusing the Sweden post
of, just pointing the other way. This is the sharpest criticism available and
the draft had no answer to it.

**Adopted.** The post now says the distraction finding is better evidenced *and
still only an association*, and it names the counter evidence itself: the
English study of 1,227 students across 30 schools that found no difference in
attainment or wellbeing. Putting the strongest objection in the post rather than
waiting for it in the comments is the Wrong Villain move anyway.

**2. Odgers would catch an untested method claim.** "We have to teach a child to
notice it" reads as something demonstrated. It is not. Nobody has shown that
teaching a child to notice a feed narrowing changes any outcome, and she would
be right to say so, because her standing objection is that no study has tested
whether the intervention everyone assumes works actually works.

**Adopted.** It is now stated as a bet, in those words. That is more honest and
it sounds more like Justin, not less.

## Where we hold the line

**Odgers would say a screens post is still a screens post.** Her consistent
argument is that this whole debate pulls attention from economic insecurity,
family stress and caregiver mental health. A sceptical post about paper versus
screens still keeps the conversation on screens.

She is right, and it is already our own rule: the hidden thread runs at one post
in ten and the other nine carry it as a single brick. GDC-043 is one of the
nine. The brick it carries is that the drivers are in the home rather than the
device, which is why the post ends on what changed in your house. No edit, but
it is logged, and the next deliberate hidden thread post is overdue.

**Neither of them gets a veto on the policy section.** Every claim about what
Sweden actually did is documented and neither disputes that sort of fact. Their
expertise is effect sizes, not Swedish statute.

**Ferguson would probably go further than we will.** He would argue the g = 0.21
paper advantage is itself inflated by publication bias and small study effects,
and that the child only estimate near zero is the better guide. He may be right.
But we have not read a bias analysis of that specific literature, so we state
both figures and let the reader see the gap, rather than adopt his correction on
trust. Advice is an input, never an instruction, including from the people whose
name we gave our own test.

## The one thing that would change our mind

If an evaluation of the Swedish reforms appears, or a randomised trial of
classroom device removal reports, this post is out of date and the honest thing
is to say so in public. The Bradford IRL Trial reporting in 2028 is the nearest
thing coming.
