## The post

Most online safety lessons are taught by someone who isn't an online safety specialist.

That isn't a criticism. It's the job.

A Year 4 teacher on a Tuesday afternoon, with the RSHE slot coming up and no evening left to prepare it.

So I built the schools version around one test: could that teacher open it cold and teach it well?

This is the whole of the start. Twenty seconds, no sound.

Type your school code.
Pick your year.
Press Teach.

The words to say are on every slide. The teacher never has to make it up on the spot.

And when the lesson ends, a note goes home to parents with one question to ask at tea.

Because what a child learns at school about scams, strangers and group chats only sticks if the conversation carries on at home.

We're running a free pilot this term. Two lessons, every printable, no card, no contract, no pupil data.

If you teach RSHE, or lead it: what's the hardest part of the online safety lessons to deliver well?

- "What goes home?" A one page parent note per lesson, with one question for the family.
