**Article text:**

One of the best things we have built is not software at all. It is a piece of paper
your child fills in, in their own handwriting.

In the last piece the research was clear. The screen rules that stick are the ones a
child helps write, because ownership is what makes a rule get followed, and a rule
imposed is a rule tested. So we built the most direct possible answer to that
finding. The family phone agreement. And nearly every design decision in it exists to
do one thing, move the pen from your hand into your child's.

Here is how it works, and why each part is the way it is. It is a deal, not a decree.
Five sections, calibrated to the stage your child is at, so a ten year old's
agreement and a fifteen year old's are not the same document. You sit down together
and you build it, and the child writes their half of the promises themselves, in
their own hand. That last detail is not decoration. A promise a child writes in their
own words is defended by them, because it is theirs. A rule you typed and printed is
argued with, because it is yours. The handwriting is the ownership, made physical.

It is built as a ceremony, not a lecture. The night a child gets their first phone,
they will remember how it felt, and our whole design push is to make it feel like
joining something, not signing surrender papers. You sit at the table, you talk it
through, you both put your names to a deal you made together. A lecture washes over a
ten year old in about ninety seconds. A ceremony they helped build sticks for years.
Same information. Completely different thing.

And it is designed to grow. This is the part I care about most. The agreement is not
a fixed fence, it is a pathway. Every school term you review the deal together, and
where trust has been kept, the deal loosens. The child can earn more. That does two
things at once. It rewards the trust you actually want to grow, and it means the
agreement is never a stale relic on the fridge, it is a living thing that keeps pace
with the child. Which quietly solves the exact failure the research described, the
plan that goes invisible within a fortnight. This one has a reason to be picked up
again every single term.

There is a printable fridge copy, because the thing needs to be visible and physical
and in the room, not buried in an app. And the whole shape of it, agree together,
write your own half, review each term, loosen as trust grows, is really just the
Pediatrics finding turned into an object a family can actually use on a Tuesday.

I want to be honest about what a piece of paper can and cannot do. It does not
enforce itself. It does not watch anyone. A family that never revisits it will get a
dead document like any other. It is not a lock and it was never meant to be. What it
is, is a structure for the conversation the research says matters, and a way to make
sure the child holds the pen. The rest is you, and the relationship, which is exactly
as it should be, because that relationship is the actual protection.

And, as with everything in this series, look at what is not at the centre. Not the
AI. A piece of paper, five sections, a pen, a termly review. Almost aggressively low
tech, built by a technology company, because the evidence said the thing that works
is ownership and a conversation, not another screen telling a family what to do.

The best parenting tool we make might be the one with no code in it at all. A deal
your child helped write, in their own hand, that grows as they do. Everything I
believe about this is in that one small object.

What is one screen rule in your house you could hand your child the pen on, and let
them write in their own words this week?
