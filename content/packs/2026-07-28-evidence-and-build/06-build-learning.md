# Article 6 (Build) — We built the screen into a thing that makes and teaches

Series: The evidence and the build. Pairs with Article 5 (the child who makes).
Image: Justin with the printables or a craft pack, or a real making shot with the
kids. No dashes. Roughly 800 words. DiGi stays in the background.

**Hook:** Paste your child's homework here and I will not tell you if it is right.

---

**Article text:**

Paste your child's homework in and the first thing our tool does is refuse to tell
you if it is right.

That refusal is the whole point, and it comes straight out of the last piece. If the
good uses of a screen are the making, the learning and the connecting, and the job
is to make the good kind the easy kind, then a product that just judges and blocks
is aimed at exactly the wrong target. So we built the other thing. Tools that put the
screen to work.

Start with the homework decoder, because it is the clearest example. Every parent
has had the evening where a child brings home a worksheet and you have no idea what
it is really testing, or how to help without either doing it for them or getting it
wrong. Paste the homework in and the decoder gives you three things. The statutory
line sitting behind it, the actual national curriculum objective. What that line is
for, in plain words, so you understand the point of it. And one thing you could do
tonight to help, a question to ask, a game to play, a way in. What it never gives you
is a verdict on whether the answer is right, because the goal is not to mark your
child. It is to hand you, the parent, a way to help. The screen becomes a bridge
between you and the learning, not a referee.

Then there are the lessons. We built a full curriculum, and the child facing version
runs as star lessons, a parent can send any lesson to a child on a simple link, and
it plays as an interactive thing they do, not a lecture they sit through. It is the
making and learning use of a screen, deliberately designed to be more engaging than
the scroll, which is exactly the tilt the research asked for. Make the good kind the
easy kind.

And then, my favourite, the printables and the craft packs. This is the part people
find strange for a technology company. We build things that pull the child off the
screen entirely, colouring sheets, build at home kits, escape kits, and we wire them
into the same reward loop, so that doing the offline thing pays out inside the
system. The screen sends the child to the kitchen table, and then quietly rewards
them for staying there. A device that is, on purpose, sometimes a door out of
itself.

Notice the through line. None of these is a lock. None of them counts hours. Each one
takes the research finding, that what a child does with a screen matters more than
how long, and turns it into a thing you can actually reach for on a Tuesday night.
The homework decoder makes the learning use easier. The star lessons make the making
use more inviting than the passive one. The printables make the offline use pay. We
are not trying to shrink the screen. We are trying to change what is on it, and what
it points a child toward.

I will be straight about the honest limits, because the last piece was. Good uses
helping is a modest, contested finding, not a magic wand, and a learning app is only
as good as the design and the child using it. We are not claiming a printable fixes
anything on its own. What we are claiming is narrower and, I think, more honest. If
the evidence says the mode matters more than the minutes, then a product should spend
its effort on the mode, on making the good things easy and rewarding, rather than
pouring all of it into counting and blocking the bad. So that is where we spent it.

And, again, look at what is not at the centre. Not the AI. A homework lookup, a
lesson player, a stack of printables and a reward loop. Plain tools, built because
the research pointed at the good uses, and almost nobody was building for the good
uses. Everyone was building the timer.

The child who makes and learns is the one we are not up at night worrying about. So
we built the tools that make that child slightly easier to raise, on a normal
evening, with a normal amount of energy left.

What is one offline thing you wish your child would choose, and would it help if the
screen itself nudged them toward it and rewarded them for going?
