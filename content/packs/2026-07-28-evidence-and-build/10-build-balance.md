**Article text:**

We built a screen time number that has almost nothing to do with screen time. It is
the thing I am proudest of, and it closes this whole series.

In the last piece I made the argument, and said the whole of the belief plainly.
Responsible use is not the hours, it is the pattern, and the pattern grows out of the
life around the screen. So the honest design question is not how do I count the
screen, it is how do I help a family build the life. This is what we made to try.

Start with the balance report. Every week, instead of showing you a single scolding
number of hours, it counts the other side of the ledger. The off screen effort. The
jobs done, the printables finished, the time spent outside, the real world things a
child actually did, set beside the screen time rather than pretending the screen time
is the only fact worth knowing. It is a deliberately different question. Not how long
was the child on a screen, but is the week in balance, is there enough in the other
column, is the screen one good thing among many or is it starting to be the only
thing in the boat. That is the pattern the research cared about, made visible, and
made improvable by adding to the good side rather than only subtracting from the bad.

Then there are the scripts. This is the least glamorous and possibly most used thing
we make. A library of more than a hundred conversation scripts for the actual
moments, the ones no evidence base and no dashboard ever helps you with at half past
six on a Tuesday. What to say when the device comes to the dinner table. What to say
when it goes off and the meltdown starts. What to say when your child pushes back,
and then what to say when they push back again. Real words, for the real doorway
moments where balance is actually won or lost, because balance is not built in a
policy, it is built in a hundred small conversations, and most of us do not have the
words ready in the moment.

Notice what both of these do. They move the whole product away from the clock and
toward the life. The balance report reframes the week around what a child did, not
just how long they stared. The scripts arm the parent for the small human moments
where the pattern actually gets set. Neither one counts hours to scold you. Both of
them help you build the fuller life that, the evidence says, is what produces the
balanced pattern in the first place.

And there is the sleep line, held as hard in the product as I held it in the last
piece. The phone out of the bedroom at night is the one boundary everything nudges
toward, because sleep sits upstream of almost everything else, and it is the single
highest return thing a tired family can do.

I will be honest about the limits, because the whole series has been. A balance
report does not fix a hard week, and a script is only words, it still needs a parent
with enough left in the tank to say them. These are not magic. What they are is an
honest attempt to build for the thing that actually matters, the pattern and the
balance, instead of the thing that is merely easy to measure, the hours. We spent our
effort where the evidence pointed, even though the number would have been easier to
sell.

And, one last time, look at what is not at the centre of any of it. Not the AI. A
weekly balance of real life against screen time, and a stack of scripts for hard
moments. Plain, human, almost analogue tools, built by a technology company that
decided, on purpose, that the answer to too much screen was not more screen, and not
a cleverer lock, but a fuller life and better words.

That is the whole series, in one line. Not the device, and not the ban. The pattern,
and the life around it, built one ordinary moment at a time, from age four. Every
piece of research pointed there, and every thing we built is an attempt to make that
life a little easier to raise on a normal week with a normal amount left to give.

If any of this is the way you want to raise your own, we made a free place to start,
at guidedchildhood.com/starter-pack. No number on it that is not true. It begins, as
all of it does, with one ordinary conversation.

What is one thing in the other column of your child's week, the offline side, that
you could add this week, so the screen has to earn its place beside it?
