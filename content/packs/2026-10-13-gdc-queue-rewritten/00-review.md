## First, the part that is better than ours

The evidence discipline in these is genuinely strong and should be kept. Every
post carries a bulletproof check, a list of wording to avoid, a KNOWN and
UNCERTAIN split, a calm reply if challenged, and a named primary source with a
date. GDC-033 states plainly that 6,310 is a count of images and not a count of
children, and that the figure is not a risk estimate for a family photo. GDC-029
refuses to rank one platform as safer than another. GDC-031 lists "phone bans do
not work" as a phrase never to use.

That passes the Odgers test cleanly, and it is more careful than most of what
our own skills produce unprompted. The rewrites below keep all of it. What
follows is about voice, hooks, structure and proof, not about honesty.

## What fails against our skills, worst first

**1. The hook is almost never the hook.** Ten of the fourteen open on a policy
event or a throat clear. "A new US bill would stop...". "America has just taken
another step...". "Norway said today...". "Yesterday a UK online safety rule
crossed a line". "There is a fairly technical court case happening in London".

Justin's own 28 day numbers say the opposite. The two posts that opened on an
admitted uncertainty did 6,008 and 3,155 impressions and carried 67 percent of
every comment he got that month. The two that opened on a figure did 1,002 and
698 and got zero comments. The news belongs in the third paragraph where it
rewards the reader, not the first where it closes the thought.

Three posts in the set already do it right, and they are the pattern to copy.
GDC-024: "When my eldest started asking for a phone, I wanted an age." GDC-027:
"One of my daughter's friends moved to Wales." GDC-032: "The most interesting
person in the smart glasses debate may be the child who never put them on."

**2. "For five years" has become a tic.** Some version of it appears in ten of
the fourteen posts. `founder-context.md` says plainly: do not repeatedly use the
same founder anecdote, look for small scenes rather than repeatedly summarising
the biography. Said once it is a real change of mind. Said in every post it
reads as a slogan, and a reader who follows him sees it every time. The rewrites
use it exactly once, in GDC-024, where it is load bearing.

**3. Hedging that reads as machine careful rather than human direct.** "I
think", "I am interested in", "I would be interested", "increasingly",
"Perhaps", "That feels much closer to", "I am becoming convinced", "may be".
Across the set the count runs to well over a hundred. Honesty about uncertainty
is the brand. Hedging every sentence is a different thing, and it costs the
authority the honesty earns. State the thing, then state its limit. Do not
soften the thing in advance.

**4. No CTA and no prepared first comment, in any of the fourteen.**
`linkedin-engagement.md` and `viral-post` both treat the first comment as half
the reach: it carries the sourced detail and the link, and the body says it is
there. Every post here ends on a question, which is right and is the strongest
thing in the set, and then nothing. No repost ask, no route to the stage check,
nothing to click. Every rewrite below has a first comment written and ready.

**5. The proof path is usually a promise rather than a product.** "That is
something I want us to build properly into the Digital Passport." "Add AI
Relationship Literacy to the Digital Passport roadmap." "That is now going
directly into the pathway." `research-radar` Phase 3 exists for this: a finding
with no proof path does not become content. We have a great deal built now, and
the rewrites point at what exists by name: the stage check at `/starter-pack`,
three questions and no sign up; DiGi's rule that it never allows and never
denies; the 29 module schools scheme on Education for a Connected World and the
2025 RSHE guidance; the 448 England objectives in DfE wording; the check in's
five faces measured against a baseline; the script deck. A roadmap item is not
a proof path.

**6. Four of fourteen are United States policy.** California SB 1119, a US House
bill, a US Senate bill, a Meta US schools programme. A UK parent at the school
gate and a UK head do not act on a bill introduced in Congress, and a run of
them makes the account read as a policy newsletter rather than a product with an
argument. The UK ones (GDC-026, GDC-028, GDC-033, GDC-034, GDC-035) are the
strongest in the set, which is the tell.

**7. A dash in finished copy.** GDC-002: "It's a tiny study, eight children, so
I don't think it tells us" is written with two em dashes. Non negotiable 4.

**8. Six of fourteen are dead on arrival today.** "Yesterday", "this week",
"today", "overnight 5 to 6 October", "the hearing is due to conclude Wednesday
7 October". It is 13 October. The ideas inside them survive; the news clock does
not, so they need rewriting as the idea rather than refreshing as the news.

**9. The series has no name on the post.** A GDC number is filing. "In
Proportion" and "The Wrong Villain" are what readers followed. The rewrites
carry the series line.

## What to do about the queue itself

The folder holds duplicates of most posts across three locations, three empty
files, and status markers written inside the body text ("GDC-030done"). That is
why it is hard to tell what has run. One folder, one file per number, and the
status in the title rather than the first line would fix it in ten minutes.

Nothing here has been posted, scheduled or sent. Justin does that.
