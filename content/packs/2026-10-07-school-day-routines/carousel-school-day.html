The finding

Orben and Przybylski at Oxford, Nature Human Behaviour 2019. Three large adolescent datasets, two American and one British, one analysis.

0.4%

The most of adolescent wellbeing that technology use explained, a correlation of about 0.06. Same datasets, same units: sleep β +0.07 to +0.25, breakfast β +0.12 to +0.17, technology medians −0.005 to −0.035.

2

What it means

Small on average, real and concentrated in the children already struggling.

Not the same as harmless. And it is every screen for every teenager: girls on heavy social media is a bigger number. Sleep and breakfast still sit above it in all three datasets.

3

To be fair

The phone in the bedroom is the right villain for the night.

The bedroom rule comes first. Among 6,616 London pupils aged 11 and 12, a phone in the hour before sleep meant 1.82 times the odds of too little sleep on a school night, 2.13 in the dark. The television at 7.30 is the wrong villain for the morning.

Self reported and cross sectional. The pattern is enough to move the charger.

Not a survey, an experiment

32 children, one hour less
for four nights.
50 teenagers, 6.5 hours
in bed against 10.

Randomised crossover designs: bedtime moved an hour each way, or 6.5 hours against 10. Mood and control over feelings moved within days. Small samples, and the big numbers are teenagers; the primary age evidence is 10,230 British children in the Millennium Cohort.

The 3.30 collapse

The meltdown is real. The tank is not.

The internet calls it after school restraint collapse, and the idea under the name is a tank that drains. 23 labs got 0.04 in 2016 against a claimed 0.62, and only small effects since. A school day does tire a child. Keep the pattern, drop the tank. ADHD, autism or a school worry have their own cause and their own professional.
