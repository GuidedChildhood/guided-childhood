The morning is decided the night before

10,230 British children.

In the Millennium Cohort, an irregular bedtime at three, five and seven predicted behaviour at seven, and improved when the bedtime did. Send 32 children aged 8 to 12 to bed an hour later for four nights and their mood moves with it. That one was a randomised experiment.

The phone is part of the night

1.82×

The odds of too little sleep on a school night for 6,616 London pupils aged 11 and 12 with a phone in the hour before sleep. So the bedroom rule comes first, and it is a night rule, not a morning one.

The hour the screen actually fills

It is not the morning.

Ofcom's meter, 701 children aged 8 to 14, online time by time of day. In the one diary study of how screen time ends, 380 endings in 28 families, the endings that went well were the routine ones and the ones the programme itself ended. Warned children were more upset, not less.

4

To be fair

The case against the morning screen is one study.

A French case control, 276 children aged three to six, about language delay, screen time from parents' memory. Even in the datasets built to study screens, 355,358 adolescents, Orben and Przybylski at Oxford found sleep and breakfast carried larger associations with wellbeing than technology use in all three.

Same surveys, same units. Take it as proportion, not proof. With three children and a job, the screen in our house is staying; this is the routine around it.

The 3.30 collapse

Keep the pattern. Drop the tank.

A school day tires a child, and hunger looks like defiance. The name the internet gives it rests on an idea 23 labs could not find in 2016, and only small effects since. For a child with ADHD, autism or a school worry, the 3.30 has its own cause and its own professional.

7

So, tonight

Three things, and they cost nothing.

A regular bedtime, and the phone charging in the kitchen. Something to eat before any screen, at 7.30 and at the gate. The end agreed before the screen goes on, not announced when it is over.
