## 2. The single strongest LinkedIn post angle

- Lead finding 1, with 9 as the setup concession, 2 as the pivot's companion, 6 as the recognition line. One idea: the two flashpoints of the school day are paid for by sleep and food, not the screen.
- Hook flavour: contrarian precise number, under 12 words, a number first. The hero number is the 355,358 adolescents with sleep and breakfast carrying associations several times the size of technology use (figures in the editor's note above). Fallback hero is Baum 2014's 6.5 against 10 hours in bed, with n 50 stated.
- The honest pivot line: "None of this makes the screen innocent. The screen is usually what moves the bedtime." Then, where finding 2 is used: "And the 3.30 collapse is real. The label is a 2016 blog post."
- The one concrete contrast: two children, same twenty minutes of television before school. One in bed by eight with breakfast inside them. One up until ten with nothing to eat (Baum 2014; Selby Year 6, 8 percent nothing before school).
- Format: native PDF carousel, eight slides (cover with the hook number, finding, meaning, honest pivot, credibility, the contrast, the point, the close), with text plus a single research card as the speed fallback. Research card, not photo.
- Meaning: the response should match the evidence, so the move is a regular bedtime and breakfast, with the screen's stop agreed in advance, not a rule about the set. A brick, not the 1 in 10. The 8 percent line gestures at poverty and stops there.
- Nameable reader: the parent whose child falls apart in the car at 3.30, or who has the television on at 7.30 and feels bad about it.
- Close: one open question only a parent can answer (what time does your child actually fall asleep on a school night), the sourced breakdown in the first comment, principled repost ask. The prepared first comment pre empts "so screens do not matter" with the pivot and names n 32 and n 50.
