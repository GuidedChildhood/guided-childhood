What it means

The 27th consecutive year the promise has been broken.

The money was pledged to health, and especially to children’s health. It went almost everywhere else.

3

It gets strange

$36 billion of it was sold to Wall Street.

States turned future payments into upfront bonds to plug budget holes. Washington took $450 million upfront. Its tobacco money went on to pay investors $848 million.

4

It gets stranger

The money grew tobacco.

North Carolina spent settlement money on tobacco curing barns and a processing plant. Health money, farming the harm.

5

It gets strangest

37 attorneys general filed in court FOR Philip Morris.

The payments scale with cigarette sales, and state budgets had come to depend on them. So in 2003 the states defended the defendant.

The count is the New England Journal of Medicine’s.

6

To be fair

The promise can be written into the contract.

The opioid settlements proved it: a binding 85 percent earmark for treatment, written into the terms. Tobacco’s failure was a drafting choice, not a law of nature.
