# Correction to post 5 and the newsletter, 13 November 2026

FOR JUSTIN TO APPLY. Nothing has been edited on LinkedIn or Substack.

## What was wrong

Post 5 ("You cannot build a report button for a child who will not tell you")
opened: "Fewer than one in five told a trusted adult", credited to Internet
Matters. The post is live on LinkedIn (seen 13 November 2026, about two months
old, 9 reactions, 1 comment, 1 repost).

The Internet Matters press release of 27 May 2025 was read at source on 13
November 2026. It says:

- 7 in 10 of 1,000 UK children aged 9 to 17 reported experiencing online harm, from a list that includes contact from strangers, hate speech and misinformation.
- 36 percent of those reported it. The summary says reported it to the platform.
- The 18 percent is the share who reported content promoting dangerous stunts or challenges.

It says nothing about how many told a parent or a trusted adult. Ofcom's 2025
research found 92 percent of 8 to 17s say they would tell someone, most often a
parent, and 56 percent say they would always tell.

The post's argument survives. A report button that two thirds of harmed
children do not press is still the point. Only the number and what it measures
change.

## 1. Edit the LinkedIn post (three dots, Edit post)

Replace the first two paragraphs with:

> Seven in ten children say they have run into some kind of harm online. Only about a third of them reported it to the platform.
>
> That is Internet Matters, a survey of a thousand UK children aged 9 to 17. The list of harms is a broad one, from contact from strangers to misinformation. Sit with the second number. The harm is happening, and the report button is mostly not being pressed.

In the third paragraph change "found the same thing" to "heard something that
sits beside it".

In the fourth paragraph change "And the children have already decided not to
use it." to "And most children are not using it."
