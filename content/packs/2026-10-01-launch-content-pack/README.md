### Monday 5 October, research: bans work at school, home is the gap

Image: the research card. Eyebrow THE CLIFF EDGE, KEY INSIGHT 01. Hero line:

```
Phone bans work. In school.

A Norwegian study of middle schools found that banning phones improved
girls' grades and cut bullying by something like four in ten.

An English trial across schools with strict phone policies found the
opposite of what everyone expected: no measurable gain in wellbeing or
attainment from the policy on its own.

Those two findings are not a contradiction.

A ban changes what happens between nine and three. It changes nothing

That is the bit nobody is building for.

Nothing, nothing, nothing, then everything at sixteen. A ban is a delay.
The cliff edge is what comes after it, and the cliff edge is where the
risk actually lives.

I am not against the delay. I am for the six years in between.

Ten minutes a day, parent and child, so that the phone arrives to a child
```

### Monday 12 October, research: the feed, not the search (the playlist post)

Image: the research card. Eyebrow THE WRONG VILLAIN, KEY INSIGHT 04. Hero
stat "35 percent" over "1 percent". Two bars: In the feed, 35 percent. They
searched for it, 1 percent.

```
Ofcom asked eleven to seventeen year olds where the harmful content came
from.

Thirty five percent met it in a feed.

One percent had searched for it.

That is the whole story of the last five years in two numbers. The risk is
not what a child goes looking for. It is what arrives next.

Ofcom wrote to TikTok and YouTube in March asking how they would make
their feeds safer for children. Neither committed to a significant change.

That number is not zero on the search side, and a feed is not the only
place harm lives. A child with an adult nearby and a plan is better off on
any platform than a child with neither.

But if the feed is the problem, then the practical question for a parent
is simple. Who chooses the next video?

For the last year I have been building a second thing alongside Guided
Childhood. The Social Billboard checks YouTube channels the same way every
time, puts a date on the check, and builds a playlist around the choices a
parent makes: age, tone, length, time of day. A session with a beginning
and an end, instead of a feed.

The first playlist is free. The link is in the comments, with the Ofcom
source.

When your child finishes a video, what usually chooses the next one?
```

### Video 1: How we check a YouTube channel before your child watches it

Title options: "How we check a YouTube channel before your child watches
it" / "What 'checked' actually means" / "The method behind a safe playlist".

```
HOOK (0 to 15s): Every "safe for kids" list has the same problem. Nobody
tells you how they decided. I am Justin, a dad of three, and this is the
method we use, the same way every time, with the date on it.

PART 1: The three hard gates. Child safety. The broadcaster test. Where the
content leads. A channel that fails any gate is out before we look at
anything else.

PART 2: After the gates: age fit, teaching quality, tone and pacing,
purpose. Screen recording of a real check.

ASK (by 90s): Your first playlist is free, link below.

PART 3: The honest limits. A single mild word in one video. Open comments
on YouTube. What we re check and how often.

END: If you want to see what happens after the video ends on YouTube Kids
versus one of these playlists, that is the next video. End screen.
```

### Video 2: What happens after the video ends

The comparison. Side by side screen recordings: YouTube Kids autoplay
after one video, then a checked playlist after one video. No child on
screen. The point lands in the first fifteen seconds: the difference is not
the video, it is the next one.

### Video 3: Twenty minutes of YouTube without the feed

The session. How to set the age, tone, length and time of day, press play,
and what the child sees at the end. Screen recording with Justin's voice.

### Video 4: What Ofcom found about feeds, and what a parent can do tonight

The evidence. The 35 percent and 1 percent finding, the honest pivot (a
