# READ ME, Louder Than The Evidence, the new series

A fair, sourced series that takes Jonathan Haidt's work on children and screens seriously, credits
what he got right, then holds the volume of the claim against the weight of the evidence, and
lands on preparation. Built from the briefing at briefings/2026-08-13-reading-haidt-honestly.md.
Justin's voice. No dashes anywhere. Held to the Odgers test.

## The posture, do not skip this

Never make it about the man. Our own philosophy warns against single villain framing, so turning
Haidt into the villain would make us the thing we criticise. The target is the proportion, the gap
between how loud the claim is and how strong the evidence is. Credit him fully and first, every
time, then turn. That honest pivot is the move that makes it land instead of bounce.

## How to run it

- Post in order, it is an argument that builds. One a day, or two a week over two and a half weeks.
- Only post 5 carries the /starter-pack link, in the body, because it is the series close. Every
  other link goes in a first comment, never the body (LinkedIn suppresses body links).
- Reply fast to early comments in Justin's voice. The replies are half the reach. Use the
  linkedin-comment-replies skill for sceptics, always conceding Haidt's good faith first.
- Publish the newsletter at the end of the week, after post 5, as the deep version for the people
  who followed the whole thread.

## Evidence discipline (the hostile expert is watching)

- Lean on Odgers, Orben and Przybylski, the 2025 Fassi registered reports, and NASEM. These carry
  the weight.
- Do not lean on Ferguson (contested, Haidt has a published re analysis). Mention once at most.
- Do not claim the Vuorre global studies measured teen social media, they measured internet and
  technology adoption. Say it precisely or not at all.
- Never say social media is harmless. The line is, the average is small, the harm is real and
  concentrated in already vulnerable children and in developmental windows, and the real drivers
  are upstream.
- The Italian study is good, which is exactly why we read it properly rather than wave it about.

## The hidden thread

The thesis (poverty, adverse childhood experiences and parental mental health outrank platform
time) lands plainly once, in post 3 and again softly in the newsletter close. The other posts each
carry one brick. Readers should arrive at the proportion themselves rather than be told it.
