# The philosophy: Louder Than The Evidence, and why education is the answer

The durable spine under Justin's evidence posts and every reply. The companion to 00-plan.md (which
holds the five post arc and the evidence discipline) and to research/01 voice and the
linkedin-comment-replies skill. Read THE-STORY.md first, everything here must relate to what Guided
Childhood actually does. No dashes anywhere. Held to the Odgers test: every line defensible to a
hostile expert. Built from the live debate on the 0.4 percent post (Alberto, Carrie, Donald, Dr Donna
Davies), where this spine was tested in public and held.

## The one line

The panic about phones is louder than the evidence, and being loud about the wrong thing hides the
real drivers of a child's mental health and lets the actual villain, the deliberately addictive
design, walk. Bans remove the app and change nothing underneath. Education is the answer, because it
is the only thing that builds judgement in the child and reaches the drivers a ban never touches. That
is Guided Childhood, the education the ban leaves behind.

## The argument, in movements (each post carries one brick, the reader arrives at the whole)

1. **The evidence.** The average effect of digital technology on teen wellbeing is small, Oxford's 0.4
   percent. The headline is the size of an epidemic. The gap between the two is the series.
2. **Small average is not no harm.** The harm is real and concentrated, in already vulnerable children
   and in developmental windows, not spread evenly across every teenager. Wrong villain was never no
   villain.
3. **The design is deliberate, and that is the real villain.** Pointing at screen time is what lets
   the platforms off, it makes it the parent's job to use less while the design that engineered the
   pull walks free. Pointing at the algorithm puts the blame where it belongs. We are harder on the
   platforms than the panic is, not softer.
4. **The panic hides the real drivers.** Poverty, family adversity, parental mental illness, trauma
   and adverse childhood experiences, the loss of a free range childhood, neurodivergence, the 11 to
   14 window, and a mental health service with an 18 month queue. Social media sits on top of these
   and, for a vulnerable child, makes a heavy load heavier. It is rarely the root. Candice Odgers,
   reviewing the science in Nature, warned the fear of phones risks distracting us from the real
   causes.
5. **The tell (the sharpest hook).** Point at the phone and you get a moral panic, a headline, a ban.
   Point at poverty or an underfunded CAMHS and the room goes quiet. Ask why. Because the phone is a
   cheap villain, you can ban it in an afternoon and feel you have acted, without spending a pound on
   services or looking at anything uncomfortable. The real drivers ask more of us, so we look away.
6. **So a ban is the false comfort.** It removes the app, leaves the design and every real driver
   exactly where they were, and hands parents a false sense of safety while the struggling child goes
   unseen.
7. **Education is the resolution.** Not prohibition, which builds nothing, and not denial, which
   abandons the vulnerable. Teach the child to understand the machine and build judgement, support the
   specific child early, and see what they are actually carrying. That is what a ban can never do, and
   it is exactly what Guided Childhood is built for.

## Why this is in line with Guided Childhood

The product is the philosophy made real. Guided Childhood is neither pro ban nor anti ban, it is for
understanding the child. It gives a parent early insight into what their particular child is actually
carrying, rather than a false sense of safety from a ban. It teaches the machine first, how the
algorithm finds and holds attention, so a child arrives with judgement rather than rules. It aims at
the vulnerable child, where the harm concentrates. It is the education and the attention the ban was
never going to provide. Every post should be able to answer, in one line, how it points back to that.

## The receipts (keep these to hand, represent each one whole)

- **Oxford, the 0.4 percent.** Orben and Przybylski, Nature Human Behaviour, about 355,000 adolescents,
  specification curve method built to stop cherry picking. Name Oxford inline every time the number
  appears, so the source answers the "your evidence is weak" hit before it is typed. The data is
  Oxford's, the reading is Justin's, own the reading.
- **Odgers, Nature 2024.** Reviewing Haidt, the rewiring claim is not supported, and the panic risks
  distracting from the real causes. She is the anchor for "the panic hides the drivers," not for
  "social media is harmless." She cites the ABCD study, the largest look at this generation, finding
  no drastic changes tied to digital use.
- **The over time test.** Vuorre, Orben and Przybylski, 2021, tested whether the association has grown
  as the tech got more addictive. No broad increase. Represent it as mixed, mostly flat with one link
  up slightly, never as "no change at all."
- **The 2025 differential use study.** Nature Human Behaviour, UK, about 3,340 teenagers assessed by
  clinicians, found those with a mental health condition use social media differently, more time, more
  comparison, more affected by feedback. Current data, same shape, the effect is uneven and tracks
  what a child carries. It is correlational and shows differential use, not cause, say so.
- **The Norway school ban.** Abrahamsson. Banning phones cut bullying for both sexes, and cut
  psychological visits and lifted grades mainly for girls, most of all girls from low income homes,
  with the author attributing part of the heterogeneity to higher usage among girls. Represent it
  whole, both the broad bullying effect and the concentrated mental health effect, or a critic lands
  the "you are cherry picking" hit. It is a classroom environment study, not a "screens rewire brains"
  study. The school ban evidence overall is mixed, England's SMART Schools trial found no benefit.

## The reply playbook (from the live debate)

- **Concede facts, never the thesis.** If an opponent surfaces a true fact you left out, grant it in a
  clause and stay on the front foot, because granting a verified fact is what keeps you the accurate
  one in the room. It is not the same as conceding the argument. Nothing in the debate defeated the
  thesis, there were only facts to fold in.
- **Represent studies whole the first time.** The selective harvest is what earns the "you are
  misrepresenting" charge. Give the full read, including the parts that cut against you, and no one can
  land it.
- **Do not die on a side quest.** School phone bans are defensible and you can support them, that is a
  different question from whether the device is rewiring every child at home. Keep the fight on your
  ground, the classroom versus the brain.
- **Never soft on the platforms.** When challenged on whether a small effect absolves the platforms,
  the answer is the opposite, and the judo is that pointing at screen time is the real absolution.
- **The register.** Sam Harris discipline, Stephen Fry warmth, Douglas Murray composure. Concede what
  is fair, hold the narrow line, never match heat, be the obvious adult in the room. The audience is
  the silent onlooker, not the opponent.
- **Drop "a vulnerable few."** After the dose response findings, the affected group is sizable, heavy
  users and vulnerable kids both. Say "uneven, concentrated in high exposure and vulnerable children,"
  which is true and still not "everyone uniformly."

## Standing rules for every future post

1. Anchor to the science, Odgers, Orben and Przybylski, and name Oxford inline for the number.
2. Never post the number naked, always pair it with the distribution, small on average and serious for
   a concentrated group.
3. Never say social media is harmless. The line is small average, real and concentrated harm,
   deliberate design, real drivers upstream.
4. Always land the "so what", which is education and the specific child, never a ban.
5. Carry one brick per post, let the reader arrive at the proportion rather than be told it.
6. End on a real question that earns a reply.
7. Pre load the top objections as ready replies before posting, so the golden window is answered fast.
