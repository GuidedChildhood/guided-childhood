## THREAD 1 (Believe)

**Board:** Parenting / Being a parent

**Title:** I stopped counting screen time hours and it has been oddly freeing

I have spent about two years in the usual low level dread about screens, counting
hours, feeling guilty when the number was high, all of it. This week I actually
sat and read some of the research instead of the headlines and I have quietly
changed how I do it.

The thing that stuck with me. A big study followed a few thousand kids for four
years and the total number of hours barely predicted anything. What did predict
trouble was the pattern, whether it was tipping into a compulsive, cannot put it
down, miserable without it sort of use. Same hours, very different children,
depending on the shape of it.

So I have stopped counting minutes and started watching the mood. Is she calm
when she comes off it or agitated. Is she making things and messaging friends, or
scrolling in silence. Is it filling a gap where something else used to be. Honest
to god it has been far more useful than any time limit I ever set, and a lot less
of a battle.

I am not saying screens are fine. For a child already struggling I think they can
clearly make it worse. Just that watching the shape of it rather than the clock
has done more in our house than any amount of counting.

Has anyone else made this switch, or am I talking myself into something.

## THREAD 3 (Solve)

**Board:** Parenting / Teenagers

**Title:** Started teaching mine the good reasons to use their phone, not just the bad

Bit of a change of tack in our house that I wanted to share.

I realised I only ever talked to my two about the dangers. Put it down, be
careful, do not talk to strangers, all the nos. And it slowly dawned on me that
the first time the phone was genuinely good for them, and it was going to be at
some point, they would just tune all of that out, because I had only ever given
them the scary version.

So I started naming the good things too, and being honest that each one has a
catch. It keeps you connected to friends, and it can make you feel you can never
switch off. It helps you find people like you, which really matters for my eldest
who felt a bit on the outside, and the same group can turn on someone. You can
make things you are proud of, and there is a pressure to chase likes. I try to
say both halves every time, never just the warning.

The other thing I have leaned into is the offline stuff. Not as a punishment or
to replace the phone, just filling their actual lives up a bit more, more time
out, more unstructured mucking about. It seems to keep the phone as one thing
they do rather than the only thing.

It has landed much better than the lectures ever did. They actually talk to me
about it now. Anyone else tried teaching the good and the bad together rather
than just the bad, and did it work.
