# Devices, and the balance. Facebook posts.

Warm, short, one idea each, local parent group register, ends on a question. No
hard sell, no dashes. One post per thread, in rotation.

---

## THREAD 1 (Believe) — Stop counting the hours

**Post text:**

I have stopped counting screen time hours in our house, and it has been the best
change I have made.

I read the research properly this year and the thing that surprised me is that
the number of hours barely predicts anything. What matters is the shape of it. Is
she calm when she comes off it, or wound up. Is it filling a gap where something
else used to be. Is she making things and talking to friends, or scrolling in
silence.

Two kids can do the exact same two hours and be in completely different places.
The clock cannot tell them apart. Only you can.

So I watch how she seems when she puts it down, not the minutes. It has told me
far more than any timer ever did.

Anyone else stopped counting minutes and started watching the mood instead?

## THREAD 2 (Build) — The one date most Year 6 parents miss

**Post text:**

Quick one for anyone with a child in Year 6.

The secondary school application deadline is 31 October. It is the one date in
primary school that genuinely does not move, and it is the one I see parents find
out about a week too late every single year.

I am building a little thing that just watches the school calendar for you and
gives you a nudge a few weeks before the moments that matter. Not a screen time
counter. A heads up. Because the tables check, the SATs, the first phone at
secondary, they all come at a time you could plan for, if someone told you they
were coming.

The first phone one is the big one. Rules you agree a fortnight before the phone
arrives are just how it works. The same rules added after feel like a punishment.
Timing is everything.

What is the parenting thing you wish someone had warned you about a month early?

## THREAD 3 (Solve) — The good reasons to reach for a screen

**Post text:**

We talk so much about why kids should put the phone down that we forget to teach
them why it is sometimes good to pick it up. And honestly, that is the bit that
keeps them safer.

A child who only ever hears no will ignore you the first time a screen is
genuinely good to them, and one day it will be. A friendship kept alive. A group
where they finally feel they belong. Something they made and are proud of. Those
are real.

So we teach the good and the catch together. Yes it keeps you connected, and yes
being always reachable can start to feel like you can never switch off. Both are
true. That is the whole skill, holding both at once.

And the offline stuff is not the enemy of the phone. It is the ballast. A kid
with a full life outside the screen reaches for it for the good reasons and puts
it down when the good runs out.

What is a genuinely good reason your child reaches for a screen, that you would
never want to take away?
