Last week I asked Mumsnet to try what I've been building. They didn't hold back.

"It's always dads with brilliant apps." "This reads like ChatGPT."

About forty parents clicked through and ten got to the end. A few of them found real problems. My bedtime line could be beaten in seconds, because any child would just say "fine, I'll put it on do not disturb". The result page left people on a screen of advice with no idea what happened next.

So this week the bedtime script got answers for the comebacks kids actually use. The result page now tells you what happens next. And the homepage leads with what children learn at each age, because the most useful reply on the whole thread came from a parent who'd raised teenagers with additional needs. She said she'd have loved to know what school would teach them, so she could get there first.

The ChatGPT comment was fair too. Some of it did read that way, so I went through the whole site and the app and took out the phrases that give it away.

I do use AI, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. I'm not a developer. I came up through accounting and small businesses and I've always learned by building things. For years turning an idea into software meant finding developers and hoping they understood what was in your head. AI changed that. A dad supporting a family can now build the thing he wished he'd had, without a team or a big pot of money.

It doesn't make the calls though. What goes in front of a parent is my decision, and every claim has to point back to a source. When someone spots a mistake, I fix it, usually the same day.

There's another reason I'm open about it. Our kids are growing up with these tools. One of the lessons for older teenagers ends with "I can use an AI tool and defend where I checked its work." I can't teach that and hide that I use it myself.

So thank you, Mumsnet. The kind comments on here are lovely, but you're the ones who made it better.
