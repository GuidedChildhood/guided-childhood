# LinkedIn, one post (children and AI lane, question led)

Hook 96 characters. Body inside 1,300 to 2,500. One closing question, nothing after it. No link in the body. No founder scene: it is an evidence post.

I do not know what a thirteen year old says to a chatbot at eleven at night.

I do not think anyone does. Including the chatbot's parent controls.

That is not a guess. A testing group spent the summer checking.

Common Sense Media's Youth AI Safety Institute ran more than 4,000 prompts through ChatGPT for Teens, on accounts registered to 13 to 17 year olds, before and after OpenAI added its teen protections.

On about 1,000 prompts from adult accounts, over seven days, the testers said they were 13. The teen experience never switched on.

On the crisis prompts its advisors judged to need a resource, the share of replies naming a hotline fell from 33 to 23 percent after the launch.

First, the part that cuts against me. This is a product audit, not a peer reviewed study. It was US only. The two testing windows ran one after the other, so the model changing and the teen settings changing are tangled together. And the parent alert test comes with a fair objection from OpenAI: linking takes about three hours before alerts fire, and some of the test accounts had been linked for less.

So I would not say the chatbot is unsafe. I would say something smaller and harder to wave away.

A parent cannot assume the tool will notice, or tell them.

That is not a reason to ban anything. It is a reason the conversation at home cannot be handed to software. Ours included. In our app the child never chats with a model, and a check in our build fails if that changes.

What would you need to see before you trusted a chatbot to tell you your child was struggling?

## First comment

Source: Common Sense Media, Youth AI Safety Institute, ChatGPT for Teens Risk Assessment, page dated 7 October 2026 (institute.commonsensemedia.org/risk-assessments/chatgpt-teens). More than 4,000 prompts across both windows; pre launch 13 July to 17 August, post launch 25 August to 28 September 2026. The 33 to 23 percent figure is hotline mentions on 201 prompts judged to warrant a crisis response, on linked age 13 accounts; the page does not state how many replies sit behind each percentage. The age detection test was about 1,000 prompts over seven days, more than 160 chats, from age 19 accounts. Limits the report states: sequential windows, no inter rater agreement. The testing was US based, and I have not checked what UK teens are offered. OpenAI told the Institute about the three hour linking time, and the Institute says it stands by its interpretation. It is a commercial safety group testing a commercial product, so read it as an audit rather than as settled science.
