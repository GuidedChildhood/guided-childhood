Fair point first: knowing how a trick works is not the same as never falling for it. And the study below was run with adults, not children. Both of those matter.

Here is what it did. Researchers made five short videos. Each one explained a single trick used to mislead people online, before anyone met it for real: emotional words built to wind you up, arguments that contradict themselves, being offered only two choices when there are more, pinning the blame on one person or group, and attacking the person and not the point.

Across six controlled studies with 6,464 adults in the United States, those who watched one were better at recognising the trick straight afterwards than those who watched an unrelated video.

The honest reading: it measured spotting the trick, in adults, straight after watching. It did not follow anyone for months and it did not test children. So we would call it promising, not proven.
