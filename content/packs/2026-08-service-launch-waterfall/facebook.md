## TUESDAY — It was never the hours

**Post text:**

A big study followed four thousand children for four years, and the number of hours on a screen barely predicted anything.

What mattered was the shape of it. The compulsive, cannot put it down, miserable without it kind of use. Same hours, very different child, depending on the pattern underneath.

So I stopped counting minutes in our house and started watching the pattern. Is she calm when she comes off it, or wound up. Is she making and chatting, or scrolling in silence. It has told me far more than any timer.

Two kids, the same two hours, can be in completely different places. The clock cannot tell them apart. Only we can.

Have you stopped counting the minutes and started watching the mood?

## WEDNESDAY — The thing no parent has a plan for

**Post text:**

Nearly three quarters of teenagers have now used an AI companion, a chatbot they talk to like a friend. Most parents have no idea.

And the first proper studies are landing. For lonely kids especially, leaning on an AI companion seems to make the loneliness worse over time, not better, and pulls them away from real people.

The honest problem is that a ban will not hold here, because a curious teenager walks straight round it. What holds is understanding. Knowing what the thing is, why it is built to feel like a friend, and when a bot is not a person.

That is the next thing we are building, a simple way to teach a child exactly that, and to give you the words for the my chatbot said conversation.

Has your child mentioned an AI they chat to, and did you know what to say?

## THURSDAY — Australia banned it, and most kids stayed online

**Post text:**

Australia did the thing a lot of us have wished for. It banned social media for under sixteens. Eight in ten of those kids are still online.

They moved to VPNs, borrowed logins and fake ages within weeks. And a separate audit this year found only four in ten of the safety features platforms tell us to trust actually work.

It is not that bans are pointless. It is that a ban is a start line, not a finish line. The kids are still going to be online, so the real job is to guide them, not just gate them.

That is why what we build is a pathway, not a wall. Growing the judgement, step by step, so that when the app arrives they are ready and not just blocked.

If a ban bought you two years, what would you want your child to have learned by the time it lifts?
