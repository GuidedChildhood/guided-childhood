## The story in one line

Parents ask whether to ban Roblox. The better question has three parts: who
can message my child, where does that conversation go next, and will they tell
me.

## 1. LinkedIn post

55 percent of children who game online play with people they have never met.

Only 43 percent of parents have a rule about who their child plays with.

Both numbers are from Ofcom, in October. I keep coming back to the gap between them.

The question parents are asking is a different one. Is Roblox full of predators, and should I ban it?

So I went looking for the rate. How many children are approached, out of how many who play. I could not find one. The police have not published it. The charities have not. Roblox has not. Every number you have seen is a count with nothing underneath it.

That does not make it safe. Children in the UK have been harmed by people they met there. In June a teenager was jailed at Sheffield Crown Court. First contact was on Roblox. Then the conversation moved to Discord.

That move is the pattern. CEOP, the police's own child protection team, tells parents that once chat leaves a monitored platform, no one is moderating it. Roblox's own lawyer told a US senator this year that a suggestion to move off platform would likely not be something they could report.

The game is where it starts. The private app is where it happens.

Roblox did change this year. Chat now needs an age check, and chat is off by default on the youngest accounts. That is real. In Ofcom's own research a nine year old was sitting in the 16 to 17 band, because someone else did the face scan for him.

A ban does lower the exposure. I will not pretend otherwise. But teenagers told the Children's Commissioner why they stay quiet when something goes wrong. They are afraid the phone gets taken away, and that stops them asking for help.

Family IT Guy tells parents to skip Roblox. He compares it to putting your child in the ring with Mike Tyson.

He is right to take the risk seriously, and he has done a lot to make parents look. I do not think the comparison holds. Nobody wants that fight, and in a ring you can at least see who you are facing. A child in a game cannot.

What plenty of children do want is to learn to box. With gloves, a coach and rules, a bit at a time. That is a long way from being thrown in.

And if we close this ring, another one opens. One we have not heard of, with no famous name on the door.

So I would rather prepare them than scare them. Teach them to spot the moment, and make it easy to tell me when it comes.

Here is where I would start.

Who you play with.

Which room you play in.

And a promise, made before anything happens. If anyone asks you to move to another app, come and tell me. You will not lose the game for telling me.

The sources are in the comments.

What is the rule in your house about who they play with? And did your child help write it?

## 5. Parent checklist

NOT READY TO SHIP. Roblox's help pages refused us, so every menu path below
must be confirmed by a person in the app first. Roblox also says settings vary
by region and does not name the UK.

1. Link your account to your child's. In Roblox this starts from the child's
   settings under Parental Controls.
2. Check which account type your child has. Roblox now runs separate accounts
   for ages 5 to 8 and ages 9 to 15. If the age is wrong, a linked parent can
   correct it once.
3. Look at who they can chat with. On the youngest accounts chat is off unless
   you switch it on. Decide, do not assume.
4. Open Trusted Friends and read the list with your child. For ages 13 to 15
   this is where chat is least filtered.
5. Agree who they play with. Write it down together.
6. Keep the game in a room you are in while they are primary age.
7. Make the telling promise before anything happens: "If anyone asks you to
   move to another app, or asks for a photo, or gives you something for free,
   come and tell me. You will not lose the game for telling me."
8. Know where to report. If you think a child is being groomed, report to
   CEOP. If a child is in immediate danger, call 999.

What this checklist does not claim: that any setting makes Roblox safe, or
that it will prevent harm. It gives you a clearer picture and an easier
conversation.
