# DiGi shorts: what is working in short form for parents and family tech, plus 15 hooks

Research date: 9 October 2026. British English, no dashes in anything I drafted. Quotes from other people are left as they wrote them.

## How this was gathered, and its limits

- **No search engine was used.** The session's shared web search allowance was already spent when this run started, so I did not run any searches. Everything below comes from pages I opened directly.
- **View counts are from TikTok's own pages, read on 9 October 2026.** I opened each account's profile in the browser pane and sorted by Popular. I then opened each video's own page and read the stats from its embedded data: plays, likes, comments, shares, saves, length, post date and sound.
- **Promoted posts are marked as promoted.** TikTok's page data has an `isAd` flag on each post. Where it is true, I treat the post as having been run as a paid promotion. The page does not show how much of the view count was paid for.
- **Only TikTok was checked.** I did not verify any Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts or LinkedIn video counts in this session. LinkedIn does not show view counts publicly.
- **The sample is limited to accounts I could name and check.** It is not a sweep of the whole platform. Bigger examples may exist, especially in the "I asked an AI" format, where I found no verified parenting example.
- **The ratios are approximate.** TikTok rounds its play counts (for example "6.5M").
- **Parent quotes are kept to a minimum.** I reopened every Mumsnet page cited below and confirmed the wording on it. Lines that were found by the Mumsnet research lens are paraphrased with their link, except for one direct quote.

## 1. Seven findings

1. **Organic expert posts get saved. Paid brand posts do not.** The best save rates in the sample are 2.6 to 2.8 percent of views. They belong to posts that give one specific usable thing (Family IT Guy's YouTube Kids setting) or that reframe a parent's own question (Dr Cath Knibbs on screen time being "the wrong question"). The promoted brand posts are near zero:
   - GoHenryUK, "Don't Blink": 5.4 million plays and 1,570 likes, which is 0.03 percent.
   - Greenlight: 3.1 million plays and 12,700 likes.

   A save means "I will use this tonight". That is our exact promise: the one thing to do and the words to say.
2. **The parent's own question is the strongest hook in this niche.** Knibbs opens with "should you take away your child's phone or games as punishment", spoken and captioned from frame one: 6.5 million plays, organic, 118,854 saves. Bark opens on a white question box from a parent of a 13 year old: 399,300 plays, promoted.
3. **A real screen recording plus one line of text works with no face and no voice.** Life360 shows its real map screen with one self aware text line: 7.2 million plays in 12 seconds, organic, 99,200 shares. This is our silent format, proven.
4. **A mascot can carry a story.** Duolingo's "RIP DUO" is 11.5 seconds long and has 67.9 million plays and 1.5 million shares, organic. DiGi as the 3D star can carry a scene, but only acting out what the product really does.
5. **The countdown is a retention device.** Dr Julie's "Top 5 things" uses a five arch rainbow stacker and takes one arch away per point, so viewers can see how much is left. 19 million plays and 279,643 saves. This is the shape for Justin's "top 5 moments".
6. **Outrage travels, and we do not use it.** Family IT Guy's Roblox post combines a news peg, a big number and a villain: 17.8 million plays and 671,700 shares (3.8 percent). It fails every one of our hidden thread tests. Our shareable alternative is the "send this to" instruction plus a moment every parent recognises.
7. **"Ready, not age" is already a mainstream UK conversation.** Netmums' Paris Fury clip, saying a child gets a phone when ready rather than at a set age, has 1.2 million plays, organic. It has only 2,067 saves (0.17 percent): people share an opinion but do not save it. We have to add the next step, which is the stage check.

**1. Knibbs, phone as punishment (87 seconds).**
- *First two seconds:* a name label at the top, and her spoken question burned in as a caption. By two seconds she says "imagine the scenario" while holding up a phone and a controller.
- *Why it works:* it is the exact question parents argue about, asked by a UK child therapist. The caption shows from frame one. The props make the question something you can see.
- *Take:* the parent's question as the first frame, with real objects in hand.
- *Leave:* the single expert authority stack. That is not our lane (`content/brand-story/reference-accounts.md`).

**2. Knibbs, dinner time and gaming (132 seconds).**
- *First two seconds:* she peeks round a bedroom door with a text box asking if your dinner time looks like this. By two seconds she is playing the parent telling the child to get off the game.
- *Why it works:* it is the problem point of view, played straight. The caption's first line asks children to send the video to their parent, so it gets shared through two audiences.
- *Take:* the role played moment, and the send instruction in the first line of the caption.
- *Leave:* any acted parent we could not stand behind. Ours uses hands and objects, Justin, or DiGi.

**3. Family IT Guy, YouTube Kids setting (3 minutes 44 seconds).**
- *First two seconds:* a talking head with a title card promising the one setting every parent should know about.
- *Why it works:* it has the highest save rate of the ten despite its length. The promise is specific and can be used straight away.
- *Take:* promise one named, usable thing in the first frame.
- *Leave:* the fear B roll. A frightening game thumbnail appears at about nine seconds.

**4. Family IT Guy, Roblox (184 seconds).**
- *First two seconds:* a talking head and a word caption. The stat carries the post caption, comparing Roblox's 40 million daily child users with the child population of California.
- *Why it works:* a news peg, a big number made easy to feel, and a villain.
- *Leave:* all of the engine. It fails the Haidt test on frightening before informing, on the platform as villain, and on less contact as the answer.

**5. Bark, Snapchat (167 seconds, posted three weeks ago).**
- *First two seconds:* a white question box from a parent of a 13 year old whose friends all have Snapchat, asking whether to say yes. Yellow word by word captions.
- *Why it matters:* this is the closest competitor content to our third problem. The format is strong. The answer is a flat no plus a list of harms. It is promoted, and gets 0.75 percent likes on its views.
- *Our counter:* DiGi never says yes or no. It shows the stage.

**6. Netmums, Paris Fury (91 seconds).**
- *First two seconds:* an interview clip with a burned in caption. She says her 14 year old does not have a phone yet.
- *Why it works:* a surprising personal fact from a known UK parent.
- *Lesson:* it got views but almost no saves. Opinion spreads, but people only save something they can use.

**7. Life360, street view (12 seconds).**
- *First two seconds:* a brand colour card with one self aware text line, then a cut to the real app map with a family member moving.
- *Why it works:* the real product, the audience's own joke, no voice and no face.
- *Take:* the format.
- *Leave:* the "stalking" humour, which would be a gift to critics of a child safety brand.
- *Warning:* the same account's [6 second "zoomies alert" post](https://www.tiktok.com/@life360/video/7680592415236869406) had 2.8 million plays. I could not verify that the feature exists. We never show a screen the product does not have.

**8. Finch, "not expecting it to work" (19 seconds).**
- *First two seconds:* a glum face with text about downloading a self care app expecting nothing. At two and a half seconds it cuts to the App Store page, and at eleven seconds to a hand holding the phone with a ticked task list.
- *Why it works:* the silent before and after, with the reward loop shown (Finch is one of our named reference apps).
- *Take:* the structure with hands and screen.
- *Leave:* the face. We cannot verify whether it is a customer or a creator, and we never generate one.

**9. Duolingo, "RIP DUO" (11.5 seconds).**
- *First two seconds:* two costumed characters carrying a coffin. The text is written as if by a fan.
- *Why it works:* the mascot is a character with a story. This is the case for DiGi as the 3D clay star acting with its eyes.
- *Leave:* stunts. DiGi acts only what the product really does.

**10. Dr Julie, Top 5 (44 seconds).**
- *First two seconds:* a title card naming the top 5 and a five arch stacker on the desk. Each point takes one arch away. The caption promises that the last one matters most.
- *Why it works:* the countdown keeps people watching and the list gets saved.
- *Take:* the exact shape for the top 5 moments short.

## 3. Hooks, retention, captions and sound: the rules, with sources

- **Hook timing.** TikTok says to put the proposition in the first 3 seconds and the hook in the first 6, to film vertical at 9 by 16, and to show 5 to 10 words per second when using text ([TikTok Business Help Center, "Creative best practices for performance ads", updated June 2025](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)). At that pace a hook of under ten words reads in one to two seconds, so the hook text has to be on screen from frame one.
- **Retention.** TikTok gives more weight to whether a user finishes a longer video from beginning to end ([TikTok Newsroom, "How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou", 18 June 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)). Meta reports that 65 percent of people who watch the first three seconds watch at least ten, and 45 percent watch thirty ([Meta for Business, "Updated features for video ads"; no date is shown on the page](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/updated-features-for-video-ads)).
- **Two lanes, from the sample:**
  - Reach: silent posts of 6 to 19 seconds (Life360, Finch, Duolingo).
  - Saves: 80 to 230 second explainers from a real expert (Knibbs, Family IT Guy, Dr Becky). For us that means Justin.
- **Captions.** Meta's internal tests found captioned video ads increased view time by an average of 12 percent (same Meta page, undated). Every one of the ten except Dr Becky's talk clip has text or captions in the first frame. TikTok's page data shows automatic subtitles on most talking heads.
- **Sound.** TikTok calls sound or music one of "the basics" (same TikTok page). Finch's top post runs on a well known chart song. I have not verified this session which music a business account is licensed to use, so check before relying on a trending track.

## 4. Platform notes

- **TikTok:** finishing a longer video is a strong signal (TikTok 2020, above). Make the silent cut loop cleanly.
- **Reels:** the most important predictions are how likely you are to reshare, to watch all the way through, to like, and to go to the audio page ([Instagram, "Instagram Ranking Explained", Adam Mosseri, 31 May 2023](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained/)). Put a send instruction in the first line of the caption. Knibbs and Dr Becky both do this: Dr Becky's grandparent post, posted this week, already has [32.5K plays](https://www.tiktok.com/@drbeckyatgoodinside).
- **LinkedIn:** ranking measures dwell time and pushes down updates people are predicted to skip ([LinkedIn Engineering, "Understanding dwell time to improve LinkedIn feed ranking", 12 May 2020](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/understanding-feed-dwell-time)). Under house rules each hook becomes a first line of 12 words or fewer, over a real photo or Justin's own clip, with the link in the first comment.
- **YouTube Shorts:** not verified this session.

## 5. The line we do not cross

Several of the best formats here use a face: Finch's glum before shot, and Bark's question box implying a parent. We copy the structure, never the person.

- **UK:** the ASA's CAP Code 3.47 requires documentary evidence that a testimonial is genuine. Code 2.3 forbids falsely implying that the marketer is a consumer ([ASA, Testimonials and endorsements](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/testimonials-and-endorsements.html)).
- **US:** the FTC rule of 14 August 2024 bans reviews that misrepresent they are "by someone who does not exist, such as AI-generated fake reviews" ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)).
- **First person hooks** run only when the line is true for Justin (`content/brand-story/founder-context.md`). Otherwise use second person.
- **Every DiGi chat on screen** comes from the real component (`ref-digi-chat`) with made up data, carries a small "Illustrative family" label, and the reply is generated by DiGi, never typed by us.
- **TikTok's AI label rules:** the support page returned no content, so I could not check them. If any frame of the DiGi star was AI generated, apply the platform's AI content label to be safe.

## 7. How to test them

- **Set up.** Each problem keeps one shared product scene and the same end card, so the hook is the only thing that changes (account mode in `.claude/skills/silent-ugc/SKILL.md`).
- **Order to post.** M1, M3, B1, B4, E2 and E4 first, because they rest on real screens or objects and carry no research risk. M2 goes up once, on early years content only.
- **What to read, in order:**
  1. How many viewers are still watching past the first two seconds, on the platform's retention graph.
  2. Saves and sends.
  3. Taps through to the stage check.

  Likes come last.
- **The three slots Justin asked for.** B4 and E4 show the real thinking lines. E2 and E5 show the real pathway. M3 is the opening for the separate screen timer film.
