# UK parent voice and problem numbers: sweep of 9 October 2026

**Scope.** I read 51 Mumsnet threads in full: about 2,260 posts dated 2020 to September 2026, with long threads capped at three or four pages. Twenty one are from 2025 and 12 from 2026. I also read four live Netmums threads and every written UK App Store review that Apple's public feed returns for Qustodio (167) and Google Family Link (500), plus Trustpilot for Qustodio. For the numbers I went to the primary documents: Ofcom (May 2026), the Children's Commissioner (2025 and 2026), DSIT and gov.uk (June to August 2026), More in Common (October 2025), Parent Zone (April 2026), Mumsnet's own polls and Smartphone Free Childhood.

**How the quotes were checked.** Mumsnet can now be fetched directly. In August it could not, so quotes came from search snippets. Every Mumsnet and Netmums quote below was cut from the page's own HTML and checked by script as an exact match. None is rebuilt from a search snippet. App Store quotes come from Apple's UK review feed. Each quote keeps the poster's own spelling and punctuation and is under 25 words. Usernames are public forum pseudonyms. Where a quote holds the poster's own dash or slash, I say so: trim it before it goes into any Guided Childhood copy.

**Limits.**
- The shared web search allowance ran out partway through the run. After that I could only open pages I already knew.
- Internet Matters, the IFS and Trustpilot's raw pages blocked automated access.
- The threads were found by topic searches. So thread counts show how parents talk once they post. They do not measure how common each problem is. For how common, use the survey numbers in section 3.

## 1. What is new or different since the 8 August sweep

1. **The ban is now government policy.** On 15 June 2026 the government said social media companies "will no longer be able to offer their services to under-16s".
   - The July response expects this "to enter into force in spring 2027".
   - 16 and 17 year olds get a default block from midnight to 6am.
   - WhatsApp style messaging stays open. Gaming gets its "harmful functions" restricted, not banned.
   - Parents in the June threads treat the ban as a shield in the home argument: "This is a law to help parents win arguments." ([GeneralPeter, 16/06/2026](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/am_i_being_unreasonable/5542980-why-do-people-want-teens-on-social-media?reply=152953673))
   - The ban does not touch the fights parents describe most: Roblox, YouTube Kids, gaming, WhatsApp and bedtime.
2. **The "9 in 10 parents" figure comes from a self selecting consultation, and the government says so.** DSIT's own release on the data says results "are not representative of parents and carers nationally" (row 14).
3. **UK conflict numbers now replace August's US ones.** August leaned on US sources (StudyFinds, Lingokids).
   - More in Common (October 2025): "A third of British parents say setting limits on their children being online leads to conflict."
   - Ofcom 2026: 32% of parents find screen time hard to control, rising to 49% for parents of 16 and 17 year olds.
4. **Ofcom 2026 is the new baseline, but its trends are only indicative.** It was published on 21 May 2026 using a new single survey of 5,008 parents and 3,426 children. Ofcom itself says "trends over time are indicative rather than absolute". New figures:
   - phone ownership goes from 56% at age 10 to 83% at age 11
   - 16% of 13 to 17 year olds say they know how to get round parental controls
5. **Roblox is now the main flashpoint at primary age.** It barely appeared in August. In the 2025 to 2026 threads it drives the switch off fights, with Dress to Impress and Dandy's World named. Ofcom: 51% of 8 to 14 year olds used Roblox in the past four weeks.
6. **Younger children's behaviour after screens is a new strand.** Parents of 6 to 11 year olds describe their child changing after a session ends (threads [5551604](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/am_i_being_unreasonable/5551604-6yo-completely-loses-the-plot-after-tvtablet-use-what-do-i-do) and [5527580](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/parenting/5527580-is-a-post-screen-comedown-after-youtube-normal-for-children)). Stricter parents now count YouTube Shorts as social media.
7. **AI chatbots have joined the list of worries.**
   - A UK Qustodio review (12/12/2025): "My teen daughter has a serious problem with AI chatbots".
   - Ofcom: 11% of AI users aged 8 to 17 have used AI "as someone to talk to or ‘as a friend’".
   - The government will ban sexualised AI chatbots for under 18s.
8. **Children's own views now back the connection thesis.**
   - The Children's Commissioner's December 2025 guide was written from focus groups with teenagers. One section heading reads "WHY COMMUNICATION MATTERS MORE THAN CONFISCATION".
   - Her April 2026 poll: the thing children most often said would make them feel safer online was "an adult to talk to if I see something bad" (35%).
9. **Parent Zone (April 2026) says the tools themselves now overwhelm parents.** "with additional tools comes additional complexity. That complexity can undermine empowerment by placing unreasonable burdens on the user." The 2026 UK app reviews say the same in plainer words.
10. **Corrections to the August file.**
    - The August quotes I checked again hold. "It's utterly miserable constantly policing screen time" is on a later page of thread 5046965 (Version4needsabitofwork, 10/04/2024).
    - August's "92 per cent concerned" and "61 per cent say their child is addicted" are not on either Mumsnet page I could open (section 4). Stop using the August line that 61 per cent of Mumsnet parents call their own child addicted until it is sourced.

## 2. The top eight UK problems, ranked by how often and how hard they hit

The ranking weighs three things: how common each problem is in surveys (section 3), how many threads open on it, and how strong the language gets (hate, war, hell, violence, harm). Problem 6 is the most intense but comes up less often day to day.
