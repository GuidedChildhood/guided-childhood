# Hostile questions and answers

**You're selling screen time as a reward. Tang 2018 and Nagata 2024 link screens used as a reward with more screen time.**

We have read them, and we don't argue with them. They are cross sectional, so they show a link, not a cause. That is exactly why the films never say earn your screens, and never claim stars cut screen time or build motivation. Stars are for jobs, never for reading or things the child already loves. Bedtime cannot be bought at any price, and there is a weekly cap. What we sell is the plan agreed together, not a reward scheme.

**Isn't this just a way to help children get phones earlier?**

No. DiGi never says yes or no to a phone. It shows the stage a child is at and what has to be true first. We are neutral on the ban: a deadline is not a plan, whichever side you are on. Nothing in the product tells a parent to hand over a phone, and the films never use the words ready or safe.

**An AI chatbot giving parenting advice? It's just ChatGPT searching the internet.**

Parenting advice comes from a research bank of over 140 findings, each with a named source, and each approved by a person before DiGi can use it. DiGi does have a web search tool, for things like app settings, the law and researcher figures, and we say so. That is why the films never feature the "not the open web" line, which is being reworded in the product.

**Going great is just a parent tapping a smiley face. That isn't evidence it works.**

Agreed, and the film says so on screen: YOUR OWN CHECK IN. It is the parent's own rating, never a measure of the child. The worry comes back a week later to check it held. We show the honest middle step, "Getting there", before Going great. No outcome is promised for any child.

**Are those real parents and real conversations?**

No, and every film says so: AN EXAMPLE FAMILY. REAL APP SCREENS, EXAMPLE REPLIES. There are no actors and no AI generated people, in line with CAP 2.3 and 3.47 and the ASA's guidance of 11 June 2026. The screens are the real components with made up data. Any DiGi reply on screen is either captured from the product or checked against its rules.

**So Orben, Przybylski and Odgers endorse your app?**

No. The card reads "Research we draw on", and every paid cut adds "Their published research, not an endorsement." Each line is a short, fair summary of their published work, for example "Small effects. How, not just how long."

**You say bedtime is protected, then the parent can say yes anyway. So it isn't protected.**

Stars cannot buy it, and the child cannot start a screen inside it. Only a grown up can say yes, and the line on screen says exactly that. The child gets a warm line, not a flat no. The parent decides, with the facts in front of them. That is deliberate: a parent is the right person to make an exception, not an app.

**Nothing locks. The child will just ignore your timer.**

True, nothing locks, and we never claim the timer cannot be got round. Children get round locks too. The timer is the messenger: it ends the session on both phones, so the parent is not the one calling stop. What holds is the deal the family agreed together.

**The two minute warning research was on toddlers.**

Yes, Hiniker et al. 2016 studied children aged 1 to 5. That is why the research tag only ever appears on the early years cut, with its age range, and never on the nine year old's timer. Scripts that still advise a two minute warning are being fixed before they appear anywhere.

**Under 16s are banned from social media from spring 2027, and phones are banned in school. Why does anyone need this?**

The ban does not cover WhatsApp, and nothing changes until spring 2027. Since September 2026 the law keeps phones out of the school day, which leaves the evening as the place a family's plan has to work. We help with the hours after the 3.15pm bell. A deadline is not a plan.

**Screens are harming children's mental health and you're making money out of keeping them on.**

We don't claim screens cause depression, and we don't claim they are harmless. The best evidence shows small average effects, and timing and how screens are used matter. We say neither "addicted" nor "safe". The product helps a family agree the plan, protect bedtime and have the hard conversation. It does not maximise anyone's time on a screen.

**Where do my words to DiGi go? Is it trained on families?**

DiGi learns about a family inside that family's own records. It is not trained on families. Anything drawn from other families is a suggestion only, never with numbers or anything identifying. A plain answer to the data question should sit one tap from the end card before any school cut goes out.
