# The first WhatsApp group: the August briefing, re checked

Checked 14 November 2026 against `briefings/2026-08-27-first-whatsapp-moment-v2.html`.
Two citation verifier agents re read the primary sources, and a third pass
looked for anything new since 1 September. This file is the checked version.
The pack beside it is written from this file and from nothing else.

## The short version

Of 37 verdict lines, 12 were confirmed, 17 corrected, 7 demoted and 1 could
not be reached. The August briefing is no longer safe to write from on its
own. Four things matter most:

1. **The blocker claim is wrong today.** The briefing said Bark and Qustodio
   state they cannot see inside WhatsApp. Bark's own help page, dated 14
   September 2026, says it scans direct and group messages on iOS and Android.
   Qustodio's own blog says its alerts monitor WhatsApp. "Preparation is the
   only category that works here" cannot be said.
2. **WhatsApp itself has changed.** Parent managed accounts for children under
   13 were announced on 11 March 2026 (the August check missed this). Parental
   controls for 13 to 17 year olds were announced on 30 September 2026, and
   they include who can add the teen to groups. Neither is confirmed as
   available in the UK. WhatsApp says only "rolling out gradually and may not
   be available in your region".
3. **Most of the alarming numbers are about teenagers.** The night waking odds
   (5.66), the 3.6 percent trajectory figure, the digital stress study, the
   confiscation quote and the bedtime meta analysis all come from samples
   aged about 13 and up. None can carry a claim about a 10 or 11 year old.
4. **Three Ofcom figures do not trace.** 86 percent of 10 to 12s holding an
   account, 52 percent of 8 to 11s on WhatsApp, and 32 percent of 8 to 17s
   bullied online. All three are removed.

## What we could not read

Ofcom, the eSafety Commissioner, the BBC, Girlguiding, Qustodio's help centre
and several journal publishers refused automated reading. Where a figure rests
on a reproduction or a search extract and not on the source itself, it says
so below, and a person should open the page once before the figure is posted.

## What this means for the August briefing

It keeps its place in the record, with a notice at the top pointing here. Its
"What This Means" section proposed builds on three claims that no longer hold:
the blockers being blind, restriction backfiring in a longitudinal study, and
the 3.6 percent reassurance at age 11. Those builds should be weighed again
before anyone picks them up.
