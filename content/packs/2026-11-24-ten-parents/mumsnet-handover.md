## The original post, and why it went wrong

Posted in Non member requests, asking ten parents to try the app via a link.
Four faults, all named by the first reply and all fixed in the rewrite:

1. It read as machine written.
2. The ask came at the end.
3. It gave a link to click rather than a site to type.

## Facts you can stand on (all checked in our briefings)

- **Addiction is a clinical word.** The WHO lists gaming disorder (ICD-11,
  2019). Social media addiction is not in either diagnostic manual (ICD-11 or
  DSM-5-TR). Apps are built to keep us scrolling, which is true, and "too much"
  is a habit you can change.
- **The biggest study:** 355,000 teenagers. Screen use explained at most 0.4%
  of the difference in wellbeing, about the same as eating potatoes (Orben and
  Przybylski, Oxford, 2019).
- **Haidt:** a review of The Anxious Generation in Nature by Candice Odgers
  (2024) said the "rewiring" claim is not supported by the science.
- **Say the debate is live**, especially for teenage girls. Never say "there is
  no evidence".
- **The product:** jobs earn screen minutes and a timer you can both see ends
  it. Short lessons from 4 to 16 on scams, group chats, AI and who they are
  really talking to in games. They line up with the new RSHE guidance, and
  schools teach the same lessons. DiGi answers from research with what to try
  for the child's age, never a yes or no. The trial is four days, free, no
  card.

## Never say

- "There is no evidence" (it can be disproved, and it isn't true).
- That ChatGPT wrote the post.
- Anything that promises an outcome for a child ("keeps them safe", "fixes").
- The village name, the children's names, or the school.
- Exact visitor numbers.
- More than one reply to a sceptic. On Mumsnet the founder who keeps defending
  loses the thread.

## How to reply from here

- Only answer a real question. A verdict ("this isn't anything") gets nothing,
  or one warm line.
- Agree first with whatever is fair, then add one thing they didn't know.
- Short. Plain. One scene from home at most.
- End by asking them something, or thanking them. Never end on a link.

## Is it going to work? What the thread taught us about selling

Read this before deciding where to put effort next.

**What the thread proves.** People clicked. About 24 from one forum post that
was criticised from the first reply. Ten finished the check. So the hook works:
"what stage is my child at" is something parents will spend a minute on.

**What it does not prove yet.** That anyone wants to pay. One sign up from ten
results, and the result page never said what came next. The fix ships in PR
1718. The next ten visitors are the real test of the product. These ten were a
test of a broken page.

**Which pitch landed and which did not.**

- "Screen time app" lost. It sounds like more screens, or like an app doing the
  parenting. Three replies read it that way.
- "Getting them ready before 16" won. Nobody argued with it, and it is the part
  no free chatbot gives you: a plan that runs from 4 to 16, the same lessons as
  school, and a record of what your child has done.
- "Mine too" won. Rules that cover the adults read as fair, not as policing.

**Where Mumsnet fits.** It is a hard room. Sceptical by default, allergic to
founders, quick to spot AI. That made it a good place to find the holes, and
we fixed four in a day. It is not the place to sell. Warmer rooms come first:
the local parents' Facebook group, WhatsApp forwards, school newsletters
through the pilot. In those rooms a parent hears about it from someone they
already trust.

**The one sentence to sell with now.** "At 16 they go from nothing to
everything overnight. This gets them ready before that day, with short lessons
you do together, and it stops screen time being a row every night."

**What to watch next.** Once PR 1718 is live, the share of people who reach the
result and then press "Save the pathway". Today it is 1 in 10. If the new card
moves that to 2 or 3 in 10, the product works and the job is getting more
people to the check. If it stays at 1 in 10, the result page itself needs a
deeper rethink before more traffic is worth sending.
