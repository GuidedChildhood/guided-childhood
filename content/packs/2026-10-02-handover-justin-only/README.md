## 1. Is this the right step 1?

Yes, for the parents lane, and it runs beside the LinkedIn founding 50 post, not
instead of it.

- The account's followers are the warmest audience the business has: parents who
  bought from the family for years. The canon counts them at about 6,000. Nothing
  on LinkedIn or the warm list is that big or that warm.
- It costs one afternoon of filming and six photos from the archive. No spend.
- It does not sell until post 6, and that is deliberate. A rename people were
  warned about is forgiven; one they were not is resented. Post 3 is the one that
  travels, and it only works with posts 1 and 2 underneath it.
- Honest maths: if one in twenty of those followers does the stage check that is
  about 300 stage checks, and at the 4 percent we plan for, about 12 paying
  families. That is a real first proof and roughly £100 a month, not £4,000. The
  £4,000 comes from the same story repeated weekly for twelve weeks across
  LinkedIn, the family account, the playlist pages and the schools door.
- Expect some unfollows. People who came for cake toppers and leave quietly are
  not a loss; the ones who stay are the first community.

So: post 1 on Monday 5 October as planned. The LinkedIn founding 50 post on
Sunday 4 October stays the revenue step 1.

## 2. One filming session does the whole series

Saturday 3 October, 14:00, the recording block already in your calendar. Three
pieces to camera, in this order, then stop.

| Order | Piece | Length | Why this order |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Post 1, the heads up | 45 seconds | Easiest. Warms you up. |
| 2 | Post 5, the new name | 30 seconds | Short, plain, done while fresh. |
| 3 | Post 3, the bit we do not usually say | 90 seconds | Hardest and the one that matters. Last, when you have stopped noticing the camera. |

Post 6 is a screen recording, not a to camera piece. It is the same 30 second
stage check demo on the weekly sheet for Friday 2 October. Film it once; it is
used four times.

**The setup, same for all three so they read as a series.**

- The kitchen table. You on one side, the window in front of you, not behind.
  No overhead light. Daylight, early afternoon.
- Phone upright on a stand or propped against a stack of books, lens at eye
  level, about an arm's length away. 1080p or 4K, 30 frames a second. Wipe the
  lens.
- Read from the cards on a second phone or a printed sheet held just beside the
  lens, not below it, so your eyes stay up.
- One card per take. Pause two seconds before and after each card. I cut the
  pauses, so never try to do a whole piece in one go.
- Say it to one person, the parent you know best who followed the account.
  Not to "everyone".
- Three takes of a card at most. The second take is usually the one.
- Nobody else in frame. No child's face at any point. A mug and a bit of mess
  on the table are fine; that is the point.

Send the clips as they are, uncut, through the app or AirDrop to the Mac. I cut,
caption, add the cream and ink titles, and hand back a vertical 9:16 for
Instagram and Facebook plus the landscape version if LinkedIn wants it.
