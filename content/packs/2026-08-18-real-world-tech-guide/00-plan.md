# READ ME, the Real World Tech Guide pack

A week of LinkedIn posts and a full email funnel, built from one third party source:
the New York Times Wirecutter guide "A Step-by-Step Tech Guide for Parents Who Live in
the Real World" (Signe Brewster and Ellen Lee, 18 August 2026). No dashes. Held to the
Odgers test and the Haidt test (.claude/skills/content-engine/hidden-thread.md Section 7).

## Why this article is the most useful thing we could have been handed

The NYT interviewed 10 experts, surveyed more than 750 parents, and read the research, and
came back with a rulebook that is almost word for word our one line story. Their exact
finding: it "isn't based on specific screen-time limits or device bans. Instead, it centers
on an intentional approach to introducing technology, as well as close communication between
adult and child."

That is us. Independent, credible, and it landed the same week we published the Australia ban
post. So the whole week is validation, not argument. We are not the lone voice saying educate
not ban. The New York Times and ten researchers just said it too. Our job is to stand next to
it, not shout over it.

## The stage map (the spine of the whole pack)

The article walks five life stages. Each maps onto one pathway stage and one thing we built.

| Article stage | Age | Our pathway stage | What we built | The article's own proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Manage early screen time | 0 to 5 | foundation (age 7) | connection framework, earned time, values first | Ito, "time based bans are exactly the wrong thing"; Davis on self regulation |
| Track or not track | 5 to 11 | builder (age 9) | trust framework, supervise not surveil | Ito, "surveillance signals lack of trust" |
| First smartphone | 11 to 13 | explorer (age 11) | the family agreement (written together), scripts, DiGi | "sign a contract"; Charmaraman, "no such thing as one big talk" |
| Healthy social media | 13 to 16 | shaper (age 13) | teach the algorithm, DiGi never a plain yes, balance reads the pattern | "review feeds together, talk about how the algorithm is designed" |
| Set free the young adult | 16 plus | independent (age 16) | independence earned in stages over years | Charmaraman, "let go before they go", tight rules leave kids unable to self regulate |

## The number that carries the close

62% of parents use parental controls. 75% rate the ones they use at 3 out of 5 or worse. The
whole week rests on this: parents are paying for, and leaning on, a tool they already know is
failing. That is the empirical case for a plan over a gate, and it is the founder close (post 7).

## Honesty and attribution discipline

- Every figure attributed at source (NYT Wirecutter survey, Pew, Common Sense Media, JAMA
  Pediatrics 2026, Gallup 2023, AAP). Verify each at source before print, egress was blocked
  this session so numbers are taken from Justin's pasted copy of the article.
- Haidt is named once (post 5) and generously. The article itself credits him for the wait until
  16 instinct, and we keep our mechanism not mandate line: the wall has a real cost (belonging),
  so teach, do not just delay. No dunk. This passes the Haidt test.
- The 9 to 13 study (an hour plus of social media linked to worse reading, vocabulary and memory
  two years later) is correlational. It lives in the lead magnet with the caveat, not in a hook.
- Jennifer Berger's Social Media Driver's License is a parallel product, not a rival. If it ever
  comes up, we are generous: more people teaching literacy is the market we want.
