## The rulebook, one page per stage

Each page is the same five part shape, so a parent can scan any stage in ten seconds.

### Stage 1, The early years (0 to 5), connection over control
- The struggle: 70% of parents give screen time because they need a break. That is human.
- The one insight: kids this age learn from people, not screens, and a screen used to calm them
  stops them learning to calm themselves (Katie Davis, University of Washington).
- Two moves: model the habit you want (phone away at dinner, out loud), and offer a real
  alternative rather than only removing the screen.
- The conversation: none yet, this is about your habits, not their rules. Be gentle with yourself.
- Where it goes next: our foundation stage, values and earned time from age four.

### Stage 2, Tracking (5 to 11), a tool, not a default
- The struggle: 39% of parents track, and it feels like the safe middle ground.
- The one insight: constant surveillance can signal you do not trust them, and erode the trust
  that actually keeps them safe (Mimi Ito, UC Irvine).
- Two moves: use tracking for a transition, not forever, and never in secret, always talk about it.
- The conversation: ask them how they feel about being tracked, and mean it.
- Where it goes next: our builder stage, independence handed in small earned steps.

### Stage 3, The first phone (11 to 13), a contract you write together
- The struggle: most kids have a phone by 11, and parents brace for one big talk.
- The one insight: there is no such thing as one big talk, it is dozens of small ones over years
  (Linda Charmaraman, Wellesley).
- Two moves: write a family agreement together before the phone arrives, and charge it out of the
  bedroom overnight (more than half of teens use phones between midnight and 4am, JAMA Pediatrics
  2026).
- The conversation: would you say this to their face, and do you have permission to share that.
- Where it goes next: our explorer stage, the written agreement and a script for every hard moment.

### Stage 4, Social media (13 to 16), teach the machine
- The struggle: about half of under 13s are already on it, and the wall leaks.
- The one insight: banning to 16 has a real cost, a teen shut out of the thing their school and
  friends run on, looking in from outside (Charmaraman). Teach, do not just delay.
- Two moves: review the feed together and show them how the algorithm is built, and agree that
  what they see is the edited highlight reel, not the true story.
- The conversation: check how it makes you feel, not just how long you were on it.
- Where it goes next: our shaper stage, a child who can see the algorithm, and a balance check that
  reads the pattern rather than counting hours.
- Held honestly: one study of 9 to 13s linked an hour or more a day to slightly worse reading,
  vocabulary and memory two years later. It is correlational, one signal among many, and the point
  stands either way, teach the skill.

### Stage 5, Setting them free (16 plus), let go before they go
- The struggle: young adulthood is a lonely, vulnerable stretch. 39% of college students report
  feeling lonely (Gallup 2023).
- The one insight: kids with the tightest lifelong restrictions coped worst on their own, because
  the wall never taught them to self regulate (Charmaraman).
- Two moves: loosen the rules while they are still home and mistakes are survivable, and hand them
  their own tools to manage screen time rather than controls you hold.
- The conversation: what is your own plan for when the phone gets in the way.
- Where it goes next: our independent stage, self regulation as a habit they own by sixteen.
