# Reels story bank

Drafted 14 October 2026. Justin saw a Family IT Guy ad ("The iPhone Safety
Setup Every Parent Needs") and asked for agents to scrape the channel and use
it for our reels.

**What we did instead of scraping.** Instagram is blocked from these sessions,
scraping breaks its terms, his scripts are his, and his engine (fear, with the
platform as the villain) fails the hidden thread test. So two agents catalogued
his public topics from TikTok, YouTube, his podcast and his site
(`research/01-youtube-podcast-site.md`, `research/02-tiktok.md`). We turned each
topic into **the parent question underneath it**, and answered that question
our way. Every hook below is ours, and every story points at a real screen or
script in the app.

Nothing here has been filmed, posted, boosted or scheduled.

## The gap we are filling

He has 296K followers on TikTok and sells a $27 iPhone settings course (the ad
Justin saw). Across about 330 pieces:

- **Fear gets his reach**, calm fixes get his saves, and his videos on the
  first phone and the conversation get the smallest numbers.
- **He says "add more as they show they are ready" and never says what ready
  looks like.** That is the stage check.
- **He almost never gives the words.** His one "all my friends play it" video
  answers with a different game, not a script. We have the script, with what to
  say and what not to say.

So our line against him is: **settings decide today. The words and the stage
decide the next eight years.** It is never said about him by name.

## How every story is built

The silent UGC format (`.claude/skills/silent-ugc/SKILL.md`):

1. **Scene one, 6 to 9 seconds.** One held shot of hands and objects, our hook
   in text over it from frame one. No face, no voice, no synthetic parent.
2. **Scene two, 4 to 6 seconds.** The real screen named in the story, recorded
   with made up data (`product-shorts` rules: never an invented screen).
3. **End card, every time:** What stage is your child? Three questions, no sign
   up. (The stage check, `/starter-pack`.)

Hooks are written in the second person on purpose. A first person line ("I
said no four times") reads as a real parent's story, and we have none to
stand behind unless it is one of Justin's true scenes in
`content/brand-story/founder-context.md`. If Justin wants a story in his own
voice, swap in his scene, never an invented one.

## The stories

The star quest leads, as it leads every list of what is inside. Each story
gives the parent question underneath his topic, our hook, the held shot, the
proof path in the app, and why ours is different. The ids in brackets point
back to the research tables.

### Star quest first

**1. The tablet goes at six and so does the mood**
- His topic: what happens when you take a device away (YouTube 26). His version is a talk about withdrawal.
- Parent question: why does my child melt down when I take the tablet?
- Hook: **Six o'clock. The tablet goes. So does everyone's mood.**
- Held shot: a tablet face down on a kitchen worktop, a timer on the fridge.
- Proof: the child app star quest. Jobs earn stars, stars become minutes, and the screen timer ends it, so the stop comes from the timer and not the parent. Script "The visible ending, for the child who finds stopping hardest" (migration 298).
- Note: this is a hook variant for the existing timer film (`content/packs/2026-10-09-digi-shorts/series-plan.md`, section 4). Use it to test the line, not to make a second film.

**2. Not every screen minute is the same minute**
- His topic: create, do not consume (TikTok 48). His most hopeful video, and one of his smallest.
- Parent question: I do not want to ban screens. What does good tech look like?
- Hook: **An hour of building something. An hour of scrolling. Same hour on the clock.**
- Held shot: a half built cardboard model next to a tablet showing a drawing app.
- Proof: the quest board, where a child can pitch their own quest and jobs earn stars.
- Why ours: he points at kit (a 3D printer, Scratch). We give the child a way to earn it and a say in what counts.

**3. Lights off at nine, glow under the door at eleven**
- His topic: no internet in the bedroom (YouTube 33, TikTok lower list). This is close to our own position.
- Parent question: how do I get the phone out of her room at night without a war?
- Hook: **Lights off at nine. A glow under the door at eleven.**
- Held shot: a dark landing, a thin line of blue light under a door.
- Proof: protected windows that no stars can buy (child app) and script "The phone is in their room all night" (migration 186). Nothing in the app buzzes a child at night.
- Why ours: he gives the rule. We give the rule, the window it lives in, and the conversation that gets the phone handed over.

## Never make

From his catalogue, these do not become ours in any form:

- Case stories of real harm to a named or identifiable child (the 764 videos, the kidnapping story, the ChatGPT case).
- Insider or whistleblower fear pieces, trial coverage and "what experts never allow" lists.
- Any video where a platform or a game is the villain, or a statistic carries the fear.
- His title cards, wording or video structure lifted line for line. We take the parent question, never the piece.
- Any claim that a child will be safer, calmer or ready because of us.
