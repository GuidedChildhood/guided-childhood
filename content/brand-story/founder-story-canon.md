**This is the one true account of why Guided Childhood exists.** Every founder
story, origin video, About page, pinned post and credentials answer comes from
here. `founder-context.md` is the biography and the privacy levels.
`founding-story.md` is the family account's canon in Natalia's voice. This file
is the public story in Justin's voice, and it is what the Drive documents are
measured against.

Written 12 October 2026, after an audit of the four founder and origin story
documents in Drive. What that audit found and changed is in
`drive-story-audit-2026-10-12.md`.

**The rule:** nothing here is invented, and nothing here needs a number it does
not have. If a version of the story needs a fact that is not in this file or in
`founder-context.md`, it does not get told until Justin supplies the fact.

## The authority, stated honestly

Justin is not a clinician and the story works better without pretending
otherwise. What he has is a different thing, and it is real.

A parent who kept finding that the simple answers did not survive contact with
his own family. Who read the research rather than the headlines, including the
researchers who disagree with each other. Who changed his mind in public when
the evidence moved him. And who had built software before, so he could build the
thing he could not find.

Say that. Do not position it against anyone. Earlier drafts put Justin above two
named clinicians by arguing that they see the damage afterwards while he spots it
first. That claim is wrong about the product and unkind about them, and it would
read badly to either of them. Our position stands on its own without a foil.

### The one sentence answer

> I am a dad of three who could not get a straight answer about raising children
> around phones and feeds, so I read the research instead of the headlines, and
> built the thing I could not find.

That is the whole credential. It is true, it needs no qualification, and it is
closer to the reader's own life than letters after a name.

### What never goes in it

- **The children's ages, names or stories.** "Dad of three" carries the same
  weight and costs them nothing. `founder-context.md` puts specific stories about
  Alma, Olga or Teo at AMBER, and anything that could embarrass them later at
  RED. An age said on camera is permanent, and it is their information, not his.
- **A claim about data we hold.** We hold no browsing data, no search history and
  no assessment data on any child, and the learning surface says so out loud.
  Never "I see it in the data" and never "what children are actually searching
  for".
- **Detection or prediction.** Never that we spot a problem before it happens,
  before a parent notices, or before a clinician would. We show a parent where
  their child is on a pathway and what to do next, which is a smaller and a true
  claim.
- **"OS", "operating system", "Device Pathway OS".** Not Justin's voice, and the
  simplification agenda in THE-STORY.md exists because one word for one thing
  matters. It is a pathway, from four to sixteen.
- **A comment for a keyword mechanic.** LinkedIn's 2026 authenticity update
  suppresses these, and repeat use can cost account level reach, not just the
  post. The link goes in the first comment and the body says so.
- **Any number of years that is not true.** The documents in Drive say two years
  in one place and three in another. Neither was checked. The honest anchor is
  the change of mind: five years ago Justin thought technology was mostly bad for
  children, and he does not think that now.

## The research position, on the evidence as it stands in October 2026

The story used to lean on one idea, that social media amplifies a vulnerability
a child already carries. That is still our thesis, and it is now standing on a
much larger pile of evidence than it was in June. State it in this shape, because
this shape survives a hostile reader.

**What the better evidence shows.** Across whole populations the average
association between screen use and children's wellbeing is small. It concentrates
in children who are already carrying something. And the strongest designs keep
finding that the population average tells you very little about any one child. A
year long study of 197 adolescents that recorded what their phones actually did,
rather than asking them afterwards, found no credible association either way for
roughly nine in ten of them, and found that how a teenager felt an hour earlier
predicted how they felt now several times more strongly than anything the phone
had done.

**The finding that costs us, said first.** In that same study, time on social
apps was followed by feeling slightly worse, which is the result the researchers
had not expected. Say it before the findings that help. That is what makes the
rest believable, and it is the move Justin's best posts already rest on.

**Who to name, and how.** Candice Odgers, Amy Orben, Andrew Przybylski, Sonia
Livingstone, Chris Ferguson, Lucy Foulkes. Spelled correctly: Candice, not
Candace, which the product fixed in September and the Drive documents did not.
Jonathan Haidt gets named as the voice Justin encountered first and does not
agree with, never grouped with the others as though they reached one conclusion.
They are on opposite sides of this argument, and listing them together as "the
research I read" is the kind of thing a hostile expert catches in one line.

**Never name a living clinician as a source inside the product.** Migration 123
took those names off the advice a parent reads, for the reason that a name next to
guidance in a paid product reads as endorsement whether it is meant that way or
not. A public post naming ten researchers as DiGi's library does the same thing
at more scale. Say that DiGi answers from a bank of published findings, each with
its source, and that a parent can see the sources. Do not read the roll call.

## The long version, for an About page or a pinned post

I did not set out to start a company. I set out to answer a question I could not
get a straight answer to as a parent: how do I raise a child who can live well
with phones, feeds and now AI, without either handing them everything or taking
it all away.

Every answer I found was one of two things. Panic, telling me my child's brain
was being rewired and the only move was to ban and delay. Or a shrug, telling me
it was a moral panic and I should relax. Neither matched what I could see at
home, which was that most children were basically fine, a smaller group were
genuinely struggling, and nobody was teaching any of them how the machine
actually works.

Five years ago I thought technology was mostly bad for children, and that
education should stay closer to paper and pen. My own family made that position
impossible to hold. My children learned real things from screens. They met
languages, science, crafts, people. And at the same time turning a screen off
could start an argument, a recommendation could lead somewhere I did not expect,
and a device could make an evening easier while building a habit that was hard to
unwind later. Technology could be genuinely useful and still cause real problems
in the same house on the same day.

So I stopped looking for an answer that needed technology to be either good or
bad, and I started reading the actual research. Not the headlines, the papers.
The researchers whose work survives scrutiny, whose data is public, whose results
their own critics can download and attack. The picture that emerged was calmer
and far more useful than the noise. Across populations the harm is small on
average. It concentrates in the children already carrying something. And the best
designed studies keep finding that for most individual children there is no
measurable link at all, which means the number on the screen time report tells
you much less about your own child than it feels like it should.

That did not make me relaxed. It moved the problem. If the phone is not the
story, then the story is what a child brings to it and what they understand about
it. The answer was never protection from technology. It was capability with it,
taught in steps, age by age, the way we teach roads or swimming.

That is Guided Childhood. A pathway from four to sixteen, for parents and for
schools. Delay while they are young, build skill as they grow, and teach every
child to see the machinery, the business model, the feed, the exits, before they
are standing in it. Not fear. Not a ban. A plan.

I am building it in public, because the evidence should be checkable and so
should I. I publish the research, sourced, before I publish the opinion. I state
the strongest counter argument in everything I write, including the findings that
make my own position harder. And when someone shows me I am wrong, I say so and
change my position.

If you are a parent who wants the honest version, or a teacher or a head who
wants the evidence trail, follow along.

## The build in public spine

The rule is process over outcomes. Outcomes are the least interesting layer.

- Why I am building this, told in fragments rather than all at once.
- A real decision and what changed my mind about it.
- The research, published sourced before the opinion.
- The honest pivots, where the evidence is uncertain and where I was wrong.
- The numbers with context, said plainly, including the ones that did not work.
- What we built and why, rather than an announcement.

The mechanic underneath it: value first, then a soft ask. The resource link goes
in the first comment with one line in the body pointing at it. The stage check at
`/starter-pack` is the free way in, three questions and no sign up, and it is
showable in fifteen seconds. The founder rate is capped at 50 and the cap is
enforced in code. Followers are not the metric. The email list is.

The staged pathway and the stage check, at `/starter-pack`.

## Hard rules for any version of this story

- No dashes anywhere. `npm run ai-tells` checks the half a machine can judge.
- Never an outcome claimed for a child. Clarity, calmer conversations, a pathway.
- Never "safe online" and never "ready for social media". Always "completed the
  preparation".
- Never a synthetic person presented as a real parent.
- Every public claim passes the Odgers test. A hostile, hype allergic researcher
  reads it without wincing, or the sentence narrows until they would.
- One scene, used once, never invented, never repeated. The biography is
  background, not a formula.
