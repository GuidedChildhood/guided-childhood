# 10 · Building it myself

**Privacy: GREEN.** Names Guided Childhood: **yes** (one of three). Proposed:
Wednesday 16 December.

**The scene:** `founder-context.md`, "The AI and building moment". After moving
to the countryside he walked in the mornings listening to entrepreneur and
business podcasts, watching AI assisted software creation develop. For years,
turning an idea into software meant finding developers, spending a lot of money
and hoping someone else understood the vision, which he had already found hard.
AI assisted coding changed what he thought was possible: he could turn the
system in his head into something real himself, which suited someone who
learns by doing. And it raised the question of what it means for children
growing up with these tools from the start.

**The close:** "Eventually the controls come off. What I want left behind is
judgement." From "What Guided Childhood is really trying to achieve". It is
reserved for the brand account's Thursday Reel on 19 November
(`weeks/2026-11-09/thursday-the-one-line.md`), so using it here is a deliberate
echo four weeks later, on a different account. **Needs his word.** The
alternative close is below.

**On honesty about the code:** the AI writes most of it, and anyone who looks
at the repository can see that. Saying so first is stronger than being found
out, so the caption says it.

**Format:** Reel on the lane.

## Caption

> I struggled at school, dropped out of college, and I'm building an app
> myself.
>
> After we moved to the countryside, I started walking in the mornings with
> podcasts about people building businesses. And I kept hearing about AI that
> could help write software.
>
> For years, turning an idea into software meant finding developers, spending a
> lot of money, and hoping someone else understood what was in your head. I'd
> been through that before.
>
> AI changed that for me. I could take the system I'd been carrying around in my
> head and actually make it. For someone who has always learned by doing, that
> was huge.
>
> To be straight with you, the AI writes most of the code. My job is deciding
> what it should do, and whether it's right.
>
> It also left me with a question I can't put down. If AI can change what
> someone like me can build, what will it mean for kids who grow up with it from
> day one?
>
> That's what Guided Childhood is for. Getting them ready, a stage at a time,
> from four to sixteen, with you beside them.
>
> Eventually the controls come off. What I want left behind is judgement.
>
> What's one thing you want your kids to be able to judge for themselves by the
> time they leave home?
>
> Thank you for reading all ten.

## Proof paths

  "safe online".
