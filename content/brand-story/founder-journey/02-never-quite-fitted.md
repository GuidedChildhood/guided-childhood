# 2 · Never quite fitted

**Privacy: GREEN.** Names Guided Childhood: no. **Replaced by 2b**, which Justin approved on 7 November. Kept as the fallback if he changes his mind.

**The scene:** `founder-context.md`, "The founder arc" and "Education and finding
his ability later". Easily distracted, very messy handwriting, struggled with
homework, grammar and sustained reading. Not disruptive, but did not quite fit.
Broke his leg at about fourteen going into a football tackle, already behind,
pushed further back. Did A levels because that seemed to be what people did
next, and did not succeed academically.

**Kept GREEN on purpose:** it says what was hard, not how it felt. "Feeling like
a failure at school" is AMBER, so it is not here. The AMBER version is 2b.

**Format:** single image.

---

## Caption

> I wasn't a naughty kid at school. I just never quite fitted.
>
> Easily distracted. Very messy handwriting. Homework, grammar, reading anything
> long. All of it was hard work.
>
> Then at about fourteen I broke my leg going into a football tackle. I was
> already behind, and the time away from school pushed me further back.
>
> I did A levels because that seemed to be what you did next. They didn't go
> well.
>
> I'm not telling you this to say school got it wrong, or that my story is
> everyone's. It isn't.
>
> But it took me years to work out that struggling with the way something is
> taught and being unable to learn it are two very different things.
>
> I had a lot of the first. It turned out I didn't have the second.
>
> Did school fit you, or did you find your thing later?
>
> Part three on Sunday: London, biology, and the bit I didn't tell my parents.

**The scene:** `founder-context.md`, "The founder arc". At about eleven, at the
dinner table, struggling with maths homework. His dad understood it and became
increasingly frustrated that he did not. Justin remembers feeling scared,
useless and like a failure. As a parent he sometimes recognises the same
frustration in himself, and tries to catch it.

## Caption

> I was about eleven, at the dinner table, stuck on my maths homework.
>
> My dad understood it. I didn't. And the longer it went on, the more
> frustrated he got.
>
> I remember feeling scared. Useless. Like a failure.
>
> He wasn't a bad dad. He was strict and he loved us, and he worked hard for
> us.
>
> I'm telling you because I have felt that same frustration come up in me,
> sitting next to my own kids.
>
> When I notice it, I try to catch it. I want them to come away from the table
> feeling something different from what I felt.
>
> Is there a moment from your own childhood you catch yourself repeating?
>
> Part three on Sunday: London, biology, and the bit I didn't tell my parents.
