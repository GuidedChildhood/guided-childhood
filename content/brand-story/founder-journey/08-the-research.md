# 8 · The research

**Privacy: GREEN.** Names Guided Childhood: **yes** (one of three). Proposed:
Wednesday 9 December.

**The scene:** `founder-context.md`, "From Social Billboard to Guided Childhood"
and "Delay and preparation". He read much more of the research. Jonathan Haidt
was one of the most prominent voices, but the certainty did not match his lived
experience of technology being both useful and a problem. He spent a long time
reading Candice Odgers, Amy Orben, Andrew Przybylski, Chris Ferguson and others.
The disagreements mattered. And he saw what a playlist could not do: teach a
child what to do in a group chat, why an algorithm recommends something, when to
ask an adult for help. A ban could delay an experience without preparing the
child for it. Delay is not useless; delaying and preparing are different jobs.

**The hidden thread brick:** the evidence is more complicated than the headlines,
and waiting and preparing are two jobs. No poverty and ACE thesis here; it is
not that post.

**Format:** carousel, seven slides.

## Caption

> I went looking for a simple answer about kids and screens. The research
> wouldn't give me one.
>
> Jonathan Haidt was the first big voice I read. He's raised a real worry and I
> take it seriously.
>
> But his certainty didn't match what I was seeing at home, where the same
> technology was helping and causing problems at the same time.
>
> So I kept reading. Candice Odgers. Amy Orben. Andrew Przybylski. Chris
> Ferguson. People who look hard at the same evidence and don't always agree.
>
> That disagreement turned out to be the most useful thing I found. When an
> answer sounds certain, it's worth asking what it's leaving out.
>
> It also showed me the gap in what I'd built. A perfect playlist can't teach a
> child what to do in a group chat. A filter can't explain why something is
> being recommended. A control can't teach them when to come and find you.
>
> And a ban can put the day off. On its own, it doesn't get them ready for it.
>
> I'm not against waiting. Sometimes waiting is the right call. But waiting and
> preparing are two different jobs, and only one of them was being done.
>
> That's when The Social Billboard grew into Guided Childhood.
>
> When you read about kids and screens, do you come away more worried, or more
> ready?
>
> Part nine on Sunday: the moment I realised all that reading hadn't told me
> what to say.
