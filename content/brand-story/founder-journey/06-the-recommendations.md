# 6 · The recommendations

**Privacy: GREEN.** Names Guided Childhood: no. Proposed: Wednesday 2 December.

**The scene:** `founder-context.md`, "The original technology belief" and "The
YouTube moment". He leaned towards screens being probably bad for children, with
paper, physical activity and traditional experiences preferable. Then he saw a
video about relationships appear through recommendations and realised how
little control a parent has over where a sequence of recommended videos leads.
He watched his children move from one thing to the next in ways that were hard
to anticipate. YouTube also held extraordinary educational material. He tried
picking videos by hand. It was time consuming, hard to maintain, and did not
solve the broader problem. The Social Billboard grew from that.

**The belief is the setup, not a second scene.** The scene is the
recommendation.

**Who saw the video:** the source does not say, so the caption does not either.

**Format:** carousel, five slides.

---

## Caption

> A video about relationships turned up in the YouTube recommendations. That's
> when I saw how little say I had.
>
> For years I'd been the dad who thought screens were mostly bad for kids.
> Paper, being outside, real things. That was the plan.
>
> Then I watched my kids move from one video to the next in ways I could never
> have guessed. And when that video appeared, it hit me how little control a
> parent has over where a chain of recommended videos ends up.
>
> But the same site had some of the best educational material I'd ever seen.
>
> So I started picking videos myself. One by one. It took ages, it was
> impossible to keep up with, and it didn't fix the real problem.
>
> That itch became The Social Billboard. The question was simple. How do kids
> get the best of YouTube without handing the next choice to the algorithm?
>
> Have the recommendations ever taken your child somewhere you didn't expect?
>
> Part seven on Sunday: the day the thing I worried about turned out to be
> holding a friendship together.
