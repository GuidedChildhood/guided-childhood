# Format playbook: what works for `family_it_guy`, rebuilt as ours

**Written 5 November 2026.** Justin asked to "pretty much copy" the
`family_it_guy` channel for the family account. This file takes his strongest
formats and rebuilds each one as a Guided Childhood format. The evidence is
`reference-accounts.md` section 11, read from his grid on the same day.

**What we copy and what we never copy.** We copy the shape: the format, the
hook structure, the length, the caption anatomy, the comment mechanics. We
never copy his words, scripts, captions, visuals, branding or face, and we
never copy his register. He sells the fix and the fear. We sell the plan.

**Every format below obeys the same five rules.**

1. Never allow or deny, in the hook, the script or the caption.
2. Every claim has a proof path in the product or a source we opened.
3. Every video ends on the stage check: *What stage is your child? Three
   questions, no sign up.*
4. No dashes anywhere, on screen or in the caption. `npm run ai-tells`.
5. Real screens and real assets only (`brand/GDC_SOCIAL_VISUAL_SYSTEM.md`).
   No synthetic parent, ever.

## 1. Does. Does not. The words.

**His version.** One new control, shown on screen, feature by feature. Then the
catch: what it does not cover. Caption with a tick and cross list and the
exact settings path. His two biggest Reels of the last 100 days are this shape,
at 48 and 77 seconds.

**Why it works.** It is useful tonight, it is honest about limits, and it is
saved because the settings path is in it. The honesty is what makes parents
trust the rest.

**What we change.** He stops at the toggle. We add the third part, the part no
setting can do: the words, and the step the child takes. That is the whole
Guided Childhood position in one Reel, and it answers him directly without
naming him.

**Slot.** Service Friday. It fits the Friday structure exactly: the problem,
what we made (the platform guide and the script), the hinge (the setting is the
easy half), the soft ask. It carries the week's comment gate.

**Hook template.**
- On screen, frame one: *[Setting name]. What it does.* (six words or fewer, so
  capitals are allowed)
- Spoken line one: *[Platform] has one setting that does the most. It also
  leaves three things undone.*
- Caption line one: *[What the setting does, in one plain sentence.]*

**The shape, 45 to 60 seconds.**
1. Justin, kitchen corner, says what the setting does. (8s)
2. Real screen recording of the setting on a test account, never a child's
   account. (10s)
3. What it does not do, three short lines, each on screen. (15s)
4. The part no setting does: the script line, shown on the real script deck in
   our app. (12s)
5. End card: the stage check line and the `/v/` link. (5s)

**Caption.** Five numbered lines: one for what it does, three for what it does
not, one for the part no setting does. Close on a consequence. Then a question
a stranger can answer. Then the gate.

**Proof path.** The platform guides at `/dashboard/social-settings` (content in
migrations 093 and 385, `social_platform_guides`), the per device setup at
`/dashboard/phone-setup`, and the matching script in the `scripts` table. Re
check the setting on a real phone the day before filming, because platforms
move toggles.

## 2. DiGi, what do I say?

**His version.** A question card at the top of the frame, labelled as from his
live chat, and him answering underneath. Also "a parent asked me" in the first
line of the caption. Three of his top ten are this shape.

**Why it works.** The parent watching sees their own question, in a parent's
words, before they see him. Recognition first, expertise second.

**What we change.** We already own this frame. The franchise is DiGi, WHAT DO I
SAY? and the format is Parent situation (`GDC_SOCIAL_VISUAL_SYSTEM.md` section
8). The question is a line parents really say, taken from our research packs,
under an IBM Plex Mono eyebrow, A THING PARENTS SAY, so it reads as
illustrative. The answer is DiGi's real reply, captured on the real app, which
never says yes or no and always gives where you are, the next step and the
words. We never fake a live chat or invent a named parent.

**Slot.** A Thursday short film at 19:30, after the fight. Thursday is a buffer
day in `weekly-rhythm.md`, so this is a proposal for Justin: one film a week,
and only that.

**Hook template.**
- On screen, frame one: the parent's line in quotation marks, *"[their words]."*
  with the eyebrow A THING PARENTS SAY.
- Caption line one: *Send this to the parent who heard "[their words]" today.*

**The shape.** The `product-shorts` arc, unchanged: ask DiGi, the script deck,
the tool, the check in tracked to Sorted, the end card. 25 to 40 seconds, plus
a TikTok cut.

**Proof path.** DiGi at `/dashboard/digi` (`lib/digi/system.ts` holds the
rails), the script in the `scripts` table, the check in at
`components/daily/ConcernCheckIn.tsx`. The end card eyebrow is the matching
stage check worry tile, word for word (`lib/pathway/challenge-labels.ts`).

## 3. How it actually works

**His version.** One invisible system made plain: why rejecting cookies does not
stop tracking, what the cloud physically is, why free games make money. These
run long, up to three minutes, and still travel.

**Why it works.** Parents feel out of their depth (47 percent feel their child
knows more about technology than they do, THE-STORY.md section 2). A clear
explanation gives them back some footing, and it is shared because it makes
the sharer look clued up.

**What we change.** He explains it to the parent. We explain it so the parent
can explain it to the child, because the passport stamp is the child knowing
how it works. Each one ends on the one sentence a parent can say at the table.

**Slot.** Happy News Saturday, roughly one in three, the competence strand.
Competence is the protection, and Saturday is where we show it.

**Hook template.**
- On screen: *Why [the thing] never [the surprising behaviour].*
- Spoken line one: *[The thing] is a recipe, not magic. Three steps.*
- Caption line one: *[One plain sentence of the mechanism.]*

**The shape.** 40 to 75 seconds. The mechanism in three steps, one on screen at
a time, then the sentence for the child, then the lesson it comes from on the
real lessons screen, then the end card. Or a seven card carousel with the same
spine and a Reel version to test against it.

**Proof path.** The lessons in `content/modules/` that a parent opens from
`/dashboard/lessons`: `ks2-06-how-algorithms-work` (the feed loop, played on
paper), `ks2-05-gaming-time-spend` (the three spend spotters),
`ks3-12-misinfo-deepfakes`, `ks4-15-manipulation-persuasion`. The passport
stage "Knows how it works" (`lib/content/readiness.ts`).

## 4. The ladder, in five

**His version.** A numbered list of five house rules, pinned, 1.4 million views.
Screenshot friendly, saveable, sold as part two so the first part gets watched
too.

**Why it works.** Five numbered things is the most saved shape on the platform,
and a list from someone's own house reads as testimony.

**What we change.** Rules become a ladder. Every rung is a stage with a next
step, which is the pathway rather than a verdict. We never say "non
negotiable". And the list comes from the product, so every line has a proof
path.

**Slot.** Happy News Saturday as a carousel in THE ROAD TO 16 franchise, with a
Reel version cut from the same cards.

**Hook template.**
- Cover card: *Nobody goes from no phone to everything in one birthday.*
- Caption line one: *Five rungs, from 4 to 16. Where is yours?*

**The shape.** Seven cards: the cover, one card per rung with the Planet Friend
for that stage on the gold pathway, then the Passport card. The save ask goes
on the last card, the stage check in the caption.

**Proof path.** `PHONE_LADDER` in `lib/content/passport.ts`, rendered at
`/dashboard/phone-setup`. The passport at `/dashboard/passport`. The family
phone agreement printable at `content/printables/family-phone-agreement/`.

## 5. The one line

**His version.** One sentence of opinion, a few seconds of him, a caption under
250 characters. 110 thousand views.

**Why it works.** It is quick to watch twice, it is easy to send, and it says
who he is.

**What we change.** The sentence has to be Justin's own and true. The
founder context holds a handful, for example: "Eventually the controls come off.
What I want left behind is judgement." One line per film, never the same line
twice, logged in `content/brand-story/founder-scenes-used.md`.

**Slot.** The Thursday short film, alternate weeks with format 2.

**Hook template.** The line itself, as the hook plate, from frame one.

**The shape.** 8 to 15 seconds. Justin says the line once to camera, the plate
holds it, the end card follows. If Justin would rather not film, the
`silent-ugc` version is the line over hands and a real object, such as a paper
star chart on the fridge.

**Proof path.** The line is from `founder-context.md`. The end card is the stage
check.

## 6. The comment gate

**His version.** "Comment [word] and I'll send you the link." On his two biggest
recent Reels, pointing at a paid guide. Comments ran at almost half of likes on
the biggest one, against 2 to 10 percent on the rest.

**What we change.** The gate sends something free, the stage check, because
every CTA routes to `/starter-pack`. One gate a week at most, on Friday, as
`reference-accounts.md` section 7 already set out.

**The word.** PACK, for the starter pack and the stage check. One word, every
time, so parents learn it.

**The replies.** Justin answers by hand at first. If the replies outgrow one
person, the gate goes fortnightly before anything is automated.

two weeks of numbers say otherwise.

## What to measure

In this order, as `reference-accounts.md` section 9 already says: saves and
shares, then DM replies to the gate, then stage checks from each film's own
`/v/` link, then follows. A format earns a second run when it beats our own
median twice. His numbers are a reference for shape, never a target.
