Justin, 13 October 2026, asked what that skill should say instead.

**The problem.** `linkedin-comment-replies` lives in Justin's own claude.ai
skills rather than in this repo, so nothing here can fix it. Its "Product facts"
section currently says the Digital Health Report is live at
guidedchildhood.com/digitalwellbeing at £9.99 for a single report and £29.99 a
month, and instructs Claude to "lead with the £9.99 report for first time
interest".

Two things are wrong with that, and one thing is not.

Not wrong: the report exists and the page works. Justin confirmed that on
13 October and kept the live links.

Wrong: it is not the main line. A reply to a potential customer should lead with
the parent app and the stage check, because that is the business, and the report
sits on its own domain. And the figures in there are old enough that nobody
should quote them from a skill file. The parent prices moved and the skill never
did, which is exactly how a wrong number got in front of a customer on
27 September.

**Replace the whole "Product facts" section with the block below.** It is written
to be pasted as is.

## Product facts (for customer replies, keep them accurate)

The product is Guided Childhood, a staged pathway for children aged 4 to 16,
for parents and for schools.

**The way in is the stage check**, free, at guidedchildhood.com/starter-pack.
Three questions about the child: their age, what the parent is dealing with
right now, and how much time they can give it. No sign up to see the result. It
returns the child's stage and the things worth practising next. Lead with this
for any first time interest, always. It is showable in fifteen seconds and it
costs the parent nothing.

**Inside the parent app:** DiGi, the guide a parent describes a real moment to,
which never allows and never denies and always returns a pathway. The scripts
library for the actual words to say. The daily check in, where a parent rates
the worries they named and watches the number move. The Digital Passport, which
makes a child's digital development visible and never declares them "safe
online". The star quest, which is the piece no competitor has.

**Prices.** Founder £7.99 a month, capped at 50 for ever and the cap enforced in
code rather than just promised in the copy. Standard £12.99 a month. Annual £99.
Free tier: a four day trial, three DiGi messages a day, five free scripts and
the starter pack. Schools £495 to £1,995 a year on invoice.

**The Digital Health Report** is a separate tool on its own domain. It works and
it is still linked from the site, but it is not the main line and it is not what
we lead with. Do not quote a price for it: if somebody asks, say it is a
separate thing, point them at the stage check, and let Justin answer the pricing
question himself.

**What we never claim.** No outcome for a child. What we claim is clarity, a
calmer conversation and a pathway. Never "safe online" and never "ready for
social media"; the phrase is "completed the preparation". Nothing in the product
is a diagnosis, and a serious worry always gets a signpost to a GP or another
professional.

**No links in comments.** Reach drops when a comment carries a URL. Drive to the
idea, and let the profile and the bio carry the address.

## Why this is worth his two minutes

A reply drafted from the old block would offer a potential customer a £9.99
report as the way in. That is not the business, the price is unverified, and the
parent would land on a separate domain rather than at the three questions that
convert. The stage check is the funnel: every CTA routes there, and 8,000 stage
checks at the observed 4 percent is the whole route to 325 paying families.

Nothing else in that skill needs touching. The register section, the Odgers
test, the eight comment types and the safeguarding rule are all good and all
still true.
