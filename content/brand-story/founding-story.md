## The timeline

**2015. Alma.**
Our first daughter is born. Somewhere in the fog of a first baby,
`[NARRATOR]` starts making customised cake toppers on a laptop in the bedroom.
Not a business plan. A thing to make, for people who wanted their child's
birthday to look like someone had bothered.

**The growth.**
Toppers become party supplies. Supplies become events. Events become the big
ones: weddings, the milestone birthdays, the days people remember for thirty
years. Inspired by Alma, named after the reason it started.

**The shop in Bristol.**
We opened our own shop. `[SHOP_YEARS]`. The proof, and it matters to the story,
that this family builds real things and finishes them. We loved the journey. We
still do.

**Olga, and the thing we had not seen before.**
With Alma it had not come up. With Olga it did. YouTube and screens turned into
a battle, and not just a battle about screens. The mood of the whole house moved
with it. The tone of the afternoon depended on how the handover went. Anyone who
has taken a tablet off a small child at five in the afternoon knows the exact
feeling being described.

**Covid, and the honest part.**
Two little girls at home. A business to keep alive and orders to get out. The
busiest and the hardest at the same time. And we used YouTube as a babysitter,
to some degree, to survive. We felt guilty about it then and there is no point
pretending otherwise now.

**The idea.**
It did not come from a market. It came from sitting there at nine at night,
after a day like that, wishing something existed that would help us manage the
devices and not just switch them off. So `[NARRATOR]` and Justin built it. First
for us, for our own family, on our own kitchen table.

**Then it got harder.**
Social media arrived on top of the devices. More screens, more accounts, more
places to keep an eye on. And now a ban at 16 that arrives like a cliff edge:
nothing, nothing, nothing, then everything at once, with no practice.

**Teo, four, and the road that changed again.**
Teo is four and mad about football. He is growing up into a world where AI has
opened a whole new road, and where the answer cannot be to keep him off it until
his sixteenth birthday and then wish him luck.

**What we built.**
The thing we wished had existed. A way to manage the devices, the schools, the
education and the safety learning, in one place, ten minutes a day.

## The hinge of the story, and how to handle it

**The guilt is the hinge.** The line about using YouTube as a babysitter to
survive Covid is the line that stops 6,000 parents scrolling, because every one
of them did the same thing and almost none of them have said it out loud.

It is the most valuable sentence we have. It is also the easiest one to ruin.

**Two ways to ruin it, both banned.**

1. **Resolving it by blaming YouTube.** Easy, cheap, and it makes us the same as
   every panic account in the space. It also is not what we think is true.
2. **Resolving it by telling parents they were fine actually.** Warm, and it
   throws away the whole reason the story is worth telling. If it was fine, we
   would not have built anything.

**The way we do resolve it.** The tool was doing a job that genuinely needed
doing. We needed the twenty minutes. What nobody had built was the version of
that tool that also taught something, that gave the time back on purpose instead
of by accident, and that made the handover the start of something rather than a
fight. So we built it.

That resolution is the entire product thesis smuggled inside a personal story,
which is why it works.

## The craft is not a previous life

The 6,000 followed a mother making beautiful things for children. That has not
stopped. It changed material.

The star charts, the routine charts, the family phone agreement, the activity
packs and the printables are the same instinct as the cake toppers: make the
thing beautiful enough that a family actually uses it, and make the ordinary day
feel like it was worth marking. Same hands, same standard, same reason.

**Say it like this, never apologise for the change:**

> We have always made things that made family life a bit better. For ten years
> they were made of card and sugar. Some of them still are. Some of them now run
> on a phone.

## What we believe, in the family's words

Not a ban. Not no ban. The bit in between that nobody is building.

- **A ban is a delay, not a preparation.** A sixteenth birthday is not a training
  course. Keeping a child away from something and then handing it over whole is
  how you get the cliff edge, and the cliff edge is the actual danger.
- **Incremental beats absolute.** A little more freedom at each stage, earned,
  with the settings easing as the judgement grows.
- **Screens can be good, used right.** Genuinely. Connection, making things,
  learning, and now AI. Pretending otherwise loses parents who can see their own
  children with their own eyes.
- **Fun, or it does not survive a Tuesday.** A system nobody enjoys gets dropped
  in a fortnight. Ours pays for real world jobs in stars and stars turn into
  screen time.
- **Safety through competence.** A child who can see the machinery, and who knows
  you are the first call, is safer than a child kept away from it and then
  dropped in.

This is the same position as the platform, in the family's voice rather than the
founder's. It does not need a new argument, only a kitchen table version of the
existing one.

## Facts we may state, and facts we may not

**May state, all confirmed.** Alma born 2015. Cake toppers started in a bedroom.
Grew through supplies to events to weddings and big birthdays. Own shop in
Bristol. Three children: Alma, Olga, Teo. Teo is four and mad about football.
The Covid babysitter admission. Built first for our own family. The ban lands at
16.

**May not state without checking.** Turnover, order numbers, follower milestones,
how many families use the product, any outcome claim about children's wellbeing,
any claim that the product improves mental health, and any clinician's name
attached to our advice. That last one is settled: on 29 July 2026 the clinicians
came off the parent facing advice deliberately, and social copy is parent facing.

**Published academic citations are fine** and stay: Odgers, Orben, Przybylski,
Livingstone. Cite the research, never imply a person endorses us.

**The Baby Einstein rule applies to every post.** We never claim developmental
outcomes. We claim calmer homes and a calibrated pathway. Those we can stand
behind.

**Never relitigate the ban, in either direction.** This is a standing rule from
the content engine and it applies here too. The legislation appears only as
settled background fact when it is genuinely needed. The conversation we own is
preparation and capability. A post arguing for or against the ban is a post we
have already lost, because it puts the platform in the main character seat.

## One naming trap to avoid

Alma, Olga and Teo were also the names of the **original cartoon characters** in
the product. They were renamed to Oliver, Zara and Sofia, and that squad has
since been superseded by DiGi and the Planet Friends: Pebble, Bloop, Orbit, Nova
and Cosmo. See `digi-squad/README.md`.

So: **our children's real names belong to the founding story only.** Never
present Alma, Olga or Teo as characters a child meets in the app, because they
are not, and a parent who goes looking for them will not find them. The story is
about three real children. The app has a golden star and five Planet Friends.
