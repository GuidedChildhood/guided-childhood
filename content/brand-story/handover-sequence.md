# The first edition: the handover sequence

**Six posts over two weeks. Paste ready. No dashes anywhere.**

This is the only time the four day rhythm bends. The principle: **explain before
you change, not after.** People forgive a rename they were told about and resent
one they were not.

Replace `[NARRATOR]` with the name the 6,000 already know before anything goes
out. Instagram versions are below; Facebook versions run longer, warmer, and may
carry a real link in the body.

## Post 2 · Where it started

> Alma was born in 2015 and I could not sit still.
>
> So I started making cake toppers on a laptop in our bedroom, at night, while
> she slept. Personalised ones. The kind where somebody's name is cut out
> properly and it looks like a person bothered.
>

>

>
> Then we opened our own shop in Bristol. I still cannot completely explain how

>
> I loved all of it. I want to be clear about that, because of what comes next

>
> We just had another child, and then a third, and something started happening in
> our house that I did not have a single tool for.
>
> More on Friday.

## Post 3 · The bit we do not usually say

**Instagram. Single image, a real one: a child from behind, a tablet, an
ordinary living room. No faces.**

**This is the big one. Do not run it early and do not dress it up.**

> With Alma it never really came up. With Olga it did.
>
> YouTube turned into a battle. Not a small one either. And it was not just about
> the screen, it was that the mood of the entire house moved with it. The tone of
> the whole afternoon depended on how the handover went at five o'clock.
>
> Then Covid happened.
>
> Two little girls at home. A business that had to keep going because it was our
> living. Orders that still had to go out. And I am going to say the thing that
> most parents I know did and almost none of us say out loud.
>
> We used YouTube as a babysitter. To survive.
>
> Not every day. But enough. And I felt guilty about it every single time, and
> then I felt guilty about feeling guilty, because what exactly was the
> alternative that week.
>
> I am not going to finish this by blaming YouTube. It was doing a job that
> genuinely needed doing. I needed those twenty minutes and there was nothing
> else offering them.
>
> And I am not going to finish it by saying it was all fine either, because if I
> thought that we would not have built anything.
>
> Here is what I actually think, and it took me about three years to be able to
> say it this plainly. The tool was fine at the job I was using it for. Nobody
> had built the version of that tool that also taught them anything, or gave the
> time back on purpose instead of by accident, or made switching it off the start
> of something rather than a fight.
>
> So we built that. That is what this account is about to become.
>
> Did you do the same thing in 2020. You can tell me. Nobody here is judging.

**Facebook variant.** Runs longer. Add two or three more sentences of specific

specific and the long.

**Reply plan.** This post will get replies from people admitting the same thing.
Answer every single one, personally, that week. That is the actual work of this
post and it matters more than the post.

## Post 4 · What we did about it

**Instagram carousel, 5 cards. Photo of the kitchen table.**

> The idea did not arrive as a business plan. It arrived at about nine at night,
> at the kitchen table, after one of those days.
>
> My husband and I started building a way to manage the devices in our own house.
> Not to switch them off. To manage them, which is a completely different job and
> much harder.
>
> Then it got harder again. Social media arrived on top of the devices. More
> screens, more accounts, more places to keep half an eye on.
>
> And now there is a ban coming at sixteen, which means for a lot of children it
> will be nothing, nothing, nothing, and then everything all at once, with no
> practice at any of it.
>
> That is the bit that worries me. Not the screens. The cliff edge.
>
> Teo is four and mad about football. By the time he is sixteen the road will
> have changed again, because of AI if nothing else. I cannot keep him off that
> road until his birthday and then wish him luck.
>
> So we did not build a blocker and we did not build a ban. We built the bit in
> the middle that nobody was building. A little more freedom at each stage,
> earned, with the settings easing as the judgement grows.
>
> Not a ban. Not no ban. The bit in between.
>
> What worries you more. The screens now, or the day they get handed everything.

**Card breakdown.**
1. "Nine at night. Kitchen table."
2. "We tried to manage the devices, not switch them off."
3. "Then social media. Then a ban at sixteen."
4. "Nothing, nothing, nothing, then everything." The cliff edge card.
5. "Not a ban. Not no ban. The bit in between."

## Post 6 · What it actually is

**The first ask of the whole sequence. Friday, week 2.**

**Instagram carousel, 7 cards. Real screens, real data, a child's name blurred.**

> Right. What we actually built.
>
> It is called Guided Childhood and it does five things, and they are all wired
> into each other, which is the whole point.
>
> It protects. The device settings get set once across every screen in the house,
> and then relaxed on purpose as they grow. Never a free for all. Never a
> lockdown.
>
> It reminds. The PE kit, the swimming bag, the week's jobs, the thing worth
> handling tonight.
>
> It sends them out. Real world jobs earn stars and stars turn into screen time,
> and the board pays the most for the things that get them off the sofa. Screen
> time that has already done its job before they sit down.
>
> It teaches. Short lessons that build actual judgement, spotting the advert,
> spotting the fake, understanding why the feed will not let go of them.
>
> And it guides you. There is an AI called DiGi for the eleven at night question,
> and it is not allowed to tell you to allow it or ban it. It gives you a
> pathway instead of a verdict, because a verdict just ends the conversation.
>
> Ten minutes a day. That is all it asks, and small enough to keep is the entire
> reason it works.
>
> If you want to know where your own child is on it, there are three questions in
> our bio. No sign up, takes about ninety seconds, and it tells you which of the
> five stages they are at and one thing to say tonight.
>
> Which one of those five would help most in your house right now.

**Card breakdown.** One card per verb: Protects, Reminds, Sends them out,
Teaches, Guides you. Then a card for "Ten minutes a day". Then the three
questions card.

**Facebook variant.** Same, with `guidedchildhood.co.uk/starter-pack` as a real
