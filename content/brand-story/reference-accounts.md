# Reference accounts: what performs, and what we take

**Written 7 September 2026.** Justin named two Instagram accounts and asked what
performs on them so we can use it on ours: `dr_catherine_knibbs` and
`family_it_guy`. This is the teardown and the playbook that comes out of it.

**Read this alongside `weekly-rhythm.md`, not instead of it.** The four anchor
days do not change. What changes is the anatomy inside them.

**How this file was built, honestly.** Instagram is blocked by this session's
network egress policy, and so are both of their own websites. So nothing here is
a scrape of their grid and there are no engagement numbers we measured
ourselves. What is here comes from their public presence everywhere else:
their sites as indexed, their podcasts, their publishers, their speaker
profiles, and several of their post captions recovered in full. Every follower
count is as reported by a third party, not counted by us. Section 10 lists what
could not be verified, so nobody builds on sand. Anything in this file that
becomes a public claim goes through the claim check first.

**The quotes in sections 3 and 4 are theirs, verbatim, dashes and all. Nothing
in our own copy ever carries one.**

## 1. The two accounts, in one line each

**Ben Gillenwater, `family_it_guy`.** A US cybersecurity dad who sells the fix.
Around 197 thousand on Instagram and around 296 thousand on TikTok as reported.
Thirty years in security, work with the NSA, Chief Technologist of a ten billion
dollar IT company, testimony to the US Senate in 2019, co-author of a child
device protection bill now law in Utah and Alabama, and an appearance in the
documentary Childhood 2.0. Three stated pillars: block harmful content, limit
screen time, keep strangers away.

**Dr Catherine Knibbs, `dr_catherine_knibbs`.** A UK child and adult trauma
psychotherapist who sells the reframe. UKCP accredited, a PhD, FRSA, a TEDx talk
called Bodies, Brains and Technology, seven books, and the person who named,
defined and built the clinical framework for CYBERTRAUMA. Individual videos
reported at three to six million views. Her Penguin book, Tech-Smart Parenting,
argues that banning screens does not work and that we are asking the wrong
question.

## 2. The finding that matters most

**They are the two halves of our own thesis, split apart, each running as a
business. Neither one is running the whole thing.**

Gillenwater sells the fix without the pathway. Every post ends at a setting: a
router filter, a PIN, a control toggled off. It works tonight and it teaches the
child nothing for the morning after. That is precisely the critique already
written into THE-STORY.md section 2, where parents review the blocking tools as
"a huge waste of money time and energy", except he is the human, likeable,
extremely competent version of it, which makes it far more persuasive than
Qustodio ever was.

Knibbs sells the reframe without the fix. Her argument is our philosophy almost
word for word: bans do not work, unfair rules push children away, connection is
what protects them. A parent finishes one of her videos with a truer picture of
the world and still no idea what to say at five o'clock tonight.

**Guided Childhood is the join.** The reframe with a fix attached, and a fix the
child grows out of. Ten minutes a day, parent and child together, calibrated
pathway instead of allow or deny. That is the gap between these two accounts and
it is the whole product.

Two consequences worth naming:

- **The audience is proven twice over.** Nobody has to be convinced that a UK
  parent will follow an evidence led, calm, anti panic account about children
  and screens. Knibbs did three to six million views on that exact position.
  What is unproven is nothing about the market. It is only our execution.
- **Our Friday is a direct head to head.** Service 12 in the service map,
  settings scattered across five devices, is Gillenwater's entire business in
  one row. We do not win that on depth of settings knowledge, because he has
  thirty years of it. We win it by being the only one whose settings post ends
  with the child taking a step, not the parent locking a door.

## 3. Ben Gillenwater, `family_it_guy`

### The engine

Fear plus a fix, inside the same thirty seconds. He names a specific danger on a
specific named platform, shows the actual screen, and hands over the toggle. The
platform is always the main character.

His recurring specifics: Roblox above all, then YouTube Kids, Snapchat, TikTok,
Instagram privacy settings, Google Family Link, router level filtering, and
NextDNS behind a PIN so the filter survives a child switching to mobile data.

### The caption anatomy, from a real one

His most transferable caption is the one about posting photographs of children.
Verbatim:

> I get it—posting cute photos of our kids online feels harmless. But here's why
> I don't do it:
> 1. Consent. If I wouldn't want embarrassing photos of me posted, why would I do
> it to my child?
> 2. Predators can take and use those images in ways you'd never want.
> 3. Every post builds your child's digital footprint before they can choose for
> themselves.
> 4. Privacy matters—kids deserve control over their own story.
> 5. Scammers use details in photos (birthdays, schools, names) for identity
> theft.
> Once a photo is online, you lose control of where it goes. That's why I choose
> not to share.

Four moves, and all four transfer:

1. **The concession is line one.** "I get it, this feels harmless." He agrees
   with the reader before he disagrees with them. We already have the honest
   pivot in Research Wednesday, but ours sits at step three. His sits at step
   one, and that is why nobody bounces off it.
2. **Five numbered reasons, one line each.** Scannable, screenshottable,
   saveable. Saves are the metric that moves an account.
3. **The close is a consequence, not a command.** "Once a photo is online, you
   lose control of where it goes."
4. **First person, his house.** "That's why I choose not to share." Not "parents
   should". He never once instructs. He reports what he does.

The same move again on Roblox: "Here's why I recommend keeping younger kids off
Roblox." Not a rule. A recommendation with a reason attached.

### The stat move

> Roblox has over 40 million daily child users — more than the entire population
> of kids in California.

A number nobody can feel, then a physical comparison that makes it feelable. We
have numbers exactly this size sitting unused in the positive canon and on the
homepage audience research.

### The funnel

A free resource library that is really a set of lead magnets: a predator warning
signs guide, age banded conversation prompts, privacy checklists per platform,
an explainer on how algorithms work, a digital footprint audit, and a family
internet agreement template. Then a podcast. Then the credibility stack.

**We already have the better version of the last one.** Our family phone
agreement printable exists at `content/printables/family-phone-agreement/`,
including a phone sized version. His is a template. Ours is a designed printable
attached to a product that keeps going after the agreement is signed.

### What we take

The concession first line. The numbered five. The consequence close. The first
person report from our own house. The physical comparison on any number. The
screen recording as the default Friday format, which our own rhythm already
prefers and which we should now stop treating as the alternative to a carousel.

### What we never take

His register. "Predators pose as kids to groom and exploit. Parents, it's time
to dig deeper" is his engine and it fails two of our checks at once. It is
panic, which Research Wednesday forbids. And it makes the platform the main
character, which is the exact thing the hidden thread test exists to catch. We
take his format and leave his fear.

## 4. Dr Catherine Knibbs, `dr_catherine_knibbs`

### The engine

The reframe. She does not answer the parent's question, she replaces it. The
recurring shape is: you have been told X, the science does not say X, here is
what it does say, and the thing you are frightened of is not the thing that
matters.

Her clearest public statement of it is the Tech-Smart Parenting position:
banning screens does not work, we are asking the wrong question, and bans plus
rules a child considers unfair push the child away at the exact moment
connection is the thing protecting them.

**That is our philosophy 2 and 3, published by Penguin, doing millions of
views.** It is the strongest external evidence we have that the calm position
outperforms the panic position with UK parents.

### The caption anatomy, from a real one

Her book announcement caption, recovered in full, is a masterclass. The moves,
in her order:

1. **A share instruction as the very first line.** "Share this with a parent 🚨"
   Before the hook, before anything. Not a CTA at the end. An instruction at the
   top, aimed at the one signal that travels furthest.
2. **Name the feeling, then peg it to this week.** "Parents are terrified about
   the impact of technology... from mental health concerns, to the issues raised
   in the new smash TV show 'Adolescence'". She hooks the post to whatever
   parents are already talking about.
3. **The authority stack, once, in a subordinate clause.** "After decades in
   this field, both researching and working on the 'front lines' as a child
   therapist".
4. **The contrarian turn.** "it's clear to me that we're getting a lot of things
   wrong when it comes to children and technology."
5. **A comment gate.** "Comment the word 'Book' on this video and I'll send you
   a personal invite to pre-order." Then, and only then, the bio link.
6. **The questions parents actually ask, verbatim.** "What age should I let my
   child have a smartphone? What games are safe? Can screen use damage my
   child's growing brain?"

That last one is worth stealing on its own. It is a content calendar written by
the audience, and it is the same language our own
`research/homepage-audience-language.md` sweep found on Mumsnet. Two independent
sources landing on the same sentences is as close to certainty as this gets.

### The special case: she is already inside our product

This is the one place where "use it for my own" needs a hard line drawn, because
Knibbs is not just a reference account to us. She is already in the codebase:
inside DiGi's system prompts, inside the research bank, and behind a whole
LinkedIn series at `content/packs/2026-08-18-cybertrauma-series/`.

The rule was settled on 29 July 2026 and it does not move:

- **Published academic work: cite it freely.** That is what the LinkedIn series
  does and it is defensible.
- **Her name next to our advice: never.** A living clinician's name beside advice
  inside a paid product reads as endorsement whether we mean it or not. Parent
  facing badges say "our research team". Migration 123 stripped the names.
- **Inside system prompts: kept, because a parent never sees them.**

So on the family account we take her structure and we never take her name. No
"as Catherine Knibbs says". No tagging her into a post to borrow reach. If a
Wednesday needs her argument, it cites the published work or it makes the
argument on our own evidence.

### What we take

The share instruction on line one. The peg to this week's conversation. The
contrarian turn stated plainly. The comment gate. The parents' own questions as
the source of topics. And the confidence to lead with the reframe rather than
easing into it.

### What we never take

The single expert authority stack. She has a PhD, seven books and a TEDx. Justin
does not have that and the family account is not the place to compete for it.
Our voice is Natalia and Justin, "we", the family, and our authority is that we
live it and we built it. Her account is closer to what our LinkedIn already
does, which is why the answer is not to copy her onto Instagram wholesale. The
family account's edge is recognition. Authority is Justin's lane, elsewhere.

## 6. What this changes on each anchor day

**Status: approved by Justin on 7 September 2026 and applied.**
`weekly-rhythm.md` now carries the Wednesday structure and the Friday format,
and it is the canon. This section is the record of where they came from, not a
second source of truth. If the two ever disagree, `weekly-rhythm.md` is right.

**Monday, Founder Monday.** Almost nothing changes, and that is the right answer.
Neither reference account has a founder story, because neither has a family
willing to be in it. Monday is the day we cannot be copied. Keep it exactly as
`weekly-rhythm.md` has it, no CTA, real photo, close on a question.

**Wednesday, Research Wednesday.** Two changes. The honest pivot moves to line
one as a concession. And a share instruction goes above it. The structure
becomes: share instruction, concession, the claim they have heard, what the
research found, the tonight line, a question.

**Friday, Service Friday.** The screen recording becomes the default and the
carousel the exception. Real screen, real data, a real child's name blurred.
Caption takes the numbered five and the consequence close. Proof path stays at
the bottom. When Friday runs service 12, settings scattered across five devices,
it is answering Gillenwater directly, so it must end on the child's step, not on
a toggle.

**Saturday, Happy News Saturday.** Unchanged in format. This is the day that
carries the whole thesis by showing rather than arguing, and neither reference
account has anything like it. Gillenwater cannot run it without contradicting
his own engine.

**Stories.** Both of them convert in DMs, not in captions. Our Stories are
already where the link lives. The comment gate feeds them.

## 7. The comment gate, in full

This is the single highest leverage mechanic here and we are not using it.

It works because it obeys the constraint we already have. Instagram gets no link
in the caption, bio only. A comment gate converts without a link: the reader
comments a word, we reply in DMs with the thing.

**The mechanics.**
1. Pick one word. Short, spellable, on brand. "AGREEMENT" for the family phone
   agreement. "SCRIPT" for the words for a specific argument. "PACK" for the
   starter pack.
2. Ask for it once, plainly, near the end of the caption, in the family voice.
   Not "comment below to unlock". Something Natalia would actually say.
3. Reply to every comment personally at first. The skill file is explicit that
   the replies are the actual work and do not get delegated to a model.
4. Send the thing, then one line about where the rest of it lives.

**The destinations, all verified today.** `/starter-pack` renders at
`app/(marketing)/starter-pack/page.tsx`, 1172 lines, no coming soon anywhere in
it. `/join` renders at `app/(marketing)/join/`. The family phone agreement
printable is at `content/printables/family-phone-agreement/`, with a phone sized
version. Nothing here is a promise we cannot keep.

**The rule that keeps it honest.** One gate per week at most, and it goes on
Friday, which is the day that already carries the ask. The ask ratio in
`weekly-rhythm.md` is roughly one post in five and this does not change it.

## 8. Never copy list

1. **The fear engine.** No predator led hooks, no scary headline screenshots, no
   "parents, it's time to dig deeper". Fails the no panic rule and the hidden
   thread test in one move.
2. **Em dashes.** Both of them use them in almost every caption. Non negotiable
   number four. Ages as "4 to 16". Restructure the sentence, never swap in a
   different dash.
3. **Hashtag stacks.** "#ProtectKidsOnline #ParentAlert #DigitalParenting" is
   their norm. Ours: occasional on Instagram, never on Facebook.
4. **Emoji sirens in evidence posts.** The 🚨 that opens her caption is
   effective and it is not us on a Wednesday.
5. **Her name attached to our advice.** Settled 29 July 2026. Cite published
   work. Never imply endorsement. Never tag to borrow reach.
6. **The single expert authority stack.** Not available to us and not our lane.
7. **Any claim we cannot point at.** Their claim about a platform is checkable by
   anyone. Ours has to be too, which is why every Friday carries its proof path.
8. **Allow or deny, in any caption.** "Keep younger kids off Roblox" is his
   sentence and it can never be ours. Ours is where you are, the next step, and
   the words.

## 9. The first six weeks, and what to measure

The posted log is empty, so this lands before the first post rather than after a
hundred. That is lucky and it means the anatomy can be right from post one.

- **Weeks 1 and 2.** Run the handover sequence exactly as
  `handover-sequence.md` has it. Do not bolt any of this onto it. It is the
  introduction and it earns the right to everything after.
- **Week 3.** First Wednesday with the new anatomy: share instruction, concession
  on line one.
- **Week 4.** First Friday as a screen recording rather than a carousel, with
  the numbered five in the caption.
- **Week 5.** First comment gate. One word, on Friday, the family phone
  agreement.
- **Week 6.** First Wednesday pegged to whatever is actually in the news that
  week.

**What to measure, in order of what actually matters.** Saves and shares first,
because they are what carried both of these accounts. Then DM replies to the
comment gate, because that is the only number that connects to the product. Then
follows. Comments and likes last: they are the ones that feel good and predict
least.

**What would tell us this is wrong.** If the concession first line lowers saves
on two consecutive Wednesdays, put the pivot back at step three. If the comment
gate produces more comments than DM replies we can personally answer, it is too
big and it goes monthly instead of weekly.
