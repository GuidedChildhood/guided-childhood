## Story 1, the lead: the new online lessons every school must now teach

**Headline:** Deepfakes, AI chatbots and online scams are now on every school's timetable by law

**Standfirst:** Since September, schools in England have had to teach pupils
about deepfakes, AI chatbots, misogynistic content and gambling style games.
A local founder is offering schools a free pilot of lessons that need no
preparation.

From 1 September 2026, every school in England has had to follow new
government guidance on relationships, sex and health education. For the first
time it names the online harms children meet every day. Secondary pupils must
learn how common deepfakes are and how they are used to hurt people. They must
learn that AI chatbots can "create fake intimacy or offer harmful advice". The
guidance covers misogynistic and violent content online, scams, gambling and
gambling style features in games, and how adverts and misinformation are
aimed at them. Primary pupils learn why social media has a minimum age, and
where to go when something online upsets them.

Young people say the gap is real. In a poll of 1,001 sixteen and seventeen
year olds in England for the Sex Education Forum, published in December 2025,
72 percent said they had learned nothing, or not enough, about deepfakes.

Justin Phillips, a father of three whose wife is a teacher, built Guided
Childhood to get children ready for life online before they turn sixteen. At
school it is a full scheme of lessons. At home the same lessons carry on, with
a Digital Passport children fill in as they go, the words parents need for the
hard conversations, a school calendar that keeps track of letters and
homework, and a fair way to handle screen time that both sides can see. Home
and school teach the same thing. He has mapped every online requirement in the
new guidance, fifty seven of them, to lessons a class teacher can open and
teach with no preparation.

"We have done the preparation, so the teacher can do the teaching," he said.

## Story 2: what inspectors look for, and what happens if it is missing

**Headline:** What Ofsted now asks schools about phones and online safety

Since November 2025 Ofsted has inspected schools with report cards instead of
a single word grade. There is no overall grade any more. Safeguarding is
judged on its own, as met or not met.

Phones are the part inspectors ask about by name. Government guidance on
mobile phones in schools is now statutory, and inspectors talk to leaders about
their phone policy, check that staff and pupils understand it, and look at its
effect on learning, behaviour and wellbeing. Where phones are hurting
behaviour and leaders are not taking effective action, Ofsted says "it would
be likely that the 'expected standard' for attendance and behaviour will not
be met".

The online lessons are a legal duty too. The new guidance is statutory, so a
school must teach it and have a written policy, drawn up with parents.

Justin Phillips said: "Heads do not need a scare story. They need to know the
duty is real and that they can show what they teach."

## Story 3: who teaches it

**Headline:** Who teaches children about deepfakes?

The new guidance is written for governing bodies, head teachers, senior
leaders and teachers. Every school must have an up to date written policy, and
must consult parents when it writes and reviews it. That policy has to say who
teaches the subject. The guidance asks for it to be taught by staff with "the
knowledge, skills and confidence" to do it, and for staff to be trained in
safeguarding, because pupils may tell a teacher something during these
lessons. In most schools the policy sits with a subject lead, the safeguarding
lead handles the same harms when they happen to a child, and the lessons are
taught by class teachers and form tutors.

That is who Guided Childhood built for. Every lesson is written out in full:
what to say, the questions pupils ask, how to adapt it for pupils with special
educational needs or who are learning English, and a version that works on
paper with no screens. The subject lead gets the mapping to every requirement
and a year plan. The safeguarding lead gets the same harms listed against the
safeguarding guidance.
