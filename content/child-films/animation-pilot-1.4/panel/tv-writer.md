# 1.4 animation pilot: the children's television writer

Panel lens: children's TV writer. 18 November 2026. Read only review of the
live 1.4 (Isla, `1.4-release/isla-lines.json`), the remake script
(`parent-reaction-series/1.4-feeling/child-film-script.md` and `build/lines.json`)
and the 15 second animation test. Measured against the best UK preschool and
infant TV: a character the child roots for, something to shout, a joke that
comes back, and a story small enough to hold in one hand.

**The short verdict.** The remake is safe, clear and well made, and it is a
lecture with pictures. Pebble appears in it but nothing happens to Pebble.
There is no want, the problem is told to "you" in general, and the win is
missing: Pebble runs to an empty doorway and nobody is there to be told. The
running joke is set up in the first five seconds and never comes back. The
lesson is right. The fix is to make it Pebble's afternoon, let the child help
Pebble, and show the telling land.

---

## 1. Beat by beat: engagement

Scored as a four year old on a sofa would score it: are they leaning in,
joining in, or looking at the cat.

**1. Hello and the upside down picture.** The best idea in the script and it
is thrown away. DiGi solves the gag in the same breath it is shown ("Oh,
Pebble. Other way round."), so the child never gets to spot it. It also gives
Pebble's catchphrase to DiGi; the character bible gives it to Pebble. A hook
exists in the first ten seconds, but only as a picture, not as a question the
child answers. *Fix: let the child shout "upside down" before anyone fixes it.*

**2. The big breath.** A good ritual and children do join in with breathing,
but only when they can see a character do it. A still of a calm Pebble asks
nothing of them. *Fix: show both characters swell up and blow out, so the
child copies a body, not a voice.*

**3. Remember when (product only).** Fine and short. "We sent a kind message"
is abstract for a four year old; the live film's version (a question, an
answer, "a real person") was stronger because it asked something.

**4. Today's word, FEELING.** The first drift point. A list of four feeling
words over a still of cushions is a vocabulary lesson. Four year olds name
feelings by making faces, not by hearing words. *Fix: Pebble pulls each face
and the child copies it. This is the cheapest big engagement win in the film.*

**5. Screens are fun.** Fine, five seconds, warm. But Pebble wants nothing
here, so there is nothing for the story to put at risk.

**6. The uh oh feeling.** The tone is right: quiet, unsure, not frightened,
nothing shown. But it is told in the second person ("something on a screen
gives you"), so it is a general warning, not a moment in Pebble's day. A child
cares far more about what is happening to Pebble than about a rule for
themselves, and the bible's distancing technique says exactly this.

**7. In your body, and Pause 1.** Good. Pointing is real participation and the
card works. The segment ends well, but it could end better: on a small
cliffhanger ("what will Pebble do?") with a reassurance first.

**8 and 9. Turn it over, go and tell.** Clear instruction and the words on
screen help. But DiGi tells and Pebble obeys, and the child has nothing to do.
The single strongest device in this genre is missing: the child tells the
character what to do, and the character hears them. And at the end of beat 9
Pebble runs to a doorway and the film cuts away. **The win never happens.** No
one is told, so no one says "thank you for telling me", and that sentence is
the reason the film exists.

**10. The catchphrase.** Said once here. The live film had the chant three
times in big voices, which is the moment children remember; the remake lost
it. Across the remake the catchphrase lands about three times in total, but
only once as a join in.

**11. The High Five game.** The best participatory beat in the remake. "Ready?
Uh oh!" with a three second gap is exactly right. Keep it.

**Pause 2.** The right card, but placed after a game, so the promise arrives
cold. It lands far harder straight after the child has *seen* a grown up keep
it.

**12. Never in trouble.** The most important line in the film is DiGi talking
to camera over a still of a sofa. A second drift point, and the worst place
for one. "Even if you were watching something you weren't meant to" is a
grown up sentence; a five year old understands it when they watch it happen
to Pebble.

**13. The feeling gets smaller.** A lovely image (the cloud floating away).
Keep it, and let the characters blow it away with the breath from beat 2, so
the opening ritual pays off.

**14. Recap.** Fine.

**15. Goodbye.** Flat. The running joke never returns, so the film has no
laugh at the end and no reason to want the next one.

**Running joke: set up, never paid off. Small story: absent. Hook in ten
seconds: half there.**

What the live Isla film did better and should come back: the modelled
conversation ("Thank you for telling me. You are never in trouble for telling
me."), the three times chant, and "I'll wait" at the pause. What the remake
rightly removed: the live film describes the upsetting things ("a scary
picture pops up", "someone says something unkind in a game"). Keep them out.

## 2. What makes a child love Pebble and DiGi, and what is missing

Children love a character who wants something, gets something wrong, and is
fine about it. They love a character who notices *them*. And they love two
characters who are clearly fond of each other.

**Pebble has the raw material and the script does not use it.** The bible
already gives Pebble a running joke (things the wrong way round), a habit
(rocking on the spot, whole body), and a reaction to being wrong (delighted:
"Oh. Other way round."). In the remake Pebble is a still that looks pleased or
unsure. Missing:

* **A want.** One more song. Every child on the sofa has wanted one more song.
* **A small flaw that is forgivable.** Pebble presses for one more after DiGi
  said last one. This is not naughtiness for laughs; it is what makes "never
  in trouble, even then" mean something. It must be played gently and never
  punished, not even with a look.
* **A catchphrase the child says too.** "Oh! Other way round!" From the second
  time, DiGi asks the child to say it first.
* **Pebble's habit as storytelling.** Pebble is always rocking. When the uh oh
  feeling comes, Pebble stops. An animator can show that with no words, and
  the stillness tells a four year old something is wrong more kindly than any
  line could.
* **Noticing the child.** Pebble cups an ear and *hears* the child shout "Turn
  it over!" The child is now part of the story.

**DiGi is a narrator, not yet a character.** Warm, calm and kind, which is
right, but a guide with no quirk is a teacher. Missing:

* **A signature.** DiGi already says "Lovely" twice. Make it DiGi's word, said
  the same way each time with a twinkle in the points, so children wait for it.
* **A fun flaw.** DiGi gets over excited and twirls too much, wobbles, steadies
  itself: "Whoops. Too much twirl." The test's shot 3 shows the star squashing
  in its twirl; written in on purpose, that becomes character rather than a
  fault (regenerate any shot where it looks broken rather than dizzy).
* **A body.** DiGi can demonstrate "turn it over" by flipping its whole star
  face down and popping back up. Children will copy that gesture.
* **The grown up role, shown.** DiGi stops what it is doing, comes down to
  Pebble's height, and says "Thank you for telling me." That is the moment a
  child learns what a good grown up does, and it lets the parent watching hear
  the words too.

**A moment of fun between them.** A missed high five: DiGi floats too high,
Pebble jumps and misses, looks at the camera, DiGi floats lower, second go
lands with sparkles. And a star hug (two points wrapped round Pebble) after the
telling. Fondness is what makes children want to come back.

**Pebble's voice.** The bible lists Pebble's voice as "to be set" and says never
to infer one. The beat sheet below gives Pebble five very short lines. If Justin
would rather Pebble stay silent (the Hey Duggee model, where the narrator speaks
for a character who only makes sounds), every Pebble line can be mimed and DiGi
says it instead ("Pebble says: other way round!"). That is a decision for him,
and it is the only thing in this sheet that needs one.

## 3. Revised beat sheet for the animated film

About 4 and a half minutes in the product (with the product only beat), three
segments for the player. About 410 spoken words, a third fewer than the live
film's 626 in 5 minutes 7, because the time goes to action, gaps and the
child's voice instead. The clock times below are generous; the silent action is
what sets the real length. DIDGEE lines are Isla. Words in
(brackets) are action. GAP marks a held silence for the child to answer; no
music fills it, by Justin's rule, so the animation must keep moving through it.

**Two places.** Keep the world small so the animator builds it once: the
**living room** (rug, easel, sofa, low table, as in `u01`, `u08`, `r11`) and the
**kitchen**, through the warm doorway at the end of the hallway (`u09`).

**[KEY]** marks the ten shots worth full character animation (the README's
budget of about ten shots a film). The rest can be gentle motion. Three already
exist as tests: B1 (`u08`), B2 (`u09`) and C5 (`r11`).

**Tone map.** Bouncy until Pebble stops rocking in A6. Quiet from A6 through
B3: no jokes, no funny sounds, no twirl. Bouncy again from C1, once the cloud
has gone.

### SEGMENT A (about 2 minutes 20)

**A1. Cold open: the upside down painting** (0:00 to 0:20) [KEY]
(Living room. Pebble, rocking happily, hangs a painting on the little easel.
It is upside down: the sun at the bottom, the grass at the top. Pebble steps
back to admire it. Tilts sideways to look. Tilts further. Topples gently onto
its bottom. Two seconds of held silence.)
(DiGi floats down into frame from the top.)
DIDGEE: Hello! It's Didgee. And this is Pebble.
DIDGEE: Pebble's done a painting. Hmm. Does it look right to you?
GAP 2s (DiGi turns to camera, one point held up by its face, listening.)
DIDGEE: It's upside down!
(Pebble looks at the camera, delighted, and spins the painting round.)
PEBBLE: Oh! Other way round!
DIDGEE: Lovely.
(DiGi's points twinkle. This twinkle comes every time DiGi says "Lovely".)

**A2. The big breath** (0:20 to 0:38)
(DiGi floats down onto the rug beside Pebble. Breathing in, DiGi swells up
rounder and Pebble's cheeks puff out, leaf lifting. Breathing out, DiGi
shrinks back with a little wobble and Pebble's breath makes the painting
flutter.)
DIDGEE: Big breath with us. Breathe in.
DIDGEE: And blow out slowly.
DIDGEE: One more. In. And out. Lovely.

**A3. Remember when** [PRODUCT ONLY] (0:38 to 0:48)
(Pebble holds up the little heart card from last time.)
DIDGEE: Last time, we sent a kind message. Today, it's all about feelings.

**A4. The special word, with faces** (0:48 to 1:15) [KEY]
(Four soft cushions on the rug, as in `u04`. WORD ON SCREEN: FEELING.)
DIDGEE: Today's special word is feeling. Say it with me. Feeling!
DIDGEE: Pebble's showing us some feelings. Can you make the faces too?
(Pebble hops onto the first cushion: an enormous grin.)
DIDGEE: Happy!
(Second cushion: bottom lip out, leaf droops.)
DIDGEE: Sad.
(Third cushion: eyes huge, leaf springs straight up.)
DIDGEE: Surprised!
(Fourth cushion: a huge yawn, eyes close, Pebble starts to sway asleep
standing up.)
DIDGEE: Sleepy. Pebble? Pebble!
(Pebble jolts awake, blinks at the camera.)
DIDGEE: All feelings are okay. Every single one.

**A5. One more song** (1:15 to 1:40)
(Pebble on the sofa with the tablet, music notes and a dancing cartoon on the
screen, as in `u05`. Pebble bounces in time. The song ends.)
DIDGEE: Pebble loves songs on the tablet. Most of the time, screens are fun.
(DiGi floats to the hallway, picks up a little watering can.)
DIDGEE: Last song, Pebble. I'm just in the kitchen.
(DiGi goes through the warm doorway. Pebble watches it go, looks at the
camera, holds up one finger: one more? Taps the screen.)
(From here the camera stays on Pebble's face. The screen is never seen again
from the front.)

**A6. The uh oh feeling** (1:40 to 2:05) [KEY] Quiet.
(Pebble's face, lit by the screen. The bouncing stops. Then the rocking
stops. Pebble is still. The leaf droops a little. One small arm goes to the
tummy, where a soft swirl appears. Cheeks go a bit pink. Unsure, not
frightened: no tears, no shaking.)
DIDGEE: Oh. Pebble's stopped bouncing.
DIDGEE: Something on the screen has given Pebble an uh oh feeling.
DIDGEE: It might be sad. Or scary. Or it just feels wrong.
DIDGEE: Pebble can feel it. A wobbly tummy. A hot face. A heart going fast.
DIDGEE: That's Pebble's body saying uh oh.

**A7. Reassure, then Pause 1** (2:05 to 2:20)
(DiGi peeks round the kitchen doorway and sends one small soft sparkle down
the hallway. Pebble does not see it. The child does.)
DIDGEE: Pebble's going to be okay. I'm right here.
DIDGEE: Pebble feels it in the tummy. (Pebble points to its tummy.)
DIDGEE: Pause me here. Where do you feel a wobbly feeling? Point to it.
CARD (live, ask1): Where do YOU feel a wobbly feeling? Point to it.

### SEGMENT B (about 1 minute 20)

**B1. Help Pebble: turn it over** (2:20 to 2:45) [KEY]
(DiGi in the kitchen doorway, close to camera. Behind, down the hallway,

DIDGEE: Welcome back. Pebble needs our help.
DIDGEE: When you get the uh oh feeling, there are two things to do.
DIDGEE: First, turn it over.
(DiGi flips its whole star face down, then pops back up.)
DIDGEE: Put the screen face down. Then you can't see it.
DIDGEE: Shall we tell Pebble? Big voice. Turn it over!
GAP 2s
(Pebble turns its head towards the camera and cups an ear: it heard the

`u08`. WORD ON SCREEN: Turn it over.)
DIDGEE: Pebble heard you!

**B2. Go and tell** (2:45 to 3:00) [KEY]
DIDGEE: Now the second thing. Go and tell your grown up.
DIDGEE: Tell Pebble! Go and tell!
GAP 2s
(Pebble hurries down the hallway, little legs pattering, the tablet left

**B3. The telling** (3:00 to 3:30) [KEY] Quiet. The centre of the film.
(The kitchen, warm light. DiGi is watering a plant. Pebble arrives and stops

PEBBLE: Didgee. I got the uh oh feeling.
(Pebble holds up one finger, sheepish: the one more song.)
DIDGEE: Thank you for telling me.
DIDGEE: You're not in trouble. Not one bit. Not even for one more song.

DIDGEE: Telling is always the right thing.

**B4. Pause 2** (3:30 to 3:40)
(DiGi turns to camera, still holding Pebble.)
DIDGEE: Grown up, now it's your turn. You have something important to say.
Pause me here.
CARD (live, ask2, the grown up says it): If a screen ever gives you the uh oh
feeling, you can always come to me. You will never be in trouble.

### SEGMENT C (about 1 minute 10)

**C1. The feeling gets smaller** (3:40 to 3:58)
(A small soft grey cloud floats above Pebble's head. DiGi and Pebble look up

DIDGEE: When you tell, the uh oh feeling gets smaller. Your grown up helps you

DIDGEE: Shall we blow it away? Big breath, Pebble. In. And blow.

DIDGEE: Lovely.

**C2. The chant, three times, with actions** (3:58 to 4:15) [KEY]
(Back on the rug. Pebble and DiGi face the camera.)
DIDGEE: Now our special sentence. Do it with us. Hands flat, and flip them over.
DIDGEE AND PEBBLE: Turn it over. Go and tell!
(Pebble flips its hands, runs on the spot. DiGi flips face down, pops up.)
DIDGEE: Louder!
DIDGEE AND PEBBLE: Turn it over. Go and tell!
DIDGEE: Biggest voice!
DIDGEE AND PEBBLE: Turn it over. Go and tell!
(DiGi pops up so hard it twirls, wobbles dizzily, steadies itself.)
DIDGEE: Whoops. Too much twirl.

**C3. The High Five game** (4:15 to 4:35) [KEY]
(DiGi floats up high. Pebble flips its hands, runs, jumps, misses. Pebble
looks at the camera. DiGi floats lower. Pebble runs again, jumps: high five,
sparkles, as in `r11`.)
DIDGEE: Tonight, play the high five game. Your grown up says uh oh. You turn
your hands over, run to them, and high five!
DIDGEE: Let's practise. Ready? Uh oh!
GAP 3s (Pebble and DiGi do it again on screen while the child does it on the
sofa.)

**C4. Recap** (4:35 to 4:42)
(Quick cuts: Pebble's hand on its tummy, the tablet face down, Pebble running
down the hallway.)
DIDGEE: So. Notice the uh oh feeling. Turn it over. Go and tell!

**C5. Goodbye, and the joke comes home** (4:42 to 4:55)
(Pebble waves goodbye, facing the wrong way, back to the camera, waving at
the wall.)
DIDGEE: Goodbye from Didgee. And from Pebble. Pebble?
DIDGEE: What do we say?
GAP 1.5s
PEBBLE: Oh! Other way round!
(Pebble turns, beams, waves at the camera. DiGi twinkles.)
DIDGEE: See you next time.

No line after this. No promise to the child or the grown up, matching the
remake.

**What this keeps from the brief.** FEELING said and shown in A4. "Turn it
over. Go and tell!" in full four times (the three times chant and the recap),
and each half shouted on its own by the child in B1 and B2. Pause 1 asks where the child feels it; Pause 2 is still the grown
up's promise, now arriving straight after the child has seen DiGi keep it.
Never in trouble, even when a rule was bent, is shown, not explained. The
upsetting thing is never shown or described.

**Public cuts.** The portrait cut for the family account writes itself from
A5, A6, B1, B2 and B3: about 75 seconds with a want, a problem, two shouts and
a win, which is a far stronger short than the remake's sequence of instructions.

## 4. Three things to avoid

1. **DiGi explaining over a Pebble who just stands there.** If you mute Isla
   and a four year old can still follow what happens to Pebble, the film
   works. Every lesson line needs something Pebble *does*. No DiGi speech
   longer than two short sentences without a cut or an action.

2. **Any joke, funny sound or big reaction near the uh oh and the telling.**
   The fun stops when Pebble stops rocking (A6) and comes back only when the
   cloud has gone (C1). No twirl, no squash, no camera move towards the screen,
   and no reaction from Pebble big enough to make a child imagine something
   terrible. Unsure is enough. This is the bible's first rule, and it is also
   good television: the quiet is what makes the hug land.

3. **Letting the running joke touch the routine, or the two turns blur.**
   Pebble never turns the tablet the wrong way, never gets the lesson wrong,
   and is never told off for the one more song, not even with a look. "Other
   way round" is a spin (the object turns round, upright again); "turn it
   over" is a flat flip, face down. Animate them as two clearly different
   gestures so a four year old never mixes them up.
