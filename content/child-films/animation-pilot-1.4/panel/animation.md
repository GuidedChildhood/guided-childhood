# 1.4 animation pilot: the animation director's lens

Panel lens 4 of 4, 18 November 2026. Read only: nothing generated, nothing
spent. Built on the 15 second test (`../../animation-test-2026-11-18/`), the
remake script (`../../parent-reaction-series/1.4-feeling/child-film-script.md`
and `build/lines.json`) and the stills in `build/pics/` of 1.4 and 1.5.

## First, one thing the panel needs to know about length

The remake runs about **3 minutes 5 seconds**, not 4 to 5. Its 23 child lines
add up to 174 seconds as recorded (Tamsin, rate minus 10), and the built
product film is 2:56 plus the two card holds. The 4:59 version is the original
51 line Isla script in `1.4-release/`, which is the old pencil look and a
different script. So either the film is a 3 minute film (fine for ages 4 to 7,
and the pause cards stretch the real watch to 4 or more), or the writer lens
adds beats. This shot list follows the remake as it stands. What an added
minute costs is at the end of section 4.

The remake's `lines.json` still names Tamsin. Every line needs recording again
in Isla before any timing below is final. Times here are Tamsin's, so read
them as about.

## How the three tiers work in this film

- **FULL**: Kling 3.0 pro, 5 seconds, silent, animated from one approved still
  as the start frame. 8.75 credits a shot.
- **LIGHT**: motion made in code on a still, no credits. What code can honestly
  do on a flat still is a push in, a slow pan, a dissolve between two stills, a
  glow pulse on DiGi, a crop to a close up, and code drawn layers (the grey
  scribble, music notes, the words). A real blink or a body bob needs either a
  second still or a cutout, so this plan does not promise them. Two free
  tricks: **the hold** (freeze the last frame of a FULL shot and keep pushing
  in, so a 5 second clip covers a 10 second line) and **the free close up**
  (the stills are 2752 by 1536, so a crop up to 1.4 times still beats 1920
  wide). A calm FULL clip played forward then back also gives a seamless idle
  of 10 seconds.
- **BROLL**: real footage, no credits.

Three rules decide the tier before cost does:

1. **A screen face in view means LIGHT, always.** No engine gets to animate a
   tablet screen, because a video model can invent things on it. The grey
   scribble is drawn in code on a blank pale grey screen, so nothing on that
   screen was ever generated. This is the safeguarding rule turned into a
   production rule.
2. **Motion that must land on a word is LIGHT.** Kling cannot hear Isla. The
   breath, the four feeling cushions and the cards need timing to the
   syllable, which only code gives.
3. **Cards stay still.** On the pause cards the words are the job; motion
   behind them pulls the eye away.

## 2. B roll: where real footage genuinely helps

My honest view: most of this film is better animated. A real hallway, a real
sofa or a real room adds nothing a four year old needs, and it puts a real home
on screen. Mixing real and animated pictures can also blur real and pretend,
which is exactly what 1.5 teaches. So real footage goes in only where the child
has to **copy a physical action with their own hands**, because children this
age imitate real hands more readily than a character's paws, and because the
grown up watching learns the game in the same moment.

| Shot | What | Where | Length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Hands lay a switched off tablet face down on a wooden table | B2, "Put the screen face down" | 3 to 4 s |
| R2 | Small hands turn over like a screen, then a grown up's hand comes in for a high five | B5, "turn your hands over like a screen" | 4 s |

Optional, only if Justin wants it after seeing the cut: R2 again under the
goodbye line "Tonight, play the high five game with your grown up". I would
not add more.

**Where it comes from: filmed by the founder, not stock.** Stock tablets almost
always show a lit screen, stock hands come with rings, nails and props we do
not control, and stock "child with tablet" clips are exactly the generic look
the visual system bans. Two phone shots on a kitchen table cost nothing and
take ten minutes.

**The rules for every real shot:**

- No faces, ever. Framed from above or the side, hands and table only.
- The tablet is switched off for the whole shot, in a plain case, no logo in
  view. No screen showing anything, at any frame.
- Nothing that identifies a home or a person: no rings, watches, tattoos,
  letters, school jumpers, windows or family photos in frame.
- A child's hands only with the parent's consent, recorded, and checked against
  the privacy levels in `content/brand-story/founder-context.md`. If that is
  not a clear GREEN, two adult hands do R2, the smaller one in a lighter
  sleeve. That is still real, so it does not break the silent UGC rule; what
  would break it is a generated pair of hands presented as a real family's.
- Never a generated real looking person or real looking hands.
- Shot in daylight, landscape, 4K, held still on a surface, 50 or 60 frames a
  second so it can slow down.
- In the cut, each real shot sits inside a rounded cream frame (16 pixel radius
  and up) over the cream ground, so the child reads it as "this is how it looks
  at home", separate from Pebble's world. A one second dissolve in and out.

## 3. Continuity rules

What drifted in the stills already, seen side by side: the tablet is four
different tablets (white on a stool in u05, silver on a stool in u06, blue on a
coffee table in u08, slate on a stool in the hallway in u09), and the screen
story moves through three rooms in four beats. A four year old must see that
the screen that gave the uh oh feeling is the same one Pebble turns over. The
new stills n05, n06 and n09 exist to fix exactly that.

**One home.**
- **The hero living room** is u08: grey sofas, the wooden coffee table, the
  patterned rug, plants on the sill. Every screen beat (A6, A7, B1) happens
  here, at this table.
- **The hallway** is u09's, and in n09 the arch shows the living room so the
  child sees where Pebble came from.
- **The grown up's doorway** is the warm open door at the end of the hallway.
  The grown up is never drawn.
- **The series rooms** stay as they are, because they are the rituals every
  film repeats: p02 (the breath), p07 (the pause cushion), r11 (the high five),
  r12 (the sofa), r13 (the goodbye). They are allowed to be different rooms;
  the screen story is not.
- The opener playroom (u01) and the feelings rug (u04) stand alone and need no
  match.

**One tablet.** The blue cased tablet from u08. It lives on the coffee table and
stays there when Pebble leaves. Three states only: screen facing away with a
glow (A6), screen facing camera, pale grey with the code scribble (A7), face
down showing the blue back (B1 onwards). Never a phone, never a second device.

**Colour.**
- Pebble's body yellow is taken from `public/digi-squad/friends/pebble.png` and
  DiGi's gold from `public/digi-squad/DiGi-star-3d.png`. These two files go in
  as references on every new still.
- One grade for the whole film, applied in the build, never per shot.
- Every Kling prompt says to keep the exact colours and lighting of the start
  frame, and lists colour shift, darkening and brown as things to avoid.
- Any frame where Pebble darkens, as in test shot 2, fails the shot. The check
  in section 5 measures it.

**Scale.** Three shot sizes only: wide (Pebble about a third of frame height),
medium (about 60 percent), close (Pebble's face about half the frame). Pebble
stands a head taller than the coffee table. DiGi is one to one and a quarter
times Pebble's body width, floats level with Pebble's sprout or higher, and is
never below Pebble's eyes.

**Light.** Warm afternoon window light in the living room and hallway for the
whole screen story. The lamp light of p02 belongs to the breath ritual only.

**Motion vocabulary**, so the characters move like the same characters every
film:
- Pebble: rocks, bops, hurries with small steps, squashes and stretches (the
  test showed this works). Calm and unsure in A7, never frightened, no tears.
- DiGi: floats, bobs, tilts no more than a small amount, glows. **No spins, no
  twirls, no flips.** Test shot 3 shows the star losing its five points the
  moment it rotates.
- Camera: still, or one slow push. No orbits, no handheld wobble, no cuts
  inside a clip.

**The Kling prompt preamble**, the same opening on every shot:
> Animate this exact frame. Keep every colour, the lighting, the room and both
> characters exactly as they are. Camera still. One action only: [the action].
> Soft 3D, gentle, slow. No text, no letters, no people, no new objects, no
> screen content. The star keeps its five points and does not spin. Avoid:
> colour shift, darkening, brown tint, deformed shapes, extra limbs, flicker.

If the account's Kling 3.0 pro accepts an end frame as well as a start frame,
use it on A2 and B3: pinning both ends is the strongest guard against colour
drift. That is untested, so try it on the first shot only.

Engines: **Kling 3.0 pro silent is the engine for every FULL shot.** Kling 3.0
standard with sound is declined: generated sound can bring music or voices, and
the no music rule stands. Seedance 2.5 at 35 credits (720p) or 80 (1080p) is
four to nine times the price; it is parked as the fallback for a single shot
that fails Kling twice, never planned.

without breaking a rule: everything left LIGHT in (b) is LIGHT by design.

If the writer lens adds a minute: a LIGHT minute is 2 to 4 credits of stills;
a FULL minute is about 12 shots, 105 credits plus regenerations.

**Recommendation: (a), about 77 credits against a balance of about 86.** It
animates the moments a child must copy or will love (the joke turn, the body
cue, go and tell, the high five, the goodbye), keeps the test shot that already
works, and leaves the timed and safeguarding beats in code where they are
better anyway. Two reasons to stop there for now: the balance is shared with
every other session, so check it right before the first spend and drop C4 to
LIGHT (saving 8.75) if it has fallen under 80; and Justin should judge the
pilot with the sound on before anything bigger is bought.

Then step to (b) on a top up if the pilot lands. It gets cheaper film by film:
the high five, the sofa and the goodbye (r11, r12, r13) and the breath (p02)

## 5. The check for every shot, before it goes in the cut

**Before spending**
1. The start still passes by eye beside `pebble.png` and `DiGi-star-3d.png`:
   colour, sprout leaf, eyes, arm count, five points.
2. No words or letters anywhere in the still. Any screen in it is blank, pale
   grey, glow only, or face down.
3. The prompt starts with the preamble and names one action.

**After each render**
4. A contact sheet of a frame every half second, with the start still and the
   two reference cutouts in the first column (`build/sheet.sh` and the test's
   `work/sheet.png` are the pattern).
5. A colour check in code: in each frame, count the pixels inside Pebble's
   yellow band (hue, saturation and lightness taken from `pebble.png`) and the
   mean lightness of those pixels, against frame 0. Flag the shot if the count
   falls by more than a quarter or the lightness drops sharply mid shot. Test
   shot 2 would have failed this. The same check on DiGi's gold.
6. By eye, every frame on the sheet, eight questions:
   - Pebble on model in every frame: colour, leaf, eyes, two arms.
   - DiGi has five clear points in every frame.
   - The room has not changed: no furniture growing, melting or moving.
   - The tablet is the same tablet, and its screen never shows anything.
   - No invented text, letters or symbols anywhere.
   - No extra character, animal or person appears.
   - Pebble's feeling matches the line (unsure in A7, never scared).
   - The one action reads at a glance with the sound off.
7. The first and last frames are clean enough to cut on or to hold (no motion
   blur, eyes open unless the shot wants them closed).

**In the cut**
8. Watched in sequence with Isla's line, sound on, at full speed and again at
   half speed. The join from a FULL clip into its hold must not jump.
9. Every real shot checked frame by frame for a face, a lit screen, a logo or
   anything that identifies a home.

**The rules on failure**
10. A failed shot is regenerated once with the prompt tightened. If it fails a
    second time, the shot changes (simpler motion, or LIGHT) instead of more
    credits going in. Seedance only for a shot that matters too much to drop.
11. Every shot goes in a ledger beside the picture ledger: shot, start still,
    job id, prompt, pass or regenerate, and the reason.
12. Nothing goes in the app or on YouTube until Justin has watched the whole
    film with the sound on.
