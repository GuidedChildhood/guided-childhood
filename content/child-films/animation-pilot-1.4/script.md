# 1.4 animated: When screens make you sad (merged script, draft 1)

18 November 2026. Merged from the four lens panel in `panel/` (curriculum,
children's TV writer, safeguarding, animation director). The story and the
beats come from the TV writer. The fixes come from curriculum and
safeguarding. The tiers and the rules come from the animation director. Every
point was weighed, and the table at the end says what was adopted, adapted,
parked or declined, and why. **For Justin's approval. Nothing generated yet.**

Stage 1, ages 4 to 7. About 4 and a half minutes, in three segments for the
player. DiGi is Isla. No music. Keyword FEELING. Catchphrase "Turn it over. Go
and tell!" Both live pause cards are kept word for word, so the app needs no
card change.

## What the child learns (from the curriculum lens)

- **Knowing.** Most of the time screens are fun. Sometimes something on a
  screen, or something someone says, gives me an uh oh feeling or just seems
  wrong. Then I turn it over and go and tell. I have more than one grown up I
  can tell.
- **Feeling.** All my feelings are okay. I am never in trouble for telling,
  even if I was doing something I wasn't meant to, and even if someone said
  keep it a secret.
- **Doing.** I turn it over, find a grown up and say "I got the uh oh
  feeling." If they are busy, I tell them again or tell another grown up.

## Four rules for the animator (from safeguarding and animation)

1. **We never see a screen.** Every tablet is shot from behind, side on or
   face down, and no engine ever draws a screen.
2. **Pebble is unsure, never frightened.** The light stays warm. There are no
   tears, no push in on the tablet, and no sound sting.
3. **Quiet from A6 to B3.** There are no jokes, twirls or funny sounds from
   the moment Pebble stops rocking until the cloud has gone.
4. **"Other way round" is a spin and "turn it over" is a flat flip.** They are
   two different gestures, so a child never mixes them up.

Tiers: **FULL** is Kling 3.0 pro, a 5 second shot animated from our own still
(8.75 credits). **LIGHT** is code motion on a still (free), used wherever the
motion must land on a word or a screen is in view. **[T]** marks a shot the
test already made.

## SEGMENT A (about 2 minutes 20)

**A1. The upside down painting** FULL
(Living room. Pebble, rocking happily, hangs a painting on the little easel,
upside down. Steps back. Tilts. Tilts further. Topples gently onto its bottom.)
DIDGEE: Hello! It's Didgee. And this is Pebble.
DIDGEE: Pebble's done a painting. Hmm. Does it look right to you?
(GAP 2s. DiGi holds a point to its face, listening.)
DIDGEE: It's upside down!
(Pebble spins the painting round, delighted.)
PEBBLE: Oh! Other way round!
DIDGEE: Lovely.

**A2. The big breath** LIGHT (timed to the words)
DIDGEE: Big breath with us. Breathe in. And blow out slowly.
DIDGEE: One more. In. And out. Lovely.

**A3. Remember when** [PRODUCT ONLY] LIGHT
DIDGEE: Last time, we sent a kind message. Today, it's all about feelings.

**A4. The special word, with faces** LIGHT (four new stills, one per face)
WORD ON SCREEN: FEELING
DIDGEE: Today's special word is feeling. Say it with me. Feeling!
DIDGEE: Pebble's showing us some feelings. Can you make the faces too?
DIDGEE: Happy! Sad. Surprised! Sleepy. Pebble? Pebble!
DIDGEE: All feelings are okay. Every single one.

**A5. One more song** LIGHT (the tablet seen from behind, notes floating up out of it)
DIDGEE: Pebble loves songs on the tablet. Most of the time, screens are fun.
DIDGEE: Last song, Pebble. I'm just in the kitchen.
(DiGi goes through the warm doorway. Pebble holds up one finger to the camera: one more? Taps.)

**A6. The uh oh feeling** FULL. Quiet.
(Pebble's face only. The bouncing stops, then the rocking stops. The leaf droops a little. One arm goes to the tummy. Unsure, not frightened.)
DIDGEE: Oh. Pebble's stopped bouncing.
DIDGEE: Something on the screen has given Pebble an uh oh feeling.
DIDGEE: It might be sad, or scary, or someone being unkind. Or it just feels wrong.
DIDGEE: A wobbly tummy. A hot face. A heart going fast.
DIDGEE: That might be Pebble's body saying uh oh.

**A7. Not sure? Still tell. Then Pause 1** LIGHT
(DiGi peeks round the kitchen doorway and sends one soft sparkle down the hallway.)
DIDGEE: And if your tummy says nothing, but something still seems wrong, you can still turn it over and tell.
DIDGEE: Pebble's going to be okay. I'm right here.
DIDGEE: Pause me here. Where do you feel a wobbly feeling? Point to it.
CARD (live, ask1): Where do YOU feel a wobbly feeling? Point to it.

## SEGMENT B (about 1 minute 30)

**B1. Help Pebble: turn it over** FULL [T] (test shot 1, Pebble lays the tablet face down)
DIDGEE: Welcome back. Pebble needs our help. There are two things to do.
DIDGEE: First, turn it over. Put the screen face down. Then you can't see it.
DIDGEE: Shall we tell Pebble? Big voice. Turn it over!
(GAP 2s. Pebble cups an ear, hears the child, lays the tablet face down. WORD ON SCREEN: Turn it over.)
DIDGEE: Pebble heard you!

**B2. Go and tell** FULL (test shot 2 made again: it had a colour shift)
DIDGEE: Now the second thing. Go and tell your grown up.
DIDGEE: Tell Pebble! Go and tell!
(GAP 2s. Pebble hurries down the hallway towards the warm open doorway, the tablet left face down. WORD ON SCREEN: Go and tell!)

**B3. The telling** FULL, two shots. Quiet. The centre of the film.
(The kitchen. DiGi is watering a plant, puts the can down at once, and floats down to Pebble's height.)
PEBBLE: Didgee. I got the uh oh feeling.
(Pebble holds up one finger, sheepish: the one more song.)
DIDGEE: Thank you for telling me.
DIDGEE: You're not in trouble. Not one bit. Not even for one more song. The uh oh thing wasn't your fault.
(A star hug. Hold it.)
DIDGEE: Telling is always the right thing. Even if someone said keep it a secret.

**B4. More than one grown up** LIGHT
DIDGEE: You don't have to explain it. Just say: I got the uh oh feeling.
DIDGEE: And if your grown up is busy, tell them again. Or tell another grown up you trust. At home, at school, anywhere.

**B5. Pause 2** LIGHT
DIDGEE: Grown up, now it's your turn. You have something important to say. Pause me here.
CARD (live, ask2, the grown up says it): If a screen ever gives you the uh oh feeling, you can always come to me. You will never be in trouble.

## SEGMENT C (about 1 minute)

**C1. The feeling can get smaller** LIGHT (code drawn cloud)
DIDGEE: Telling can help the uh oh feeling get smaller. Your grown up can help you carry it.
DIDGEE: Shall we blow it away? Big breath, Pebble. In. And blow.
(The cloud shrinks to a puff and drifts away. Pebble starts rocking again.)
DIDGEE: Lovely.

**C2. The chant, three times, with actions** FULL
DIDGEE: Now our special sentence. Do it with us. Hands flat, and flip them over.
DIDGEE AND PEBBLE: Turn it over. Go and tell!
DIDGEE: Louder!
DIDGEE AND PEBBLE: Turn it over. Go and tell!
DIDGEE: Biggest voice!
DIDGEE AND PEBBLE: Turn it over. Go and tell!
(DiGi pops up so hard it twirls, wobbles dizzily, and steadies itself.)
DIDGEE: Whoops. Too much twirl.

**C3. The High Five game** FULL (a missed high five, then the real one)
DIDGEE: Tonight, play the high five game. Your grown up says uh oh. You turn your hands over, hurry to them, and high five!
DIDGEE: Let's practise. Ready? Uh oh!
(GAP 3s. Pebble and DiGi play it on screen while the child plays it on the sofa.)

**C4. Show your grown up** LIGHT (quick cuts of the three moves)
DIDGEE: Show your grown up what we do. Notice the uh oh feeling. Turn it over. Go and tell!

**C5. Goodbye, the joke comes home** FULL
(Pebble waves goodbye facing the wall, back to the camera.)
DIDGEE: Goodbye from Didgee. And from Pebble. Pebble? What do we say?
(GAP 1.5s)
PEBBLE: Oh! Other way round!
(Pebble turns, beams and waves.)
DIDGEE: See you next time.

No line after this. No promise about the child.

## Parent note, beside the film (text, not narration; from safeguarding M4)

When they tell you: say thank you, stay calm, and look at the screen yourself
so they don't have to describe it. They are not in trouble, even if a rule was
broken. If someone was talking to them, don't delete anything: report it at
ceop.police.uk. If it was a sexual picture of a child, don't save or share it:
report the link to the IWF. If they are in danger now, call 999. Worried and
not sure? NSPCC helpline, 0808 800 5000. If you change anything afterwards,
like a setting or where the tablet lives, do it later and tell them why: to
keep them safe, never because they told you.

This goes into the lesson's `parent_note` only when the film goes into the
app. It is a database change, so it waits for Justin's yes.

## One decision for Justin

**Does Pebble speak?** The character bible leaves Pebble's voice unset. In
this script Pebble has three short lines, and the most important one is "I got
the uh oh feeling", which models the exact sentence a child should say. The
alternative is Pebble staying silent and DiGi saying "Pebble says: I got the
uh oh feeling." That is free, but the modelled sentence lands less strongly.
A Pebble voice needs a short voice test first, under 2 credits.
