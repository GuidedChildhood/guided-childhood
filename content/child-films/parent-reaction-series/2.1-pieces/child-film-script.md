# 2.1: One piece is never just one piece (strangers in games)

Child lesson film, Stage 2 (ages 8 to 10), watched with a grown up. The first
NEW film of the series, for subject group D in `../subjects-2026-11-16.md`
(strangers and contact in games: Family IT Guy's strongest topic). Built from
the approved script `content/lesson-scripts/stage-2-films/2.1-what-we-keep-private-online.md`
(v3) and the verified Roblox pack `content/packs/2026-11-13-roblox-pilot/pack.md`.
Draft 1, 17 November 2026. NOT RECORDED. Nothing generated.

## What it takes from 2.1, and what it adds

Kept from 2.1: the lesson (private information is collected in small pieces
and joined up), the keyword JIGSAW, the catchphrase "One piece is never just
one piece!", the piece test, the six things, the wrappers, "nah, not that one",
the Five Pieces game, and the close with Childline.

Added, from the Roblox pack: the one move worth knowing by name. A friendly
player asking you to chat somewhere else, or to keep it secret, is always a
check with your grown up moment (the pack's pattern: the game is where it
starts, the private app is where it happens).

Changed for the series standard: Bloop and DiGi in the house look, Tamsin's
voice, about 760 spoken words (2.1 ran to 915), no game or app named, no
closing promise beyond "never in trouble for telling".

## The lesson

- **Knowing**: private information is collected in small pieces and joined
  up. I can run the piece test, I know the six things, and I know that a
  friendly player asking to move somewhere else is a check moment.
- **Feeling**: clever and in charge, not watched or told off. Telling my
  grown up about something I already typed in is good news, never trouble.
- **Keyword**: JIGSAW
- **Catchphrase, three times**: "One piece is never just one piece!"
- **Routine practised**: the Five Pieces game.
- **Bloop's joke**: building a jigsaw, always one piece short. Pats its
  pockets, finds the piece somewhere daft at the very end.

## What it does not do

- Shows no game, no app and no real chat. Every screen is drawn, and every
  word on a screen is set in code, so nothing reads as a real product.
- Never says "stranger danger" or makes other players sound bad. Most people
  in a game are other children; the skill is spotting a piece and a move.
- No figures for children.

## SEGMENT A

**1. Hello** (q01)
PICTURE: A warm lamp lit living room. Bloop at a low table with a nearly finished jigsaw, one gap in it, patting its pockets. DiGi on the arm of the sofa. A tablet lies face down on a cushion.
DIDGEE: Hello, you two. Yes, both of you, grown up as well. And Bloop, who has lost a jigsaw piece again.
DIDGEE: By the end of this, you'll spot a trick that works on grown ups every day.

**2. Calm me** (q02)
PICTURE: Bloop and DiGi breathing slowly, a soft glow growing and shrinking.
DIDGEE: Three breaths, out longer than in. In, two, three, four. Out, two, three, four, five, six. Once more. This is a spotting job, and a fast brain walks straight past small things.

**3. Remember when** [PRODUCT ONLY] (q03)
PICTURE: Bloop looking at a small drawn shield pinned on the wall.
DIDGEE: Remember the shield? Your name, where you live, your school, your photos, all kept behind it. Tonight, things are asking to come out.

**4. Today's one thing** (q04)
WORD ON SCREEN: JIGSAW
PICTURE: Jigsaw pieces scattered on a table in warm light.
DIDGEE: Today's word is jigsaw. Small pieces that join up into a picture. And tonight, the picture is you.

**5. Teach: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday** (q05, q06, q07)
WORDS ON SCREEN (in code): MONDAY: "Put your real name on the leaderboard!" · TUESDAY: "What animal are you? Pop in your birthday!" · WEDNESDAY: "which school do you go to? my cousin might be there"
DIDGEE: Here's how it works. Monday. A game ends, and a box pops up: put your real name on the leaderboard. So you do.
DIDGEE: Tuesday. A quiz. What animal are you? Pop in your birthday. Otter.
DIDGEE: Wednesday. Someone in your team chat, nice to you for weeks, asks: which school do you go to? My cousin might be there. And you nearly answer, because it would feel rude not to.

**6. The pieces join** (q08)
PICTURE: Three glowing jigsaw pieces clicking together into one card with a small padlock opening on it.
DIDGEE: Three little things, and not one of them felt like anything. Now watch them join.
DIDGEE: Your name. Your birthday. Your school. That's enough to answer the little questions when someone taps forgot my password on your account. Somebody walking in through the front door.

**7. Nobody asks like this** (q09)
PICTURE: A cartoon pop up with three empty boxes, looking sheepish. Bloop walking off to find a grown up.
WORDS ON SCREEN: NOBODY ASKS LIKE THIS.
DIDGEE: Here's the bit to keep. Nobody asks for all three at once. You'd spot it straight away.
DIDGEE: So it comes one piece at a time, on different days, and every piece feels like nothing. That isn't you missing something. That's the trick, working as it was built. And a trick you can see has stopped working.

**Pause and ask 1** (q19)
CARD: Grown up, ask: "Which three pieces did DiGi join up, and what did they make?" · Older child: "Why is it cleverer to ask for three pieces in three places than all three at once?"
DIDGEE: Pause me here. Grown up, ask the question on the screen. I'll wait.

## SEGMENT B

**8. The piece test** (q10)
WORDS ON SCREEN: Could this help somebody FIND me? · GET INTO something of mine? · PRETEND to be me?
DIDGEE: So how do you catch a piece? Three questions. Could this help somebody find me? Get into something of mine? Pretend to be me? One yes, and it's a piece. Pieces stay on your side of the screen.

**9. The six things** (q11)
WORDS ON SCREEN: Full name · School · Address · Phone number · Passwords · A photo of where you live or learn
DIDGEE: Six things are always a yes, so you don't even need the test. Full name. School. Address. Phone number. Passwords. Any photo that shows where you live or learn.
DIDGEE: Sometimes they do have to go in somewhere, like a school account. That's fine, because a grown up is doing it with you. On your own, they stay out.

**10. The wrapping** (q12)
WORDS ON SCREEN: FREE COINS · WHAT ANIMAL ARE YOU? · you're really good at this
DIDGEE: A piece hardly ever looks like a question. It comes wrapped in something good. Free coins. A fun quiz. Somebody being really friendly. When something lovely turns up with a box underneath, that's your moment.

**11. The move** (q13, new to this film)
WORDS ON SCREEN: "let's chat somewhere else" · "don't tell anyone"
DIDGEE: And there's one move to know by name. Someone from a game says: let's chat somewhere else. Or: don't tell anyone.
DIDGEE: That's always a check with your grown up moment. Every time. Even if they've been lovely.

**12. Nah, not that one** (q14)
WORDS ON SCREEN: "nah, not that one. what's your best score?"
DIDGEE: You're not being rude, and you're not deciding alone. In a chat you can say: nah, not that one, what's your best score? A box, you just shut. Then you mention it to your grown up. That's checking, not telling on anyone.

**Pause and say 2** (q19)
CARD: Grown up, say these one at a time and let them call it, PIECE or NOT A PIECE: "my favourite dinner" · "the name of our street" · "my football team" · "the day I was born".
DIDGEE: Pause me again. Grown up, say a few things out loud, one at a time. Piece, or not a piece?

**13. Say it together** (q15)
WORDS ON SCREEN: One piece is never just one piece!
DIDGEE: Out loud, both of you. Grown up first. One piece is never just one piece!
DIDGEE: Again, together. One piece is never just one piece!
DIDGEE: Last one, properly loud. One piece is never just one piece!

## SEGMENT C

**14. Try it tonight** (q16)
DIDGEE: Tonight, play Five Pieces. Your grown up asks you five friendly questions. Some are pieces, some are just nice. A point for every piece you catch.
DIDGEE: Then swap, and see if you can catch them out. Grown ups, let yourself get caught. It's the best bit.

**15. If you already typed something** (q17)
WORDS ON SCREEN (stays to the end): Your grown up · A teacher · A nan or an auntie · Childline 0800 1111
DIDGEE: One last thing, and it matters most. If you're thinking, I've already put some of that in somewhere, then good. Go and tell a grown up you trust. Tonight, or next week.
DIDGEE: Usually that's the person sitting next to you. A teacher counts. A nan or an auntie counts. And Childline is free, on oh eight hundred, eleven eleven, any time.
DIDGEE: Nothing is ruined. Some of it you change, some you take down. And you will never, ever be in trouble for telling.

**16. Goodbye** (q18)
PICTURE: Bloop finds the missing jigsaw piece stuck to its own foot, pops it in, the picture complete. DiGi waves.
DIDGEE: Oh, Bloop. It was on your foot. One piece is never just one piece.
DIDGEE: Goodbye from Didgee. See you next time.

## Parent note, for beside the film (from 2.1, kept)

Tonight's promise is "you will never be in trouble for telling me what you
have already put in". Respond to the telling, not to the thing: say thank you,
then fix it together. If what comes out is bigger than a piece of information
(an adult asking your child things, someone asking to meet, anything sexual,
images), stop and do not question them further. Write down what they said in
their own words, report it to CEOP at ceop.police.uk, and ring 101, or 999 if
your child is in danger right now.
