## The lesson

- **Knowing**: I can notice the uh oh feeling in my body, turn the screen over,
  and go and tell a grown up.
- **Feeling**: all feelings are okay, and telling is never naughty.
- **Special word**: FEELING
- **Catchphrase, three times or more**: "Turn it over. Go and tell!"
- **Routine practised**: the High Five game. The grown up says "uh oh", the
  child pretends to turn a screen over, runs to them, and they high five.
- **Pebble's joke**: a picture on the easel hung upside down, then turned round.

## Safeguarding, built in

- **The upsetting thing is never shown.** Not in a picture, not described. The
  screen in the film shows only a soft grey scribble. A four year old learns
  the routine without meeting the thing it is for.
- **Never in trouble, even when a rule was broken.** The film says it out loud,
  matching the live parent note, because a child who expects a telling off for
  what they were watching will hide what they saw.
- **The grown up says the promise, not DiGi.** Pause card 2 is the grown up's
  line, so the promise is made by the person who has to keep it.
- No fact about the body is claimed. "A wobbly tummy, a hot face" are offered
  as things you might feel, not as how bodies work.

## SEGMENT B

**8. Turn it over** (u08)

DIDGEE: When you get the uh oh feeling, here's what to do. First, turn it over.
DIDGEE: Put the screen face down. Now you can't see it.

PICTURE: Pebble hurrying along a cosy hallway towards a warm open doorway, hands empty, the tablet left behind face down.

DIDGEE: Then, go and tell. Find your grown up, and tell them what you saw.
