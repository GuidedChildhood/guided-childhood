# 1.6 remake: Screens go to bed first

Child lesson film, Stage 1 (ages 4 to 7), watched with a grown up.
Subject group C in `../subjects-2026-11-16.md` (sleep and the phone at night).
16 November 2026. Recorded and built: see README.md. The spoken words as recorded are in build/lines.json.

The routine stays, because it was the good part: screens go to bed first, then
three cosy things.

## The lesson

- **Knowing**: I can say that my body needs sleep, and that screens go to bed
  before I do.
- **Feeling**: bedtime is cosy, not a telling off. It is never my fault if I
  find it hard to stop, and I can always tell my grown up if something on a
  screen is stuck in my head at night.
- **Special word**: WIND DOWN (said "wind down", shown as a little winding key)
- **Catchphrase, three times**: "Screens go to bed first."
- **Routine practised**: put the tablet to bed in its spot, then three cosy
  things the family picks (the film shows teeth, story, cuddle, and says "your
  three might be different").
- **Pebble's joke**: Pebble tucks the picture book in upside down first, then
  turns it round. Never the lesson itself.

## Cast and voice

## Shape

The same three segment shape the player expects (A, pause, B, pause, C), so the
product can swap it in for the live 1.6. About 540 spoken words, about 4:40 at
118 words a minute.

Every picture is drawn 16 by 9 with the main action inside the middle square
(the centre 1080 by 1080 of a 1920 by 1080 frame). That middle square is what
the portrait cut uses, placed on cream with the captions underneath, the way the
1.3 release test did it. So one picture set makes both cuts.

## SEGMENT A

**1. Hello** (0:00)
PICTURE: A cosy room at dusk, cream walls, a window with a soft orange sky. DiGi the gold star floats in and glows hello. Pebble pops out from behind a picture book.
DIDGEE: Hello, it's Didgee. And here's Pebble.
DIDGEE: Grown up, come and sit close. This one is about bedtime, and it is a cosy one.

**2. Calm me** (0:12)
PICTURE: DiGi's glow slowly gets bigger and smaller, like breathing. Pebble copies, rocking gently.
DIDGEE: Let's breathe together. Breathe in, and blow out slowly, like cooling a cup of warm milk.
DIDGEE: One more. In. And out. Lovely.

**3. Remember when** [PRODUCT ONLY] (0:30)
PICTURE: A small picture from the last film in a frame on the wall.
DIDGEE: Last time we learned that someone made everything on a screen. Today, something everybody does every single night.

**4. Today's one thing** (0:40)
PICTURE: The words WIND DOWN appear beside a little winding key that turns slower and slower and stops.
DIDGEE: Today's special words are wind down.
DIDGEE: Winding down is the slow, quiet bit before sleep. Like a toy that slows down, and slows down, and stops.

**5. Teach** (0:58)
PICTURE: Night outside the window. Pebble yawns a huge yawn and stretches. Small stars appear in the sky one by one.
DIDGEE: When you sleep, your body gets the rest it needs.
DIDGEE: That is why everybody needs sleep. Grown ups too. And Pebble. Pebble needs a lot.

PICTURE: A tablet on a table, showing a bright, busy, happy cartoon. Pebble's eyes are wide open, very awake. The tablet is drawn friendly, not scary.
DIDGEE: Screens are fun. They are bright and busy and exciting.
DIDGEE: And exciting things make it hard for your brain to wind down. Even when you are really tired.

**Pause and ask 1** (1:30)
ON SCREEN: PAUSE. Grown up, ask: "What helps YOU feel sleepy?"
Older child line on the card: "What is the last thing you do before you go to sleep?"
DIDGEE: Pause me here, and ask each other the question. I'll wait.

◆ platform slide: QUICK CHECK. Tap the sleepy things: a story, a cuddle, a bright game, a warm bath. (The bright game is the one that is not.)

## SEGMENT B

**6. Teach, apply** (1:45)
PICTURE: The tablet gives a big sleepy yawn of its own. Pebble picks it up gently.
DIDGEE: So here is our bedtime rule. Ready?
DIDGEE: Screens go to bed first.

PICTURE: A little charging spot in the kitchen, a basket with a cushion. Pebble lays the tablet in it, tucks it in with a tiny blanket. Beside it, a grown up's phone already tucked in.
DIDGEE: The tablet goes to its own bed. Not in your bedroom, in its special spot.
DIDGEE: And look. The grown up's phone goes to bed too. Screens go to bed first. Everybody's.

**7. Pebble's turn** (2:15)
PICTURE: Pebble tucks the picture book into the basket upside down, looks at it, turns it the right way round, and takes it out again with a smile.
DIDGEE: Oh, Pebble. The book does not go to bed. The book comes with us. Books are for bedtime.

**8. The three cosy things** (2:30)
PICTURE: Three cosy pictures in a row, lighting up one at a time: a toothbrush, an open storybook, a cuddle on the bed.
DIDGEE: Then it's time for three cosy things. Teeth. Story. Cuddle.
DIDGEE: Your three cosy things might be different. A song, a bath, a chat about your day. You and your grown up can pick.

**Pause and ask 2** (2:55)
ON SCREEN: PAUSE. Grown up, ask: "What shall our three cosy things be?"
Older child line on the card: "Where shall the screens sleep in our house?"
DIDGEE: Pause me here and choose your three together. Take your time.

◆ platform slide: MY THREE COSY THINGS. Pick three pictures. The choice is saved for the grown up to see.

## SEGMENT C

**9. If it's stuck in your head** (3:10)
PICTURE: Pebble in bed, eyes open, a small thought cloud with a muddled scribble in it. A door opens a crack and warm light comes in.
DIDGEE: Sometimes, at bedtime, something you saw on a screen gets stuck in your head.
DIDGEE: If that happens, you can always tell your grown up. Even in the night.
DIDGEE: You will never be in trouble for telling.
PICTURE: The scribble cloud floats away. Pebble snuggles down.

**10. Practise** (3:35)
PICTURE: DiGi beside a small drawn sign that says SCREENS GO TO BED FIRST with a sleeping tablet on it.
DIDGEE: Let's practise. Grown up, you say: it's nearly bedtime.
DIDGEE: And then the little one answers: screens go to bed first.
DIDGEE: Let's try it now. Pause me if you need to.

**11. Recap** (3:55)
PICTURE: The winding key, the tablet in its basket, the three cosy pictures, in a row.
DIDGEE: So. We wind down before sleep. Screens go to bed first. Then three cosy things.

**12. Goodnight** (4:10)
PICTURE: The room at night, the tablet asleep in its basket downstairs, Pebble asleep with the book. DiGi dims to a soft glow and waves.
DIDGEE: Tonight, put the tablet to bed in its spot, and do your three cosy things.
DIDGEE: Goodnight, Pebble. Goodbye from Didgee. See you next time.

No line after this. No promise to the child or the grown up.

## The one line about the body

Beat 5 first said that the brain "tidies away" the day during sleep. No cleared
source in the register covers that for children of 4 to 7, so on 16 November it
became the fallback, "When you sleep, your body gets the rest it needs", which
claims nothing beyond the obvious. Nothing else in the film states a fact about
the body.

## Parent note, for beside the film

Your child just learned that screens go to bed first, then three cosy things.
Tonight, pick the spot where the screens sleep, put your own phone there too,
and choose your three cosy things together. If they say something on a screen is
stuck in their head, thank them for telling you.
