# Module 6: Influencers and the sell

**13+ Social Media Literacy curriculum. Built in all three versions, on the approved Module 1 template.** British English, no dashes anywhere.

**The big idea:** you can feel like you know a creator who has no idea you exist, and that closeness is exactly what makes their selling work. Once you can see the bond and the business behind it, the recommendation goes back to being just an advert.

**Learning outcome (one line, all versions):** I can explain why a creator I feel close to can still be selling to me, and one thing I do before I buy.

**Mental health link, held calmly:** the warm feeling you get from a creator you watch every day is real, and it is not a fault in you. It is called a one sided bond, where you feel you know them and they do not know you. The catch is that this bond quietly softens your guard, so a "paid partnership" label sails past you and a lot of what you watch becomes a nudge to want more and buy more. The point is not that liking a creator is bad. It is that closeness plus selling is a strong mix, and seeing it is what puts you back in charge of your own money and your own wanting.

**Evidence anchors:** parasocial relationships, the one sided bond a viewer forms with a creator who does not know them. Influencer marketing and sponsored content, where a recommendation is a paid advert. Materialism and purchase pressure linked to influencer content. Ad disclosure and its limits, drawing on the PMC review on disclosing influencer marketing to children, which finds that strong parasocial bonds can neutralise a disclosure so the young viewer stays open to persuasion, and that persuasion knowledge is still developing at this age. Framing per Odgers (preparation over fear), Livingstone 4 Cs (this module sits mostly in Commercial and Conduct), Przybylski (a Goldilocks note, following creators is fine, it is the unseen selling that needs the skill).

**Cast:** first taught at 14, so KS3 into KS4, host Nova. DiGi is the through line guide.

# VERSION A. SCHOOLS IN DEPTH

**Stage:** Years 9 to 10, ages 14 to 15. **Length:** one lesson, about 55 minutes. **Format:** seven beat Rosenshine.

**Statutory and framework mapping:**
- RSHE (statutory): Internet safety and harms, how information and data is shared and used, the risks of a distorted picture online, and the 2026 guidance emphasis on how commercial content and platforms are designed to influence.
- UKCIS Education for a Connected World strands: Managing online information, Online reputation, Health wellbeing and lifestyle.
- Computing national curriculum KS3 and KS4: using technology safely, responsibly and critically.
- PSHE Association: media literacy and financial capability, critical evaluation of what pupils see online.

**Ground rules (set before teaching, every lesson in this curriculum):** we talk about the design and the scenarios, not about each other. No naming of creators to mock them, and no personal disclosures about spending. Questions are always welcome. If anything here worries you about yourself or a friend, the end of the lesson shows exactly where to go.

**Baseline check (Rosenshine, start where they are):** one question on entry, "think of a creator you watch a lot, how do you think they make their money?" Collect three answers on the board, return to them at the end.

### Beat 1 · Recall and Do Now (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Last time we looked at how the feed holds your attention. Quick recall on your whiteboards, name one way an app is designed to keep you watching longer than you meant to. Take three, no comment yet. Today we follow the money one step further, to the people inside the feed who are selling to you.
**On screen.** The lesson title and the entry question, how does a creator you watch make money.
**Purpose.** Retrieval, activates prior schema, links Module 1 to today.

### Beat 2 · New input in small steps (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Four ideas today, one at a time. One, the one sided bond. When you watch someone daily, you start to feel you know them, their voice, their room, their week. That feeling has a name, a parasocial relationship, and it is one sided, they do not know you back. It is normal and it is not a weakness. Two, the selling. Creators are often paid to feature a product, that is influencer marketing, and a paid recommendation is an advert wearing the clothes of a friend's tip. Three, the pull to want more. A feed of new clothes, gadgets and lifestyles quietly raises what you think you need, which researchers link to materialism and a low grade pressure to buy. Four, the label and its limits. Adverts are supposed to be disclosed, "ad", "paid partnership", but the PMC research on disclosing influencer marketing to children finds that when the bond is strong, the label barely registers, and that your persuasion radar is still developing at this age, so the disclosure does not protect you as much as it looks like it should.
**On screen.** Four labelled cards revealed one at a time: The one sided bond, The paid recommendation, The pull to want more, The label and its limits.
**Purpose.** Small steps, one idea per card, respects working memory.

### Beat 3 · Worked example, model it aloud (8 min)
**Teacher script.** Watch me pull one apart. Here is a made up creator, someone I have watched every morning for a year. She holds up a water bottle, casually, mid story about her day. First thing, the bond, I trust her because I feel I know her, that is idea one doing its job. Second, tucked at the top corner in grey, the word "ad", that is idea four, disclosed but easy to miss and easy to forgive because I like her. Third, by the end I sort of want the bottle, not because I need water differently, but because wanting has been nudged, that is idea three. Notice I did nothing foolish. A real bond, a real advert and a small nudge all did their job at once. Naming them as they happen is the skill.
**On screen.** A mocked post with the bond, the tiny "ad" label and the want nudge called out one by one as the teacher narrates.
**Purpose.** Worked example, models the analysis before pupils try it.

### Beat 4 · Check for understanding, hinge question (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Hinge question, hold up A, B, C or D. A creator you have followed for years shows a skincare product with a small "paid partnership" tag. Why does research say that tag protects you less than it should? A because tags are often fake, B because the strong bond you feel makes the label barely register, C because skincare is not really advertising, D because the tag is illegal. Show me. If most say B, we move on. If not, we look again.
**On screen.** The multiple choice hinge.
**Purpose.** Whole class check, do not proceed below about 80 percent.

### Beat 5 · Guided practice, we do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Together now. In pairs you have three short scenario cards, a favourite gamer who "just loves" an energy drink that turns out to be a sponsor, a haul video that leaves someone feeling their own clothes are not enough, a "my honest review" that is a paid partnership. For each, name what is really going on, the bond, the sell, the nudge or the missable label, and name one move that takes control back, spot the ad, wait before buying, ask would I want this if a stranger held it up. I will take the first one with you, then you do two and three.
**On screen.** The three scenario cards and a two column frame, what is going on, my move.
**Purpose.** Guided practice with high success, scaffolded.

### Beat 6 · Independent practice, you do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** On your own. Pick one real recommendation you have seen from a creator, no names needed. Write a short "reading the sell" account. Was it disclosed, how strong is the bond you feel with that creator, and what is one habit you will use before buying something a creator shows you, a rule like wait a day, or ask what problem this actually solves for me. This is yours, I am collecting the analysis, not your shopping.
**On screen.** The independent task and a reminder of the four ideas.
**Purpose.** Independent application, the skill transferred to their own feed.

### Beat 7 · Review, consolidate, signpost (7 min)
**Teacher script.** Back to the board. Our entry answers on how creators make money, which holds up now? A lot of it is selling, sometimes openly, often softly through a bond you feel. Say the headline back to me, a creator I feel close to can still be selling to me, and I can see it now. Following creators you enjoy is fine, this is not "delete everyone", it is knowing an advert when the advert has a friendly face. And if watching lifestyles ever leaves you feeling low about what you have or how you look, you can always talk to a trusted adult here, or Childline on 0800 1111.
**On screen.** The one line summary, a forward hook to Module 7, and the support signpost.
**Purpose.** Spaced review, Goldilocks balance, help routes named.

**Endpoint assessment:** compare entry and exit answers to the "how do they make money" question, plus the independent Beat 6 analysis, against the outcome, can the pupil explain why a trusted creator can still be selling and name one countermove. **Differentiation:** support, the four cards stay visible and pairs get sentence starters; stretch, ask why disclosure is still worth having even though it is weak, and where following creators is genuinely positive (learning, community, inspiration) so the lesson never tips into "influencers are bad". **DSL note:** not a sensitive module, but if a pupil's work discloses money worries, pressure to spend, or low mood tied to comparison, follow the school's normal pastoral route.

# VERSION B. KID (interactive lesson)

**Stage:** ages 14 to 15. **Host:** Nova. **Format:** the seven beat explainer, about 4 minutes, then one interactive choice. Non judgemental, agency first, the young person is the decision maker. This is the child facing re cut of the same idea.

### Beat 1 · Hook
**Narration.** There is a creator you watch most days. You know their voice, their dog, how their week went. It feels like a friend. Then one day they hold up a product, all casual, and you kind of want it. Here is the thing they are counting on. You feel close to them. They have no idea you exist.
**On screen.** A phone showing a warm, familiar creator, then a small grey "ad" tag blinking on in the corner while the viewer keeps smiling.

### Beat 2 · Big idea and the guide
**Narration.** Nova here. One idea today. That closeness you feel is real, it even has a name, a one sided bond. It is not soppy and it is not weak, everyone gets it. But when someone you feel close to sells you something, your guard drops without you noticing. Closeness plus selling is a strong mix.
**On screen.** Nova drawing a line from a viewer to a creator, then the same line going only one way with a little "they do not know you back".

### Beat 3 · The sell, in small steps
**Narration.** Watch how the sell works. Creators often get paid to show a product, so a "just love this" can be an advert. A feed full of new clothes and gadgets slowly makes you feel yours are not enough. And yes there is meant to be a label, "ad", "paid partnership", but when you like someone, that tiny word barely lands. None of this is an accident. All of it is designed.
**On screen.** Three quick icons, a hidden price tag under a heart, a shopping cart that keeps growing, a grey "ad" tag so small you have to squint.

### Beat 4 · Wrong then right
**Narration.** The trap feels like this, they would not sell me something rubbish, we are basically mates. Here is the move. Say it out loud, this is an advert. Then ask one question, would I still want this if a total stranger held it up? If the answer is no, it was the bond talking, not the thing.
**On screen.** Split, left a warm "we are basically mates" glow, right Nova calmly stamping "this is an advert" and asking the stranger question.

### Beat 5 · The twist
**Narration.** Here is the part they hope you never notice. The bond is not a bug in the plan, it is the plan. The closer you feel, the better the label works on them and the worse it protects you. So the label is not your shield. You are.
**On screen.** A grey "ad" tag shrinking as a heart grows bigger, then Nova stepping in front as the real shield.

### Beat 6 · Your move (interactive choice)
**Narration.** Pick one thing to try this week. Spot and say "advert" out loud the next time a creator shows a product. Wait a day before buying anything you saw on your feed. Or ask the stranger question before you tap buy. Choose one now.
**On screen.** Three tappable cards, say advert, wait a day, ask the stranger question. The young person taps one and Nova reflects it back as their plan. No wrong answer.

### Beat 7 · Close
**Narration.** One line to keep. A creator I feel close to can still be selling to me, and now I can see it. Keep watching the ones you love, that part is fine. Just know an advert when it smiles at you. Next time, Nova looks at how people treat each other in group chats, when the crowd turns.
**On screen.** The chosen card glows as "my move this week". Nova gives a knowing nod. A small preview of a buzzing group chat, hinting at the next module.

# VERSION C. PARENT (co view guide)

**Stage band:** Explorer to Shaper (ages 11 to 15), extends the existing 092 spine. **Format:** the 092 lessons pattern, a short guide plus conversation starters, for the `public.lessons` table. Stance, curious not critical, coach not judge.

**the_idea.** Your child can feel genuinely close to a creator they watch every day, a one sided bond where they feel they know the person who has no idea they exist. That closeness is warm and normal, and it is also exactly what makes influencer selling work. A lot of what they watch is paid promotion, and even when it carries an "ad" label, research finds that a strong bond makes the label barely register, so the persuasion still lands.

**why_it_matters.** When you understand the bond, two things change. You stop treating your child's love of a creator as silly or a red flag, which keeps them talking to you about what they watch and want. And you can teach them the one skill that actually protects them, seeing the sell behind the friendship, which matters because researchers link a steady diet of influencer content to materialism and a quiet pressure to buy, and because at this age the internal radar for spotting persuasion is still developing. This protects far better than banning a creator they will just watch elsewhere.

**try_this.** Watch one video together with a creator your child likes, and let them be the expert. "Tell me why you like them." Then, gently, "how do you think they make their money?" Let them find the answer, that a lot of it is selling. Next time a product appears, spot the "ad" tag together as a team, not a telling off, and try one question out loud, "would we want this if a stranger held it up?" Agree one small family habit, wait a day before buying anything from the feed, or a quick check of what problem it actually solves. A habit they help set is one they keep.

**key_message.** Loving a creator is fine. The skill is seeing the advert when it has a friendly face. Your job is not to ban the people they watch, it is to help them read the sell so the choice to buy stays theirs.

**digi_prompt (for DiGi, calibrated pathway, never allow or deny).** When a child says a creator they love recommended something and they really want it, DiGi does not judge and does not lecture. It warms to the bond as real, names calmly that a recommendation can be a paid advert, asks one question about whether they would want it from a stranger, and hands them one small move and a trusted adult to try it with.

## Template notes (what every module carries, proven on Module 1)
- Three versions from one idea: schools full seven beat with assessment and statutory mapping, kid short interactive with a real choice and the stage host, parent short co view guide in the 092 fields.
- Non judgemental throughout, scenario first via distancing (a made up creator, not "has this happened to you"), agency out, help signposted in the schools and kid versions.
- Calm mental health framing, never scare tactics, and a deliberate Goldilocks stretch so it never tips into "influencers are bad".
- Claims on parasocial bonds, disclosure and persuasion knowledge attributed to the PMC review on disclosing influencer marketing to children, kept general, no invented statistics.
- Wiring is the build session's job later: schools and kid rows into school_lessons, parent row into lessons, per the existing handoff pattern.
