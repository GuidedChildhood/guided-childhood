# Module 14: Taking the wheel

**13+ Social Media Literacy curriculum, built in all three versions, following the Module 1 template. British English, no dashes anywhere. Extends the existing 092 lesson, Taking the wheel at 16.**

**The big idea:** by 16 the guard rails start coming off. The default protections that were switched on for you as a child thin out as you get older, so the judgement that used to be built into the app now has to live inside you. This is the handover, you take the wheel.

**Learning outcome (one line, all versions):** I can run my own social media on purpose, take a break when I need one, shape my feed, decide whether and how to be on a platform, and think about the footprint I am leaving for the adult I am about to be.

**Mental health link, held calmly:** agency is protective, being the one who decides is good for you. Breaks are one of the few levers with real evidence behind them, the APA reported in 2023 that cutting use for a few weeks improved how teenagers felt about their appearance. And here is the honest part, the design protections that were on by default when you were younger get weaker as you age up under the Children's Code, so the judgement has to become yours. Nobody is coming to switch it on for you. That is not a threat, it is the point of growing up.

**Evidence anchors:** APA 2023, a short break from social media improved teenagers' feelings about their appearance and body image. ICO Children's Code, the built in protections for young users are strongest for children and thin out with age, so self governance has to replace them. Framing per Odgers (preparation over fear), Przybylski (the Goldilocks middle, breaks reset toward it), Livingstone 4 Cs (this module is the young person now managing all four themselves), boyd (literacy is taught, not innate, so the handover has to be explicit). A rights and citizen lens throughout, the learner is treated as a near adult decision maker.

**Cast:** first taught at 16 plus, the Independent stage, which is beyond the KS4 Nova stage, so the guide here is DiGi, near adult to near adult. Least scaffolding of any module.

# VERSION A. SCHOOLS IN DEPTH

**Stage:** Post 16, ages 16 plus, sixth form or college. **Length:** one lesson, about 55 minutes. **Format:** seven beat Rosenshine, run with the lightest scaffolding of the course, more debate, more independent judgement.

**Statutory and framework mapping:**
- RSHE (statutory) foundations carried into post 16 provision: online reputation, how information and data is shared and used, managing the impact of online activity on wellbeing, in line with the 2026 guidance emphasis on the footprint young people leave.
- UKCIS Education for a Connected World strands: Online reputation, Privacy and security, Health wellbeing and lifestyle, Self image and identity.
- Post 16 study programme and personal development, preparation for adult life, digital citizenship and rights.
- Links to the data protection rights a young person gains as they age, and the ICO Children's Code changes to default protections with age.

**Ground rules (set before teaching, every lesson in this curriculum):** we talk about the decisions and the scenarios, not about each other. No personal disclosures. At this stage you are near adults, so this is a conversation between decision makers, not a set of rules handed down. If anything here worries you about yourself or a friend, the end of the lesson shows exactly where to go.

**Baseline check (Rosenshine, start where they are):** one question on entry, "who is in charge of how you use social media now, and who will be in a year." Collect three answers on the board, return to them at the end.

### Beat 1 · Recall and Do Now (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Across this course a lot of the protection was built in for you, age settings, default privacy, limits switched on because you were a child. Quick recall on your whiteboards, name one protection that was on by default when you were younger. Take three, no comment yet. Here is the thing we build on today, those defaults get weaker as you get older, so the judgement has to move inside you.
**On screen.** The lesson title and the entry question, who is in charge now and in a year.
**Purpose.** Retrieval, activates prior schema, frames the handover from built in protection to internal judgement.

### Beat 2 · New input in small steps (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Four levers you now hold yourself, one at a time. One, breaks, deliberate time away resets your mood and how you feel about your body, and this is not soft, the APA reported in 2023 that teenagers who cut their use for a few weeks felt better about their appearance, a real measured effect. Two, curating your feed, you are not stuck with what you are fed, unfollow what makes you feel worse, hit not interested, follow what actually lifts you, you can reshape the whole thing on purpose. Three, the account decision, you get to choose whether you are on a platform at all, when, and how, being on it on purpose is completely different from being on it by default. Four, your footprint, you are near adults now, and an old post can be seen by a college or an employer years from now, so a bit of judgement today is a gift to the person you are about to be. And under the ICO Children's Code, the protections that used to be automatic thin out as you age, so all four of these levers are now yours to run.
**On screen.** Four labelled cards revealed one at a time: Take a break, Curate your feed, The account decision, Your footprint.
**Purpose.** Small steps, one lever per card, respects working memory, each is an act of self governance.

### Beat 3 · Worked example, model it aloud (8 min)
**Teacher script.** Watch me run one properly. Take the break. Here is a made up sixth former, they notice they feel flat and picky about how they look every time they close the app. Instead of feeling guilty or deleting everything in a panic, they make a decision, two weeks off that one app, on purpose, with an end date. That is the APA finding put to use, a short deliberate break, and the evidence says the appearance worries tend to ease. Notice the moves, they read their own mood, they chose a lever, they set the terms themselves. No one made them. That is taking the wheel, spotting the signal, picking the tool, deciding on your own terms.
**On screen.** The sixth former closing the app feeling flat, then a calm two week marker on a calendar, then the same person after, lighter, no drama.
**Purpose.** Worked example, models self governance as a calm decision, not a punishment or a purge.

### Beat 4 · Check for understanding, hinge question (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Hinge question, hold up A, B, C or D. You are 17. Which statement is true about the protections the app used to give you by default? A they stay exactly the same forever, B they get weaker as you get older, so more of the judgement has to be yours, C they get stronger as you get older, D there were never any. Show me. If most say B, we move on. If not, we look again, because that shift is the whole reason this module exists.
**On screen.** The multiple choice hinge.
**Purpose.** Whole class check, do not proceed below about 80 percent, the correct answer is the handover itself.

### Beat 5 · Guided practice, we do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Together now. In pairs you have three short scenario cards, someone whose feed leaves them anxious every night, someone deciding whether to even make an account on a new platform everyone is joining, someone about to post something they might not want a future employer to see. For each, name the lever they hold, take a break, curate, the account decision, or the footprint, and name the decision you would make and why. You are near adults, so I want the reasoning, not just the answer. I will take the first with you, then you do two and three.
**On screen.** The three scenario cards and a two column frame, the lever, my decision and my reason.
**Purpose.** Guided practice, low scaffolding, reasoning foregrounded, treats learners as decision makers.

### Beat 6 · Independent practice, you do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** On your own. Write your own handover plan, no names, no detail you would not want read. Pick one lever you will actually run, a break you will take, a feed you will curate, an account decision you will make, or a footprint habit you will change, and write why, in your own reasoning. This is not me telling you what to do. At 16 plus, this is you deciding, and writing down why you decided it. That is what makes it stick.
**On screen.** The independent task and a reminder of the four levers.
**Purpose.** Independent application, self authored, the point where judgement moves inside.

### Beat 7 · Review, consolidate, signpost (7 min)
**Teacher script.** Back to the board. Our entry answers on who is in charge now and in a year, which one holds up. You are, more and more, because the defaults are stepping back. Say the headline back to me, the protections are thinning out as I grow up, so the judgement has to be mine now, and I know the levers I hold. That is not being left on your own, it is being trusted with the wheel. And being near adult does not mean handling everything alone, if your use is affecting your sleep, your mood, or how you feel about yourself, a trusted adult is still exactly the right call, or Childline on 0800 1111 while you can still use it. Taking the wheel includes knowing when to ask for a hand.
**On screen.** The one line summary, the four levers, and the support signpost.
**Purpose.** Spaced review, consolidates the handover, names help as part of self governance not a failure of it.

**Endpoint assessment:** compare entry and exit answers on who is in charge, plus the independent Beat 6 handover plan, against the outcome, can the learner name a lever, make a reasoned decision, and account for the thinning defaults. **Differentiation:** support, the four lever cards stay visible and pairs get the reason frame with sentence starters; stretch, open the rights lens, if the app's built in protections weaken as you age, where should the line sit between the platform's duty and your own judgement, a genuine citizen debate (ICO Children's Code as the text). **DSL note:** not a sensitive module, but this age group can carry heavier situations privately, so if a learner's independent work discloses use that is affecting sleep, mood or body image, follow the college's normal pastoral route, and remember the handover framing, widen their support, do not simply hand them a rule.

# VERSION B. KID (interactive lesson)

**Stage:** Independent, ages 16 plus. **Host:** DiGi (this stage is beyond the KS4 Nova stage, so DiGi guides it, near adult to near adult). **Format:** the seven beat explainer, about 4 minutes, then one interactive choice. Least scaffolding, treats you as the decision maker you nearly are.

### Beat 1 · Hook
**Narration.** For years, a lot of your social media was looked after for you. Settings switched on because you were a child. Limits you never even saw. Here is what nobody announces. Those get weaker as you get older. Not gone overnight, just quietly stepping back. Which means the judgement that used to be built in now has to be yours.
**On screen.** A car with someone else's hands on the wheel, then those hands lifting away one finger at a time, and the wheel sitting there, waiting.

### Beat 2 · Big idea and the guide
**Narration.** DiGi here, and this one is a bit different. You are near adult now, so this is not a lesson, it is a handover. One idea. The protections thin out as you grow up, so you take the wheel. Not because you are being abandoned, because you are ready. Let me show you the four things that are yours to run now.
**On screen.** DiGi in the passenger seat, calm, gesturing to the driver's side, the wheel now lit up.

### Beat 3 · The levers, in small steps
**Narration.** Four levers, all yours. Take a break, deliberate time off actually resets your mood and how you feel about your body, that is a real finding, not a guilt trip. Curate your feed, you are not stuck with what you are fed, unfollow what drags you down, hit not interested, follow what lifts you. The account decision, you choose whether you are even on a platform, when, and how, on purpose beats by default. And your footprint, an old post can be read by a college or an employer years from now, so a little judgement today is a favour to future you.
**On screen.** Four quick icons, a pause button, a feed being reshaped by hand, a switch marked on my terms, a footprint stretching forward on a timeline.

### Beat 4 · Wrong then right
**Narration.** The wrong version of this is the panic delete, everything gone at 2am because you feel bad, and back on it by Friday. That is not control, that is a mood swing. Here is the real move. Read your own signal first, am I flat, picky about how I look, up too late, and then pick one lever on purpose, with a reason and an end date. Calm decision, not a purge. That is what taking the wheel actually looks like.
**On screen.** Split, left a frantic delete everything at night then reinstalling days later, right DiGi making one calm marked decision with a clear reason.

### Beat 5 · The twist
**Narration.** Here is the part that is easy to miss. Nobody is coming to switch the judgement on for you. The defaults are stepping back on purpose, because the plan was always that it becomes yours. That is not the app abandoning you. That is you growing up. And taking the wheel includes one more thing most people forget, knowing when to ask for a hand. A driver who never asks for directions is not more independent, just more lost.
**On screen.** The hands fully off the wheel now, your hands on it, and beside you a passenger seat with a trusted adult in it, still there, available, not driving.

### Beat 6 · Your move (interactive choice)
**Narration.** Pick one lever to actually run this week. Take a deliberate break with an end date. Curate your feed, unfollow three things that drag you down. Or check your footprint, look back at what a future employer would find. Choose one now, and know why you chose it.
**On screen.** Three tappable cards, take a break, curate my feed, check my footprint. The young person taps one and DiGi reflects it back as their decision, and asks them for their reason. No wrong answer, but the reason is theirs to give.

### Beat 7 · Close
**Narration.** One line to keep. The protections are thinning out as I grow up, so the judgement is mine now, and I know the levers I hold. That is not a warning. That is the keys. Drive it on purpose. And if things get heavy, sleep, mood, how you feel about yourself, asking a trusted adult is not going back to being a kid, it is exactly what grown ups do. Childline is on 0800 1111 while you can still use it.
**On screen.** The chosen card glows as "my move this week". DiGi steps out of the car, gives a small nod, and you drive.

# VERSION C. PARENT (co view guide)

**Stage band:** Independent (ages 16 plus), extends the existing 092 lesson, Taking the wheel at 16. **Format:** the 092 lessons pattern, a short guide plus conversation starters, for the `public.lessons` table. Stance, curious not critical, coach not judge, and at this age, increasingly, peer to peer.

**the_idea.** By 16 the handover is happening whether you plan it or not. The default protections that were built into apps when your child was younger get weaker as they age, under the ICO Children's Code, so the judgement that used to be automatic has to become theirs. There are four levers they now hold themselves, taking deliberate breaks, curating their feed, deciding whether and how to be on a platform at all, and thinking about the footprint they leave for the adult they are becoming. Your role shifts from setting the rules to handing over the wheel.

**why_it_matters.** Agency is protective, being the one who decides is genuinely good for a young person, and breaks are one of the few levers with real evidence behind them, the APA reported in 2023 that teenagers who cut their use for a few weeks felt better about their appearance. At the same time the built in safeguards are quietly stepping back as your child ages, so if the judgement has not moved inside them, nothing replaces the protection they are losing. The handover is not a risk to manage, it is the whole goal, but it only works if you make it deliberate rather than letting it happen by accident.

**try_this.** Treat your 16 plus as the near adult they are. Instead of a rule, offer a question. "You are old enough now that the app is not really protecting you by default any more, it is on you. What is your own plan for keeping it healthy?" Let them answer, and take their reasoning seriously even when you would decide differently. If they mention feeling flat or picky about how they look after using it, you could say, "some research found a short break actually helped with that, might be worth a try, your call." Notice the phrase, your call. This is the age where you hand over the wheel on purpose, staying in the passenger seat, available, not gripping the dashboard. And make clear that asking for help is part of driving, not a return to childhood.

**key_message.** The protections thin out as your child grows up, so the judgement has to become theirs, and that is the goal, not the danger. Your job now is to hand over the wheel deliberately, trust them with real decisions, and stay in the passenger seat so that asking you for help still feels like a grown up move, not a step back.

**digi_prompt (for DiGi, calibrated pathway, never allow or deny).** When a near adult says they want to sort out their own social media use, DiGi does not set a rule and does not wave them off. It treats them as the decision maker they are, names the levers they hold, a break, curating the feed, the account decision, the footprint, asks what signal they are reading in themselves, and hands back one deliberate move with a reason, plus the reminder that asking a trusted adult is part of taking the wheel, not a failure of it.
