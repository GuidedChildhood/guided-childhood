# Module 11: The inner life

**13+ Social Media Literacy curriculum. Built in all three versions, on the approved Module 1 template.** Extends the existing 092 lesson "The honest check on your mood". British English, no dashes anywhere.

**The big idea:** the feed can shape how you feel without ever telling you it is doing it. The honest mood check is the tool that puts you back in the room. You notice how a session actually leaves you, not how you hoped it would, and you decide from there.

**Learning outcome (one line, all versions):** I can honestly check how a session leaves me feeling, and I can name one habit that protects my mood.

**Mental health link, held calmly:** three things sit under this module, kept plain and evidence based. Cutting back on heavy use tends to lift mood (APA 2023). Doomscrolling, that is compulsively feeding on bad news past any point it helps, is linked to higher anxiety and stress (Sharma and colleagues, 2022). And pinning your sense of worth to likes and numbers is one of the real soft spots the design leans on. None of this means you are fragile. It means the honest check is a skill worth having, and it works.

**Evidence anchors:** the honest mood self check as an evidence based habit; APA Health Advisory on adolescent social media use (2023), reducing use improves wellbeing; doomscrolling research (Sharma and colleagues, 2022), compulsive negative consumption is linked to higher anxiety and stress; validation and the metrics of worth as a core vulnerability; identity and self presentation, the main account versus the finsta, the gap between the online self and the real self. Framing per Odgers (preparation over fear), Przybylski (Goldilocks, moderate use beats both extremes), Livingstone 4 Cs (this module sits mostly in Conduct and the young person's own wellbeing). UK anchor for usage and support: Ofcom Children and Parents 2025, YoungMinds.

**Cast:** first taught at 14 to 15, so mid adolescence, host Nova. DiGi is the through line guide.

# VERSION A. SCHOOLS IN DEPTH

**Stage:** KS4, Years 10, ages 14 to 15. **Length:** one lesson, about 55 minutes. **Format:** seven beat Rosenshine.

**Statutory and framework mapping:**
- RSHE (statutory): the benefits and risks of social media on mental wellbeing, how to recognise the impact of online activity on your own mood and mental health, where and how to seek support. Aligns to the 2026 guidance emphasis on wellbeing and online life.
- UKCIS Education for a Connected World strands: Health wellbeing and lifestyle, Self image and identity, Online reputation.
- PSHE Association: mental health and emotional wellbeing, media and digital literacy.
- Sits inside the school's KCSIE safeguarding duty.

**Ground rules (set before teaching, every lesson in this curriculum):** we talk about the design and the scenarios, not about each other. No personal disclosures. Questions are always welcome. If anything here worries you about yourself or a friend, the end of the lesson shows exactly where to go.

**Baseline check (Rosenshine, start where they are):** one question on entry, "when you close an app, how often do you actually notice how it left you feeling?" Collect three honest answers on the board, return to them at the end.

### Beat 1 · Recall and Do Now (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Earlier in this curriculum we said the app is built to hold your attention, not to look after you. Today we go one layer in, to your mood. Quick recall on your whiteboards, name one feeling you have had at the end of a long scroll. Flat, wired, low, restless, fine, anything. Take three, no comment yet. We are about to give you a tool for exactly that moment.
**On screen.** The lesson title and the entry question, do you notice how it leaves you.
**Purpose.** Retrieval, activates prior schema, brings the real feeling into the room without personal disclosure.

### Beat 2 · New input in small steps (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Four ideas today, one at a time. One, the honest mood check. Before you open, clock how you feel. After you close, clock it again. Not how you hoped it would go, how it actually landed. That gap is the data. Two, doomscrolling, that is when you keep feeding on bad or upsetting content long past the point it tells you anything useful. Research links that habit to higher anxiety and stress, and the feed makes it easy because alarming content travels furthest. Three, validation, likes and follower counts feel like a score for you as a person, and the research is blunt that tying your worth to those numbers is one of the real vulnerabilities here. Four, identity, the main account versus the finsta, the polished self versus the real one. Everyone is showing an edit. Forgetting that is what makes the comparison hurt.
**On screen.** Four labelled cards revealed one at a time: The honest mood check, Doomscrolling, Likes as worth, The online self versus the real self.
**Purpose.** Small steps, one idea per card, respects working memory.

### Beat 3 · Worked example, model it aloud (8 min)
**Teacher script.** Watch me run the honest check. I open the app a bit low, hoping it will pick me up. Twenty minutes in I am on the third video about something upsetting I can do nothing about, that is doomscrolling, I keep going because the feed keeps serving it. I close it. Honest check, am I lifted? No. I am flatter and a bit anxious. Now here is the thing, the app did not warn me it would go that way. The check did. Naming the gap, hoped for a lift, got a dip, is the whole skill. Next time that memory is the stop.
**On screen.** A mocked session with a mood dial before and after, and the doomscroll spiral labelled as the teacher narrates.
**Purpose.** Worked example, models the reflective thinking before pupils try it.

### Beat 4 · Check for understanding, hinge question (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Hinge question, hold up A, B, C or D. Someone keeps scrolling upsetting news they can do nothing about, feeling worse with every post but unable to stop. Which is that? A the honest mood check, B doomscrolling, C likes as worth, D a finsta. Show me. If most say B, we move on. If not, we look again.
**On screen.** The multiple choice hinge.
**Purpose.** Whole class check, do not proceed below about 80 percent.

### Beat 5 · Guided practice, we do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Together now. In pairs you have three short scenario cards, someone whose whole day sours when a post gets fewer likes than they expected, someone who cannot put down a feed of bad news and feels wound up, someone who feels like a fraud because their real life looks nothing like their main account. For each, name what is going on, and name one move that protects the mood. I will take the first with you, then you do two and three.
**On screen.** The three scenario cards and a two column frame, what is going on, my move.
**Purpose.** Guided practice with high success, scaffolded.

### Beat 6 · Independent practice, you do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** On your own. Design your own honest mood check. Write the two questions you will ask, one before you open and one after you close, and write one habit you will try for a week, a doomscroll limit on one app, muting accounts that leave you flat, or a rule that a like count never gets to decide your day. This is yours. I am not collecting personal detail, I am collecting the plan.
**On screen.** The independent task and a reminder of the four ideas.
**Purpose.** Independent application, the skill transferred to their own life.

### Beat 7 · Review, consolidate, signpost (7 min)
**Teacher script.** Back to the board. Our entry answers on whether you notice how it leaves you, what changes now you have the check? Say the headline back to me, the feed can move my mood without telling me, and the honest check puts me back in charge of it. Worth knowing, the research says cutting back on heavy use tends to lift mood, so this is not about quitting, it is about steering. And if the honest check keeps coming back low, or a friend's does, that matters and it is worth saying out loud. Talk to a trusted adult here, or Childline on 0800 1111. YoungMinds has support too.
**On screen.** The one line summary, a reminder that moderate use is fine, and the support signpost.
**Purpose.** Spaced review, Goldilocks stretch, help routes named.

**Endpoint assessment:** compare entry and exit answers on noticing mood, plus the Beat 6 self check design, against the outcome, can the pupil run an honest mood check and name one protective habit. **Differentiation:** support, the four cards stay visible and pairs get sentence starters and a ready made mood dial; stretch, ask why moderate use can still lift mood and connection (Przybylski Goldilocks, APA 2023 notes benefits alongside risks) so the lesson never tips into "social media wrecks your mood". **DSL note:** wellbeing content, handled calmly. If a pupil's independent work discloses persistent low mood, anxiety or compulsive use affecting daily life, follow the school's normal pastoral and safeguarding route.

# VERSION B. KID (interactive lesson)

**Stage:** ages 14 to 15. **Host:** Nova. **Format:** the seven beat explainer, about 4 minutes, then one interactive choice. Non judgemental, agency first, the young person is the decision maker. This is the child facing re cut of the same idea.

### Beat 1 · Hook
**Narration.** You open the app feeling okay. You close it twenty minutes later feeling a bit flat, a bit wound up, and you are not sure why. The app never told you it would go that way. But there is one quick move that would have caught it.
**On screen.** A phone opening, a small mood face going from steady to slightly grey while a clock spins.

### Beat 2 · Big idea and the guide
**Narration.** Nova here. One skill today. The honest mood check. Before you open, notice how you feel. After you close, notice again. Not how you wanted it to go. How it actually landed. That little gap tells you the truth the app never will.
**On screen.** Nova drawing two mood dials, a before and an after, with a bright gap between them.

### Beat 3 · The traps, in small steps
**Narration.** A few things pull the mood down without asking. Doomscrolling, when you keep feeding on bad news you can do nothing about, and it winds you up more with every post. Likes, when a number starts to feel like a score for you as a person. And the highlight gap, everyone is posting an edit of their life, so comparing your whole self to their best bits never ends well. None of it is you being weak. All of it is worth spotting.
**On screen.** Three quick icons, a spiral of bad news, a like count flashing, a polished post next to a messy real one.

### Beat 4 · Wrong then right
**Narration.** The trap sounds like this, one more sad video, one more check of the likes, and you feel worse each time but you cannot stop. Here is the move. Run the honest check out loud. I opened this low, hoping for a lift, and it made me flatter. The second you name that, the spell breaks, because now you can see what it did.
**On screen.** Split, left the doomscroll winding tighter, right Nova calmly reading the mood dial and the spiral easing.

### Beat 5 · The twist
**Narration.** Here is the part they hope you never notice. The app has no reason to care how it leaves you. It measures time, not mood. So the mood check has to come from you. And the research backs it, people who ease off heavy use tend to feel better, not worse. Steering beats quitting.
**On screen.** An app measuring only minutes, then Nova adding a mood meter it never had.

### Beat 6 · Your move (interactive choice)
**Narration.** Pick one thing to try this week. Run the honest check every time you close an app. Mute a few accounts that always leave you flat. Or set one rule, a like count never gets to decide my day. Choose one now.
**On screen.** Three tappable cards, run the check, mute what drains me, likes do not run my day. The young person taps one and Nova reflects it back as their plan. No wrong answer.

### Beat 7 · Close
**Narration.** One line to keep. The feed can move my mood without telling me, and the honest check puts me back in charge of it. If your check keeps coming back low, that is worth saying to someone you trust, a trusted adult, or Childline on 0800 1111. You are allowed to feel good after using it. This is how you steer toward that.
**On screen.** The chosen card glows as "my move this week". Nova gives a steady nod. The support line stays on screen.

# VERSION C. PARENT (co view guide)

**Stage band:** Explorer to Shaper (ages 11 to 15), extends the existing 092 spine and its "The honest check on your mood" lesson. **Format:** the 092 lessons pattern, a short guide plus conversation starters, for the `public.lessons` table. Stance, curious not critical, coach not judge.

**the_idea.** Social media can shift how your child feels without either of you clocking it. A session can leave them flat, wound up or low, and the app never flags it, because it counts minutes, not mood. Three habits do the damage quietly, doomscrolling on bad news, treating likes as a score for their worth, and comparing their real self to everyone else's edited highlight reel. The fix is small and teachable, an honest check on how a session actually leaves them.

**why_it_matters.** When your child can name how the feed lands on their mood, they stop being carried by it. The research is encouraging here, cutting back on heavy use tends to lift wellbeing (APA 2023), and doomscrolling is linked to more anxiety and stress (Sharma and colleagues, 2022), so a simple honest check is not fussing, it is protective. And because it is a habit they run themselves, it lasts far longer than a limit you impose. This is steering, not banning. Moderate use is genuinely fine.

**try_this.** Model the check yourself first, out loud, no lecture. "I felt okay before that, a bit meh after, funny how that happens." Then ask, gently, "when you come off an app, do you ever notice how it left you?" Not as a trap, as a shared curiosity. Watch one real moment together, a bad news spiral or a post that did not get the likes they hoped, and name it as a team, that is doomscrolling, that is the like count getting into your head. Agree one small thing together, a mood check when they close an app, muting a few accounts that leave them flat, or a rule that likes never get to run the day. A habit they help write is a habit they keep.

**key_message.** The feed can move your child's mood without telling them, and the honest check puts them back in charge of it. Your job is not to police the feeling, it is to help them notice it, so they can steer.

**digi_prompt (for DiGi, calibrated pathway, never allow or deny).** When a child says an app leaves them flat, low or wound up, DiGi does not judge and does not lecture. It walks them through the honest check, before and after, asks what the app was doing in that session, and hands them one small move and a trusted adult to try it with. If the mood keeps coming back low, DiGi calmly names Childline on 0800 1111 and a trusted adult as the next step.
