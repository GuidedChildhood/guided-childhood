# Module 13: The good side, held honestly

**13+ Social Media Literacy curriculum, built in all three versions, following the Module 1 template. British English, no dashes anywhere.**

**The big idea:** social media can genuinely be good for you. It keeps friendships alive, it helps you find your people, it gives you a voice, and it lets you make things instead of only watching. But every one of those good things arrives with a risk attached, so we learn the good and the catch together, never one without the other.

**Learning outcome (one line, all versions):** I can name a real way social media is good for me, the risk that sits alongside it, and how I keep the good without getting caught by the catch.

**Mental health link, held calmly:** feeling connected and like you belong is good for every teenager, and it matters most for anyone who does not have that at home. For young people who feel on the edge of things, an LGBTQ+ teen, a young carer, a teen with a rare condition, finding their people online can lift a real weight. That is true and it is worth saying plainly. The same research also calls it a double edged sword, the place you belong is the same place that can pile on or pull you down, so we hold the good with its risk, not instead of it.

**Evidence anchors:** the Digital Wellness Lab on belonging and connection online. A systematic review on social media and LGBTQ youth wellbeing, connection can reduce mental health concerns for marginalised youth, and the same reviews call it double edged. Ofcom Children and Parents 2025 on content creation, a large share of UK young people make their own content rather than only consuming it. Framing per Odgers (preparation over fear, do not overstate into harmless), Przybylski (the Goldilocks middle), Livingstone 4 Cs (this module is mostly Contact and Content held on the upside).

**Cast:** first taught woven through earlier modules, then its own module at 14 plus, KS4, host Nova. DiGi is the through line guide.

# VERSION A. SCHOOLS IN DEPTH

**Stage:** KS4, Years 10 to 11, ages 14 to 15. **Length:** one lesson, about 55 minutes. **Format:** seven beat Rosenshine.

**Statutory and framework mapping:**
- RSHE (statutory): the benefits of the internet and the positive effect of online activity on wellbeing, alongside the risks, in line with the 2026 guidance that platforms carry both. Online relationships and how they can support wellbeing.
- UKCIS Education for a Connected World strands: Self image and identity, Online relationships, Health wellbeing and lifestyle.
- Computing national curriculum KS4: using technology safely, respectfully and responsibly.
- PSHE Association: media and digital literacy, and the positive contribution online communities can make.

**Ground rules (set before teaching, every lesson in this curriculum):** we talk about the design and the scenarios, not about each other. No personal disclosures. Questions are always welcome. Belonging is personal, so if a scenario is close to your own life you never have to share it. If anything here worries you about yourself or a friend, the end of the lesson shows exactly where to go.

**Baseline check (Rosenshine, start where they are):** one question on entry, "name one thing social media actually gives you that is good for you." Collect three answers on the board, return to them at the end and add the risk that sits with each.

### Beat 1 · Recall and Do Now (5 min)
**Teacher script.** For a lot of this course we have looked at what the machine does to you. Today we flip it. Quick recall on your whiteboards, name one thing social media does for you that you would genuinely miss if it vanished tomorrow. Take three, no comment yet, we are about to hold each one up to the light.
**On screen.** The lesson title and the entry question, what is one good thing social media gives you.
**Purpose.** Retrieval, activates prior schema, sets the positive frame without dropping the critical guard.

### Beat 2 · New input in small steps (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Five good things, and the catch that comes with each, one at a time. One, connection, it keeps friendships alive across distance, the mate who moved away, the cousin abroad, the group that keeps going after the trip ends, and the catch, staying reachable all the time can start to feel like you can never log off. Two, belonging, it helps you find your people, and this matters most if you feel a bit on the edge of things, and the catch is that the same community can turn or pile on. Three, voice, activism and identity, you can stand for something and be heard, and the catch is that being visible online also means being a target. Four, learning and creativity, making something beats scrolling past everyone else's, and Ofcom finds a large share of UK young people create their own content, not just watch, and the catch is the pressure to perform and chase numbers. Five, support and help, you can find people who get exactly what you are going through, and the catch is that a stranger who is warm is still a stranger, and online advice is not the same as a trained adult. Every good thing here is real. Every one has a catch. The skill is keeping the good and seeing the catch at the same time.
**On screen.** Five labelled cards revealed one at a time, each split into two halves, the good and the catch: Connection, Belonging, Voice, Creativity, Support.
**Purpose.** Small steps, one paired idea per card, respects working memory, models that positives are never taught clean.

### Beat 3 · Worked example, model it aloud (8 min)
**Teacher script.** Watch me hold one up properly. Take belonging. Here is a made up teenager, they have a health condition almost no one at their school has ever heard of, so at school they feel like the odd one out. Online they find a group of people with the same condition, and for the first time someone says, yes, that happens to me too. That is huge, and the research backs it, feeling you belong is genuinely good for your mental health, especially when you do not get that at home or at school. Now the catch, in the same breath. That group could give brilliant support, or it could hand out advice that a doctor would never give, or it could turn on someone on a bad day. So the good is real and the guard stays up. The move is to enjoy the belonging and still ask, would I check this with an adult I trust before I act on it.
**On screen.** The teenager, the isolated school on one side, the online group saying "me too" on the other, then a small guard icon appearing over the group, not shutting it down, just watching.
**Purpose.** Worked example, models holding the good and the risk together rather than picking one.

### Beat 4 · Check for understanding, hinge question (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Hinge question, hold up A, B, C or D. Someone says, my online community is the only place I feel understood, so nothing on there could ever be bad for me. What is the most honest response? A they are right, if it feels good it is safe, B the belonging is real and good, and the same place can still carry risk, so both are true, C they are wrong, online communities are dangerous, D you cannot tell. Show me. If most say B, we move on. If not, we look again, because that both is true answer is the whole module.
**On screen.** The multiple choice hinge.
**Purpose.** Whole class check, do not proceed below about 80 percent, the correct answer is the double edged idea itself.

### Beat 5 · Guided practice, we do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Together now. In pairs you have three short scenario cards, a young carer who finally finds other young carers online, a teen posting about a cause they believe in, a teen learning to edit videos and getting good at it. For each, name the real good it brings, name the catch that sits with it, and name one move that keeps the good and manages the catch. I will take the first one with you, then you do two and three.
**On screen.** The three scenario cards and a three column frame, the good, the catch, my move.
**Purpose.** Guided practice with high success, scaffolded, every card forces both halves.

### Beat 6 · Independent practice, you do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** On your own. Pick one real way social media is good in your own life, no names, no detail you would not want read out. Write the good it brings, write the catch honestly, and write one thing you already do or could do to keep the good without getting caught. This is yours, I am not collecting personal detail, I am collecting the thinking, the good, the catch, the move.
**On screen.** The independent task and a reminder of the five paired cards.
**Purpose.** Independent application, the double edged skill transferred to their own life.

### Beat 7 · Review, consolidate, signpost (7 min)
**Teacher script.** Back to the board. Our entry answers on the good things, let us add the catch to each one now, out loud, together. Say the headline back to me, social media can be genuinely good for me, and every good thing comes with a catch, and I can hold both at once. That is not being cynical, that is being clear eyed. And if anything today made you think that your only support is online, that is worth a conversation, a good online community and a trusted adult are not rivals, you can have both. You can always talk to a trusted adult here, or Childline on 0800 1111.
**On screen.** The one line summary, the five goods each now paired with their catch, and the support signpost.
**Purpose.** Spaced review, consolidates the double edged idea, help routes named.

**Endpoint assessment:** compare entry answers (goods only) with exit answers (goods paired with catches and a move), plus the independent Beat 6 work, against the outcome, can the pupil name a good, its risk, and how they keep the good. **Differentiation:** support, the five paired cards stay visible and pairs get the three column frame with sentence starters; stretch, ask where the Goldilocks middle sits (Przybylski), when does a good community start to cost more than it gives, so the lesson never tips into "so it is all fine". **DSL note:** not a sensitive module, but belonging is personal, so if a pupil's independent work discloses that their only support is online, or hints at isolation at home, follow the school's normal pastoral route and gently widen their support rather than removing the online one.

# VERSION B. KID (interactive lesson)

**Stage:** KS4, ages 14 to 15. **Host:** Nova. **Format:** the seven beat explainer, about 4 minutes, then one interactive choice. Non judgemental, agency first, the young person is the decision maker. This is the child facing re cut of the same idea.

### Beat 1 · Hook
**Narration.** Most of this course has been about what the app does to you. Today, a different truth. Sometimes social media is the best thing in your day. The mate who moved away and you still talk every night. The group who finally gets you. That is real, and it counts. And it still has a catch. Both of those are true at once.
**On screen.** A phone lighting up with a warm group chat, faces, laughter, then a small shadow settling quietly behind it, not scary, just there.

### Beat 2 · Big idea and the guide
**Narration.** Nova here. One idea today. The good side of social media is real, do not let anyone tell you it is all poison. And every good thing on there comes with a catch. So we keep the good and we keep our eyes open. Good and catch, together, every time.
**On screen.** Nova holding two things at once, a bright heart in one hand, a small watchful eye in the other, both up, neither dropped.

### Beat 3 · The good things, in small steps
**Narration.** Here is what it gives you. It keeps friendships alive across any distance. It helps you find your people, and if you have ever felt like the odd one out, that can change everything. It gives you a voice for something you care about. And it lets you make things, edit, draw, write, build, loads of you already create more than you watch. All of that is genuinely good for you.
**On screen.** Four quick warm icons, a friendship across a map, a group forming around one person, a raised voice, a pair of hands making something.

### Beat 4 · Wrong then right
**Narration.** The trap is thinking you have to pick a side. Either social media is amazing and you switch your brain off, or it is all bad and you feel guilty for loving it. Both of those are wrong. Here is the move. Enjoy the good thing fully, and keep one quiet question running, is this lifting me up or starting to weigh me down. You can love it and still watch it.
**On screen.** Split, left two doors marked "all good" and "all bad" both crossed out, right Nova walking through a third door in the middle marked "good, eyes open".

### Beat 5 · The twist
**Narration.** Here is the part worth knowing. The place you belong is the same place that can pile on. The community that saves your week is the same one that can turn on a bad day. That is not a reason to leave. It is a reason to keep a trusted adult in your life too, so you are never leaning on just one thing. A great group and a real adult are not rivals. Have both.
**On screen.** A bright online group, and beside it a real person, a parent, a coach, a teacher, both lit up together, not competing.

### Beat 6 · Your move (interactive choice)
**Narration.** Pick one thing to hold onto this week. Notice one good thing social media gives you and let yourself enjoy it. Or check in on one community you are in, does it lift you or drain you. Or make something and post it, instead of only scrolling. Choose one now.
**On screen.** Three tappable cards, name a good thing, check a community, make something. The young person taps one and Nova reflects it back as their move. No wrong answer.

### Beat 7 · Close
**Narration.** One line to keep. The good side of social media is real, and every good thing comes with a catch, and I can hold both at once. That is not being negative. That is being awake. And if the only place you feel understood is online, tell a trusted adult too, you deserve people in both worlds.
**On screen.** The chosen card glows as "my move this week". Nova holds the heart and the eye up together one more time.

# VERSION C. PARENT (co view guide)

**Stage band:** Shaper to Independent (ages 14 to 16), extends the existing 092 spine. **Format:** the 092 lessons pattern, a short guide plus conversation starters, for the `public.lessons` table. Stance, curious not critical, coach not judge.

**the_idea.** Social media is genuinely good for your child in real ways. It keeps their friendships alive across distance, it helps them find people who understand them, it gives them a voice, and it lets them create instead of only consume. For a teenager who feels on the edge of things, an LGBTQ+ teen, a young carer, a child with a condition no one around them shares, finding their people online can lift a weight you may never fully see. That good is real. And every part of it comes with a catch, so we hold the two together.

**why_it_matters.** If you only ever name the dangers, your child learns that you do not understand the part of their life that matters most, and they stop talking to you about it. When you can name the good out loud, and genuinely mean it, you earn the right to talk about the catch too. The research is honest that belonging online is good for wellbeing, especially for teens who lack support at home, and that the same spaces are double edged. Naming both, not just the risk, is what keeps you in the conversation.

**try_this.** Ask your child to show you the good bit. "Show me a group or an account that actually makes your day better. What do you get from it?" Listen properly, and mean it when you say that sounds good. Then, gently, "and is there ever a version of that group that gets harder, that piles on or wears you down?" Let them tell you. If they say a community online is where they feel most understood, take that seriously and do not compete with it, instead widen it, "I love that you have that, who else in your actual day would have your back too." A good online community and a trusted adult are not rivals. Aim for both.

**key_message.** The good side of social media is real, and worth naming out loud, and every good thing on there comes with a catch. Your job is not to talk them out of the good, it is to help them keep it with their eyes open, and to make sure their online people are not their only people.

**digi_prompt (for DiGi, calibrated pathway, never allow or deny).** When a child says an online community is the only place they feel they belong, DiGi does not warn them off it and does not wave it through. It takes the belonging seriously, names it as real and good, then gently asks what the group is like on a hard day, and hands them one small move plus a trusted adult, so the good is kept and the catch is watched.

## Module notes
- Built to the Module 1 template, three versions from one idea, schools full seven beat with statutory mapping and assessment, kid short interactive with a real choice and the stage host Nova, parent short co view guide in the 092 fields.
- Positives are always taught with their risks attached, never as a clean win, the double edged sword is the spine of every beat.
- Calm mental health framing, belonging is protective and especially so for marginalised teens, held honestly alongside the risk, never scare tactics and never "so it is all fine".
- Claims attributed and kept to source, Digital Wellness Lab on belonging, a systematic review on social media and LGBTQ youth wellbeing, Ofcom Children and Parents 2025 on content creation, no invented statistics.
- Wiring is the build session's job later, schools and kid rows into school_lessons, parent row into lessons, per the existing handoff pattern.
