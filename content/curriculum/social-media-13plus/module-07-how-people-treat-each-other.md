# Module 7: How people treat each other

**13+ Social Media Literacy curriculum. Built in all three versions, on the approved Module 1 template.** British English, no dashes anywhere.

**The big idea:** online, a group can turn on one person fast, and a private message can go public in a screenshot. Knowing how it happens, and knowing your tools, means it does not have to swallow you.

**Learning outcome (one line, all versions):** I can name the ways people can be treated badly online, and the tools I use to protect myself or a friend.

**Mental health link, held calmly:** being got at online is different from a fallout in the corridor, because it follows you home, into your bedroom, onto the phone in your hand, so there is no natural moment where it stops. That is why it can weigh so heavily, and the research is clear it can cause real, lasting harm. Exclusion, being cut out of a chat or kicked from a game on purpose, hits the same deep need to belong. Girls are more often the target. None of this is your fault when it happens, and it is not a reason to fear being online. The point is to know the moves before you need them, block, report, keep evidence, tell someone, so you keep your footing and you are never handling it alone.

**Evidence anchors:** group chats and pile ons; cyberbullying in its forms, critical messages, fake accounts, spreading rumours; exclusion used as a deliberate weapon, removed from a chat, kicked from a game; public shaming and pile ons; screenshots leaked from private chats. Drawing on Anti Bullying Alliance and Internet Matters findings that online bullying follows a child home and can cause lasting psychological harm, that exclusion strikes at belonging, and that girls are more often targeted. Framing per Odgers (preparation over fear), Livingstone 4 Cs (this module sits in Conduct and Contact), and the safeguarding pattern of naming help every time.

**Cast:** first taught at 13 to 14, so KS3, host Orbit. DiGi is the through line guide.

# VERSION A. SCHOOLS IN DEPTH

**Stage:** KS3, Years 8 to 9, ages 13 to 14. **Length:** one lesson, about 55 minutes. **Format:** seven beat Rosenshine.

**Statutory and framework mapping:**
- RSHE (statutory): Respectful relationships including online, online bullying and how to get help, the fact that the same rules of behaviour apply online as off, and how information shared online can be misused.
- UKCIS Education for a Connected World strands: Online bullying, Online relationships, Online reputation.
- Computing national curriculum KS3: using technology safely, respectfully and responsibly, recognising acceptable and unacceptable behaviour.
- PSHE Association: bullying including cyberbullying, and how to seek support. Inside the school's KCSIE safeguarding duty.

**Ground rules (set before teaching, every lesson in this curriculum):** we talk about the scenarios and the tools, not about real incidents or real people in this room. No naming, no reliving specific fallouts. Questions are always welcome. This lesson names help at the end, and if anything here brings something up for you, you can speak to the named adult straight after.

**Baseline check (Rosenshine, start where they are):** one question on entry, "why can being got at online feel heavier than a fallout at school?" Collect three answers on the board, return to them at the end.

### Beat 1 · Recall and Do Now (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Quick recall on your whiteboards, name one tool a phone or app gives you to protect yourself, block, report, mute, privacy settings, anything. Take three, no comment yet. Today is about how people can treat each other online, and crucially, the tools you already have when they do.
**On screen.** The lesson title and the entry question, why online can feel heavier.
**Purpose.** Retrieval, and it opens on agency, not on threat.

### Beat 2 · New input in small steps (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Five shapes it can take, one at a time, and we name them so they lose their power to confuse. One, the pile on, a group chat or comment section where many people turn on one person at once, so it feels like everyone. Two, cyberbullying in its plain forms, cruel messages, fake accounts set up to get at someone, rumours spread to damage them. Three, exclusion used on purpose, being removed from a chat, kicked from a game, left out where everyone can see, which the research from the Anti Bullying Alliance treats as real bullying because it strikes at belonging. Four, public shaming, a mistake or a private moment held up for a crowd to judge. Five, the leaked screenshot, something said in private copied out and passed around. Two things to hold. The Internet Matters and Anti Bullying Alliance work shows this can follow you home and cause lasting harm, and that girls are more often targeted. And, this is not most of your time online, we name it so you are ready, not so you are afraid.
**On screen.** Five labelled cards revealed one at a time: The pile on, Cyberbullying, Exclusion on purpose, Public shaming, The leaked screenshot.
**Purpose.** Small steps, naming reduces fear, evidence attributed.

### Beat 3 · Worked example, model it aloud (8 min)
**Teacher script.** Watch me handle one calmly. Here is a made up situation. Someone in a class group chat posts a screenshot of a private message a classmate sent them, and others start piling on with laughing reactions. If that were me, first move, I do not fire back, that just feeds it. Second, I keep evidence, I screenshot what is there before it is deleted, times and names. Third, I block the account sending the worst of it and I report the post to the app. Fourth, I tell a trusted adult, because handling it alone is the trap. Notice the order, protect myself, keep proof, use the tools, tell someone. Calm and in sequence beats fast and angry every time.
**On screen.** A mocked chat with four labelled steps appearing as the teacher narrates, do not react, keep evidence, block and report, tell someone.
**Purpose.** Worked example, models a calm procedure before pupils try it.

### Beat 4 · Check for understanding, hinge question (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Hinge question, hold up A, B, C or D. Someone is spreading a rumour about a friend in a group chat and it is getting worse. What is the strongest first move? A screenshot it and reply with your own insult, B keep evidence, then block, report and tell a trusted adult, C leave the chat and say nothing to anyone, D wait and hope it stops. Show me. If most say B, we move on. If not, we look again.
**On screen.** The multiple choice hinge.
**Purpose.** Whole class check, do not proceed below about 80 percent.

### Beat 5 · Guided practice, we do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Together now. In pairs you have three short scenario cards, someone quietly removed from the group chat before a party, a fake account set up in a classmate's name, a private screenshot passed around to shame someone. For each, name what is happening, exclusion, a fake account, a leaked screenshot, and name the tools you would use in order, evidence, block, report, tell. I will take the first one with you, then you do two and three.
**On screen.** The three scenario cards and a two column frame, what is happening, my tools in order.
**Purpose.** Guided practice with high success, scaffolded on the procedure.

### Beat 6 · Independent practice, you do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** On your own. Write a short "if it happened, my plan" for a friend who came to you being targeted online, no real names, keep it general. What would you tell them to do first, which tools would you help them use, and which adult in this school or which service would you point them to. This is yours, I am collecting the plan, not any real story.
**On screen.** The independent task, a reminder of the tools, and the help routes for reference.
**Purpose.** Independent application, the skill framed as helping others, which builds agency.

### Beat 7 · Review, consolidate, signpost (7 min)
**Teacher script.** Back to the board. Our entry answers on why online can feel heavier, which holds up now? It follows you home, there is no natural stopping point, which is exactly why you use the tools and tell someone rather than carry it alone. Say the headline back to me, if people treat me or a friend badly online, I keep evidence, block, report and tell someone. This does not mean people online are cruel, most of your time is fine, this is the kit for when it is not. And this one matters, if you or a friend are being targeted, please talk to a trusted adult here, our named safeguarding lead, Childline on 0800 1111, or use the school's anti bullying route. You never have to handle it on your own.
**On screen.** The one line summary, a forward hook to Module 8, and the full support signpost.
**Purpose.** Spaced review, agency and help foregrounded, non frightening close.

**Endpoint assessment:** compare entry and exit answers to the "why it feels heavier" question, plus the independent Beat 6 plan, against the outcome, can the pupil name the forms of harm and the tools in a sensible order. **Differentiation:** support, the five cards and the four step order stay visible and pairs get sentence starters; stretch, ask what a bystander in a pile on can do, and how to tell reporting apart from telling tales, so agency extends to defending others. **DSL note:** this is a mild sensitivity module. Brief the DSL before teaching. If a pupil discloses that they or a friend are being bullied online, follow the school's safeguarding and anti bullying procedures the same day, and signpost the trusted adult, Childline on 0800 1111 and the school's anti bullying route as in the lesson close.

# VERSION B. KID (interactive lesson)

**Stage:** KS3, ages 13 to 14. **Host:** Orbit. **Format:** the seven beat explainer, about 4 minutes, then one interactive choice. Non judgemental, agency first, the young person is the decision maker. This is the child facing re cut of the same idea.

### Beat 1 · Hook
**Narration.** A fallout at school stays at school, you go home and it is quiet. Online is different. It comes with you, into your room, onto the phone in your hand, and there is no bell that ends it. That is why it can feel so big. It is not you being dramatic. It genuinely does not switch off on its own.
**On screen.** A phone lighting up again and again on a bedside table at night, notifications stacking, no off switch in sight.

### Beat 2 · Big idea and the guide
**Narration.** Orbit here. One idea today. When people treat each other badly online, you are not powerless and you are not meant to handle it alone. You have real tools, and using them early keeps a small thing from becoming a heavy thing. This is a kit, and today you learn to reach for it fast.
**On screen.** Orbit opening a small toolbox with four tools inside, keep evidence, block, report, tell someone.

### Beat 3 · The shapes it takes, in small steps
**Narration.** It shows up in a few ways. A pile on, when loads of people turn on one person at once so it feels like everyone. Cruel messages, fake accounts made to get at someone, rumours spread on purpose. Being cut out, removed from a chat or kicked from a game so you feel left behind, that counts too. And the leaked screenshot, something private copied out and passed around. Naming it is step one, because now you can see it for what it is.
**On screen.** Four quick icons, a crowd of arrows at one dot, a fake profile, an empty chair where a chat used to be, a screenshot flying out of a locked box.

### Beat 4 · Wrong then right
**Narration.** The trap feels like this, fire back, get the last word, sort it yourself. But firing back feeds it and handling it alone lets it grow. Here is the move. Do not react. Keep evidence, screenshot it. Block and report using the app's own buttons. Then tell someone you trust. In that order. Calm beats fast.
**On screen.** Split, left an angry back and forth getting louder, right Orbit calmly doing the four steps in order and the noise dropping away.

### Beat 5 · The twist
**Narration.** Here is the part that matters most. Telling an adult is not snitching and it is not weak. The people who get at others online are counting on you staying quiet and dealing with it alone. The moment you tell someone, you are not carrying it by yourself any more, and that is the strongest move there is.
**On screen.** One figure alone under a heavy cloud, then a second figure stepping in beside them and the cloud lifting.

### Beat 6 · Your move (interactive choice)
**Narration.** Pick one thing to lock in now, so it is ready when you need it. Learn where the block and report buttons are in your main app. Decide the one adult you would tell first. Or make the rule, keep evidence before I do anything else. Choose one now.
**On screen.** Three tappable cards, find block and report, name my one adult, keep evidence first. The young person taps one and Orbit reflects it back as their plan. No wrong answer.

### Beat 7 · Close
**Narration.** One line to keep. If people treat me or a friend badly online, I keep evidence, block, report, and tell someone. It does not follow me around if I do not carry it alone. And if this is happening to you right now, tell a trusted adult, or call Childline on 0800 1111, any time, day or night. You do not have to sort it by yourself. Next time, Orbit looks at messages from people you do not know.
**On screen.** The chosen card glows as "my move". The four tools sit ready in the toolbox. The Childline number stays clearly on screen. A small preview of a message from an unknown account, hinting at the next module.

# VERSION C. PARENT (co view guide)

**Stage band:** Explorer to Shaper (ages 11 to 15), extends the existing 092 spine. **Format:** the 092 lessons pattern, a short guide plus conversation starters, for the `public.lessons` table. Stance, curious not critical, coach not judge.

**the_idea.** Being treated badly online is not the same as a playground fallout, because it follows your child home. It arrives in the bedroom, on the phone in their hand, with no natural point where it stops, which is why it can weigh so heavily. It takes several shapes, a pile on where a group turns on one person, cruel messages, fake accounts and rumours, being deliberately cut out of a chat or a game, public shaming, and private screenshots leaked and passed around. The Anti Bullying Alliance and Internet Matters find it can cause real, lasting harm, that exclusion strikes at belonging, and that girls are more often targeted.

**why_it_matters.** When you understand that it follows them home, two things change. You take it seriously rather than telling them to just log off, which keeps them coming to you. And you can teach the response that actually helps, a calm sequence of keep evidence, block, report and tell someone, so your child feels equipped rather than trapped. Children who believe telling an adult will only make it worse tend to stay silent, so the most protective thing you can offer is to be the calm adult who helps them use the tools, not the one who takes the phone away.

**try_this.** Pick a quiet moment, not a crisis, and ask, "if someone was being got at in a group chat, what would you do?" Let them think it through, then add the four moves together, keep evidence, block, report, tell someone. Walk through where the block and report buttons live in their main app so it is not new when they need it. Agree that if anything happens, to them or a friend, the deal is they tell you and you help, you do not confiscate. Name the outside help too, so they know it exists, Childline on 0800 1111 and the school's anti bullying route.

**key_message.** Online unkindness follows a child home, so the answer is tools and support, not silence and not blame. Your job is to be the calm adult who helps them keep evidence, block, report and tell, so they never carry it alone.

**digi_prompt (for DiGi, calibrated pathway, never allow or deny).** When a child says someone is being cruel to them or a friend online, DiGi does not judge and does not brush it off. It stays calm, names that this is not their fault and not theirs to carry alone, walks them through keep evidence, block, report, and points them firmly to a trusted adult and to Childline on 0800 1111, holding a clear pathway rather than a yes or a no.

## Template notes (what every module carries, proven on Module 1)
- Three versions from one idea: schools full seven beat with assessment and statutory mapping, kid short interactive with a real choice and the stage host, parent short co view guide in the 092 fields.
- Non judgemental throughout, scenario first via distancing (made up situations, not "has this happened to you"), agency out, help signposted clearly in the schools and kid versions.
- Mild sensitivity handled per the safeguarding pattern, calm and non graphic, opens and closes on agency and support, names a trusted adult, Childline 0800 1111 and the school's anti bullying route, and carries a DSL note.
- Claims on lasting harm, exclusion and girls being more often targeted attributed to the Anti Bullying Alliance and Internet Matters, kept general, no invented statistics.
- Wiring is the build session's job later: schools and kid rows into school_lessons, parent row into lessons, per the existing handoff pattern.
