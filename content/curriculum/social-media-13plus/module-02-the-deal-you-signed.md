# Module 2: The deal you signed

**13+ Social Media Literacy curriculum. Built in all three versions, following the Module 1 template shape.** British English, no dashes anywhere. Extends the existing 092 lesson "Before you make an account", going deeper for 13+, not repeating it.

**The big idea:** making an account is signing a contract. You hand over your data and your attention, and in return you get an app that keeps a record of you. The age 13 line is not the day you are ready, it is the day the law lets you agree to that deal on your own.

**Learning outcome (one line, all versions):** I can explain what I am really agreeing to when I make an account, and one thing I do to protect my future self.

**Mental health link, held calmly:** knowing that a post can be screenshotted, resurface, or be read by family, school and strangers all at once can leave you watching yourself, editing yourself, never quite off duty. That low hum of self monitoring is real, and it is tiring. The point is not to be scared of posting. It is that understanding the contract, what is kept and what is not private, hands the agency back to you. You choose what you sign.

**Evidence anchors:** the age 13 rule sits in UK GDPR Article 8, where 13 is the age a child can consent to their own data being processed. It is a data protection line, not a maturity judgement. Sources kept disciplined and UK: House of Commons Library on the minimum social media age (age 13, UK GDPR); the ICO Children's Code on how children's data should be handled; Ofcom Children and Parents 2025 for any UK usage figure. Framing per Odgers (preparation over fear), Livingstone 4 Cs (this module is squarely the Contract C), boyd (permanence and context collapse must be taught, they are not obvious). No invented statistics.

**Cast:** first taught at 13, so KS3, host Orbit. DiGi is the through line guide.

# VERSION A. SCHOOLS IN DEPTH

**Stage:** KS3, Years 8 to 9, ages 13 to 14. **Length:** one lesson, about 55 minutes. **Format:** seven beat Rosenshine.

**Statutory and framework mapping:**
- RSHE (statutory): how data is generated, collected, shared and used online, and how information and data is shared and used online. Aligns to the 2026 guidance emphasis on data and how platforms are designed.
- UKCIS Education for a Connected World strands: Privacy and security, Online reputation, Copyright and ownership.
- Computing national curriculum KS3: using technology safely, respectfully and responsibly, and understanding how data is stored.
- PSHE Association: media and digital literacy, rights and responsibilities online.

**Ground rules (set before teaching, every lesson in this curriculum):** we talk about the deal and the scenarios, not about each other. No personal disclosures, nobody has to share anything they have posted. Questions are always welcome. If anything here worries you about yourself or a friend, the end of the lesson shows exactly where to go.

**Baseline check (Rosenshine, start where they are):** one question on entry, "when you tap I agree to make an account, what do you think you are actually agreeing to?" Collect three answers on the board, return to them at the end.

### Beat 1 · Recall and Do Now (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Last time we saw the app is built to hold your attention, and that you are the product. Quick recall on your whiteboards, in one line, why is the app free. Take three. Now today's question. When you make an account, you tap a button that says I agree. Agree to what? Hold that thought, we are about to read the small print together.
**On screen.** The lesson title and the entry question, what are you agreeing to.
**Purpose.** Retrieval, links to Module 1, surfaces the misconception that an account is just a login.

### Beat 2 · New input in small steps (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Four things to get straight, one at a time. One, the age 13 rule. Thirteen is not the law saying you are ready or mature. It is UK GDPR Article 8. Thirteen is simply the age you are allowed to say yes to your own data being collected and used. It is a data protection line, not a maturity line, and the House of Commons Library sets it out plainly. Two, the deal itself. Making an account is signing a contract. You agree to terms most people never read, and in return the platform collects what you do, how long you watch, what you tap, who you talk to, and builds a profile of you from it. Three, transparency. The law, through the ICO Children's Code, says services should be clear with children and put your interests first, but a lot of what is collected is still hard to see. Four, permanence. Once something is out, you cannot fully guarantee it is gone. That is the deal. Not scary. Just true, and worth reading before you sign.
**On screen.** Four labelled cards revealed one at a time: The age 13 rule, The contract, What they collect, Permanence.
**Purpose.** Small steps, one idea per card, corrects the maturity misconception directly.

### Beat 3 · Worked example, model it aloud (8 min)
**Teacher script.** Watch me trace one post. Someone posts a photo to their story, thinking it vanishes in 24 hours. Here is what actually happens. A friend screenshots it, so now there is a copy the poster does not control, that is permanence. The same photo can be seen by their nan, their form tutor and a stranger three towns over, all in the same minute, that is context collapse, an audience you never pictured all in one place. Two years later it resurfaces in a group chat. Notice, the poster did nothing wrong and broke no rule. They just did not know the deal. The photo was never really temporary, and the audience was never really just their mates. Naming that before you post is the skill.
**On screen.** A mocked story post, then labels appearing, screenshot, resurfaces, seen by family and school and strangers at once.
**Purpose.** Worked example, makes permanence and context collapse concrete before pupils try it.

### Beat 4 · Check for understanding, hinge question (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Hinge question, hold up A, B, C or D. Under UK law, why is 13 the age you can make your own account? A because you are mature enough at 13, B because it is the age you can consent to your own data being processed, C because the apps decided it, D because school allows it. Show me. If most say B, we move on. If not, we look again, because this one matters.
**On screen.** The multiple choice hinge.
**Purpose.** Whole class check on the core correction, do not proceed below about 80 percent.

### Beat 5 · Guided practice, we do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Together now. In pairs you have three scenario cards. One, a disappearing photo that did not disappear. Two, an old post from Year 7 read by a sixth form college years later. Three, a free app and the question, if I am not paying, what is it collecting from me. For each, name what is really going on, the contract, permanence or context collapse, and name one move that protects the person. I will take the first with you, then you do two and three.
**On screen.** The three scenario cards and a two column frame, what is really happening, my move.
**Purpose.** Guided practice with high success, scaffolded onto the four cards.

### Beat 6 · Independent practice, you do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** On your own, no names and nothing personal you do not want to write. Design a "future self test" you could run before posting anything. Write three questions you would ask yourself, for example, who could end up seeing this, would I mind this being screenshotted, would I be fine with this turning up in two years. Then write one setting or habit that protects your data, a private account, thinking before you tap I agree, checking what an app asks to access. I am collecting the thinking, not anything you have posted.
**On screen.** The independent task and a reminder of the four cards.
**Purpose.** Independent application, the digital footprint idea transferred to their own choices.

### Beat 7 · Review, consolidate, signpost (7 min)
**Teacher script.** Back to the board. Our entry answers on what you agree to, which one holds up now? You agree to hand over your data and to a record that can last. Say the headline back to me, making an account is signing a deal, and now I can read it before I sign. And this is not a reason to post nothing or fear the app. Plenty of what you share is fine and good. It is about knowing the deal so you choose on purpose. Next lesson we lock the doors, the settings that decide who can reach you and see you. And if anything today worried you about your own footprint or a friend's, you can always talk to a trusted adult here, or Childline on 0800 1111.
**On screen.** The one line summary, a forward hook to Module 3, and the support signpost.
**Purpose.** Spaced review, Goldilocks balance, links forward, help routes named.

**Endpoint assessment:** compare entry and exit answers to the "what are you agreeing to" question, plus the Beat 6 future self test, against the outcome, can the pupil explain the deal and name one protective move. **Differentiation:** support, the four cards stay visible and pairs get sentence starters, "what is really happening here is ...", "one move is ..."; stretch, ask whether the age 13 rule protects children well or just moves the problem, and why moderate, thoughtful posting is still healthy (Przybylski Goldilocks) so the lesson never tips into "never post anything". **DSL note:** not a sensitive module, but if a pupil's independent work discloses distress about something already posted, an image shared without consent, or pressure to post, follow the school's normal safeguarding route to the DSL.

# VERSION B. KID (interactive lesson)

**Stage:** KS3, ages 13 to 14. **Host:** Orbit. **Format:** the seven beat explainer, about 4 minutes, then one interactive choice. Non judgemental, agency first, the young person is the decision maker. This is the child facing re cut of the same idea.

### Beat 1 · Hook
**Narration.** You tap I agree. You barely look at it, nobody does. But that tap is you signing a deal. And it is worth knowing what is on your side of it before you shake hands.
**On screen.** A finger tapping a giant I AGREE button, then the button unfolding into a long scroll of small print.

### Beat 2 · Big idea and the guide
**Narration.** Orbit here. One idea today. Making an account is signing a contract. You hand over your data, what you watch, tap and say, and the app keeps a record of you. Here is the bit people get wrong. The reason you can make your own account at 13 is not that 13 is when you are ready. It is just the age the law lets you agree to your own data being used. A line about data, not about you.
**On screen.** Orbit turns "I AGREE" into a handshake, then flips a card that says "13 = your call on your data", not "13 = ready".

### Beat 3 · The deal, in small steps
**Narration.** So what are you handing over. What you post, plus everything they quietly collect, how long you watch, what you skip, who you message. They build a profile from it. And most of that is hard to see, even though the rules say apps should be clear with you. That is the deal. Not evil. Just real.
**On screen.** Three quick icons, a post, a hidden data stream, a profile being built.

### Beat 4 · Wrong then right
**Narration.** Here is the trap. You think a disappearing photo disappears. But someone screenshots it, and now there is a copy you do not control. Here is the move. Before you post, picture the whole audience, your mates, yes, but also your nan, a teacher, a stranger, and future you two years from now, all looking at once. If it passes that, post it. If not, you just saved yourself.
**On screen.** Split, left a photo vanishing then a screenshot flash catching it, right Orbit showing the same photo surrounded by nan, teacher, stranger, future you.

### Beat 5 · The twist
**Narration.** Here is the part they do not put in big letters. Nothing online is guaranteed to be truly gone, and nothing is guaranteed to be truly private. Screenshots exist. Old posts come back. That is not a reason to panic and post nothing. It is a reason to post on purpose, knowing the deal.
**On screen.** An "delete" button pressed, then a faint copy floating away that the button cannot catch, then Orbit calmly setting it down.

### Beat 6 · Your move (interactive choice)
**Narration.** Pick one thing to try this week. Run the future self test before you post, would I mind this in two years. Set your account to private so you choose who sees you. Or actually read what an app asks to access before you tap I agree. Choose one now.
**On screen.** Three tappable cards, future self test, go private, read before I agree. The young person taps one and Orbit reflects it back as their plan. No wrong answer.

### Beat 7 · Close
**Narration.** One line to keep. Making an account is signing a deal, and now you can read it before you sign. You still get to post, still get to enjoy it. You just do it with your eyes open. Next time, Orbit shows you the settings that decide exactly who can reach you and see you.
**On screen.** The chosen card glows as "my move this week". Orbit gives a small nod. A padlock appears, hinting at the settings module.

# VERSION C. PARENT (co view guide)

**Stage band:** Explorer to Shaper (ages 11 to 15), extends the existing 092 spine. **Format:** the 092 lessons pattern, a short guide plus conversation starters, for the `public.lessons` table. Stance, curious not critical, coach not judge.

**the_idea.** When your child makes an account, they are signing a contract. They hand over their data, what they watch, tap and say, and the platform keeps a record and builds a profile. The age 13 rule is often misread as "13 is when they are ready". It is not. Under UK GDPR it is simply the age a child can consent to their own data being processed, a data protection line, not a maturity judgement.

**why_it_matters.** Two things follow from getting this right. First, you stop treating 13 as a green light for readiness and start treating it as the moment to teach the deal, which is a very different conversation. Second, the thing that quietly wears on teenagers is permanence and context collapse, the sense that anything posted can be screenshotted, resurface, or be seen by family, school and strangers all at once. That feeds self monitoring and a low level anxiety. Understanding the contract does the opposite, it hands them back agency. They post on purpose instead of feeling watched.

**try_this.** Sit together and ask them to walk you through it, not test them. "When you made your account, did you read what you agreed to? Shall we look at what it actually collects?" Then, gently, the future self test, "who could end up seeing this, and would you be fine with it turning up in two years?" Make it a team habit, not a telling off. Agree one small thing together, a private account, reading what an app asks to access, or running that quick test before posting. And be clear it is not about posting nothing. It is about posting with their eyes open. A habit they help set is a habit they keep.

**key_message.** Making an account is signing a deal, and 13 is the age they can sign it, not the age they are ready. Your job is not to ban the post, it is to help them read the contract so they choose what they share on purpose.

**digi_prompt (for DiGi, calibrated pathway, never allow or deny).** When a child worries about something they have posted, or asks whether they should delete an account, DiGi does not judge and does not lecture, and it never simply says yes or no. It explains the deal calmly, asks what they are actually worried about being seen or kept, and hands them one small protective move plus a trusted adult to try it with.
