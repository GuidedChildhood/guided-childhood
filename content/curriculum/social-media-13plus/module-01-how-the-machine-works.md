# Module 1: How the machine works

**13+ Social Media Literacy curriculum. The template module, built in all three versions.** Approve this shape and the remaining modules follow it. British English, no dashes anywhere.

**The big idea:** the feed is built to hold your attention, not to look after you. Once you can see the design, you can take the wheel back from it.

**Learning outcome (one line, all versions):** I can name how social media is designed to keep me scrolling, and one thing I do about it.

**Mental health link, held calmly:** the design is built to remove stopping points and pull you back, which is why a session runs longer than you meant and can leave you flat, tired or wired. The point is not that you are weak. The machine is very good at holding on. Seeing how it works is the first move back to control.

**Evidence anchors:** the attention economy and the free app business model; addictive design features (infinite scroll, autoplay, notifications, streaks) linked to compulsive use; the ICO Children's Code ban on manipulative dark patterns. UK anchor for usage: Ofcom Children and Parents 2025. Framing per Odgers (preparation over fear), Livingstone 4 Cs (this module is mostly the Contract C), boyd (literacy must be taught, the digital native myth is false).

**Cast:** first taught at 13, so KS3, host Orbit. DiGi is the through line guide.

# VERSION A. SCHOOLS IN DEPTH

**Stage:** KS3, Years 8 to 9, ages 13 to 14. **Length:** one lesson, about 55 minutes. **Format:** seven beat Rosenshine.

**Statutory and framework mapping:**
- RSHE (statutory): Internet safety and harms, how data is generated, collected, shared and used online, over reliance on social media. Aligns to the 2026 guidance emphasis on how platforms are designed.
- UKCIS Education for a Connected World strands: Managing online information, Privacy and security, Health wellbeing and lifestyle.
- Computing national curriculum KS3: using technology safely and responsibly.
- PSHE Association: media and digital literacy.

**Ground rules (set before teaching, every lesson in this curriculum):** we talk about the design and the scenarios, not about each other. No personal disclosures. Questions are always welcome. If anything here worries you about yourself or a friend, the end of the lesson shows exactly where to go.

**Baseline check (Rosenshine, start where they are):** one question on entry, "why do you think apps like TikTok and Snapchat are free to use?" Collect three answers on the board, return to them at the end.

### Beat 1 · Recall and Do Now (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Last time we agreed a screen has no natural stop, you bring the stop. Quick recall on your whiteboards: name one design trick that keeps you on an app longer than you planned. Take three, no comment yet, we are about to explain every one of them.
**On screen.** The lesson title and the entry question, why is the app free.
**Purpose.** Retrieval, activates prior schema, surfaces misconceptions.

### Beat 2 · New input in small steps (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Here is the whole thing in one sentence. The app is designed to hold your attention, because your attention is what it sells. Now the six tools it uses, one at a time. One, the algorithm, it does not show you what is true or good, it shows you what keeps you watching. Two, infinite scroll and autoplay, the page never ends and the next thing plays itself, so there is no natural place to stop. Three, notifications, timed to pull you back, like a tap on the shoulder you did not ask for. Four, streaks, they turn using the app into a duty you feel bad breaking. Five, the business model, the app is free because you are not the customer, you are the product, your attention and your data are what is being sold to advertisers. Six, dark patterns, the off switch is hard to find on purpose, and the ICO Children's Code actually bans the worst of these for under 18s, which tells you they are real.
**On screen.** Six labelled cards revealed one at a time: Algorithm, Infinite scroll, Notifications, Streaks, The business model, Dark patterns.
**Purpose.** Small steps, one idea per card, respects working memory.

### Beat 3 · Worked example, model it aloud (8 min)
**Teacher script.** Watch me pull one apart. Here is a made up feed. I open it to check one message. First thing, a notification says someone you follow just posted, that is tool three, pulling me in. I tap, and it autoplays the next video before I have decided to watch, tool two. The video is exactly my thing, because the algorithm learned it, tool one. Twenty minutes later I look up and I never sent the message I came for. Notice I did nothing wrong. Four design tools did their job. Naming them as they happen is the skill.
**On screen.** A mocked feed with the four tools labelled as the teacher narrates the "just one more" spiral.
**Purpose.** Worked example, models the thinking before pupils try it.

### Beat 4 · Check for understanding, hinge question (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Hinge question, hold up A, B, C or D. An app shows you a video you never searched for, on a topic you once paused on for three seconds. Which tool is that? A notifications, B the algorithm, C streaks, D dark patterns. Show me. If most say B, we move on. If not, we look again.
**On screen.** The multiple choice hinge.
**Purpose.** Whole class check, do not proceed below about 80 percent.

### Beat 5 · Guided practice, we do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Together now. In pairs you have three short scenario cards, a broken streak that ruins someone's morning, an ad that seems to know a private conversation, a two hour session that started as one message. For each, name the design tool, and name one move that takes control back. I will take the first one with you, then you do two and three.
**On screen.** The three scenario cards and a two column frame, the tool, my move.
**Purpose.** Guided practice with high success, scaffolded.

### Beat 6 · Independent practice, you do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** On your own. Write a short "how it held me" account of a real recent session, no names, just the design. Which tools were working on you, and what is one setting or habit you will change, notifications off during homework, autoplay off, a stop you set before you start. This is yours, I am not collecting personal detail, I am collecting the analysis.
**On screen.** The independent task and a reminder of the six tools.
**Purpose.** Independent application, the skill transferred to their own life.

### Beat 7 · Review, consolidate, signpost (7 min)
**Teacher script.** Back to the board. Our entry answers on why the app is free, which one holds up now? The app is free because your attention is the product. Say the headline back to me, the app is built to hold my attention, not to look after me, and I can see the design now. Next lesson we open the settings and lock the doors, so the machine reaches you less in the first place. And if anything today made you think about your own use or a friend's, you can always talk to a trusted adult here, or Childline on 0800 1111.
**On screen.** The one line summary, a forward hook to Module 3, and the support signpost.
**Purpose.** Spaced review, links forward, help routes named.

**Endpoint assessment:** compare entry and exit answers to the "why is it free" question, plus the independent Beat 6 analysis, against the outcome, can the pupil name the design and one countermove. **Differentiation:** support, the six cards stay visible and pairs get sentence starters; stretch, ask why moderate use can still be fine (Przybylski Goldilocks) so the lesson never tips into abstinence messaging. **DSL note:** not a sensitive module, but if a pupil's independent work discloses compulsive use affecting sleep or mood, follow the school's normal pastoral route.

# VERSION B. KID (interactive lesson)

**Stage:** KS3, ages 13 to 14. **Host:** Orbit. **Format:** the seven beat explainer, about 4 minutes, then one interactive choice. Non judgemental, agency first, the young person is the decision maker. This is the child facing re cut of the same idea.

### Beat 1 · Hook
**Narration.** You open the app to do one thing. Reply to a message. Twenty minutes later you are deep in videos you never went looking for, and you still have not sent the message. That is not you being weak. That is the app doing exactly what it was built to do.
**On screen.** A phone, a single message notification, then the screen filling with an endless ribbon of videos while a clock spins.

### Beat 2 · Big idea and the guide
**Narration.** Orbit here. One idea today. The app is designed to hold your attention, because your attention is the thing it sells. It is free for a reason. You are not the customer. Your attention is the product.
**On screen.** Orbit lettering the words HOLD YOUR ATTENTION, then flipping a price tag from free to "you are the product".

### Beat 3 · The tricks, in small steps
**Narration.** It uses a few tricks. The feed shows what keeps you watching, not what is true. The scroll never ends and the next thing plays itself, so there is no natural stop. Notifications tap you on the shoulder to pull you back. Streaks make using it feel like a duty. None of it is an accident. All of it is designed.
**On screen.** Four quick icons, the feed, infinite scroll, a notification, a streak flame.

### Beat 4 · Wrong then right
**Narration.** The trap feels like this, just one more video, just five more minutes, and five more minutes never arrives. Here is the move. Name the trick out loud while it happens. That is autoplay. That is a notification. The second you name it, it loses half its grip, because now you can see it.
**On screen.** Split, left a spiral of "just one more", right Orbit calmly naming "that is autoplay" and the spiral stopping.

### Beat 5 · The twist
**Narration.** Here is the part they hope you never notice. There is no stop built in on purpose. The app will never tell you that is enough. So the stop has to come from you. That is not a punishment. It is you taking back the wheel the design took from you.
**On screen.** An endless ribbon, then Orbit setting a bright stop point on it.

### Beat 6 · Your move (interactive choice)
**Narration.** Pick one thing to try this week. Turn notifications off while you do homework. Turn autoplay off. Or set your stop before you start, not during. Choose one now.
**On screen.** Three tappable cards, notifications off, autoplay off, set a stop. The young person taps one and Orbit reflects it back as their plan. No wrong answer.

### Beat 7 · Close
**Narration.** One line to keep. The app is built to hold your attention, not to look after you. Now you can see it, which means you can steer it. Next time, Orbit opens the settings that keep the machine from reaching you in the first place.
**On screen.** The chosen card glows as "my move this week". Orbit winks. A small preview of a padlock, hinting at the settings module.

# VERSION C. PARENT (co view guide)

**Stage band:** Explorer to Shaper (ages 11 to 15), extends the existing 092 spine. **Format:** the 092 lessons pattern, a short guide plus conversation starters, for the `public.lessons` table. Stance, curious not critical, coach not judge.

**the_idea.** Social media apps are free because your child's attention is the product being sold. The app is designed, deliberately, to be hard to put down. The feed, the endless scroll, the notifications, the streaks, none of it is an accident, and none of it is your child being weak.

**why_it_matters.** When you know the app is engineered to hold attention, two things change. You stop blaming your child for struggling to stop, which keeps them talking to you. And you can teach them to see the design, which is the skill that actually protects them, far more than a time limit they will resent. The research is clear that seeing the mechanism, not fearing the device, is what builds lasting judgement.

**try_this.** Sit with your child and ask them to teach you. "Show me how your For You page works. Why do you think it shows you that?" Then, gently, "the app is free, so how do you think it makes its money?" Let them work out that the answer is their attention. Watch one real "just one more" moment together and name the trick out loud as a team, not as a telling off. Agree one small thing together, notifications off at homework or bedtime, autoplay off, a stop set before they start. A rule they help write is a rule they keep.

**key_message.** The app is built to hold their attention, not to look after them. Your job is not to police the minutes, it is to help them see the design so they can steer it themselves.

**digi_prompt (for DiGi, calibrated pathway, never allow or deny).** When a child says they cannot stop scrolling, DiGi does not judge and does not lecture. It names the design calmly, asks what the app did to pull them in, and hands them one small move and a trusted adult to try it with.

## Template notes (what every module carries, proven here)
- Three versions from one idea: schools full seven beat with assessment and statutory mapping, kid short interactive with a real choice and the stage Friend, parent short co view guide in the 092 fields.
- Non judgemental throughout, scenario first, agency out, help signposted in the schools and kid versions.
- Calm mental health framing, never scare tactics, and a deliberate Goldilocks stretch so it never tips into "social media is bad".
- Claims anchored to UK sources where learner facing, settings detail deferred to Module 3 so it lives in the database and stays current.
- Wiring is the build session's job later: schools and kid rows into school_lessons, parent row into lessons, per the existing handoff pattern.
