# Module 8: Strangers, DMs and grooming

**13+ Social Media Literacy curriculum. Built in all three versions, following the Module 1 template.** British English, no dashes anywhere. SENSITIVE module, DSL flagged.

**The big idea:** most contact online is fine, but a small number of adults use private messages to get close to young people and pull them somewhere private. You cannot always spot the person, so you learn to spot the pattern, and you always have a move.

**Learning outcome (one line, all versions):** I can name the signs that a private chat is going wrong, and the steps I take if it does.

**Mental health link, held calmly:** grooming works by making someone feel special, then making them feel they cannot tell anyone. That is a heavy thing to carry alone, and it is meant to feel that way. Naming the pattern out loud takes the weight off, because the plan is simple and the responsibility is never the young person's.

**The one line that must land:** if a chat ever feels wrong, you are not in trouble, and telling a trusted adult is always the right move. A child is never at fault for what an adult does to them.

**Evidence anchors:** NSPCC, criminals use private messaging to build trust with children and move them towards harm, which is why direct messages are the setting that matters here. Ofcom, under the Online Safety Act platforms must bring in measures to protect children from grooming, such as not suggesting adults as friends to child accounts and limiting who can message them. Reporting route, CEOP (the Child Exploitation and Online Protection command) for anything involving an adult contacting a child. Framing per Odgers (preparation over fear), Livingstone 4 Cs (this is squarely the Contact C), boyd (literacy must be taught, young people are not born knowing this).

**Cast:** first taught at 14, host Nova. DiGi is the through line guide, a calibrated pathway, never allow or deny.

# VERSION A. SCHOOLS IN DEPTH

**Stage:** KS3 to KS4, Year 9, ages 13 to 14. **Length:** one lesson, about 55 minutes. **Format:** seven beat Rosenshine. **SENSITIVE module, read the DSL note before teaching.**

**Statutory and framework mapping:**
- RSHE (statutory): how to recognise pressure from others online and have the confidence to resist it, that people can behave differently online including pretending to be someone they are not, the risks of contact with people the pupil does not know, and how and where to report concerns. Aligns to the 2026 guidance emphasis on spotting people who are not who they say they are.
- UKCIS Education for a Connected World strands: Online relationships, Privacy and security, Managing online information.
- PSHE Association: keeping safe, healthy relationships.
- Within the school's KCSIE safeguarding duty. Grooming is child abuse and is a safeguarding matter.

**Ground rules (set before teaching, and especially here):** we talk about the signs and the scenarios, not about each other. No personal disclosures in the room. Nothing here is about blaming anyone who has had a chat like this, because the fault is always with the adult, never the young person. Questions are always welcome. The end of the lesson shows exactly where to go, in and out of school.

**Baseline check (Rosenshine, start where they are):** one question on entry, "a stranger sends a friendly message and seems really nice, is that a problem?" Collect three answers on the board. The aim is to move the class from a yes or no to seeing that it is the pattern over time, not the first message, that tells you.

### Beat 1 · Recall and Do Now (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Last time we looked at how people treat each other in group chats. Today we go one to one, into private messages. Quick recall on your whiteboards, name one reason most of what happens online is in messages you cannot see from the outside. Take three. Hold that thought, because private is exactly where today's lesson lives.
**On screen.** The lesson title and the entry question, is a friendly stranger a problem.
**Purpose.** Retrieval, activates prior schema, sets the private messaging frame.

### Beat 2 · New input in small steps (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Start with the calm truth. Most people you meet online are fine, and most direct messages are nothing to worry about. This lesson is about a small number of adults who use private chat to get close to a young person, and how you spot it early. The NSPCC is clear that criminals use private messaging to build trust with children, because it is hidden, so that is where we look. Now the signals, one at a time. One, the flattery is turned up high, you are so mature, you are not like other people your age. Two, gifts or offers, game credits, money, followers, something for nothing. Three, secrecy, keep this between us, do not tell your parents. Four, moving the chat, come off here, message me on this other app instead, somewhere quieter and harder for an adult to see. Five, the requests escalate, small at first, then more personal, a photo, where you live, a meet up. Six, the mood swings, warm and lovely one minute, then guilt or pressure if you pull back. No single one of these proves anything. The pattern is the signal.
**On screen.** Six labelled cards revealed one at a time: Over the top flattery, Gifts and offers, Secrecy, Moving the chat, Escalating requests, Pressure and guilt.
**Purpose.** Small steps, one signal per card, builds the pattern without a graphic scenario.

### Beat 3 · Worked example, model it aloud (8 min)
**Teacher script.** Watch me read a chat the way I want you to read one. This is invented, no real person. A new follower messages a made up account, let us call the account Sam. First few messages, really warm, you seem so mature for your age, that is signal one. A day later, I can get you free credits for that game, that is signal two, the gift. Then, this app is annoying, add me on the other one, just us, that is signals three and four together, secrecy and moving the chat. Notice what I am doing. I am not deciding if this person is bad. I am counting the signals stacking up. Three signals in, Sam does not need to be sure. Sam needs to stop replying and tell a trusted adult. That is the whole skill, count the pattern, act early, do not wait to be certain.
**On screen.** A mocked message thread with each signal labelled as the teacher narrates. No images, text only, calm.
**Purpose.** Worked example, models pattern reading and early action, never dramatised.

### Beat 4 · Check for understanding, hinge question (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Hinge question, hold up A, B, C or D. Someone you have only ever talked to online says, delete these messages and add me on a different app so it is just us. What is that? A a normal thing friends do, B two grooming signals together, secrecy and moving the chat, C a technical problem with the app, D nothing to worry about. Show me. If most say B, we move on. If not, we look again at the six cards.
**On screen.** The multiple choice hinge.
**Purpose.** Whole class check, do not proceed below about 80 percent.

### Beat 5 · Guided practice, we do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Together now, in pairs, three short scenario cards, all invented, all through characters not yourselves. One, a new online friend keeps saying you are so much more grown up than your real friends. Two, someone offers a younger player free in game money if they send a photo back. Three, a person who was lovely last week turns cold and says you clearly do not care about them when they say no to a video call. For each, name the signal or signals, then name the response steps in order. I will do the first with you. The steps are the same every time, do not reply, do not meet, keep the evidence with a screenshot, block, and tell a trusted adult, who can report to CEOP.
**On screen.** The three scenario cards and a two column frame, the signal, my steps.
**Purpose.** Guided practice with high success, distancing through characters, response rehearsed.

### Beat 6 · Independent practice, you do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** On your own, and this stays yours. Design a short "how I would help a friend" card. A friend tells you a chat has started to feel off, ticking some of the signals we named. Write what you would say back, and write the five steps in the right order so your friend has them. No personal detail, no names, I am collecting the plan, not a story. The reason we practise helping a friend is that it is often easier to see clearly for someone else first, and it means you are ready if a friend ever comes to you.
**On screen.** The independent task and a reminder of the six signals and the five steps.
**Purpose.** Independent application, framed as helping, which keeps it safe and outward facing.

### Beat 7 · Review, consolidate, signpost (7 min)
**Teacher script.** Back to the board. Our entry answers on the friendly stranger, which one holds up now? It is not the first nice message, it is the pattern over time that tells you. Say the headline back to me, if a chat feels wrong I am never in trouble, and telling a trusted adult is always the right move. Two things to hold. You are never at fault for what an adult does, ever. And you are never stuck, there is always a step and always a grown up. If anything today brought something to mind, for you or a friend, you can talk to a trusted adult here in school, or Childline any time on 0800 1111. Anything involving an adult contacting a young person can be reported to CEOP, and the person on the reception of that is trained and calm. Next lesson we look at private images, the law, and how it protects you, not catches you out.
**On screen.** The one line summary, the two things to hold, and the full support signpost, a trusted adult, Childline 0800 1111, CEOP for reporting.
**Purpose.** Spaced review, agency and support out, help routes named in full, forward hook to Module 9.

**Endpoint assessment:** compare entry and exit answers on the friendly stranger, plus the Beat 6 helper card, against the outcome, can the pupil name the signs and lay out the response steps in order. **Differentiation:** support, the six cards and five steps stay visible and pairs get sentence starters; stretch, discuss why platforms now limit who can message under 18 accounts (Ofcom, Online Safety Act measures) so pupils see the system is being built to protect them, not just the individual being told to be careful. **DSL note (read first, this is a sensitive module):** grooming is child abuse and any disclosure is a safeguarding matter. Do not investigate or question a pupil yourself, do not promise secrecy, and do not remove or delete anything. Follow the school's safeguarding procedure and pass to the designated safeguarding lead the same day, following the school's normal timescale for an urgent concern. If a pupil discloses during the lesson, respond calmly, thank them for telling you, and follow procedure. Have the DSL briefed that this lesson is running.

# VERSION B. KID (interactive lesson)

**Stage:** ages 13 to 14. **Host:** Nova. **Format:** the seven beat explainer, about 4 minutes, then one interactive choice. Non judgemental, agency first, the young person is the decision maker. Calm, never fear based, never graphic. This is the child facing re cut of the same idea.

### Beat 1 · Hook
**Narration.** Most people you talk to online are fine. This is about the small number who are not, and how you spot them early, so you are never caught out. A stranger messages you, and they are really nice. Nice is not the whole story. What matters is what happens next.
**On screen.** A calm phone screen, one friendly message arriving, then a gentle question mark, no menace, just curiosity.

### Beat 2 · Big idea and the guide
**Narration.** Nova here. One idea today. A small number of adults use private messages to get close to young people, because private is hidden. So we do not try to guess who is good or bad from one message. We learn the pattern, and we always have a plan.
**On screen.** Nova drawing a private message bubble, then lettering the words THE PATTERN, NOT THE PERSON.

### Beat 3 · The signals, in small steps
**Narration.** Here are the signs a chat is going wrong. Flattery turned up too high, you are so mature, not like others your age. Gifts or offers, free credits, money, something for nothing. Secrecy, keep this between us, do not tell anyone. Moving the chat, come off here onto a quieter app, just us. Requests that creep bigger, a photo, where you live, a meet up. And guilt if you say no. One on its own might be nothing. Two or three of these stacking up is the signal to stop.
**On screen.** Six quick calm icons, a heart turned up too bright, a gift, a finger to lips, an arrow to another app, a growing staircase, a guilt cloud.

### Beat 4 · Wrong then right
**Narration.** The trap sounds like this, but they are so nice to me, I do not want to be rude, maybe I am overthinking it. Here is the move. You never owe a stranger a reply, and being unsure is enough. You do not have to be certain, you do not have to explain, and you never have to keep a secret an adult asks you to keep.
**On screen.** Split, left a worried "am I overthinking it", right Nova calm, a shield lighting up, the words YOU DO NOT OWE THEM A REPLY.

### Beat 5 · The twist
**Narration.** Here is the part that matters most. If a chat has felt wrong, you are not in trouble, not even a little. Nothing an adult does to a young person is ever the young person's fault. The secrecy is the trick, it is meant to make you feel you cannot tell. The moment you tell a trusted adult, the trick stops working.
**On screen.** A locked padlock labelled "our secret" quietly opening, the pressure lifting off, Nova steady beside it.

### Beat 6 · Your move (interactive choice)
**Narration.** If a chat ever feels off, here is your plan, five steps. Do not reply. Do not meet. Keep the evidence, take a screenshot. Block them. Tell a trusted adult, who can report to CEOP. Tap the step you think comes first.
**On screen.** Five tappable cards in order, do not reply, do not meet, screenshot, block, tell an adult. The young person taps and Nova reflects the whole plan back calmly. No wrong answer, every path leads to tell an adult.

### Beat 7 · Close
**Narration.** Two lines to keep. It is the pattern, not one message, that tells you. And if it ever feels wrong, you are never in trouble and never alone. Talk to a trusted adult, or Childline any time on 0800 1111. Anything with an adult contacting you can go to CEOP, and the people there are calm and on your side. There is a tool called Report Remove for private images too, and we cover that next time.
**On screen.** The five step plan glows as "my plan". The support routes stay on screen, a trusted adult, Childline 0800 1111, CEOP. Nova gives a steady nod.

# VERSION C. PARENT (co view guide)

**Stage band:** Explorer to Shaper (ages 11 to 15), extends the existing 092 spine. **Format:** the 092 lessons pattern, a short guide plus conversation starters, for the `public.lessons` table. Stance, curious not critical, coach not judge. Sensitive topic, calm tone throughout.

**the_idea.** A small number of adults use private messaging to build trust with a young person and pull the chat somewhere hidden. It is not about a scary stranger in a hoodie, it is a pattern, over the top flattery, gifts, secrecy, moving to another app, requests that slowly grow, and guilt if the child pulls back. Most contact your child has online is completely fine. The point is that they know the pattern and know they can always come to you.

**why_it_matters.** The single thing that keeps a child safe here is that they will tell you if something feels off. Grooming works by making a child feel they cannot tell anyone, so anything that makes home feel safe to talk in is real protection. The NSPCC is clear that private messaging is where this happens, because it is hidden. A child who knows they will not be blamed or have their phone taken is a child who talks. A child who fears the reaction stays quiet, which is exactly what an abuser relies on.

**try_this.** Keep it calm and keep it about the pattern, not about frightening them. Try, "if someone you only knew online started saying keep this between us, what would you make of that?" Let them reason it out. Agree together that no adult ever has a good reason to ask a young person to keep a secret from their parents, and that a request to move onto a different, quieter app is worth a pause. Most of all, say the sentence that matters, "if anything online ever feels wrong, you can tell me, and you will never be in trouble, and I will never just take your phone away." Then mean it, because the first time you react with calm, you have earned every conversation after it.

**key_message.** It is the pattern that tells you, not one nice message, and a child is never at fault for what an adult does. Your job is not to scare them off strangers, it is to be the calm place they come to the moment something feels off.

**digi_prompt (for DiGi, calibrated pathway, never allow or deny).** When a child says a chat has started to feel strange, or that someone wants to move to another app and keep it secret, DiGi does not judge and does not lecture. It names the pattern calmly, reassures the child they have done nothing wrong, and walks them to one clear step and a trusted adult, and where it fits, to Childline on 0800 1111 or reporting to CEOP.
