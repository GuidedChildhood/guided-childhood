# Module 9: Nudes, the law and sextortion

**13+ Social Media Literacy curriculum. Built in all three versions, following the Module 1 template.** British English, no dashes anywhere. SENSITIVE module, the most careful one, DSL flagged.

**The big idea:** the law here exists to protect young people, not to catch them out, and knowing that is what lets a victim ask for help instead of hiding. If a private image is ever used against you, you have done nothing wrong, you are not alone, and there is a clear route out.

**Learning outcome (one line, all versions):** I can explain, calmly, why the law is on a young person's side here, and I know the exact steps and help if a private image is ever shared or used against someone.

**Mental health link, held calmly:** the reason this topic matters so much is not the image, it is the fear. Fear of getting in trouble, fear of what people will think, is exactly what stops a young person telling someone, and silence is what lets it get worse. So we lower the fear on purpose. When a young person knows they are a victim, not a criminal, and knows help exists, the panic drops and the right steps become possible.

**The one line that must land, said more than once:** if this ever happens to you or a friend, you are not in trouble, you have done nothing wrong, and you are not alone. Help is calm, and it works.

**Evidence anchors:** UKCIS, Sharing nudes and semi nudes, advice for education settings, the response is protective and proportionate and children should not be unnecessarily criminalised. NSPCC and the National Crime Agency (NCA) on sextortion, financially motivated sexual extortion, with teenage boys aged 14 to 17 particularly targeted. Ofcom on the rise of AI generated and deepfake images. The IWF (Internet Watch Foundation) Report Remove tool, which helps a young person get a nude image of themselves taken down. Reporting route for an adult offender, CEOP. Framing per Odgers (preparation over fear), Livingstone 4 Cs (Conduct and Contact), boyd (this must be taught explicitly and calmly).

**Cast:** first taught at 14 to 15, host Nova. DiGi is the through line guide, a calibrated pathway, never allow or deny.

**A note on the law claim, kept accurate:** teach the principle, that making, sharing or possessing an indecent image of anyone under 18 is a criminal offence, even if the young person made it of themselves and shared it willingly. Then immediately teach the balance, that UK guidance is clear children should not be unnecessarily criminalised and that formal criminal sanctions are used only in exceptional cases, with a recorded outcome that avoids criminalising the young person available to police. Do not quote specific offence section numbers to learners unless confident. The whole point of teaching both halves together is that the second half is what makes it safe to seek help.

# VERSION A. SCHOOLS IN DEPTH

**Stage:** KS4, Years 10 to 11, ages 14 to 15. **Length:** one lesson, about 55 minutes. **Format:** seven beat Rosenshine. **SENSITIVE module, the most careful in the curriculum, read the DSL note before teaching.**

**Statutory and framework mapping:**
- RSHE (statutory): the law relating to sharing and viewing indecent images of children, that sharing and viewing such images is a criminal offence which carries severe penalties, and what to do and who to tell if concerned. Aligns to the 2026 guidance which names sextortion explicitly.
- UKCIS Education for a Connected World strands: Online relationships, Online reputation, Privacy and security, Health wellbeing and lifestyle.
- PSHE Association: intimate relationships, keeping safe.
- Within the school's KCSIE safeguarding duty, and follow UKCIS Sharing nudes and semi nudes as the specific response guidance.

**Ground rules (set before teaching, and held firmly here):** we talk about the law and the scenarios, never about each other, and no personal disclosures in the room. Nobody is being accused of anything and nobody is being warned off, we are learning how the law protects young people and what to do if something goes wrong. If you have ever been affected, this lesson is on your side, and the end of it shows exactly where to go. Questions can be written anonymously if that is easier.

**Baseline check (Rosenshine, start where they are):** one anonymous written question on entry, "true or false, if a teenager sends a private photo of themselves and it gets shared, they are the one in trouble." Collect the split without reading names. The whole lesson works to move the class from a false fear to an accurate, calmer understanding.

### Beat 1 · Recall and Do Now (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Last time we looked at how strangers use private messages. Today stays private but goes to a harder subject, images, the law, and a scam called sextortion. Before anything else, one promise from me. Nothing today is a warning or an accusation. It is information that protects you. Quick recall on your whiteboards, name one reason a young person might be scared to tell an adult if something went wrong online. Hold those, because fear is exactly what today takes apart.
**On screen.** The lesson title and the entry true or false about who is in trouble.
**Purpose.** Retrieval, names the fear early so the lesson can lower it, sets a safe frame.

### Beat 2 · New input in small steps (12 min)
**Teacher script.** Three things, calmly, one at a time. First, the law. Making, sharing or possessing an indecent image of anyone under 18 is a criminal offence, even if a young person made it of themselves and shared it willingly. That sounds frightening, so here is the half that matters just as much. UK guidance, UKCIS, is clear that children should not be unnecessarily criminalised, and formal criminal action against a young person is used only in exceptional cases, with police able to record an outcome that does not criminalise the young person. The reason you learn both halves is this, the law is built to protect you, so being a victim never makes you a criminal, which means you can always ask for help. Second, sextortion. This is a scam, financially motivated, a flirty new contact online turns friendly fast, asks for a private image, then threatens to share it unless you pay. The NCA reports that teenage boys aged 14 to 17 are particularly targeted. It is not a relationship, it is organised crime, and the person on the other end does this many times a day. Third, AI and deepfakes. Some apps claim to nudify an ordinary photo, making a fake image of a real person who never sent anything. Ofcom has flagged the rise of this. It is treated as a real harm, not a joke, and the person in the fake image is a victim, full stop.
**On screen.** Three labelled cards revealed one at a time: The law, and why it protects you. Sextortion, a money scam. AI and deepfake images. Text only, calm, nothing graphic.
**Purpose.** Small steps, the law taught with both halves together so it reduces fear rather than adding it.

### Beat 3 · Worked example, model it aloud (8 min)
**Teacher script.** Watch how the response works, through an invented character, no real person. Call him Alex, 15. A new contact online is flirty and quick, and within a day asks Alex for a private photo, then the moment it is sent, the mood flips, pay up or I send this to everyone you know. Now watch what Alex does, because this is the plan. Alex does not pay, paying only tells them it works and the demands keep coming. Alex does not send anything more, and does not keep talking. Alex keeps the evidence, screenshots the threat and the account. Alex tells a trusted adult, tonight, not alone. And Alex uses Report Remove, the IWF tool that helps get an image of an under 18 taken down. Notice the one thing Alex feels, panic, and the one thing that is not true, that this is his fault. He did nothing wrong. He was targeted by a criminal. The plan works, and the fear was the only thing standing between him and the plan.
**On screen.** A calm text only flow of the five responses in order, do not pay, do not comply further, keep evidence, tell a trusted adult, Report Remove. No image content shown at any point.
**Purpose.** Worked example, models the response and separates the victim from any blame, never dramatised or graphic.

### Beat 4 · Check for understanding, hinge question (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Hinge question, hold up A, B, C or D. A teenager is targeted by a sextortion scam and is terrified they will be in trouble. Which of these is true? A they are the criminal here, B paying will make it stop, C they are the victim, the law is on their side, and telling a trusted adult is the right move, D they should just delete the app and say nothing. Show me. If most say C, we move on. If not, we go back to the law card, because that fear is exactly what we are here to fix.
**On screen.** The multiple choice hinge.
**Purpose.** Whole class check, confirms the core message, victim not culprit, do not proceed below about 80 percent.

### Beat 5 · Guided practice, we do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Together, in pairs, three short scenario cards, all invented, all through characters. One, a friend is being pressured to pay after a private image was taken by a scam account. Two, someone finds out a fake nudify image of a classmate is being passed around. Three, a person is frozen with fear and sure they will be arrested if they tell anyone. For each, name what is actually happening, name the response steps in order, and name one true thing you would say to steady the person. I will take the first with you. The steps are steady every time, do not pay, do not comply further, keep the evidence, tell a trusted adult, and use Report Remove, and for a fake image of a classmate, it is a real harm and a safeguarding matter, tell an adult in school.
**On screen.** The three scenario cards and a three column frame, what is happening, the steps, the true thing I would say.
**Purpose.** Guided practice with high success, distancing through characters, response and reassurance rehearsed together.

### Beat 6 · Independent practice, you do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** On your own, and this stays yours. Write a short "steady a friend" card. A friend comes to you frightened after something like this. Write the five steps in the right order, and write three true, calm sentences to lower their panic, for example, you are not in trouble, this is not your fault, we will sort this together with a trusted adult. No personal detail, no names, no story, I am collecting the plan and the reassurance, nothing else. We practise steadying a friend because in a real moment, calm is the thing that helps most, and because you may be the person a friend comes to.
**On screen.** The independent task, the five steps, and a reminder of the support routes.
**Purpose.** Independent application, outward facing and safe, centres reassurance as a skill.

### Beat 7 · Review, consolidate, signpost (7 min)
**Teacher script.** Back to the board, and back to our entry question about who is in trouble. The accurate answer, a young person targeted here is a victim, the law is designed to protect them, and formal action against a young person is used only in exceptional cases. Say the headline back to me, if this ever happens to me or a friend, I am not in trouble, I have done nothing wrong, and I am not alone. Three things to hold. The fear is the trap, and the truth breaks it. There is always a plan, do not pay, do not comply further, keep evidence, tell a trusted adult, use Report Remove. And you are never alone with it. Talk to a trusted adult here in school, or Childline any time on 0800 1111. Report Remove, run by the IWF, helps get an image of an under 18 taken down. Anything involving an adult can be reported to CEOP. Everyone on the other end of those is calm and on your side. That is the lesson, the law protects you, and help works.
**On screen.** The one line summary, the three things to hold, and the full support signpost, a trusted adult, Childline 0800 1111, CEOP for reporting, and the IWF Report Remove tool.
**Purpose.** Spaced review, maximum reassurance, agency and support out, all four help routes named in full.

**Endpoint assessment:** compare the anonymous entry split with the exit understanding, plus the Beat 6 steady a friend card, against the outcome, can the pupil explain calmly why the law protects a young person and lay out the response steps and reassurance. **Differentiation:** support, the three cards, five steps and reassurance sentences stay visible and pairs get sentence starters; stretch, discuss why teaching the protective half of the law openly is thought to help victims come forward (UKCIS rationale) so pupils understand the reasoning, not just the rule. Keep every extension non graphic. **DSL note (read first, this is the most sensitive module):** any disclosure of a shared, requested or fake indecent image involving an under 18 is a serious safeguarding matter, and possible sextortion is treated as urgent. Do not view, request, forward, save or delete any image under any circumstances, even to check, as that can itself be an offence and can destroy evidence. Do not investigate or question the pupil yourself and do not promise secrecy. Follow the school's safeguarding procedure and pass to the designated safeguarding lead immediately, the same day, treating sextortion as urgent. The DSL follows UKCIS Sharing nudes and semi nudes and involves police and the IWF Report Remove route as appropriate. Brief the DSL before this lesson runs, and respond to any in lesson disclosure calmly, thank the pupil, and follow procedure.

# VERSION B. KID (interactive lesson)

**Stage:** ages 14 to 15. **Host:** Nova. **Format:** the seven beat explainer, about 4 minutes, then one interactive choice. Non judgemental, agency first, the young person is the decision maker. Maximum calm, never graphic, never fear based. This is the child facing re cut of the same idea.

### Beat 1 · Hook
**Narration.** This one is important, and I am going to keep it calm the whole way through. It is about private images, the law, and a scam. Before anything, one thing you need first, not last. If any of this ever happens to you or a friend, you are not in trouble, you have done nothing wrong, and you are not alone. Now let us understand it, so it can never trap you with fear.
**On screen.** A calm, steady screen, Nova present, the words YOU ARE NOT IN TROUBLE held quietly on screen from the start.

### Beat 2 · Big idea and the guide
**Narration.** Nova here. The law about private images of under 18s exists to protect young people, not to catch them out. Yes, images of under 18s are against the law, even self made ones, but UK guidance is clear that young people should not be unnecessarily criminalised, and formal action is only for very rare cases. Why does that matter so much? Because the fear of being in trouble is the exact thing that stops people asking for help. So we take that fear away right now.
**On screen.** Nova drawing a shield labelled THE LAW around a young person, not a pointing finger.

### Beat 3 · The three things, in small steps
**Narration.** Three things to know. One, the law is on your side, a young person in trouble here is a victim, not a criminal. Two, sextortion, a flirty new contact turns fast, asks for a private photo, then threatens to share it unless you pay. It is a money scam run by criminals, and boys around 14 to 17 are targeted a lot. It is not a relationship and it is not your fault. Three, fake images, some apps make a fake nude from an ordinary photo of a real person. That is a real harm and the person in it is a victim, always.
**On screen.** Three calm icons, a shield, a money scam mask, a fake image marked with a clear "not real". Nothing graphic.

### Beat 4 · Wrong then right
**Narration.** In the scary moment, the wrong voice says, just pay them and it will stop, and do not tell anyone. Both of those are the trap. Paying tells a criminal it works, so the demands keep coming. And staying silent is exactly what they are counting on. The right move is the opposite. Do not pay. Do not send anything more. And tell a trusted adult, tonight, not alone.
**On screen.** Split, left a panicked "just pay, tell no one", right Nova steady, the words DO NOT PAY, DO NOT STAY SILENT.

### Beat 5 · The twist
**Narration.** Here is the part that changes everything. The person doing this only has power while you are afraid and alone. The second you tell someone, that power drops. You are not the first person this has happened to, the people who help have seen it many times, and they are calm about it. And there is a tool made just for this, Report Remove, run by the IWF, which helps get an image of an under 18 taken down.
**On screen.** A panic cloud shrinking as a trusted adult and a calm helper appear beside the young person. The words YOU ARE NOT ALONE.

### Beat 6 · Your move (interactive choice)
**Narration.** Here is your plan if this ever happens, to you or a friend, five steps. Do not pay. Do not comply further, send nothing more. Keep the evidence, screenshot it. Tell a trusted adult. Use Report Remove to get an image taken down. Tap the step you think matters most. There is no wrong answer, and every one of them ends with you not being alone.
**On screen.** Five tappable cards, do not pay, send nothing more, screenshot, tell an adult, Report Remove. The young person taps and Nova reflects the full calm plan back.

### Beat 7 · Close
**Narration.** The lines to keep, and I will say them plainly. If this ever happens to you or a friend, you are not in trouble, you have done nothing wrong, and you are not alone. The law is on your side, and help is calm and it works. Talk to a trusted adult, or Childline any time on 0800 1111. Use Report Remove, run by the IWF, to get an image of an under 18 taken down. Anything with an adult can go to CEOP. Every one of those people is on your side.
**On screen.** The five step plan glows as "my plan". The full support list stays on screen, a trusted adult, Childline 0800 1111, CEOP, and the IWF Report Remove tool. Nova gives a steady, reassuring nod.

# VERSION C. PARENT (co view guide)

**Stage band:** Shaper (ages 14 to 16), extends the existing 092 spine. **Format:** the 092 lessons pattern, a short guide plus conversation starters, for the `public.lessons` table. Stance, curious not critical, coach not judge. The most sensitive topic in the curriculum, calm and reassuring throughout.

**the_idea.** There are three things a teenager needs to understand here, and all three are calmer than the fear around them. The law on private images of under 18s exists to protect young people, and UK guidance is clear they should not be unnecessarily criminalised. Sextortion is a money scam, a fast, flirty contact asks for an image then threatens to share it unless the young person pays, and teenage boys are heavily targeted. And AI can now fake a nude from an ordinary photo, which is a real harm to the real person in it. In every case, the young person is the victim, never the one at fault.

**why_it_matters.** The thing that hurts children here is not the image, it is the fear and the silence. A young person caught in a sextortion scam is often terrified they are in trouble and will lose everything, so they pay, and comply, and say nothing, which is exactly what the criminal wants. The single most protective thing you can do is make sure your child already knows, before anything ever happens, that they will not be in trouble with you and that you will help calmly. That knowledge is what turns a frightening night into a phone call to a parent instead of a spiral alone.

**try_this.** You do not need a heavy sit down, you need one clear message delivered calmly. Try, "I want you to know something, in case you ever need it. If anyone online ever pressures you about a private photo, or threatens you, you come straight to me, and you will not be in trouble, and we will sort it together." If it helps, explain the scam plainly, that it is organised crime, that they move fast and target lots of people, and that the answer is never to pay and never to face it alone. Name the tools together, Childline on 0800 1111 and the IWF Report Remove tool for getting an image taken down. The goal is simple, that your child hears from you, first, that you are their safe place, so that fear never wins.

**key_message.** In every version of this, your child is the victim, not the culprit, and the law is on their side. Your job is to lower the fear before it ever arrives, so that if the worst happens, your child comes to you calmly and quickly, and nobody faces it alone.

**digi_prompt (for DiGi, calibrated pathway, never allow or deny).** When a child raises private images, a threat to share one, or a fake image of someone they know, DiGi stays calm and never judges. It says plainly that the child is not in trouble and not alone, names the steady steps, do not pay, do not comply further, keep evidence, and get a trusted adult, and points to Childline on 0800 1111, the IWF Report Remove tool, and reporting to CEOP where an adult is involved.
