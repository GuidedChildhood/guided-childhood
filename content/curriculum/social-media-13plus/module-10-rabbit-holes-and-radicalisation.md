# Module 10: Rabbit holes and radicalisation

**13+ Social Media Literacy curriculum. Built in all three versions, following the Module 1 template. Sensitive module, written calm and non graphic, closes on agency and support. British English, no dashes anywhere.**

**The big idea:** the same engine that keeps you scrolling also narrows what you see, pushing you toward more extreme versions of whatever held your attention. Once you can spot the rabbit hole, you can climb out of it, and you can tell engineered outrage from the truth.

**Learning outcome (one line, all versions):** I can spot when a feed is narrowing toward more extreme content, and I can name one way I check a source or curate my way out.

**Mental health link, held calmly:** extreme content is not showing up because it is true or because it is what most people think. It is showing up because fear and anger hold attention, and the machine has learned that. Content that frightens you, that tells you the world is against you, or that pulls you toward hurting yourself, is engineered to provoke, not to help. Naming that is the first step back to solid ground. Nobody is weak for being pulled in. The pull is designed.

**Evidence anchors:** the rabbit hole and echo chamber effect (engagement optimisation narrows a feed toward more extreme versions of what held you); misinformation and fake news; deepfakes eroding trust in video and audio (Ofcom on misinformation and synthetic media); harmful content the algorithm can recommend, including self harm and suicide content (the Molly Russell inquest, where the coroner concluded that harmful content she had not gone looking for contributed to her death in a more than minimal way); and misogyny, the manosphere and extremism, where algorithms amplify manfluencer content especially to boys and trade in fear, anger and gender myths (The Conversation on online misogynist radicalisation). Framing per Odgers (preparation over fear), Livingstone 4 Cs (mostly the Content C, with Contact where extreme communities recruit), boyd (literacy must be taught explicitly).

**Cast:** first taught at 15, so KS4, host Nova. DiGi is the through line guide. Sensitive module, DSL flagged.

# VERSION A. SCHOOLS IN DEPTH

**Stage:** KS4, Year 10 and into Year 11, ages 15 to 16. **Length:** one lesson, about 55 minutes. **Format:** seven beat Rosenshine. **Sensitivity:** this lesson touches self harm and suicide content, misogyny and extremism. Teach it calm and non graphic, no images of harmful content, no method detail ever, agency and support first.

**Statutory and framework mapping:**
- RSHE (statutory): Internet safety and harms, how information and content online can be manipulated or false, the impact of viewing harmful content, misinformation and disinformation. Aligns to the 2026 guidance additions on deepfakes, spotting fake and AI generated accounts, and misogyny and incel culture.
- UKCIS Education for a Connected World strands: Managing online information, Online relationships, Health wellbeing and lifestyle.
- Computing national curriculum KS4: using technology safely, respectfully and responsibly, recognising inappropriate content.
- PSHE Association: media literacy and digital resilience, and the safeguarding standard for teaching sensitive content.
- KCSIE: this lesson sits inside the school's safeguarding duty and the Prevent duty. Notify and plan with the DSL before teaching.

**Ground rules (set before teaching, reinforced for a sensitive lesson):** we talk about how the content works and about the characters, never about ourselves or naming anyone. No personal disclosures, no describing specific harmful content or accounts. Questions are always welcome and can be written anonymously. If anything here worries you about yourself or a friend, the end of the lesson shows exactly where to go, and you can come to me at any point.

**Baseline check (Rosenshine, start where they are):** one question on entry, "how do you think a feed decides to show someone more and more of one kind of thing?" Collect three answers on the board, return to them at the end.

### Beat 1 · Recall and Do Now (5 min)
**Teacher script.** In Module 1 we agreed the feed shows you what keeps you watching, not what is true. Hold that thought. On your whiteboards, one line: what happens to a feed if the thing that keeps you watching is something that scares or angers you. Take three, no comment yet. We are about to follow that thread all the way down, and back up again.
**On screen.** The lesson title and the entry question about how a feed narrows.
**Purpose.** Retrieval, links to prior learning, surfaces the mechanism before the content.

### Beat 2 · New input in small steps (10 min)
**Teacher script.** One idea first. A rabbit hole is what happens when the feed keeps offering more extreme versions of whatever held you, because stronger reactions hold attention longer. Now the shape of it, one step at a time. One, the echo chamber, you get shown more of one view until it feels like everyone thinks that way, when really the feed just narrowed. Two, misinformation, false claims spread faster than corrections because they are built to be shared. Three, deepfakes, video and audio can now be faked well enough that seeing is no longer believing, which Ofcom has flagged as a growing problem for trust. Four, harmful content the algorithm can recommend, including self harm and suicide content. At the inquest into the death of Molly Russell, the coroner concluded that harmful content she had not searched for, content the algorithm served her, contributed to her death. That is why this is on the curriculum, and why the skill matters. Five, misogyny and the manosphere, where so called manfluencers push fear, anger and myths about girls and boys, and the algorithm amplifies it, especially to boys, because outrage performs. None of this is an accident of the machine. It is the machine working exactly as built.
**On screen.** Five labelled cards revealed one at a time: Echo chamber, Misinformation, Deepfakes, Recommended harm, Misogyny and extremism. No harmful imagery, icons only.
**Purpose.** Small steps, one idea per card, the mechanism named before any content.

### Beat 3 · Worked example, model it aloud (8 min)
**Teacher script.** Watch me follow one thread, using a character so we keep it at arm's length. This is Sam, sixteen. Sam watches one gym video, then a second that is half fitness and half a man telling him girls have it easy and he is being lied to. Sam pauses on it, just curious. The feed reads that pause as interest. By the weekend the feed is mostly that, angrier each time, and it feels like the truth because it is everywhere Sam looks. Notice what happened. Sam did not go looking for it. One pause, and the engine narrowed the world to the thing that provoked the strongest reaction. Now the climb out. Sam names it, this is a rabbit hole, this is engineered to make me angry. He checks who is actually saying it and why, he follows a couple of accounts that push the feed the other way, and he talks to someone he trusts. The feed did its job. Seeing it, and curating against it, is the skill.
**On screen.** A simple thread diagram, one neutral video narrowing toward an outrage account, then arrows showing the three moves out. No real accounts, no harmful content.
**Purpose.** Worked example through a character, distancing technique, models spotting and curating out.

### Beat 4 · Check for understanding, hinge question (5 min)
**Teacher script.** Hinge question, hold up A, B, C or D. A feed keeps showing someone more and more extreme videos on a topic they only paused on once. What is the best reading of why? A the topic is genuinely the most important thing happening, B the algorithm narrowed toward what provoked the strongest reaction, C most people agree with the videos, D the person secretly searched for it. Show me. If most say B, we move on. If not, we look again, because that reading is the whole lesson.
**On screen.** The multiple choice hinge.
**Purpose.** Whole class check, do not proceed below about 80 percent.

### Beat 5 · Guided practice, we do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** Together now. In pairs you have three short character cards, all at arm's length. One, a viral clip that turns out to be a deepfake, how would you check it before you share. Two, an account insisting everyone agrees with it, how do you test whether that is real or just a narrowed feed. Three, a friend whose feed has turned angry and bleak, what do you notice and what do you do. For each, name the mechanism, and name one move, check the source, curate the feed, or reach a trusted adult. I will take the first one with you, then you do two and three.
**On screen.** The three character cards and a three column frame, the mechanism, my move, who I would tell.
**Purpose.** Guided practice with high success, scaffolded, keeps the friend scenario pointed at help.

### Beat 6 · Independent practice, you do (10 min)
**Teacher script.** On your own, and this stays yours. Write a short "how I would spot it" plan. Pick one of the five, echo chamber, misinformation, deepfakes, recommended harm, or misogyny content, and write down the two or three signs you would look for, and the one thing you would do first. This is analysis, not a personal disclosure, so no accounts and no detail about specific content. If while you write this anything comes to mind about yourself or a friend, that is exactly what the end of the lesson is for.
**On screen.** The independent task and a reminder of the five mechanisms, with the support line already visible.
**Purpose.** Independent application, the skill transferred, help route kept in sight throughout.

### Beat 7 · Review, consolidate, signpost (7 min)
**Teacher script.** Back to the board and our entry answers on how a feed narrows. Which one holds up now? The feed narrows toward whatever provokes the strongest reaction, and extreme content is engineered, not agreed. Say the headline back to me. I can see the rabbit hole, and I can curate my way out. Two things to carry. Extreme content is designed to provoke you, so the anger or fear it stirs is a signal to slow down and check, not to share. And you never have to sit with the dark stuff alone. If anything you see online is about self harm or suicide, or is frightening you, please talk to a trusted adult here, or Childline on 0800 1111, any time, and for anything involving contact from someone that feels wrong you can report to CEOP. Next lesson we look at the inner life, how all this lands on your mood, and how to run your own honest check.
**On screen.** The one line summary, a forward hook to Module 11, and the support signpost, trusted adult, Childline 0800 1111, CEOP.
**Purpose.** Spaced review, links forward, help routes named clearly for a sensitive topic.

**Endpoint assessment:** compare entry and exit answers to the "how a feed narrows" question, plus the Beat 6 plan, against the outcome, can the pupil spot a narrowing feed and name one check or curation move. Judge understanding of the mechanism, never any personal disclosure. **Differentiation:** support, the five cards stay visible and pairs get sentence starters, and the friend scenario always offers "tell a trusted adult" as a complete answer; stretch, ask why the same amplification that spreads a rabbit hole can also spread a correction, so the lesson stays about seeing the machine rather than fearing the internet. **DSL note:** sensitive module, self harm, suicide content, misogyny and extremism. Plan with the DSL before teaching and flag the class in advance. If a pupil discloses viewing self harm or suicide content, contact from an extreme group, or their own distress, follow the school safeguarding procedure the same day and route to the DSL, and consider the Prevent duty where extremism is disclosed. Have the support signpost visible throughout, not only at the end.

# VERSION B. KID (interactive lesson)

**Stage:** KS4, ages 15 to 16. **Host:** Nova. **Format:** the seven beat explainer, about 4 minutes, then one interactive choice. Non judgemental, agency first, calm and non graphic, no harmful content shown. The young person is the one who spots it and steers out.

### Beat 1 · Hook
**Narration.** You pause on one video. Just curious. You did not follow it, you did not search for it, you just did not scroll past fast enough. By the weekend your feed is full of it, angrier every time, and it starts to feel like the truth, because it is everywhere you look. That is not you. That is a rabbit hole, and the feed dug it.
**On screen.** A single neutral video, a thumb pausing, then the feed narrowing into one repeating angry theme while the rest fades out. No real content, shapes and colour only.

### Beat 2 · Big idea and the guide
**Narration.** Nova here. One idea today. The feed narrows toward whatever gets the strongest reaction out of you, and nothing gets a stronger reaction than fear and anger. So the more something scares you or winds you up, the more you get. Not because it is true. Because it works.
**On screen.** Nova drawing a wide feed that funnels down to a single intense point, with the word NARROWS.

### Beat 3 · The shapes it takes, in small steps
**Narration.** It wears a few disguises. An echo chamber, where it feels like everyone agrees, when really your feed just got narrow. Misinformation, false stuff that spreads fast because it is built to be shared. Deepfakes, video and audio faked well enough that seeing is not believing any more. And the darker end, content about self harm, or so called manfluencers selling fear and anger about girls and boys, pushed hardest at people it thinks will react. None of it is an accident. All of it is the engine.
**On screen.** Four quick icons, an echo, a fake headline, a glitching face, a warning triangle. Calm, no detail.

### Beat 4 · Wrong then right
**Narration.** The trap sounds like this. Everyone knows this. They do not want you to see this. You are the only one being honest. That pull, that heat in your chest, that is the tell. Here is the move. When something makes you angry or scared enough to share it right now, that is the exact moment to stop and check. Who is really saying this, and why. Name it out loud. This is a rabbit hole. The second you name it, it loses its grip.
**On screen.** Split, left a feed glowing hot with outrage, right Nova calmly labelling it "engineered to provoke" and the heat draining out.

### Beat 5 · The twist
**Narration.** Here is the part they hope you never notice. The extreme stuff is not winning because it is right. It is winning because it holds attention, and attention is all the machine measures. And you are not stuck with the feed you have. You can curate it. Follow the things that pull it back toward calm and true. A feed is a garden, not a fixed thing, and you hold the trowel.
**On screen.** An angry narrowed ribbon, then Nova planting a few new accounts into it and the ribbon widening and cooling.

### Beat 6 · Your move (interactive choice)
**Narration.** Pick one thing to keep. When a post makes me want to share it out of anger, I check who made it first. When my feed feels like everyone agrees, I remember it just got narrow. Or, if something online is ever about self harm or is frightening me, I tell a trusted adult, I do not sit with it alone. Choose one now.
**On screen.** Three tappable cards, check before I share, remember it narrowed, tell a trusted adult. The young person taps one and Nova reflects it back as their move. No wrong answer, and the third card links straight to support.

### Beat 7 · Close
**Narration.** One line to keep. I can see the rabbit hole, and I can curate my way out. Extreme content is built to provoke you, so the anger it stirs is your cue to slow down, not to share. And you never have to carry the dark stuff on your own. If anything you see is about self harm or suicide, or is scaring you, talk to a trusted adult, or Childline on 0800 1111, any time. Next time, we look at how all of this lands on your mood, and how to check in with yourself honestly.
**On screen.** The chosen card glows as "my move". Nova gives a steady nod. The support line, trusted adult and Childline 0800 1111, stays on screen.

# VERSION C. PARENT (co view guide)

**Stage band:** Shaper (ages 14 to 16), extends the existing 092 spine. **Format:** the 092 lessons pattern, a short guide plus conversation starters, for the `public.lessons` table. Stance, curious not critical, coach not judge. Sensitive topic, calm and non graphic.

**the_idea.** A rabbit hole is what happens when a feed keeps serving more extreme versions of whatever held your child's attention, because stronger reactions hold attention longer. One pause on an angry video can be enough, and soon the feed is narrowed and heated, and it feels like the truth because it is everywhere they look. This is how misinformation, deepfakes, self harm content and the manosphere reach children who never went looking for any of it. At the inquest into the death of Molly Russell, the coroner concluded that harmful content she had not searched for contributed to her death. That is the risk, held calmly, and it is a design problem, not a failing in your child.

**why_it_matters.** When you understand that the extreme content is engineered to provoke, two things change. You stop reading your child's anger or fear as their new personality and start reading it as something the feed may be feeding, which keeps you on the same side. And you can teach the actual protective skill, spotting the rabbit hole and curating out of it, which lasts far longer than trying to police every video. The evidence is that seeing the mechanism, not fearing the internet, is what builds durable judgement, and that scare tactics tend to backfire at this age.

**try_this.** Watch something together without judgement and get curious about the machine, not the content. "Why do you think it showed you that? What would it show you if you paused on it?" If a strong claim comes up, check it side by side as a team, who is really saying this and why, and look at whether a striking clip might be a deepfake before anyone shares it. Agree a simple family line, that a post making you want to share it out of anger is the exact moment to slow down and check. And keep the door wide open on the hard end. Tell them plainly that if they ever see content about self harm or suicide, or something frightening, you want to hear it, you will not overreact or take the phone away, and you will help. A child who knows telling you makes things better is a child who will tell you.

**key_message.** The extreme content your child meets online is engineered to provoke, not agreed to be true. Your job is not to police every video, it is to help them spot the rabbit hole, curate their way out, and know they can always bring the dark stuff to you.

**digi_prompt (for DiGi, calibrated pathway, never allow or deny).** When a child describes a feed that has turned angry, frightening or bleak, DiGi does not judge and does not lecture. It names the mechanism calmly, that the feed narrows toward whatever provokes the strongest reaction, asks what the content is trying to make them feel, and hands them one small move, check the source or curate the feed, alongside a trusted adult to talk to. If a child raises self harm, suicide content or something that frightens them, DiGi stays calm, does not explore the detail, and routes them straight to a trusted adult and Childline on 0800 1111.
