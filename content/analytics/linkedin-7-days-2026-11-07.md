## What it says against the standing rules

1. **The opening rule holds.** The best post opens on a hard fact inside a
   complete sentence a stranger can finish: a deadline, a regulator, a child
   and an age. Second and third place both put a news fact in the first line.
2. **Scene openings still do not recruit.** "I have watched a YouTube feed
   narrow in my own house" (145) and "I set a timer on my children's screens"
   (88) sit near the bottom, exactly where the year put the scene openings.
   Same lesson: the scene belongs in paragraph five.
3. **The document did not rescue a soft opening.** Before the Box, 9 pages,
   169 and one reaction in three days. Donnelly's data says carousels do twice
   as well; ours, on one post, says the opening still decides. One post is a
   hypothesis. Try the next document with a number led first line before
   judging the format.
4. **Articles stay weak for reach** (269, 134), as the year said. They do earn
   comments: the Pathway Problem drew 7 on 134 impressions.
5. **Nine posts in seven days is the likeliest reason the week ran at half.**
   Our own rule is three to five, never two within about eight hours, and the
   year showed one strong post lifts the whole account. Nine posts split the
   week's attention nine ways. Donnelly posts nine a week with 1.2 million
   followers and a team. At our size, three good ones beat nine.
6. **Children and AI, and the Meta story, draw conversation.** The Meta whisper
   post has 6 comments in 10 hours, the highest comment rate of the week.
