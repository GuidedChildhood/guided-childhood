## The near controlled experiment, and the rule it gives us

Two Australia posts, same story, same month.

| Opening | Impressions |
|---|---|
| "Australia banned social media for under 16s. Three months later, more than eight in ten of those teenagers were still on it." | **15,186** |
| "Australia did the thing everyone is asking for. It banned social media for under sixteens. Eight in ten of those..." | **under 1,154** |

At least thirteen times the reach. Same subject, same evidence, same fortnight.
The only difference is where the number sits. In the winner it lands in sentence
two and the sentence is complete. In the loser it is still arriving in sentence
three, behind a throat clear.

**The rule, and it beats both rules we had before: open on a number inside a
complete sentence that contradicts what the reader expects.**

Not a bare number. "1.8 percent." on its own did 1,002 and nobody commented. Not
a delayed number either. The number has to be the surprise, and it has to arrive
in a sentence a stranger can finish reading.

## What this corrects

**The 28 day table was wrong, and it was four posts.** On 6 October we wrote
"open on the question you cannot answer, not the number you found", and today a
red team and I both leaned on it again. On a year of data the biggest post by a
factor of five opens on a number, and so do the second, third and seventh. The
declared uncertainty openings are good, around 6,036 and 3,173, but they sit
sixth and fourteenth, not first. Both are strong. Neither beats the other. The
28 day sample was too small to say anything and we twice let it overrule a
corpus study and a 75,842 impression post.

**The personal story question is settled, and the masterclass advice loses.**
Every scene led or confessional opening in the year sits in the bottom third.

| Opening | Impressions |
|---|---|
| "Last Tuesday evening, around 8pm, I sat with a parent..." | 1,701 |
| "I write a lot about how Haidt overreaches. This week I caught myself doing his trick." | 1,167 |
| "My legs were aching from old man's football." (video) | 1,164 |

Against 75,842 and 15,186 for the evidence openings. Note the ER though: the
scene posts run 3.76 and 1.11 percent, so the people who did read them engaged
normally. Scenes do not repel readers, they just do not recruit them. **That is
exactly the job they already do in our posts: paragraph five, not line one.**

**Reach and conversation are different products.** The highest ER of the year,
4.87 percent with 64 comments on only 3,288 impressions, is "For parents and
educators trying to navigate this landscape, the academic debate can feel
abstract." The biggest reach post ran 2.09. Naming a live argument or asking a
real question makes people talk. A surprising number makes strangers look.
Decide which one a post is for before writing it.

**One post is a quarter of the year.** The beta = 0.061 post is 75,842 of
275,018 impressions, and the chart shows the whole account stepping up when it
landed in June. Everything after it rides a bigger baseline.

- The Australia ban post is **15,186**, not 9,703. It has kept running since the

  showing up in the numbers.

## What to do more of

1. A precise, checkable number in sentence one or two, inside a full sentence.
2. A named researcher or institution in the first three lines.
3. A claim that cuts against the headline everyone has already read.
4. The live argument named directly when conversation is the goal.
5. Children and AI, which is the newest lane and already at 6,036 in one month.
6. Nothing in the body that asks the reader to leave. Thirteen clicks a year.
