## What it says against the standing rules

1. **The opening rule holds, and by a wide margin.** The winner opens on a
   number, a place and a finding in one complete sentence a stranger can
   finish. It earned five times the next post.
2. **The top three share a shape, and it is the one the Research Radar makes.**
   Each takes a claim people have already met and checks it against the source:
   "the press release went further than the paper did", "nobody has shown it
   was ever carried out", "here is what the viral version leaves out". The
   named thing, then the catch, then the limit, then a question.
3. **The winner was short.** Five sentences and a question, with the detail in
   the linked article. The year said articles are weak for reach. This one was
   not, on one post. The working guess: the intro stood on its own, so the
   article cost nothing. Try it again before believing it.
4. **Opening on what we cannot do did not recruit this time.** The readiness
   post opens on an admitted uncertainty about our own product and has 81 after
   two days. The October table that put uncertainty openings first
   (`drive-post-review.md`, section 1) is now two files out of date. The
   uncertainty belongs in the second paragraph, after the fact.
5. **The product led document is still low.** The Class Chat, 231 and no
   comments in a day, on Justin's own profile. A lesson for heads may simply be
   on the wrong account: that is the schools lane's post, not his.
6. **The question that worked asked for expertise.** "If hours is the wrong
   measure, what would you want researchers to count instead?" drew 18
   comments. "What did you settle before your child's first phone?" drew none.
7. **Eight posts in seven days again.** The rule is three to five. The winner
   had the feed to itself for a day. More posts on one profile has not meant
   more reach two weeks running.

## For the next posts

Open on the named study or body and what it found, in one complete sentence.
Second paragraph: the gap between the headline and the source. Then the limits
the source itself names. One closing question that asks what the reader would
measure, count or do. Three posts a week on Justin's profile. Anything written
for heads goes to the schools lane.

## What the screen cannot tell us

Clicks, saves, sends, follower change and who the viewers were. Each post's own
analytics page shows them and LinkedIn will export it. Until a post has a
reading at the same age as the post it is compared with (24 hours, 72 hours,
7 days), a younger post is understated. The register and the report that keep
this honest are `linkedin-post-register.json` and `linkedin-report-2026-11-11.md`
in this folder, built by `scripts/analytics/linkedin.mjs`.
