# Spanish KS2 spine: notes for the reviewer

This page explains the choices in `spine.json`, the file every Spanish lesson is generated from. It is written for a native Spanish speaking primary teacher who is checking it. Please treat every word, sound and example as open to challenge.

## What the spine is

- Years 3 to 6 (ages 7 to 11), six units a year, six lessons a unit, about 40 minutes a lesson.
- Year 3 is planned lesson by lesson. Years 4, 5 and 6 are planned unit by unit for now.
- French is built on exactly the same frame, so a teacher who teaches both meets the same unit topics in the same order.
- Castilian Spanish (Spain) is the model. Where Latin America says or pronounces something differently, the word's note says so, and children who use the Latin American form are right.

## Where the words come from

Topics are only the context. Words are chosen because they are frequent and because they lead straight into the GCSE lists, so a child who leaves Year 6 has a head start rather than a pile of words secondary school never uses again.

Each word carries two flags:

| Flag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `dfe_annex_e` | Every headword behind the entry is in Annex E of the DfE GCSE subject content. That annex is Crown copyright under the Open Government Licence, so it is our licensed core. 62 of the 136 Year 3 entries. |
| `gcse_list` | In Annex E, or on both the AQA and the Pearson Edexcel GCSE Spanish lists. The board lists are their copyright: we checked membership only and copied nothing. All 136 Year 3 entries. |

The DfE does not publish a full word list. Annex E holds the words the grammar needs (articles, pronouns, irregular verb forms, a few exemplars); the rest of each 1,700 word list is written by the exam boards to a DfE rule that 85% of words come from the 2,000 most frequent. One judgement call: sí is on the AQA list but the Edexcel list does not include yes at all, so its note says so.

Phrases taught as chunks (me llamo, ¿cuántos años tienes?, no sé) are checked through their parts, and inflected forms (mira, dice, llueve) through their dictionary form.

**Why Year 3 has 136 words, not 100.** Every lesson teaches three to five new words, which already means at least 108. On top of that, numbers 1 to 20 and the seven days are closed sets that have to arrive whole. Years 4, 5 and 6 then aim for about 105 each, so KS2 lands at about 450.

## The sound sequence

Every lesson opens with a short phonics slot. The order follows the 29 sound and spelling links in Annex C of the GCSE content, easiest and most frequent first. All 29 are covered by the end of Year 4.

| Year 3 lesson | Sound | Year 3 lesson | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| U1 L1 | a, o | U3 L1 | j |
| U1 L2 | e, i | U3 L2 | ga, go, gu |
| U1 L3 | u | U3 L3 | ge, gi |
| U1 L4 | ll | U3 L4 | single r |
| U1 L5 | ch | U3 L5 | rr, and r at the start |
| U1 L6 | ñ | U3 L6 | ue |
| U2 L1 | ca, co, cu | U4 L1 | ie |
| U2 L2 | ce, ci | U4 L2 | y |
| U2 L3 | z | U4 L3 | ay, ey, oy, uy |
| U2 L4 | que, qui | U4 L4 | written accents |
| U2 L5 | silent h | U4 L5 to U6 L6 | review in contrasting pairs |
| U2 L6 | v and b | | |

From Unit 4 Lesson 5 the slot pairs sounds children confuse: hard and soft c, hard and soft g, pero and perro, silent h and strong j, ll and y.

Year 4 adds gue and gui, cu before a vowel, the stress rule, soft d, soft b and v, ü, x, cc, consonant clusters, au and eu, the split vowels in día and frío, and letter names for dictation. Dictation starts in Year 4 because the new GCSE tests it.

**Pronunciation choices to check.** We model the Castilian th sound for ce, ci and z and accept s (seseo). We model ll and y as the same sound (yeísmo), and the tip mentions the sh sound heard in Argentina and Uruguay. For j we model the Castilian sound and say it is softer in much of Latin America.

## Grammar, little and often

One grammar focus a lesson, said plainly, then recycled. The Year 3 verbs are chunks first: soy, estoy, tengo, hay, me gusta, voy. Es and está arrive in Units 3 and 6 and are contrasted explicitly in Unit 6 Lesson 4. Gender comes in Unit 2 with un, una, el, la, and agreement follows with colours. Year 4 opens ser, tener and estar in the singular and the first regular verb pattern. Year 5 takes verbs to all six persons and adds the near future. Year 6 brings the past as chunks, then three time frames in one paragraph as the bridge to Year 7.

## How words come back

A word taught in one lesson returns in the next lesson, three lessons later, a unit later and a unit and a half later. When that falls after the last Year 3 lesson it moves into the first units of Year 4 (`recycled_words_by_lesson` in the outline). So every Year 3 word is retrieved at least three times and every one gets a long gap return. The class starter is built from these lists, and the same words go into each child's home practice.

New words are taught in themed groups. Mixing only happens between things children confuse: un and una, soy and estoy, the r sounds.

## The Year 3 story

Every word in the story is taught before it is read. The Planet Friends have no gender, so the story uses feliz and triste, which do not change. Speech uses Spanish quotation marks rather than dashes, and appears in speech bubbles in the lesson.

> Hoy es sábado. Hace sol. ¿Dónde está Bloop? ¿Está en la casa? No.
>
> Pebble está en el parque con Orbit. ¡Mira! Hay un perro. ¿Es Bloop? No.
>
> Pebble está triste. ¿Dónde está Bloop? ¿Está en la clase? No.
>
> «¡Mira!», dice Orbit. «¡Bloop está aquí!» Ahora Pebble está feliz. «¡Vamos!»

Every chant and grammar example in Year 3 is 100% known words. The floor is 95% for reading with the teacher and 98% for anything read at home.

## Please check these first

1. **Every Spanish example and chant line.** Is it natural Spanish a child in Spain would hear? Pay most attention to the story, the chants in Unit 4 and the weather sentences.
2. **Chunks before grammar.** Is it sound to teach estoy and soy as chunks in Unit 1 and leave the full contrast to Unit 6?
3. **Classroom commands.** We teach mira, escucha and repite in the tú form. Would you use the vosotros form (mirad, escuchad) with a class from the start?
4. **The sound tips.** Especially ll and y, the j, and the advice for v and b.
5. **Words we chose, and words we left out.** We left out lápiz (on neither GCSE list) in favour of bolígrafo, and chose naranja over manzana because only naranja is on both lists. Would you swap any back?
6. **¿Qué tal?** and **buenos días**: right register for a 7 year old greeting a teacher?
7. **Agreement in examples.** Any feminine form or article that reads wrong.

Mark anything you would change straight onto the JSON note field or into a reply. Every change you make becomes the version the lessons are written from.
