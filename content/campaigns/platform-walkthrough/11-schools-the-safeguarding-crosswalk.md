## Post text, LinkedIn (Justin)

A good online safety lesson makes a disclosure more likely.

That is what it is for. You tell a class they are never in trouble for
telling, and sometimes a child tells.

The lesson plan does not always say so, and then the teacher finds out at
ten past two on a Thursday.

So 17 of our 34 modules are safeguarding flagged, and each one carries a note
the teacher reads before the class sees slide 1. What this lesson can bring
up. What to say first. Who it goes to.

Before a flagged lesson starts, the screen asks three things of the adult:
who your safeguarding lead is, where a child goes if they need to step out
quietly, and it folds the script away so the class cannot read the notes
meant for you.

There is one page for the DSL that lists every flagged module with the
statutory ground it stands on. You type your lead, your deputy and your
policy once, and they print at the top of every staff briefing. That stays in
your browser. Nothing is sent to us, and the platform records no disclosures.
Every concern follows your school's own system.

The crosswalk is open to read. The staff briefings come with the free pilot:
two lessons for a term, no card, no contract, no pupil data.

Links in the first comment.

DSLs: what do you wish staff knew before they taught this content?
