## The three decisions inside that order

**The Bradford issue goes first, and it is not the strongest.** The Manchester
issue is better. But Bradford has a clock on it and Manchester does not, and an
issue that expires beats an issue that improves with waiting.

**The Readiness Papers run four weeks unbroken, in January.** They were built as
a series and they refer to each other. Breaking them up across two months loses
the thing that makes a series worth having. January also puts paper four, the one
with the ask, six weeks before the restrictions are expected, which is when a
parent is most likely to act on it.

**The Ferguson response opens the new year.** It is the most uncomfortable thing
in the set, since it concedes that a critic has correctly identified our
commercial interest. That is exactly why it should not be buried in week nine. An
audience that sees us publish a criticism of ourselves in the first week of
January reads everything after it differently.

## What would change this

**If the Ofcom or eSafety PDFs open**, nothing here moves, because none of these
thirteen depends on them. It would free GDC-050, 051 and 052, which are posts.

**If the regulations are laid**, which was due before the end of 2026 and had not
happened when this was written, the ban issues get more urgent and the order
should compress: move "The one number" and "What to do before March" forward.

**If the Bradford trial reports**, which has no published date, stop and write a
new issue. The first UK randomised result on this question is worth breaking any
schedule for.

## The cadence, and the honest case against it

One a week for thirteen weeks is a lot of long reading for one list, and these
are 1,800 to 2,400 words each.

Twenty seven issues have gone out before these and **we hold no open rate for any
of them**, anywhere in this repository. So this order is built on what the issues
are about and when they stop being true, which is knowable, rather than on what
the list responds to, which is not.

The first thing to do after issue one is paste the open rate somewhere. Three
issues of real data would change this plan more than any amount of reasoning
about it.
