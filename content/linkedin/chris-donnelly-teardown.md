# How Chris Donnelly grew past a million LinkedIn followers, and what we copy

Written 7 November 2026, from a research agent's sweep of his interviews, two
third party analyses of his feed, and press. LinkedIn itself is behind a login,
so nothing here was read off his profile. Most of his growth numbers are his
own, said out loud on a podcast. Treat them as his story, not audited fact.

Justin asked to copy the strategy. Per the feedback filter, his playbook is an
input, not an instruction: each point gets a verdict against our own goal (more
parents to the stage check) and our own year of numbers
(`content/analytics/linkedin-365-days-2026-10-20.md`), which win wherever they
disagree with him.

## The short version

He did not grow by posting a lot. He grew by finding out which topics were
already winning for other people, then turning them into one page reference
graphics people save and repost. One of those, a productivity cheat sheet, got
24,000 likes and 20,000 new followers in a single day. Everything after that was
doing more of the same, and later hiring a team to do it nine times a week.

**The number nobody puts in the success story.** His median likes per post fell
from 2,504 in 2024 to 1,452 in 2025 to 817 in 2026, about two thirds down
(MagicPost). The follower count keeps climbing on momentum while each post does
less. That is the 2026 LinkedIn he would be starting in today, not the 2023 one
he grew in.

## His playbook, with our verdict on each point

| # | What he does | Verdict | Why, and what it looks like for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pre validated topics.** Every morning he logged the follower counts of hundreds of creators and which posts gained them followers, then built his brand around six topics that were already winning. Copied the topics, not the posts. | **Adopt** | This is the part nobody copies and it is the engine. Each Monday the `linkedin-week` pass logs which posts in kids, screens, AI and social media are gaining followers for others, and we choose five pillars inside our lane from that, not from a hunch. |
| 2 | **The one page cheat sheet** as the signature format. His breakout, and still 78 percent of his feed as single images. | **Adopt** | Our best post of the year (beta = 0.061, 75,842 impressions) was already a one page research card. Make it a named series people save: one card per question parents actually ask, each with a proof path in the product (the stage by age card, the what the research actually says card, the first phone checklist). Built with `viral-post/stat-card.html`. |
| 3 | **A number in the first line.** 56 percent of his hooks, against 22 percent for the average creator. | **Already built** | Our own year says the same thing independently: the number inside a complete sentence that contradicts the reader. His data confirms the rule, it does not change it. |
| 4 | **Long, scannable copy.** About 400 words, nine word sentences, no hashtags. | **Already built** | Our 1,300 to 2,500 character body rule is the same length. Keep it. |
| 5 | **Batch writing.** Three or four hour blocks, never writes live. | **Adopt** | One Monday block for the week, which the Monday pass already sets up. Justin edits the batch in one sitting rather than writing on the day. |
| 6 | **Teach first, then a short P.S. with the offer.** | **Adapt** | A P.S. pointing to the profile, not a link. Our profile button gets 165 clicks a year against 13 from links in posts. "P.S. The stage check is three questions, no sign up. It is on my profile." |
| 7 | **Profile built like a landing page.** Headline ends "Follow for posts on...", banner with one button pointing at Featured, Featured pushes the newsletter. | **Adopt** | Cheapest win on the list, because the profile is already our funnel. Headline ending "Follow for the research on kids, phones and AI", banner pointing at Featured, Featured item one the stage check. The banner already exists in `content/linkedin/featured-banner/`; check it carries the single pointer. |
| 8 | **Newsletter early**, the follower he owns. | **Already built** | The Substack exists. The lesson is to put it second in Featured, after the stage check. |
| 9 | **A peer group at your level** who comment on each other's posts early. | **Adapt** | A handful of people already in our lane (researchers, child safety voices, parent writers) Justin genuinely talks with, commenting with something real in the first hour. Not a pod and never a swap of empty "great post" comments, which the 2026 authenticity update penalises. |
| 10 | **Comment a keyword to get the free thing.** About a third of his posts, his best performing mechanic. | **Decline as a default** | Our notes record it being throttled by the March 2026 authenticity update, he said himself in 2025 that audiences are tiring of it, and it does not fit an evidence first voice to parents. At most once a quarter, for a resource that genuinely helps. |
| 11 | **Eight or nine posts a week.** | **Decline** | It takes a content lead, two editors, a designer and a paid assistant. Van der Blom's data says posts under about eight hours apart eat each other. We stay at three to five, which our 90 day read set as the floor. |
| 12 | **A paid assistant replying to comments.** | **Decline** | Replies stay in Justin's own voice in the first hour. That is where the conversation posts earn their reach. |
| 13 | **Broad topics** (business, AI, leadership, LinkedIn itself). | **Decline** | The 2026 ranking rewards depth in one lane. Our narrow, research led lane is the advantage. Going broad to copy him would throw it away. |
| 14 | **Webinars and paid cohorts** as the money engine. | **Park** | Needs hundreds of thousands of followers to fill. Our engine is the stage check, then the product. |

## What does not transfer, said plainly

1. **He did not start from zero.** 20,000 to 35,000 followers and an eight
   figure exit story on day one.
2. **He grew in 2023 and 2024.** Organic views across LinkedIn are down about
   half since (van der Blom 2025, secondary summary). The cheat sheet format
   was new then and is everywhere now, so ours has to be the research card
   nobody else in our lane is making, not another productivity grid.
3. **Survivorship.** Thousands of people ran the same playbook and did not get
   to a million. His format worked because the topics were pre validated. That
   is the part to copy.

## The three things to do this month

1. **Profile as a landing page.** Headline, banner pointer, Featured order
   (stage check first, newsletter second). One evening.
2. **Start the topic log.** Each Monday, record which posts in our lane gained
   followers for the people who wrote them. After four weeks, fix five pillars.
3. **Launch the cheat sheet series.** One research card a week, saveable on its
   own, built from the stat card template, every number through the citation
   verifier first.
