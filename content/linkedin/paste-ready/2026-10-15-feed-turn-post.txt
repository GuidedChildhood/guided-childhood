I have watched a YouTube feed narrow in my own house, and I still could not tell you the moment it turned.

The Social Reckoning hands you that moment, and someone to blame for it.

Aaron Sorkin's drama about the Facebook Files is in UK cinemas now. It is a drama, not a documentary, and one critic summed up where it lands: it "aims the onus of blame on algorithms".

Some of that blame is earned. How a feed behaves is the responsibility of the people who design it and the people who regulate it, and since July last year Ofcom has required risky platforms to filter harmful content out of children's feeds.

But blame is a verdict. It arrives after the damage, and it does nothing for the child scrolling at nine o'clock tonight.

Independent tests keep finding feeds that drift somewhere dark for teenage accounts, in one test within five or six hours. They were test accounts, not real children, so I would not overstate it. The direction is still consistent.

So the thing worth teaching is not how an algorithm works. It is two smaller things.

A child who notices. This is getting narrower. This is getting darker. I did not choose this.

And a parent they will still tell. That one depends on what happened last time. If telling you once cost them their phone, the next thing they see is more likely to stay a secret.

I would back that connection over any setting I can switch on.

What would tell you that a feed had started choosing for you?
