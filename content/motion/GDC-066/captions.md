## LinkedIn (with the PDF)

RAND asked about a thousand American parents what they tell their teenagers about AI and homework. One in five had never raised it.

Among the rest, 56 percent encourage it in some way and 24 percent discourage it altogether. And of the teenagers who felt their parents discouraged it, 38 percent said they used it for homework anyway. That last figure comes from a small, unweighted group in one survey, so it can't show that what a parent says changes what a teenager does. None of it is British.

RAND's own advice is that parents probably need to be much more explicit for the message to land. "No AI in this house" is one sentence. The explicit version takes longer: what it may do, what it may not, and how the two of you check what it says.

The report is linked in the first comment. Five pages, swipe through.

## First comment

Schwartz and Diliberti, "Higher-Income Parents Use AI More and Encourage Their Children to Do the Same", RAND, RR-A4742-5, 12 November 2026. Parents: about 1,000, with a child aged 12 to 17, surveyed May 2026. The 38 percent comes from 533 teenagers matched to their parents, unweighted. US only.

## Instagram caption

One in five parents in a US survey had never talked to their teenager about AI and homework.

Of the teenagers who felt their parents discouraged it, 38 percent used it anyway. A small group, one survey, and not British, so treat it as a question rather than a verdict.

If you have said "no AI", try the longer version too: what it may do, what it may not, and how you'll check its answers together.
