THE STORY
  proposed title: A regulator has said out loud what the time limit debate keeps missing. It is the feed, not the clock.
  angle: I am not sure a time limit is the right question, and this week a regulator gave me a reason to keep doubting it. The European Commission's preliminary view of TikTok is about what the feed does with a child's attention once it has it, not about how many minutes they spend. Be exact about what this is: a preliminary finding, under EU law, that the company can answer. Then give a parent the one thing they can do with it, which is to look at what the feed is serving and not only at the timer.
  ONE STORY: On 6 November the European Commission said it had preliminarily found TikTok in breach of the Digital Services Act because of the way its For You feed keeps recommending more of whatever a user engages with, including content about sadness, isolation and body image.
  ONE TENSION: It is a regulator's preliminary view, not a ruling and not a study, and it does not apply in the UK. It is also the clearest official statement so far that the risk sits in how a feed is built, which is something a screen time limit does not touch.
  ONE GDC BELIEF: Screen time is not one thing. Two children can spend the same hour on the same app and be shown two different worlds. What a child is being served matters more than how long the timer ran.
  ONE TAKEAWAY: This week, sit beside your child for five minutes of their feed and ask what it has been showing them lately. You are looking at the feed, not the clock.

CONTRADICTORY OR NULL EVIDENCE
  The government's feasibility study reviewed today (GDC-RR-e22bd17322) found low certainty that social media use causes poorer mental health across the population. A regulator acting on a belief does not raise that certainty.
  The same study does support one half of the Commission's point: its narrative review found the type of content consumed plays a key role, positive or negative. That is support for 'content matters', not for 'this feed harms children'.
  TikTok's own account of its recommender system and its safeguards was not read. It exists and it will dispute this.

CLAIM BY CLAIM
  [VERIFIED] The European Commission has preliminarily found TikTok in breach of the Digital Services Act for the rabbit hole effect of its recommender system.
      basis: The first sentence of the Commission's page, published 6 November 2026. The word 'preliminarily' is the Commission's own and has to travel with the claim every time.
  [VERIFIED] The Commission says the For You feed shows a continuous stream of personalised videos, and that the more a user interacts with a type of content the more of it the feed recommends, including content related to mental health struggles and body image.
      basis: Stated on the page. Verified as what the Commission says about the design. It is the regulator's description, not an independent measurement.
  [QUALIFIED] These rabbit holes can have a detrimental impact on users' mental health, especially that of minors and vulnerable adults.
      basis: The page says exactly this, with the word 'can'. It is the Commission's assessment in a preliminary finding. The evidence behind it is not set out on the page and the longer press release was not read. Write it as what the Commission says, never as an established effect.
  [VERIFIED] The preliminary findings concern both TikTok's risk assessment and its mitigation measures.
      basis: Stated on the page.
  [UNSUPPORTED] TikTok has been found to have broken the law, or has been fined.
      basis: A preliminary finding is a step in a procedure. The company has the right to reply and no decision has been taken. Nothing on the page mentions a penalty.
  [UNSUPPORTED] This shows TikTok damages children's mental health.
      basis: It shows that a regulator believes the design can do so and is acting on that belief. That is evidence about the regulator, not about children.
  [UNSUPPORTED] This changes what TikTok must do in the UK.
      basis: The Digital Services Act is European Union law. The UK has its own regime. The same app is used here, which is the whole of the UK relevance.
