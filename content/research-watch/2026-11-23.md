SUPPORT: GTA VI Shouldn’t Spark a Moral Panic. It Should Spark New Rules.
  Type and date: blog post, After Babel (Substack), 2026-11-20
  Link: https://www.afterbabel.com/p/gta-vi-regulating-design
  Authors: Mariana Olaizola Rosenblat
  How much was read: full
  Study design: Policy essay by Mariana Olaizola Rosenblat and Srivatsan Raj, first published by the NYU Stern Center for Business and Human Rights on 18 November 2026, reposted on After Babel on 20 November with an introduction by Jon Haidt and Zach Rausch. Not a study. Cites a 2023 Resolver threat assessment, Rosenblat's own 2023 NYU survey of multiplayer gamers, Streams Charts viewing data and company results.
  Finding: Argues GTA VI should prompt new design rules and not a moral panic. States that decades of studies have found no established causal link between video games and violence. Says the age rating answers only the content question and misses contact with strangers and pressure to spend. Proposes a design code for games: private profiles and chat off by default, one tap reporting, parental controls over contact and spending, and a rating that covers interactive features.
  Limitations: Advocacy, and the lead author cites her own earlier survey. The 19 seconds grooming figure and the 51 percent extremism figure were read only as quoted in the essay, not at source, so neither is cleared. Sales and revenue figures are press estimates. US framing (ESRB, Congress).
  Our position: Closest After Babel has come to our own line: name the design fault, do not make the game the villain, and do not keep children from playing. Already in the pipeline as GDC 064 (PR 1660), so no new candidate from this run. Haidt and the authors may be named when quoted accurately.
  Parent problem it speaks to: none

CHALLENGE: Why Parental Consent Policies for Social Media Do Not Help Families
  Type and date: blog post, After Babel (Substack), 2026-11-09
  Link: https://www.afterbabel.com/p/parental-consent-social-media
  Authors: Jon Haidt
  How much was read: full
  Study design: Policy essay (first published in Tech Policy Press, 2 November 2026). Cites an American Economic Review study on the collective action trap, a 2025 Harris Poll, a JAMA cohort on reading scores, a Karolinska cohort on inattention, a 2026 Nature Human Behaviour study of Italian students, and PISA 2025.
  Finding: Argues parental consent laws keep families in a collective action trap and do not protect children through puberty. Calls for enforced age minimums, safer design, and the burden moved from parents to companies.
  Limitations: Advocacy, not a study. The brain plasticity passage is a mechanism asserted, not shown. The reading score and attention figures are correlational. The OECD names reading habits as one suspect among several for the PISA fall.
  Our position: Mostly agree: the trap is real and companies should carry the burden. Challenge on the evidence only: a minimum closes one door, but the next app and the day it lifts still need a prepared child. The UK ban is already law for Spring 2027, so the consent debate is context for us. Haidt may be named and quoted accurately.
  Parent problem it speaks to: Everyone else has one, so I gave in
