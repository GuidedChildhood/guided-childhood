## Candice Odgers (researcher)

Ally. Small average effects, risk concentrated in vulnerable children, against moral panic. Closest to our thesis.

- **School Phone Bans and Adolescent Mental Health in Iceland.**
  paper, JAMA network open, 2026-10-01
  https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.40893
  Review: SUPPORT. Read the abstract (JAMA Network Open, Odgers a co author). 70 Icelandic schools, 22 brought in a phone ban between 2018 and 2023, difference in differences over three survey waves. Bans were not associated with meaningful changes in depression, anxiety, hostility or wellbeing; the authors conclude bans "implemented in isolation may be insufficient". Backs our line that removing the device is not the plan, preparing the child is. Care: the depressive symptoms estimate sits just above zero (CI 0.01 to 1.30), so say "no meaningful improvement", never "made things worse" or "proved bans fail". Repeated cross sectional, not causal. Candidate for the radar.

## Jonathan Haidt (researcher)

Respected opponent on mechanism. Agrees the worry is real; we differ on cause (screen time versus what the child carries in and what the feed does with it). Challenge only where the evidence lets us.

- **Why Parental Consent Policies for Social Media Do Not Help Families**
  post, After Babel (Substack), 2026-11-09
  https://www.afterbabel.com/p/parental-consent-social-media
  Review: CHALLENGE, carefully, and mostly agree. Read in full. Argues parental consent laws keep families in a collective action trap and do not protect puberty; wants enforced age minimums and the burden moved to companies. Where we agree: the trap is real, and companies should carry the burden. Where we can challenge on the evidence: a minimum moves the trap to the next unregulated app and to the day the ban lifts, so preparation is still the parent's job (Justin's boxing ring point); the brain plasticity and reading score passages are correlational or mechanism asserted, and the PISA fall is named by the OECD as one suspect among several. UK note: our ban is already law for Spring 2027, so this US and EU consent debate is context, not our fight. Answer post angle: "A ban closes one door. Who prepares them for the next one?" Haidt may be named, quoted accurately.

- **Two Practices That Understate the Mental Health Risks Associated With Adolescent Social-Media Use.**
  paper, Perspectives on psychological science : a journal of the Association for Psychological Science, 2026-10-20
  https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916261482033
  Review: CONTEXT, worth a full read before any reply. Haidt and Rausch argue correlation coefficients and blended exposures understate harm, and that risk elevations should be reported. Partly fair, and close to our own point that the average hides the vulnerable tail. Do not challenge until the paper itself is read; agreeing on the tail is the stronger move.
