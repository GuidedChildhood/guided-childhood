# Primary maths in England, Reception to Year 6
## Schemes of learning, practice apps, intervention, and the two statutory checkpoints

Lane: primary maths, England. Compiled 17 September 2026 for Guided Childhood.
Purpose: so we can tell a parent what their child is learning, which resources
their school is likely using, and what device use their child will meet at
school, and so we can build our own parent resources in the same genre in our
own words without ever redistributing another publisher's material.

## READ THIS FIRST: how everything here is known

**Not one page in this file was fetched.** The session egress proxy answered 403
to CONNECT for every external host attempted, including `www.gov.uk`,
`assets.publishing.service.gov.uk`,
`explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk`, `whiteroseeducation.com`,
`thirdspacelearning.com`, `cirrushq.com` and `en.wikipedia.org`. A direct curl
confirmed it: `curl: (56) CONNECT tunnel failed, response 403`. That is an
organisation egress policy, not the sites refusing us, and per
`/root/.ccr/README.md` it must be reported rather than retried or routed around.
The session WebSearch budget then reached 200 of 200.

So the evidence tag on every single row below is **[SEARCH INDEX ONLY]**. There
are zero [FETCHED] rows. The column is carried per row anyway so the tag travels
with the claim when rows are lifted out of this file.

**Three consequences you must respect when using this file.**

1. **There is not one exact quotation in here.** Where a distinctive string is
   worth preserving I have written it as `index snippet: ...`. An index snippet
   is what a search result returned about a page, which is not the same thing as
   the sentence on the page. Do not put any of it in quotation marks in
   published copy, and do not attribute it as a publisher's own wording, until
   somebody opens the page.
2. **Prevalence figures are the weakest thing here and some are absent on
   purpose.** Where a number needs a primary source I could not reach, the cell
   says "not found, fetch blocked" rather than carrying an estimate. That
   includes the White Rose usage figure, which is the one the market most wants.
   Section D explains why I am not giving you a number for it.
3. **The inventory itself is the reliable part**, and that is where the value
   is: resource name, publisher, subject, year groups, what it is, whether a
   device is needed and whose device. Those attributes are well attested across
   multiple independent search results and are safe to build a parent facing
   resource map on.

**The two figures that survive scrutiny best** are the DfE's own Maths Hubs
management information percentages, because they are counts of programme
participation published by the department rather than vendor marketing. Even
those are [SEARCH INDEX ONLY] and need the PDF opened before publication.

## A. PARENT FACING MATERIAL

This is the genre we are entering, so this is the section to build from. Every
shape described here is [SEARCH INDEX ONLY]. Confirm lengths and formats before
we imitate them, because a wrong length is the kind of detail that makes a
competitor's parent resource look researched and ours look guessed.

### White Rose Education, by a distance the most generous

White Rose is the one publisher in this lane that treats the parent as a real
audience rather than an afterthought, and its parent offer splits into four
distinct shapes.

1. **Free downloadable home learning packs.** Reported as released during term
   breaks, each pack a workbook holding a week's worth of activities, free to
   download and print, with answer sheets included. That is the exact shape we
   should copy: one week, printable, answers included, pegged to a school
   holiday so the parent has a reason to open it.
   https://whiteroseeducation.com/parent-pupil-resources/maths/free-downloads
2. **Topic videos.** Reported to run roughly five to ten minutes and to explain
   not only what children are learning but why it is taught that way. That
   second half is the whole reason these work. A parent taught column
   subtraction one way, watching their child taught another, does not need a
   worksheet. They need that five minute video.
   https://whiteroseeducation.com/parent-pupil-resources/maths
3. **The 1 Minute Maths app.** Reported as free, one minute tasks on adding and
   subtracting, multiplying and dividing and subitising, with randomly generated
   questions so the child learns the concept rather than a sequence of answers,
   instant on screen marking, and no login and no internet needed. One minute is
   the smallest credible unit of maths practice anyone in this market has
   shipped, and it sits right next to our own ten minutes a day.
   https://whiteroseeducation.com/1-minute-maths
4. **The scheme documents left public.** Reported that parents can download the
   scheme of learning and topic overviews for their child's year, which is what
   lets a parent line up what is happening in class this term.
   https://whiteroseeducation.com/resources/maths/primary

**Calculation policies**, the "how we teach column addition" document Justin
asked about. I did not get the White Rose version before the budget ran out.
What the index did show is the shape these take in practice: a school hosted PDF
titled "Power Maths White Rose Edition LKS2 Calculation Policy", carrying a
Pearson copyright line and the footer string
`index snippet: Copying permitted for purchasing institution only`. So in this
lane the calculation policy is typically a Pearson or White Rose document that
individual schools republish on their own websites for parents, one per key
stage phase, method by method. **We write our own from scratch. We cannot host
theirs.**
https://weobleyprimary.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Power-Maths-White-Rose-Edition-LKS2-Calculation-Policy.pdf

## B. DEVICE EXPOSURE

### The two statutory checkpoints, which is the question the product turns on

**The Year 4 Multiplication Tables Check is taken on screen.** This is the
best attested statutory fact in the file. Multiple independent search results
agreed, including the gov.uk assessment framework listing, the DfE Education Hub
parent explainer and the Explore Education Statistics release. The format
reported consistently across them: 25 questions on the 2 to 12 times tables,
6 seconds to answer each question, a 3 second rest between questions, 3 practice
questions in a try it out area beforehand, a 3 week window in June, no longer
than 5 minutes on average to complete, and no expected standard or pass mark.
Pupils are reported to take it at school on a desktop computer, laptop or tablet
such as an iPad. Tag: [SEARCH INDEX ONLY], and the framework PDF should be
opened before any of this is quoted.
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/multiplication-tables-check-assessment-framework
https://educationhub.blog.gov.uk/2022/11/what-is-the-multiplication-tables-check-and-why-is-it-important/

**The Year 6 Key Stage 2 maths SATs are taken on paper.** Reported consistently:
three papers, Paper 1 arithmetic at 40 marks in 30 minutes, Paper 2 reasoning at
35 marks in 40 minutes, Paper 3 reasoning at 35 marks in 40 minutes. Reported
weighting is 83 to 93 marks (75 to 85%) for Number, Ratio and Algebra and 17 to
27 marks (15 to 25%) for Measurement, Geometry and Statistics, with Paper 1
entirely Number, Ratio and Algebra. Search results state that the 2026 KS2 tests
continue to be administered in paper based format and that arrangements for
2025 to 2026 remain unchanged. Tag: [SEARCH INDEX ONLY]. **This is the claim
most worth verifying**, because "on paper" is a negative claim and the
administration guidance is the document that settles it.
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/key-stage-2-tests-test-administration-guidance-tag/key-stage-2-test-administration-guidance

**So the clean line for a parent, once verified: screen at Year 4, paper at
Year 6.** That single contrast is the most useful thing in this file for us. A
Year 4 parent in England has a child sitting a timed, on screen, 6 seconds per
question national test, and schools overwhelmingly prepare for it with a screen
based app. So a Year 4 parent is negotiating device practice whether they have
framed it that way or not.

**The administration is going digital even though the tests are not.** Reported:
the Primary Assessment Gateway has been replaced by the National Curriculum
Assessments Portal built by Pearson, with multi factor authentication, and KS2
test attendance registers are no longer paper based but completed online.
Reported: the Standards and Testing Agency has a transformation programme that
will explore the potential for digital approaches in its assessments. So on
screen KS2 tests are a direction of travel, not an announced plan. **No pilot
announcement was found. Do not claim one.**
https://plc.pearson.com/en-GB/news-and-insights/news/pearson-awarded-uk-government-contract-englands-national-curriculum

We name a resource, describe what it does in our own words, link to the

should build to regardless of what those terms pages turn out to say.

Three figures circulate in search results, 70%, 80% and 85% of primary schools,

and KS1, and 6.6% (1,101) on Mastering Number at KS2. Those are departmental
counts rather than vendor marketing, which makes them the figures to lean on,
and they are still [SEARCH INDEX ONLY] until the PDF is opened.
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67460515cdd295aea880995e/Engagement_with_the_maths_hubs_programme.pdf

**What we can safely say today:** White Rose Maths is very widely described as
the most used primary maths scheme in England. We can say "the most widely
used". We cannot put a percentage next to it. A hostile expert would take an
uncited 80% apart in one question and we would deserve it.
