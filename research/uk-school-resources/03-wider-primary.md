# The wider primary curriculum in England, Reception to Year 6
## What schools actually use, subject by subject, excluding English and maths

Researched 17 September 2026 for Guided Childhood. Lane: every primary subject
except English and maths.

---

## METHOD AND ITS LIMITS. Read this before trusting any row.

**WebFetch was blocked for every domain in this session.** The agent proxy
returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED` for www.kapowprimary.com, explorify.uk,
teachcomputing.org, www.gov.uk, assets.publishing.service.gov.uk and
en.wikipedia.org. The proxy README says to report a policy block rather than
route around it, so I did not.

**Everything below therefore rests on WebSearch**, which returns result links
plus a summary of the indexed pages. That is a real retrieval, so the URLs and
the figures attached to them are evidence, but it is second hand evidence:
I read a summary of the publisher's page, not the page. Consequences:

1. **Publisher claims are labelled as publisher claims.** "Trusted by over 4500
   schools" is what the seller says, not an audited figure. Where two figures
   from the same publisher disagree I print both and say so.
2. **Statutory and curriculum quotes are marked "wording as reported" unless
   I can see the words inside a quoted string in a search result.** House rule
   2 says quote never paraphrase. Where I could not get the words safely I
   write "verbatim text not obtained" instead of reconstructing them. Nothing
   in this file is a reconstruction.
3. **The search budget ran out at 200 calls** partway through the sweep, so some
   named resources have a verified official URL and a "not verified" in the
   other columns. Those gaps are listed at the end under WHAT IS STILL OPEN.
   Nothing was filled in by inference.
4. Where a figure is US only or global only, the row says so. A global figure
   is not a UK adoption figure.

**Useful side finding.** schemesupport.co.uk maintains subject by subject
comparison pages of the primary schemes schools are choosing between (science,
music, PE, computing, RE, French). It is a competitor map for this whole lane:
https://www.schemesupport.co.uk/science ,
https://www.schemesupport.co.uk/subjects/primary-pe-schemes ,
https://www.schemesupport.co.uk/computing , https://www.schemesupport.co.uk/re ,
https://www.schemesupport.co.uk/music , https://www.schemesupport.co.uk/french

**A note on Kapow.** Kapow Primary sells a multi subject scheme. I verified its
computing page and its RSE and PSHE pages directly in search results. I did not
get to its science, history, geography, art, DT, music, French or RE pages
before the budget ran out, so those subjects appear as one honest row rather
than eight invented ones.

## A. PARENT FACING MATERIAL

The honest summary: most of this lane publishes nothing for parents. Schemes
are sold to a subject lead, and the parent is reached only through whatever the
school chooses to forward. There are four exceptions, and they are the genre we
should be building in.

**1. The RSHE parent leaflet. Two to four sides of A4, and the clearest model
we have.**

Jigsaw publishes two distinct documents and this distinction matters. One is a
general "Jigsaw Information Leaflet for Parents and Carers", which explains what
Jigsaw is, what the six puzzles are and what mindfulness means in practice,
naming the Jigsaw Chime and Jigsaw Jerrie Cat. The second is a separate
"RSHE: a guide for parents and carers" leaflet, dated September 2023, which
exists only because RSE is the part parents ask about. Shape: a short leaflet,
in the region of two to four sides, laid out as headed sections rather than
prose, written to be sent home whole. Hundreds of individual schools host copies
on their own sites, which is how a parent actually receives it.
Sources: https://jigsawlivestcmsuk.blob.core.windows.net/umbraco-media/u2gblcnk/rshe-a-guide-for-parents-and-carers-leaflet-sep-2023.pdf ,
https://gorsehall.tameside.sch.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Jigsaw-Information-Leaflet-for-Parents-and-Carers.pdf ,
https://www.learningopps.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Jigsaw-RSE-information-for-Parents-and-Carers.pdf

Kapow does the same thing in two pieces: an "RSE and PSHE Parent Information"
page, described as information to share with parents about what children learn,
the key topics covered and curriculum expectations; and a separate
"RSE and PSHE Parents' and Carers' Guide" in its subject toolkits, which is the
document a school hands out. Kapow also runs sessions for schools on how to
communicate with families about RSE, which tells you the real customer problem
is the conversation, not the content.
Sources: https://www.kapowprimary.com/subjects/rse-pshe/curriculum/rse-pshe-parent-information/ ,
https://www.kapowprimary.com/subject-toolkits/rse-parents-guide/

SCARF approaches the same need from the policy end rather than the leaflet end:
a free Relationships Education policy template and guidance document, which is
the document a school consults parents with.
Source: https://www.coramlifeeducation.org.uk/scarf

1decision publishes guidance to schools on working with parents on the 2025
changes: https://www.1decision.co.uk/news/rshe-guidance-how-schools-can-work-with-parents

**2. The knowledge organiser. One side of A4, and the thing that actually comes
home in the bag.**

Shape, verified: "the process of categorising, clarifying and condensing the key
facts of a particular topic and arranging the information into a user-friendly
format, ideally on a single A4 page", and it "sets out in detail what children
are expected to know by heart by the end of the topic". It carries the exact
facts, dates, events, characters, concepts and precise definitions. The parent
use case is stated explicitly in the sources: it lets parents keep track of what
their child is learning, and it gives them "an easy tool for parents to quiz
children at home (and children to quiz parents!)". Schools publish sets by year
group and by term, and some write their own parent guide to how to use one.
That last document, a school's own guide to using the organiser, is the format
closest to what Guided Childhood should publish.
Sources: https://thirdspacelearning.com/blog/knowledge-organisers/ ,
https://www.stbarnabasprimaryschool.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Parent-Guide-to-Knowledge-Organisers.pdf ,
https://www.stanleyroad.oldham.sch.uk/knowledge-organisers.html

**3. The online safety publishers, who are parent facing by design.**

Childnet runs a full parents and carers hub, and its Smartie the Penguin stories,
six of them for ages 3 to 7, are free to download and explicitly intended to be
shared as a family as well as in class. CEOP Education publishes parent pages
for both Jessie and Friends (4 to 7) and Play Like Share (8 to 11), so the same
cartoon a child watches in school has a parent page behind it. Be Internet
Legends is delivered with Parent Zone, whose whole business is parents. Common
Sense Media's parent side is the best known version of this anywhere.
Sources: https://www.childnet.com/parents-and-carers/ ,
https://www.childnet.com/resources/smartie-the-penguin/ ,
https://www.ceopeducation.co.uk/parents/jessie-and-friends/ ,
https://www.ceopeducation.co.uk/parents/playlikeshare/ ,
https://parentzone.org.uk/article/be-internet-legends-google

**4. The platforms whose parent side is the product.** Tapestry, ClassDojo and
Seesaw. Covered in section B, because for a parent they are not reading material,
they are an app to install.

**Also worth knowing.** The DfE itself published a parent facing explainer when
the new RSHE guidance landed, on the Education Hub blog, in plain language:
"New RSHE guidance: what parents need to know", July 2025.
https://educationhub.blog.gov.uk/2025/07/new-rshe-guidance-what-parents-need-to-know/
That is a direct competitor for the same reader, and it is Crown copyright, so
worth checking whether it is OGL and therefore buildable on.

## B. DEVICE EXPOSURE. What a Reception to Year 6 child's screen time through
## school actually consists of.

This is the section we care most about, so it is written as what a parent would
recognise, and every claim is sourced.

### The shape of it, honestly

A primary child's school device exposure is not one thing. It is four different
things, and only two of them put a screen in the child's own hands.

**Layer one, the teacher's screen, which is most of it.** Charanga, Kapow, Snap
Science, Explorify, BBC Ten Pieces, Jessie and Friends, Jigsaw songs, Get Set 4
PE technique videos. In all of these the device is at the front of the room and
the child is watching or singing or talking. Charanga is the clearest case: the
whole scheme is delivered through an on screen player. Explorify is a picture
on the board to argue about. For a parent's purposes this is television, not
device use, and it is worth saying so plainly, because it is where most of the
"my child is on screens all day at school" worry actually comes from.

**Layer two, the child's own login, which is the real thing.** This is Purple
Mash, Seesaw, Espresso Coding, Discovery Education Coding, Oddizzi, Natterhub,
Scratch, Code.org, Duolingo for Schools, and, in schools that have gone all in,
a full Google or Microsoft account. Here the child has a username, a password
and a record.

**Layer three, the parent's app.** Tapestry, ClassDojo, Seesaw Family, Class
Charts Parents. The child does not touch these. The parent installs them and
receives the child.

**Layer four, the physical device that is not a screen.** The BBC micro:bit.
Programmed in a browser, then flashed to a board with sensors and lights. Every
UK primary school was offered a class set of 30, almost 700,000 devices, funded
by Nominet, delivered between September 2023 and March 2024.
Source: https://microbit.org/news/2023-05-15/bbc-microbit-the-next-gen-groundbreaking-initiative-announced/

### The five to be concrete about

**Purple Mash (2Simple).** Each child has their own login. Purple Mash is a
walled garden, so the child's browsing is inside 2Simple's own environment
rather than the open web. Two things a parent should know. First, **free home
access is included for all pupils**, so the login works from the sofa, which
means a Year 2 child can have a real school account before they have anything
else. Second, **every school has a parent portal**, off by default: the school
ticks "Use parent code" when it prints pupil login cards and a parent code is
generated automatically on each child's card. So the parent's access exists
whether or not their school has switched it on, and that is a question worth
a parent asking.
Sources: https://www.2simple.com/blog/purple-mash-parent-portal/ ,
https://help.2simple.com/parent-resources/parent-how-do-i-log-a-child-into-purple-mash ,
https://help.2simple.com/logins-and-passwords/what-is-a-school-quick-login

**Seesaw.** The child's device captures the work: "The drawing, typing, photo,
recording and video tools let children be creative and experimental". So the
child is photographing and recording themselves, which is a different kind of
exposure from typing answers. On the family side the school invites parents by
email address, phone number or a printable QR code, and **families must create
an account** to see anything, through the web at app.seesaw.me or the separate
Seesaw Family app on iOS and Android. So this is a school asking a parent to
install an app. The headline reach figure, "10M teachers, students and families
in over a third of elementary schools", is US.
Sources: https://seesaw.com/ ,
https://www.commonsense.org/education/articles/teachers-essential-guide-to-seesaw ,
https://help.seesaw.me/hc/en-us/articles/17964262819341-The-Learning-Journal-Navigating-The-Learning-Journal-as-a-teacher

**Tapestry.** This is the earliest device relationship in a child's school life
and it belongs to the parent, not the child. It replaces the paper learning
journey with a cloud journal, aligned to the EYFS and to Development Matters and
Birth to 5 Matters. "Tapestry provides each individual child with their own
Learning Journal held online, with parents and carers given their own log-in
using their email and password", and parents can comment directly on
observations. So before a child can read, a parent is already receiving
notifications about them and replying inside a platform. Tapestry now extends
beyond EYFS into Key Stages 1 and 2.
Sources: https://tapestry.info/ , https://tapestry.info/eyfs-learning-journal/ ,
https://tapestryjournal.com/ ,
https://www.structural-learning.com/post/tapestry-eyfs-teachers-complete-guide-online

**ClassDojo.** The one a parent is most likely to already have on their phone.
Behaviour points, a class story, and teacher to parent messaging. Verified scale:
"more than 51 million children, families, and teachers across 180 countries",
and "over 95% of K-8 schools in the U.S." A UK specific figure was not found, so
we must not claim one. The device exposure worth naming for a parent is not the
child's, it is the parent's: points arriving on a phone in real time is a
behaviour system that follows the child home.
Sources: https://www.classdojo.com/en-gb/ , https://www.classdojo.com/about/ ,
https://www.classdojo.com/en-gb/press/

**Google Classroom and Google Workspace for Education.** This is the full
managed account, usually on a Chromebook. Two verified specifics a parent should
have. First, on age: "Children aged 13 and under should only use Classroom with
a Google Workspace for Education or Workspace for Nonprofits account", which is
exactly why a primary child gets a school Google account rather than a personal
one. Second, on defaults: "All users in primary or secondary institutions default
to under 18 and get a restricted experience in some Google services if admins
don't identify users that are 18 and over", and from March 2025 Google required
consent re confirmation for under 18 education users accessing Additional
Services. In plain terms: the school is the account administrator, the school
decides which Google services the child can reach, and the restriction is set by
an adult at the school, not by the parent. Global reach: "over 170 million
students and educators across 230 countries and territories".
Sources: https://support.google.com/edu/classroom/answer/7582372 ,
https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/getting-started/editions/service-categories-available-to-google-workspace-for-education-users ,
https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2025/03/consent-reconfirmation-for-under-18-education-users-accessing-additional-services.html ,
https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/education/around-the-world-and-back/

### The one that deserves a flag of its own

**Natterhub.** A simulated social media platform for ages 5 to 11, where the
child posts and comments for real inside a network "gated to the class and
supervised by the class teacher", across more than 300 lessons. This is the
closest thing in the English primary system to our own thesis: you do not
prepare a child for social media by keeping them off it, you give them a
supervised version first. It is now free to UK primary schools that hold a
Twinkl Ultimate subscription, which means adoption could move fast. A parent
whose Year 4 child comes home talking about posting at school is probably
describing this.
Sources: https://natterhub.com/ ,
https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/twinkl-to-offer-free-uk-access-to-natterhub-as-schools-tackle-online-safety-pressures ,
https://natterhub.com/categories/curriculum-resources

### Things that need no device at all, worth telling a worried parent

Barefoot Computing is deliberately unplugged as well as on screen. iCompute
states it "can be taught using any device" and "requires no additional
hardware". Empiribox ships physical equipment. Projects on a Page is paper.
PLAN Primary Science is PDFs. Opening Worlds is print and talk. Most PSHE is
discussion. A knowledge organiser is one side of A4.

### And the one that is going away

**Duolingo for Schools.** New accounts can no longer be created and existing
classrooms remain only until 31 July 2027. Any school using it for primary
French or Spanish has a cliff edge. Separately, on the consumer app: Duolingo
"asks anyone under 13 to enter their parent's email address when creating an
account, automatically setting them up with a child account", and those accounts
have no leaderboards or friends. In class, under 13s can be added by the teacher
anonymously through a join link or class code.
Sources: https://duolingoschools.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/6830454446093-What-is-Duolingo-for-Schools ,
https://www.internetmatters.org/advice/apps-and-platforms/skills-building/duolingo/

**Two structural points about copyright in schools worth holding.** The CLA
Schools Licence is what permits schools to copy from published material at all
(https://cla.co.uk/cla-products/schools-licence/), and the ERA Licence is the
equivalent for broadcast material. Neither extends to us. A school may lawfully
photocopy something we could not lawfully publish.
