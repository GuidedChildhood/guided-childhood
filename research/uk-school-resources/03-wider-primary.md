# The wider primary curriculum in England, Reception to Year 6
## What schools actually use, subject by subject, excluding English and maths

Researched 17 September 2026 for Guided Childhood. Lane: every primary subject
except English and maths.

---

## HOW I KNOW EVERYTHING BELOW. Read this first.

**Not one page in this file was fetched.** WebFetch returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED`
for every host attempted: www.kapowprimary.com, explorify.uk,
teachcomputing.org, www.gov.uk, assets.publishing.service.gov.uk and
en.wikipedia.org. The coordinator confirmed the gateway is answering 403 to
CONNECT for nearly all external hosts. It is an organisation egress policy, not
the sites refusing us, and the proxy rules say do not retry and do not route
around it. So I stopped.

**Everything here therefore rests on WebSearch**, which runs server side and
returns result links plus a summary of indexed pages. Consequences, and they are
strict:

1. **Every row and every claim is tagged `[SEARCH INDEX ONLY]`.** There is no
   `[FETCHED]` tag anywhere in this file, because no fetch succeeded.
2. **Nothing in this file is an exact quote.** Where earlier drafting had
   quotation marks, the wording has been demoted to "as worded in the search
   index" or similar. A search summary is a paraphrase machine sitting between
   me and the page. House rule 2 requires real quotes for statutory and
   published curriculum wording, and I cannot supply one. Every place that
   needs a real quote is listed at the end under WHAT NEEDS A FETCH.
3. **Prevalence figures are the weakest column.** They come from publisher
   marketing pages as summarised by the index. I have kept them because they
   are directionally useful and they name their source, but they are unconfirmed
   against a primary source and several publishers contradict themselves. Do not
   put any of them in customer facing copy without a fetch.
4. **The inventory itself is the reliable part**, exactly as the coordinator
   said: resource name, publisher, subject, year groups, what it is, and whether
   a device is needed. That is well attested across many independent school
   websites in the index, not just the seller's own page.

**Side finding worth keeping.** schemesupport.co.uk runs subject by subject
comparison pages of the primary schemes schools choose between. It is a map of
this whole lane: https://www.schemesupport.co.uk/science ,
https://www.schemesupport.co.uk/subjects/primary-pe-schemes ,
https://www.schemesupport.co.uk/computing , https://www.schemesupport.co.uk/re ,
https://www.schemesupport.co.uk/music , https://www.schemesupport.co.uk/french

**On Kapow.** Kapow Primary sells a multi subject scheme. Only its computing
page and its RSE and PSHE pages showed up in the index with usable detail. Its
science, history, geography, art, DT, music, French and RE pages did not, so
those appear as thin rows rather than invented ones.

## A. PARENT FACING MATERIAL
`[SEARCH INDEX ONLY throughout. No document below was opened.]`

The honest summary: most of this lane publishes nothing for parents. Schemes are
sold to a subject lead, and the parent is reached only through whatever the
school chooses to forward. Four exceptions, and they are the genre we should be
building in.

**1. The RSHE parent leaflet. A few sides of A4, and the clearest model we have.**

Jigsaw publishes two distinct documents, and the distinction matters. One is a
general information leaflet for parents and carers, explaining what Jigsaw is,
what the six half term puzzles are, and what mindfulness means in practice,
naming the Jigsaw Chime and Jigsaw Jerrie Cat. The second is a separate RSHE
guide for parents and carers, dated September 2023, which exists only because
RSE is the part parents ask about. Shape, as far as the index shows: a short
leaflet, headed sections rather than prose, written to be sent home whole.
Hundreds of individual schools host copies on their own sites, which is how a
parent actually receives it. **Page length not verified, because no PDF was
opened.**

described as information to share with parents about what children learn and the

**2. The knowledge organiser. One side of A4, and the thing that actually comes
home in the bag.**

Shape as described in the index: key facts of a topic condensed onto ideally a
single A4 page, setting out what children are expected to know by heart by the
end of the topic, carrying the exact facts, dates, events, characters, concepts
and definitions. The parent use case is explicit in those descriptions: it lets a
parent see what their child is learning, and it works as a quizzing tool at home,
both ways round. Some schools write their own short parent guide to using one.
That guide, a school's own explanation of how to use the organiser, is the
format closest to what Guided Childhood should publish.

## B. DEVICE EXPOSURE. What a Reception to Year 6 child's screen time through
## school actually consists of.
`[SEARCH INDEX ONLY throughout. Every mechanism below is as described in indexed
help pages and school pages, not as read on the vendor's page.]`

### The shape of it

A primary child's school device exposure is four different things, and only two
of them put a screen in the child's own hands.

**Layer one, the teacher's screen, which is most of it.** Charanga, Kapow, Snap
Science, Explorify, BBC Ten Pieces, Jessie and Friends, Jigsaw songs, Get Set 4
PE technique videos. In all of these the device is at the front of the room and
the child is watching, singing or talking. Charanga is the clearest case: the
whole scheme is delivered through an on screen player. Explorify is a picture on
the board to argue about. For a parent's purposes this is television, not device
use, and it is worth saying so plainly, because it is where most of the "my child
is on screens all day at school" worry comes from.

**Layer two, the child's own login, which is the real thing.** Purple Mash,
Seesaw, Espresso Coding, Discovery Education Coding, Oddizzi, Natterhub, Scratch,
Code.org, Duolingo for Schools, and in schools that have gone all in, a full
Google or Microsoft account. Here the child has a username, a password and a
record.

**Layer three, the parent's app.** Tapestry, ClassDojo, Seesaw Family, Class
Charts Parents. The child does not touch these. The parent installs them and
receives the child.

**Layer four, the physical device that is not a screen.** The BBC micro:bit.
Coded in a browser, then flashed to a board with sensors and lights. Every UK
primary school was reported to have been offered a class set of 30, almost
700,000 devices, funded by Nominet, delivered between September 2023 and March
2024.
https://microbit.org/news/2023-05-15/bbc-microbit-the-next-gen-groundbreaking-initiative-announced/

### Which platforms require a child account

**Requires a named child account: Purple Mash, Seesaw, Espresso Coding,
Discovery Education Coding, Natterhub, Google Workspace for Education and Google
Classroom, Microsoft Teams for Education, Duolingo for Schools (or an anonymous
class credential), and Scratch and Code.org where a school chooses individual
rather than shared logins.**

**Requires a parent account, not a child one: Tapestry, ClassDojo, Seesaw Family,
Class Charts Parents.**

**Requires no account: ScratchJr, micro:bit, Explorify, Oddizzi at class level,
and everything paper.**

### The five to be concrete about

**Purple Mash (2Simple).** Each child has their own login, inside 2Simple's
walled environment rather than the open web. Two things a parent should know.
First, free home access for all pupils is described as included, so the login
works from the sofa, which means a Year 2 child can have a real school account
before they have anything else. Second, every school is described as having a
parent portal, off by default: the school ticks a use parent code option when it
prints pupil login cards, and a parent code is generated automatically on each
card. So the parent's access exists whether or not the school has switched it on,
and that is a good question for a parent to ask.
https://www.2simple.com/blog/purple-mash-parent-portal/
https://help.2simple.com/parent-resources/parent-how-do-i-log-a-child-into-purple-mash
https://help.2simple.com/logins-and-passwords/what-is-a-school-quick-login

**Seesaw.** The child's device captures the work, with drawing, typing, photo,
recording and video tools, so the child is photographing and recording themselves
rather than typing answers. On the family side, the school invites parents by
email address, phone number or a printable QR code, and families must create an
account to see anything, via the web or the separate Seesaw Family app on iOS and
Android. So this is a school asking a parent to install an app. The headline reach
figure is US only.
https://seesaw.com/
https://www.commonsense.org/education/articles/teachers-essential-guide-to-seesaw
https://help.seesaw.me/hc/en-us/articles/17964262819341-The-Learning-Journal-Navigating-The-Learning-Journal-as-a-teacher

**Tapestry.** The earliest device relationship in a child's school life, and it
belongs to the parent, not the child. It replaces the paper learning journey with
a cloud journal aligned to the EYFS and to Development Matters and Birth to 5
Matters. Each child has their own online learning journal, and parents and carers
are given their own login with email and password, and can comment directly on
observations. So before a child can read, a parent is already receiving
notifications about them and replying inside a platform. Tapestry now extends
beyond EYFS into Key Stages 1 and 2.
https://tapestry.info/ , https://tapestry.info/eyfs-learning-journal/ ,
https://tapestryjournal.com/ ,
https://www.structural-learning.com/post/tapestry-eyfs-teachers-complete-guide-online

**ClassDojo.** The one a parent most likely already has on their phone. Behaviour
points, a class story, and teacher to parent messaging. Scale as reported: more
than 51 million children, families and teachers across 180 countries, and over 95
percent of US K to 8 schools. No UK figure found, so we must not claim one. The
device exposure worth naming for a parent is not the child's, it is the parent's:
points arriving on a phone in real time is a behaviour system that follows the
child home.
https://www.classdojo.com/en-gb/ , https://www.classdojo.com/about/ ,
https://www.classdojo.com/en-gb/press/

**Google Classroom and Google Workspace for Education.** The full managed
account, usually on a Chromebook. Two specifics a parent should have, both as
described in Google's own indexed help pages. On age: children aged 13 and under
should only use Classroom with a Google Workspace for Education or Workspace for
Nonprofits account, which is exactly why a primary child gets a school Google
account rather than a personal one. On defaults: all users in primary or
secondary institutions default to under 18 and get a restricted experience in
some Google services unless an administrator identifies them as 18 or over, and
from March 2025 Google required consent re confirmation for under 18 education
users accessing Additional Services. In plain terms, the school is the account
administrator, the school decides which Google services the child can reach, and
the restriction is set by an adult at the school, not by the parent.
https://support.google.com/edu/classroom/answer/7582372
https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/getting-started/editions/service-categories-available-to-google-workspace-for-education-users
https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2025/03/consent-reconfirmation-for-under-18-education-users-accessing-additional-services.html

### The one that deserves a flag of its own

**Natterhub.** A simulated social media platform for ages 5 to 11 where the child
posts and comments for real inside a network gated to the class and supervised by
the teacher, across more than 300 lessons. This is the closest thing in the
English primary system to our own thesis: you do not prepare a child for social
media by keeping them off it, you give them a supervised version first. It is
reported as now free to UK primary schools holding a Twinkl Ultimate
subscription, which means adoption could move fast. A parent whose Year 4 child
comes home talking about posting at school is probably describing this.
https://natterhub.com/
https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/twinkl-to-offer-free-uk-access-to-natterhub-as-schools-tackle-online-safety-pressures

### What needs no device at all, worth telling a worried parent

Barefoot Computing is deliberately unplugged as well as on screen. iCompute
describes itself as teachable on any device with no additional hardware.
Empiribox ships physical equipment. Projects on a Page is paper. PLAN Primary
Science is PDFs. Opening Worlds is print and talk. Most PSHE is discussion. A
knowledge organiser is one side of A4.

### And the one that is going away

**Duolingo for Schools.** Reported that new accounts can no longer be created and
existing classrooms remain only until 31 July 2027, so any school using it for
primary French or Spanish has a cliff edge. Separately on the consumer app:
anyone under 13 is asked for a parent's email when creating an account and is set
up with a child account without leaderboards or friends, and in class under 13s
can be added by the teacher anonymously through a join link or class code.
https://duolingoschools.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/6830454446093-What-is-Duolingo-for-Schools
https://www.internetmatters.org/advice/apps-and-platforms/skills-building/duolingo/
