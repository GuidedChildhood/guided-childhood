# Language Angels: what a paying school actually gets (study, 9 October 2026)

A read only study of a logged in Language Angels school subscription, made so our KS2 French and Spanish scheme can beat it. It feeds `plans/2026-10-09-languages-plan.md`, in particular "Images and animation: better than theirs, and ours" and "The learning design".

**How this was done.** Pages were read as text in the built in browser. Nothing was downloaded, exported, bought, submitted or changed, and no screenshots were saved. Their words, word lists, lyrics and images are not reproduced here; everything is paraphrased.

**Limits, stated plainly.**
- The subscription covers **Spanish only**. French unit data returns empty for this account, so French is described from the platform demo (which uses a French unit) and from the document names on the guidance page.
- The class pages (My Classes, Teach Now) were blocked by this session's safety check because they sit next to pupil management controls. A live lesson could not be opened slide by slide. Section 2 is built from the official 17 minute platform demo video hosted inside the account (it walks a French Intermediate lesson on pets), the lesson plan and teacher notes shown in that video, and the lesson descriptions in the unit catalogue. A true slide by slide pass needs Justin to open one lesson himself (see the end).
- No school, teacher or pupil names from the account appear in this file.

## 1. Scheme structure

**Levels.** Units are tagged by "teaching type", not by year group:
- EYFS and KS1: Starting Off, Moving On, Moving Up, plus one off extras.
- KS2: Early Language Teaching, Intermediate Teaching, Progressive Teaching, plus Supplementary Resources.
- Each unit carries a difficulty rating shown as a row of small mascot hat icons.

**Size (Spanish, this account).** 87 units in all. Core KS2 units: 11 Early Language, 15 Intermediate, 9 Progressive. Around 25 KS2 extras: grammar one offs, phonics, numbers, days, months, time, festivals, literature, dictionary skills, European Day of Languages, language days and assemblies. KS1 has about 20 core units plus extras. Several topics exist twice, once at KS1 level and once at KS2 level (fruit, animals, shapes, vegetables, instruments, seasons, Little Red Riding Hood).

**Units and lessons.** A standard unit is 6 lessons. Lesson 6 is always "revise everything and sit the end of unit assessment". Extras run 1 to 4 lessons. EYFS units are 6 lessons with a song in every lesson.

**Lesson length.** The lesson plan in the demo sets a 5 minute starter, a 35 minute main teaching block on the slides, a 5 minute plenary, then 15 minutes of desk based consolidation from the worksheets. So about 45 minutes of teaching plus optional written follow up, nearer an hour in all.

**Progression is topic led.** The year is assembled from themed units (pets, fruit, the café, the Romans, the Vikings, the Olympics, the weather, the planets, a healthy lifestyle). Vocabulary is the topic's nouns, usually 8 to 10 per unit, taught 5 at a time across lessons 1 and 2, then consolidated. High frequency verbs arrive inside topics (for example "to be" in the first person in lesson 5 of the animals unit; "I have" in lesson 2 of the pets unit). There is no published frequency spine: the word list is whatever the topics contain.

**There is no fixed sequence.** Schools build their own long term plan. The Unit Planner page asks five questions (key stage, mixed age or not, prior teaching, how often, how long) and returns a pre made planner file to download. For the combination tried it returned "no default planner found". There is a 46 minute webinar on how to build a long term plan, and a further live webinar is advertised on how to plan a unit. Planning is clearly a known pain.

**Phonics.** Two light touches, not a strand:
- A 4 lesson supplementary phonics unit, one lesson per teaching type (one set of sounds for Early Language, two for Intermediate, one for Progressive).
- A phonics panel on each unit's knowledge organiser naming two to four sound spelling points met in that unit (in the demo: two vowel spellings, silent final letters, elision), and pupil and teacher phonics mats per language.
Nothing in the catalogue shows a phonics slot at the start of every lesson or a set order of sounds across years.

**Grammar.** Two routes:
- Inside units, as one focus per unit (articles and gender, plurals, "I like", negatives, first person verbs, simple agreement).
- As stand alone explicit lessons in the extras, each marked as not linked to a unit: nouns and gender, definite and indefinite articles, adjectival agreement, possessive adjectives, regular verbs (4 lessons, one per verb family) and irregular verbs.
A "Grammar Targets Unit Mapping" document and a grammar grid map which unit touches which point. Recycling across later units is not planned in the material we could see.

**Pedagogy framing.** The guidance page has a "Three Pillars" document (phonics, vocabulary, grammar) and a retrieval practice document. The vocabulary of the research is adopted; the structure underneath is still topic first.

## 2. One lesson walked through (French Intermediate, pets, lesson 1, from the demo)

**What the teacher opens.** Teach Now, choose a class, choose a teaching type tab, choose a unit from a long scrolling list, then a lesson button 1 to 6. Each lesson page has four tabs: Lesson, Lesson Plans and Support Materials, Worksheets, Songs and Lyrics, plus a "Mark lesson as complete" button.

**The slides.** An embedded presentation (it plays inside the page with next and previous arrows and a drawing tool, and is also offered as a zip download). Slide one is a full width cartoon scene: a pet shop, a girl, a cat, the unit question as a title in a blue pill, and a speaker icon to play the native recording. Every target language item has a speaker icon; the teacher clicks it and the class repeats. All audio is pre recorded by a native speaker.

**Phases (from the lesson plan in the demo).**
1. Starter, 5 minutes: class chat about pets in English and sharing the unit objective.
2. Main, 35 minutes: introduce the 8 pet nouns with the indefinite article on the slides, following the teacher notes.
3. Plenary, 5 minutes: which pets do you remember, how are they spelt, which were masculine or feminine.
4. Consolidation, 15 minutes: worksheet activities.
Then suggested follow ups such as a pets classroom display.

**How a new word is shown.** A flat vector clip art image, the written word with its article, and a speaker icon for the native recording. Gender is shown by the article and explained in the notes. The sound spelling link is not taught on the slide in the way a phonics programme would; it sits on the knowledge organiser's phonics panel.

**What the teacher is told.** Two documents per lesson, both Word files converted to PDF:
- **Lesson Plan**: one page, objectives, the four phases with timings, EAL, SEN and gifted columns left blank for the teacher, follow ups.
- **Teacher Support Notes**: about four pages. A key language table (target word, English meaning), a plain English grammar explanation (here, what an indefinite article is and why gender matters), dictionary skills, and a slide by slide breakdown with advice.
There is no word for word script on the slide itself, and no phonetic respelling for the teacher next to each word. Pronunciation help is the native audio button. A separate product, Pronunciation Pal, adds word by word native audio with images in class and at home, but it is a paid bolt on (£39 to £59 a year on top of the subscription, prices excluding VAT).

**Practice and games.** Inside the slides: repeat after the audio, show me, guess the hidden picture style activities (from the descriptions and the testimonial material, not seen live). Outside the slides: karaoke songs and self correcting interactive games in a separate "Play Games and Songs" area, which teachers assign to the class or to individual pupils.

**Speaking.** Choral repetition after the recording is the core move. Partner talk and the end of unit "short presentation" appear in later lessons (for example the presenting myself unit ends with a short presentation).

## 3. Images

- **Style:** flat vector cartoon, bright and clean, children and animals with simple faces, consistent line weight. It reads as competent commercial clip art, the same family of style seen across many UK primary resources.
- **Consistency:** strong within a unit (one scene, one cast). The brand mascot, a cartoon angel girl with a mortarboard, appears on title slides, the games menu and the karaoke screens.
- **Quality:** good on the slides. On the printables the vocabulary pictures are smaller, sometimes mixed with more photographic clip art, and the layouts are crowded.
- **Representation:** the demo scene shows a child of colour as the lead, which is good. Cultural content for the target countries sits mostly in separate culture lessons and the "me in the world" unit rather than in everyday scenes.
- **Reuse:** the same picture of a word is reused across slides, knowledge organiser, vocabulary mat and games, which helps recognition.
- **Weaknesses:** pictures are decorative scenes rather than teaching images. A crossed out animal is used for the negative. Nothing ties a picture to a sound (no mouth shape, no sound picture link), and there is no character the children follow across the whole year apart from the mascot.

## 4. Animations, songs and videos

- **Songs:** each relevant lesson has a song video plus a separate lyrics file ("sing" and "lyrics" thumbnails on the Songs and Lyrics tab). Songs reuse the same flat vector art with gentle motion: characters bobbing, scene pans, text appearing. EYFS units are built around one song per lesson.
- **Karaoke:** a pupil facing karaoke area with the mascot holding a microphone. The karaoke format implies on screen lyrics; whether each word is highlighted as it is sung could not be checked without opening a class.
- **Captions:** the 17 minute platform demo video has no captions track. We could not confirm captions on the song videos.
- **Production feel:** tidy and child friendly, but it is motion graphics over clip art rather than character animation. Pacing in the platform demo is slow and screen recorded, with no chapters.
- **No sound teaching animation:** nothing animates how a sound is made or how a spelling maps to a sound.

## 5. Teacher admin

All of it is a list of downloadable files on one "Curriculum Guidance" page, arranged as eight steps. Almost all are PDF; two policies are Word documents. Nothing is generated from what the class has actually taught.
- **Step 1:** subject leader checklist.
- **Step 2:** the DfE programme of study, a languages policy and an intent, implementation and impact policy (both Word templates).
- **Step 3, teaching and learning:** a programme of study compliance grid and attainment targets; the three pillars; a progression map and skills progression grids by year group; SEND, greater depth, pupil premium and EAL guidance; a grammar grid; classroom activities per skill per year; a language skills grid by unit; curriculum drivers; mixed age teaching; retrieval practice; protected characteristics; substantive and disciplinary knowledge with worked examples per teaching type per language; British values; EYFS and KS1 early learning goals.
- **Step 4, methodology:** vocabulary mats and phonics mats (pupil and teacher) per language, grammar targets mapped to units, a document on the three teaching types.
- **Step 5:** an Ofsted questions and answers document dated 2026.
- **Step 6:** questionnaires (planning, inspection readiness, pupil voice, staff voice, SEND, an audit).
- **Step 7:** a KS2 to KS3 transition checklist (a checklist, not a per class record of what was taught).
- **Step 8:** whole school signage in the target language and bilingual.

**Assessment and tracking (inside each class):** the teacher types a score per pupil for speaking, listening, reading and writing per completed unit, with an absent box, then saves. A Class PoS Report and a Pupil PoS Report colour in the 12 KS2 programme of study statements as units are marked complete. Class reports and a game performance report sit alongside. These need full pupil records (first name, surname, date of birth, pupil premium, SEN and EAL flags) entered by hand or imported from a spreadsheet template.

**Long and medium term plans:** the questionnaire driven planner download described in section 1, or the school uploads its own. Medium term planning is the unit page plus the downloadable documents.

**CPD:** live bespoke training is sold separately. In the account the CPD page is an advert for it.

## 6. Printables

Per unit (from the unit page in the demo): knowledge organiser for the teacher and for pupils, class display materials, a pupil picture vocabulary sheet, a pupil "language builder" sheet, plus worksheets per lesson, all offered with a "Download All".
- The **knowledge organiser** is a single landscape page with three coloured panels (phonics, vocabulary, grammar) and a "what I will learn" checklist of five objectives. Useful structure, but it is a PowerPoint saved as PDF, dense, with small images and small type.
- The **language builder** is a sentence building mat: sentence starters in coloured boxes, pictures with words, a conjunction box, and the negative shown with crossed out pictures. Good idea, busy page.
- Printing means downloading each file and printing it from another program. There is no print room view in the browser and no answer keys seen.

## 7. Home and pupil access

- Pupils get logins (created by the teacher) for the karaoke songs and games, on the web and in a free Language Angels pupil app. Teachers assign games and songs to the class or to named pupils, and can see a game performance report.
- Pronunciation Pal works at home through the same app, but only if the school pays for the bolt on.
- There is no parent facing view, no parent letter in the guidance list, and no spaced review schedule: home practice is whatever games the teacher assigned.

## 8. Clunky

- **Many clicks to teach:** class, teaching type, unit list, lesson number, tab, then a slide viewer or a zip. The unit list is long and scrolls inside a panel.
- **Office files everywhere:** plans, notes, knowledge organisers and worksheets are Word or PowerPoint files saved as PDF, which open in new tabs. In the demo, half a dozen PDF tabs were open within a few minutes.
- **Lesson as a zip download** sits beside the in browser viewer, so teachers can end up teaching from an offline copy that drifts.
- **Planning is left to the school:** a questionnaire that returned no planner for the options tried, an hour long webinar, and a live webinar on unit planning.
- **Assessment is manual data entry** per pupil per skill per unit, with personal data the school has to hold and justify.
- **Mobile:** at phone width the unit catalogue opened to a header and an empty panel above the fold. The site is built for a desktop and a whiteboard.
- **Upsells inside the teaching account:** subscription countdown on every page, a pronunciation bolt on, paid CPD, webinar banners.
- **Accessibility:** no captions found on the help video; pages are mouse driven tables and accordions; long PDFs are not screen reader friendly.
- **Load speed:** pages were quick; the heavy part is the documents and video files.

## Where we beat them

| Their approach | The gap | Our feature (see the languages plan) |
|---|---|---|
| Topic led units, about 10 nouns each, no frequency spine | Pupils learn many nouns and few useful words; the trap the Ofsted research review names | A frequency spine of about 450 words per language, topics as context only ("The learning design" 2; `spine.json`) |
| Phonics as a 4 lesson extra and a panel on the knowledge organiser | No set order of sounds, no daily practice, no sound spelling link on the slide | A three minute sounds slot opening every lesson, in a set order across Y3 and Y4, with a `sounds` slide type (learning design 1) |
| Grammar as one point per unit or a stand alone lesson "not linked" to units | No planned recycling, so grammar is met once and forgotten | Grammar little and often, recycled in later units, checked by the spacing guard (learning design 3; `check-language-spacing.mjs`) |
| Revision only in lesson 6 of each unit | No spaced retrieval; old units are never revisited | Words return at about 1, 3, 7 and 21 days; the starter is built from words that are due (learning design 4 and 5) |
| Native audio button; phonetic help and word audio sold as a £39 to £59 bolt on | A non specialist still has to guess at mouth shapes and stress; the best help costs extra | A word for word teacher script and a pronunciation tip on every slide, native audio on every word, included ("What we take from each provider"; voice section) |
| School builds its own long term plan via a questionnaire and webinars | Hours of planning; for some options no planner exists | One fixed KS2 sequence, 6 units of 6 lessons a year, with long term, medium term and progression maps generated from the rows ("Admin done for you") |
| Admin as a page of static PDFs and Word templates | Documents drift from what is taught; nothing reflects the class | Coverage matrix, progression map, deep dive pack and policy generated from lesson rows and guarded in CI (`check-languages-coverage.mjs`) |
| Transition is a checklist | Secondary schools still start again from zero | A per class KS2 to KS3 transition record listing the words, sounds and grammar actually taught ("Admin done for you") |
| Assessment by hand typed scores per pupil, needing names, dates of birth and SEN flags | Data protection work for the school and data entry for the teacher | No pupil accounts or pupil data in school; a real 70% pass in the player and printable end of unit checks (learning design 11; "Where it lives") |
| Home practice is assigned games, no parent view | Under an hour a week of exposure, with no schedule and no family involvement | A per child word bank with spaced reviews and stars in the child app, with parent involvement (learning design 4; Phase 3) |
| Clip art scenes, decorative, one mascot | Pictures do not teach; no characters the children follow; no sound pictures | Our own house style art with the Planet Friends, a picture for every concrete word, and animated sound pictures made with GSAP and HyperFrames ("Images and animation") |
| Song videos as motion graphics over clip art; no captions found | Hard to follow for SEND and EAL; words not reliably shown as sung | Our `song` slide type with word by word highlighting and captions on every video ("Images and animation"; player slide types) |
| Lessons as embedded presentations plus a zip, plans as separate PDFs | Many tabs and clicks; offline copies drift | Press one button to teach (projector plus script in one view) or to print (`/languages/teach`, `/languages/print`) |
| Printables are PowerPoints saved as PDF, crowded | Hard to read, hard to fit, no print view | Print room routes built on the existing print kit, page fit checked by `check-print-fit.mjs` |
| Desktop only, upsells inside the teacher account | Poor on a phone; distraction while planning | Mobile and desktop checked before done; one licence code, no adverts inside teaching |
| Readers are retold fairy tales and themed texts | No guarantee pupils know the words in the text | A mini reader per unit using at least 95% known words, checked by `check-language-vocab.mjs` (learning design 6) |
| No dictation | Misses a skill the new GCSE tests | Dictation from Year 4 with a printable dictation sheet (learning design 8) |

**What they do well that we must at least match:** native recorded audio on every target language item; a complete teacher support pack per lesson (plan, notes, grammar explained in plain English for a non specialist); a knowledge organiser per unit with phonics, vocabulary and grammar side by side; and a large, inspection ready subject leader bundle (policy, intent and impact, progression, SEND, Ofsted questions, questionnaires).
