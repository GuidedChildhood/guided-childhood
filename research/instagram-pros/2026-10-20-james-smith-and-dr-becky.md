# How James Smith and Dr Becky look so professional, and how Justin gets there

From Justin's screen recording of both Instagram accounts, 20 October 2026
(research/instagram-pros/raw/rec1.mp4, not committed). What they do, measured
from the reels, then the same moves for Guided Digital Childhood.

## James Smith: direct and at ease

- **One tight shot.** Head and shoulders, eyes a third of the way down,
  shallow focus. A dark, softly lit background (shelves falling into shadow),
  one window or soft light to the side, so the face has shape.
- **He never leaves the lens.** No B-roll for long stretches. The authority is
  the eye contact; the edit only tightens the pauses and punches in now and then.
- **A prop that proves the point.** He holds the creatine gummy while he talks
  about it. The object does the explaining.
- **He admits something.** "Now I feel pretty stupid admitting this. I was
  brainwashed for years." The confession is what makes him believable.
- **Series memory.** He opens on the last video ("Remember I exposed..."), so
  every reel feels like the next episode.
- **Same uniform every time.** Cap, black tee, same room. You know it is him
  before he speaks.
- **Captions are quiet.** Two to five words, white, sentence case, centred, no
  boxes, no colour.

## Dr Becky: the scripts, in many rooms

- **A headline box that is the promise.** "3 Sentences Parents Forget They're
  Allowed to Say" sits on screen the whole time, then numbered boxes land as she
  reaches each one. The structure is visible.
- **The exact words.** Every reel gives the parent a sentence to say. That is
  her whole product, and it is ours too.
- **Variety comes from rooms, not angles.** Same close, handheld selfie framing,
  but a different wall, top and light each time, so the feed never looks
  repetitive while the format never changes.
- **Hands and warmth.** Close to the lens, hands in frame, quick smile,
  conversational speed.
- **Hooks that flip an instinct.** "Did you know that when your kid says I'm the
  worst on my team, it lowers their confidence when you try to make them feel
  better?"

## Prompts for Justin, before every take

1. Sit a couple of metres from the wall, window to one side, lens at eye level.
2. Pick the prop if there is one: the phone, a printed script card, the star
   chart, the kitchen timer. Hold it while you talk about it.
3. Start mid thought. No "Hi everyone", no "So today".
4. Say one thing that costs you something, from your true scenes only
   (content/brand-story/founder-context.md). Never invent one.
5. Look at the lens on every full stop.
6. If it continues a series, open on the last one: "Remember the bedtime fight?"
7. End on the stage check, said the same way every time.
