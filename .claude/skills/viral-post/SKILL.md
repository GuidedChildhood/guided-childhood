# Viral Post, the Wrong Villain formula

This skill exists to duplicate one specific success: Justin's highest performing LinkedIn post,
the Wrong Villain "beta = 0.061" post, which reached about 73,911 impressions. It reverse
engineers exactly what made that post and its image work, turns it into a repeatable template,
and folds in best in class 2026 LinkedIn tactics so future posts can hit the same way.

Use it alongside content-engine (voice, hidden thread, evidence discipline) and
linkedin-engagement.md (the algorithm rules). Where this skill and the older engagement notes seem
to disagree on CTAs, this skill wins, because it is modelled on the post that actually performed.

Two non negotiables carry over: no dashes anywhere in the copy, and Justin's voice, warm, plain,
a fellow parent who did the homework, never a guru. Every stat traces to a named source.

## PART 1. THE GOLD STANDARD POST, ANNOTATED

This is the reference. Read it before writing anything. The post, then the anatomy that made it
travel.

### The post

> A peer reviewed meta analysis published this year looked at 46 studies and 79 effect sizes on the relationship between social media use and adolescent mental health.
>
> The pooled result: beta = 0.061.
>
> Below the threshold the researchers themselves set for evidentiary value. Statistically indistinguishable from noise. Pre registered on Open Science Framework. Data and methodology publicly available.
>
> That number is not zero. Social media has a measurable association with child mental health outcomes. But beta = 0.061 is smaller than the effect of wearing glasses. That is not a metaphor I invented. It is how Andrew Przybylski at Oxford described his own independent finding when he and Amy Orben at Cambridge MRC ran the same calculation with a different dataset and reached essentially the same result.
>
> Four research groups. UC Irvine. Cambridge MRC. Oxford. Stetson University. Different datasets, different methodologies, different countries, different starting assumptions. Same conclusion: real, measurable, and very small.
>
> Meanwhile child poverty accounts for an estimated 32% of child mental health outcomes consistent with every major longitudinal study including the Avon Longitudinal Study and the British Journal of Psychiatry 2025. Adverse childhood experiences account for roughly 24%.
>
> Parental mental health difficulties for around 14%.
>
> Social media sits at roughly 3%.
>
> If you want to understand why the response to the children's mental health crisis keeps missing the mark, that ratio is the answer. The 3% problem is receiving the vast majority of parliamentary time and policy energy. The 32% problem is chronically underfunded and almost invisible.
>
> This is The Wrong Villain series continued.
>
> The full 54 year interactive data tool is live drop PATHWAY in the comments and I'll send it to you a link.
>
> If this landed with you, repost it. The response to this crisis should be proportional to the evidence. That starts with more people understanding what the evidence actually shows.
>
> And if this is something you agree with, follow Justin Phillips for more. This conversation needs more people in it.

### Why it travelled, the ten moving parts

1. **The hook is a precise, strange number, not a claim.** "46 studies and 79 effect sizes"
   then "beta = 0.061". No throat clearing, no rhetorical question. A specific number the reader
   cannot pattern match to a cliche, so they have to read on to find out what it means. The
   first two lines are the whole hook, and they open a gap, what does 0.061 mean and why should
   I care.
2. **It teaches, it does not preach.** The post explains a real statistic the reader has never
   been given. Depth is the moat. People forward things that made them feel smarter.
3. **Credibility density.** Named researchers with named institutions, stacked, "Andrew
   Przybylski at Oxford", "Amy Orben at Cambridge MRC", "UC Irvine. Cambridge MRC. Oxford.
   Stetson University." Plus the registration markers, "pre registered on Open Science
   Framework, data and methodology publicly available." This is what makes a stranger trust a
   stranger.
4. **The honest pivot, mid post, the signature move.** "That number is not zero." He concedes
   the strongest opposing point before the reader can raise it. This is the single most
   important sentence in the whole formula. It converts a sceptic into a reader. Never publish
   without it.
5. **The borrowed comparison.** "smaller than the effect of wearing glasses. That is not a
   metaphor I invented." A vivid, concrete, slightly funny image, and he immediately attributes
   it so it reads as evidence, not spin.
6. **The ratio, the concrete contrast.** 32% poverty vs 3% social media. One vivid number pair
   that makes an abstract argument physical. Every high performer has one. The reader remembers
   the ratio long after the post.
7. **The turn from data to meaning.** "If you want to understand why the response keeps missing
   the mark, that ratio is the answer." He does not leave the numbers as trivia, he tells the
   reader what they are for, and it is a mission point (the hidden thread, poverty is the real
   driver) landed plainly.
8. **Series branding.** "This is The Wrong Villain series continued." Signals a body of work,
   creates return readers, makes Justin a position not a post.
9. **The CTA stack, in order** (see Part 3). Comment keyword for the lead magnet, principled
   repost ask, follow ask. Each is a specific instruction, and the repost ask carries a
   principle ("the response should be proportional to the evidence") so sharing feels like
   taking a stand, not doing him a favour.
10. **Length.** It fills nearly the whole space. Long, dense, evidence rich, but broken into
    short paragraphs and single sentence lines so it reads fast on a phone. A half length post
    reads as everyone else's content. Depth is the differentiator.

### The one flaw to fix when reusing it

The lead magnet line has a typo and a stumble, "drop PATHWAY in the comments and I'll send you
the link" got mangled to "send it to you a link". Keep the mechanic, fix the wording. And the
whole post uses no dashes, hold that.

## PART 2. THE COPY TEMPLATE (fill this in every time)

Write in this order. Each block is load bearing. Aim for 1,800 to 2,900 characters, close to the
3,000 limit, in short paragraphs with frequent single sentence lines and air between them.

1. **Hook, lines 1 to 2, about 12 words to the fold.** Open on the most precise, surprising fact
   you have. A number, a registered finding, a quoted sentence. Never a question, never scene
   setting. The second line deepens the tension, it does not resolve it.
   - Flavours that work: the precise number ("beta = 0.061"), the contrarian ("We are all
     counting screen time, it is the one number that barely matters"), the vulnerable ("I took
     the numbers off my own homepage, and they were working"), the honest middle ("The ban is
     coming, I am building the thing the ban forgets").
2. **The setup, 2 to 4 short paragraphs.** Lay out the finding plainly. Stack credibility,
   named researchers, institutions, sample sizes, registration and openness markers. Precision
   is trust.
3. **The honest pivot, one line, mandatory.** Concede the strongest opposing point before the
   reader raises it. "That number is not zero." "None of this means social media is harmless."
   "Wrong villain does not mean no villain." If the post does not have this line, it is not
   finished.
4. **The concrete contrast, one vivid ratio or two children.** 32% vs 3%. Two children, same
   three hours, different homes. One physical image per post that makes the abstraction
   stick.
5. **The meaning, the turn.** Say what the numbers are for. This is where the mission lands,
   the hidden thread (poverty, adverse childhood experiences, parental mental health are the
   real drivers), stated plainly in roughly one post in ten and carried as a single brick in
   the rest.
6. **Series branding, one line.** "This is [Series Name] continued." Name the series so the
   post belongs to a body of work.
7. **The CTA stack** (Part 3). In order, comment keyword, principled repost, follow.
8. **A prepared first comment.** Write the first comment that gets ahead of the strongest
   objection, in Justin's voice, and post it yourself immediately. Comments are half the reach.

**Hard rules:** no dashes. UK English. Every stat sourced. One clear idea per post. Never claim
outcomes for children, claim clarity, calmer conversations, a pathway. Positive not panic, the
reader finishes more capable, not more afraid.

## PART 3. THE CTA STACK (what the winner did, and the 2026 correction)

Important, read this whole section, because the CTA is the one place the proven post has since
aged. The winning post closed with three moves, comment keyword lead magnet, principled repost,
follow ask. That stack worked in 2023 and 2024. In 2026 one third of it has become risky, so we
keep the shape and swap the risky part.

**What the winner did (the era it worked in):**
1. Comment keyword lead magnet, "drop PATHWAY in the comments and I will send you the link."
2. Principled repost ask, "if this landed, repost it, the response should be proportional to the
   evidence."
3. Follow ask, "follow Justin Phillips for more."

**The 2026 correction (do this now):** LinkedIn's 2026 authenticity update trained classifiers to
detect and silently suppress comment for a keyword mechanics ("comment PATHWAY and I will DM you"),
and the suppression can hit account level reach, not just the one post, with longer penalties for
repeat use. So the automated keyword DM tactic is now high risk. It is the single biggest live
mistake to avoid. Do not anchor the funnel on it.

**The stack to use in 2026, in order:**
1. **One specific open question that needs real experience to answer.** This is the new engine.
   Questions that pull long comments matter most, comments over about 15 words carry roughly
   double the weight of a short one, and a wave of real comments in the first hour is what lifts
   reach. "What age did you give your child their first phone, and would you do it the same
   again" beats "do you agree". Ask the thing only a parent could answer.
2. **The link in the first comment, referenced in the body.** Put the resource link in your own
   first comment and write one plain line in the post, "the sourced breakdown is in the comments."
   A bare link in the body is suppressed 25 to 60 percent. This replaces the keyword mechanic and
   carries no penalty.
3. **The principled repost ask, kept.** Reshare with commentary is a genuinely strong 2026 signal,
   so keep it, "if this landed, repost it, the response to this should be proportional to the
   evidence, not the panic." Sharing as a stance still works.
4. **Earn the follow with the profile, not a hard in post ask.** A one line follow ask is low
   value now and can read as bait. Every viral post drives profile visits, so the profile converts
   the follow. A soft "follow for the rest of the series" at a series close is fine, do not stack
   it every post.

**The prepared first comment does double duty.** It carries the link and it gets ahead of the
strongest objection. Post it the second the post goes live, then reply fast to every early comment
in the first hour with a follow up question, because each reply is itself a comment that lifts
velocity in the golden hour.

The /starter-pack CTA is the rare body link exception, used sparingly and only at a series close.

## PART 4. THE IMAGE, THE RESEARCH CARD (how to replicate it)

The image is half the stop scroll. The winning one is an editorial "research card", not a photo
and not a busy infographic. Its power is restraint, one big number, one accent colour, a lot of
calm cream space. Replicate this exact system.

### The anatomy of the card (top to bottom)

1. **Eyebrow.** Small, uppercase, wide letter spacing, muted terracotta or taupe. The series
   name and the insight number: "THE WRONG VILLAIN, KEY INSIGHT 03". (In copy we never use
   dashes, so use a comma or a middle dot as the separator, not an em dash, even though the
   original used one.)
2. **Headline.** Two lines, large high contrast serif, ink. A claim, not a label: "The Effect
   Size That Changes Everything".
3. **A thin hairline rule.** Ink at low opacity, full width of the text column.
4. **The hero stat.** Huge, the largest thing on the card, in the single accent colour
   (terracotta): "beta = 0.061". One number is the whole point. Never two hero numbers.
5. **The caption.** One tight block of short declaratives under the stat, ink, serif: "46
   studies. 79 effect sizes. Below the evidentiary threshold. Statistically indistinguishable
   from noise."
6. **The comparison bars.** Two horizontal bars that carry the ratio. The big driver in ink,
   long, labelled (32%). The small one in terracotta, short, labelled (3%). This is the one
   piece of "data viz" and it must be dead simple, two bars, left label, right percentage.
7. **The italic kicker.** One centred italic serif line of corroboration: "Four independent
   research groups. Same conclusion."
8. **The footer.** Small, terracotta, the brand line: "The Guided Digital Childhood,
   guidedchildhood.com".

### The design tokens (the look that reads as authoritative, not AI)

- **Format:** portrait 4 by 5, 1080 by 1350 px. Best feed real estate on LinkedIn.
- **Background:** warm cream, about #F6F0E4. Never white. The cream is the calm.
- **Ink:** warm near black, about #232019, for headline, caption, the big bar.
- **Accent:** terracotta, about #B4552F, used on exactly three things, the hero stat, the short
  bar, the footer. One accent, three appearances, nothing else coloured. This discipline is why
  it looks designed and not generated.
- **Muted:** taupe, about #9B7E63, for the eyebrow and the bar labels.
- **Type:** a high contrast editorial serif for the headline and the hero number (Playfair
  Display or Fraunces), a readable serif for the caption and labels (Source Serif, Lora, or
  Georgia as fallback), the eyebrow and footer in the same serif, uppercase, widely letter
  spaced. Note this departs from the app's Nunito and IBM Plex Mono on purpose, the serif reads
  as a journal, which is the authority we want for an evidence post. Keep this serif card as its
  own sanctioned style for research posts. A Nunito variant is fine for non evidence posts.
- **Space:** generous margins, roughly 10 percent each side, and a lot of empty cream. The
  emptiness is the premium feel.

### Reusable template

`stat-card.html` in this skill folder reproduces the card exactly. Drop in a new eyebrow,
headline, hero stat, caption, two bar values and labels, and the kicker, open it in a browser at
1080 by 1350 and screenshot, or export to PNG. One number, one accent, keep the restraint. For a
photo led post instead (founder story, softer posts), fall back to a real photo of Justin woven
into the story, per linkedin-engagement.md.

## PART 5. BEST IN CLASS LINKEDIN, 2026 (what the algorithm rewards)

Sourced from the primary data, Richard van der Blom's Algorithm Insights 2025 (1.8 million posts)
and AuthoredUp's post corpus studies (310,000 to 372,000 posts). Trust the direction of the agency
blog numbers, not their exact digits, they are folklore. The numbers below are the primary ones.

- **Dwell time is the master signal.** LinkedIn measures how long people actually read, not
  likes. A post read for 30 seconds beats one with 50 quick likes. Everything else, hook,
  formatting, image, serves dwell.
- **The golden hour is 30 to 60 minutes.** Early engagement velocity decides the reach
  trajectory. Be present for the first hour, reply to every comment with a follow up question,
  each reply is another comment that lifts velocity.
- **Comments beat likes, and long comments win.** Comments over about 15 words carry roughly
  double the weight. This is why the close is a real question, not a like bait. (The "comments
  are worth 15 times a like" figure is folklore, the ranking is not.)
- **The hook must land before the fold, about 140 characters on mobile.** Shorter hooks win,
  first lines of 1 to 5 words earn the most, and a number in the first line earns about 35 median
  likes versus 26 without. Lead with the number. This is the beta 0.061 move, confirmed in the
  data.
- **Body length sweet spot, 1,300 to 2,500 characters.** About 27 percent higher engagement than
  posts under 400 characters. One idea per line, heavy white space, that spacing is the mechanical
  driver of dwell. (Slightly shorter than "fill the 3,000 limit", so aim 1,300 to 2,500 and let it
  run longer only when every line earns its place.)
- **Format reach hierarchy, strongest to weakest: native PDF carousel (document post), multi
  image, native video, text plus single image, polls (effectively dead).** See Part 5b, this is
  the biggest lever available to us.
- **No body links.** A bare external link in the body cuts reach 25 to 60 percent. Link in the
  first comment, reference it in the body. A link post that is also a genuinely good standalone
  read is penalised less, because it still earns dwell.
- **Post 3 to 5 times a week, never twice in a day.** Posts less than about 8 hours apart
  cannibalise each other's golden hour. Weekday mornings UK time for this audience.
- **Reshares with commentary and saves are strong signals.** A repost with your own commentary is
  treated close to original content and far outperforms a bare reshare. Pods and like for like
  rings are detected, never use them.
- **Topic authority compounds (the 2026 "360Brew" shift).** The algorithm now rewards consistency
  within one defined expertise area over raw volume. Staying in the children, screens, evidence
  lane is not just on brand, it is an algorithmic advantage. About 90 days of consistent posting
  and commenting is where reach and inbound visibly compound.
- **2 to 4 hashtags, and they barely move reach now.** Topic categorisation, not a lever.
- **The profile converts, not the post.** Every viral post drives profile visits. Keep the photo,
  banner and a plain, keyword rich headline sharp, that is what turns a reader into a follower.

### PART 5b. THE BIGGEST LEVER WE ARE NOT USING, CAROUSELS

A single static image is consumed in under 2 seconds, so a text plus one image post sits in the
lower reach tier. The single highest ROI upgrade available to Justin is to turn the best research
explainers into native PDF carousels (document posts), the top of the 2026 format hierarchy, with
2 to 3 times the dwell of any other format. 8 to 10 slides, one idea per slide, the research card
design system from Part 4 applied slide by slide. Keep text plus card for speed and for story
posts, but build the flagship research explainers (the beta 0.061 type posts) as carousels. This is
the one change most likely to produce another 70,000 impression post. When Justin asks for a
flagship evidence post, offer the carousel version, not just the text post.

`carousel.html` in this skill folder is the reusable eight slide carousel template, matching the
research card design system, pre filled with the beta 0.061 Wrong Villain carousel as a worked
example. The slide shape is cover, finding, meaning, honest pivot, credibility, the ratio, the
point, the close, one idea per slide, one accent. Open in Chrome, Print, Save as PDF with margins
none and background graphics on, one slide per page, then upload to LinkedIn as a document. The
cover slide is the thumbnail, so it must carry the hook and tease a number.

## PART 6. THE PRE PUBLISH CHECKLIST (run on every post)

1. First line, 12 words or fewer, a precise number or a sharp claim, forces the "see more" click.
2. Credibility stacked, named researchers, institutions, sample sizes, registration markers.
3. The honest pivot line is present ("that number is not zero"). If not, it is not finished.
4. One concrete contrast, a ratio or two children.
5. The meaning is stated, what the numbers are for, mission carried (1 in 10 lands it plainly).
6. Series branded, "[Series] continued".
7. Close on the 2026 stack, one specific open question, link in the first comment (never a comment
   for a keyword DM, it is throttled now), a principled repost ask. Follow earned via the profile.
8. A prepared first comment written, carrying the link and the pre empt of the main objection,
   ready to post the second the post is live.
9. Short lines, air, close to but under 3,000 characters.
10. No dashes anywhere. UK English. Every stat sourced. Positive not panic. Never claim child
    outcomes.
11. Image is a research card (one number, one accent, cream space) or a real photo woven in,
    never a generic graphic.

If a post misses any of 1 to 4 or 7, it will underperform. Fix before publishing.

## PART 8. WHAT OUR OWN ANALYTICS SAY (analysed 27 August 2026, 90 day export)

Evidence from Justin's real numbers, 30 May to 27 August 2026: 183,020
impressions, 89,279 members reached, 8,984 followers. These findings are the
account's own data and they override folklore where they conflict.

1. **Consistency is the engine, and decay is fast.** The June wave (the beta
   0.061 post) peaked at 53k weekly impressions, then posting slowed and the
   account decayed to 2,000 weekly impressions by mid August, near cold.
   Resuming daily posting from 19 August rebuilt impressions 378 percent week
   on week within eight days (1,300 to 7,100 a day). The 90 day authority
   compounding claim in Part 5 is confirmed in our own data, and so is its
   inverse: two quiet weeks give most of it back. Never let the account go
   quiet during a series; 3 to 5 posts a week is a floor, not a target.
2. **Two metrics, two different jobs, judge every post on both.** Impressions
   measure new audience, engagement rate measures resonance. Account
   benchmarks from the 90 days: median ER on top posts ~2.8 percent; ER above
   3.5 percent = strong resonance; ER below 1.5 percent on a high reach post =
   audience mismatch, not success. The champion (75,174 impressions) ran 2.64
   percent, high reach always dilutes ER a little.
3. **The Australia lesson: news hooks export reach to the news country.** The
   Australia ban post (22 Aug, the handwritten 8 in 10 card, Part 04) is the
   best August post by reach (9,703 impressions) and the third best of the
   whole 90 days, but its ER was 1.19 percent and its content demographics
   were roughly a third Australian (Melbourne 13, Sydney 11, Brisbane 4,
   Perth 3 percent, plus Australian universities and education departments).
   Reach without the home audience converts weakly. The fix, not the ban of
   the format: keep the news hook, and re anchor to the UK inside the post
   ("the same pattern is heading here in Spring 2027") so the algorithm keeps
   a UK signal. News hook posts are authority plays (professors and
   departments reached), never funnel plays.
4. **Naming the live debate earns the highest resonance.** The top ER posts of
   the 90 days all engage the named argument directly: the school leadership
   piece (4.98), Haidt's supporters and 2020 (4.89), Haidt's 2024 book and
   2012 (4.39), the developmental psychologist tracker (4.34), being accused
   of defending big tech (4.17). Naming Haidt or Odgers and taking a position
   is worth roughly double the ER of a neutral summary. The champion post
   fused both modes: a precise number hook plus a position in the named
   debate. Aim every flagship at that fusion.
5. **Reach without resonance does not compound.** The 15 June post reached
   9,958 impressions at 0.82 percent ER and left no follower bump. The 4 June
   champion converted ~700 followers inside its week. Follower conversion
   happens in the viral window, which is why golden hour presence matters
   most exactly when a post is taking off.
6. **The audience is senior and the register must stay evidence grade.** 22
   percent London, one third senior plus 16 percent director; founders,
   headmasters, professors and CEOs in the top job titles, and the 90 day
   content reach is thick with King's, UCL, Oxford, Melbourne and Utrecht.
   The people reading are literally the hostile experts the Odgers test
   imagines. Nothing below that bar ever ships.
7. **Series numbering works.** The Part 04 labelled post is the best August
   performer; parts create return readers. Keep numbering every series post.
8. **August cadence proof points for the next series**: the 0.4 percent post
   (20 Aug) ran 4,179 impressions at 2.87 ER and the new study post (24 Aug)
   2,838 at 2.26, both healthy; the ban series built the 26 August spike day
   (7,109 impressions). A daily series compounds day on day, which is the
   operating model for Follow the Money week.

## PART 9. THE BETA = 0.061 CITATION (pinned 28 August 2026)

The winning post's centrepiece stat finally has its verified citation. Use it whenever
the stat appears anywhere:

> Ferguson, C.J., Kaye, L.K., Branley-Bell, D., and Markey, P. (2025). "There Is No
> Evidence That Time Spent on Social Media Is Correlated With Adolescent Mental Health
> Problems: Findings From a Meta-Analysis." Professional Psychology: Research and
> Practice, 56(1), 73 to 83. DOI 10.1037/pro0000589.

Verified against the indexed primary: 46 studies, pooled beta = .061, below the
authors' preset r = .10 threshold for evidentiary value. Three rules:

1. DROP two flourishes from the old copy until someone reads the full PDF: "79 effect
   sizes" and "pre registered on the Open Science Framework". Neither is pinned to the
   text yet. "46 studies" and the beta are safe.
2. Know the counterattack: Rausch and Haidt contest Ferguson's methods (SSRN 5224958).
   Concede the dispute exists, then stand on the convergence: NASEM 2024 consensus
   ("did not support the conclusion that social media causes changes in adolescent
   health at the population level"), Orben and Przybylski's 0.4% of variance
   (n=355,358), and the causal work (Braghieri AER 2022: severe depression +7%,
   anxiety disorder +20%, concentrated in predisposed students). The argument never
   rests on one paper.
3. Never conflate this meta analysis with Ferguson's separate experiments meta
   (Psychology of Popular Media 2024, 27 experiments, d = 0.088). Different paper,
   different design, different number.
