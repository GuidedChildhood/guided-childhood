# LinkedIn engagement rules (2026) — apply to every post we write

## Not optional. This is the shape every LinkedIn post ships in.

Distilled from the 2026 LinkedIn growth playbook Justin gave us. Four levers move
a post: the algorithm, the hook, the image, the profile. We control the first
three at the writing stage. No dashes anywhere, as ever.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT THE 2026 ALGORITHM ACTUALLY MEASURES

LinkedIn is not counting likes. It is measuring two things:
1. **Dwell time.** How long a person actually reads the post.
2. **Conversation.** How many people stop to comment, and the replies underneath.

A post 50 people read fully and comment on beats a post 100 people skim and
quick like. In 2026 comments often out reach the post itself, because LinkedIn is
pushing conversation hardest.

**So we write for dwell time and comments:**
- Storytelling over statement. A story is read to the end. A list of claims is skimmed.
- Short lines with air. Never a wall of text. One idea per line, frequent breaks,
  so it is easy to consume on a phone.
- End on a real question that a stranger can answer from their own life. Every post.
- No hard sell in the body. The point is the conversation, not the pitch.

**What kills reach, avoid all of it:**
- **External links in the body.** 2026 LinkedIn suppresses posts that send people
  off platform. If a link is essential, it goes in the first comment, never the body.
  (Our /starter-pack CTA is the rare exception, used sparingly and only near a series close.)
- **Engagement bait.** No "comment BELOW and I will DM you the guide." LinkedIn punishes it.
- **Engagement pods.** No like for like rings. LinkedIn sees through them.
- **Topic hopping.** Stay in the niche, children, screens, the evidence, the build.
  Consistency is rewarded.

**After posting:** reply fast to early comments to start real conversations. That
is the signal LinkedIn wants. (Use the linkedin-comment-replies skill.)

## SECTION 2: THE HOOK IS 80 PERCENT OF THE POST

The first line or two is all a reader sees before the "see more" fold. Roughly 12
words, about 2 seconds of attention. If those words do not earn the click, the
rest of the post does not exist. Spend most of the writing effort here.

**Hook rules:**
- First line 12 words or fewer where possible. Punchy. No throat clearing.
- It must open a curiosity gap or a tension the reader needs resolved.
- It must be true to the post and true to the evidence. No bait we cannot pay off.
- The second line keeps the tension, it does not release it.

**The three hook types that work (pick one per post):**

1. **Contrarian.** Challenge a belief the reader already holds, then make them click
   to find out why they might be wrong.
   - "We are all counting screen time. It is the one number that barely matters."
   - "Every app in this space builds a screen time counter. I built the opposite."

2. **Results first.** Lead with a specific, believable, interesting number or outcome.
   - "I built a safety test to catch our own AI being unsafe. It scored two out of seven."
   - "One EU study, all 27 member states, and it points the finger away from the phone."

3. **Vulnerable.** Honest confession. Nobody scrolls past honesty because almost
   everyone else is showing off.
   - "I took the numbers off my own homepage. On purpose. And they were working."
   - "I almost built the exact tool I now tell parents to avoid."

Our own default fourth flavour, earned by the brand: **the honest middle.** State
the calm, unfashionable truth that cuts against the panic, as the tension itself.
- "The ban is coming. I am building the thing the ban forgets."

## SECTION 3: THE IMAGE

On LinkedIn a real photo of a person beats a polished infographic almost every
time. People follow a person and a journey, not a Canva graphic or an AI diagram.

- Prefer a genuine photo of Justin in a real setting, at work, on a trip, playing
  sport, with the team, holding something up in a square in Budapest.
- Build and reuse a bank of these. One post, one honest photo, one strong hook.
- Only use a graphic when the data genuinely is the point (a chart that carries the
  argument). Never a generic stock or AI infographic for its own sake.
- When a post has a real photo, weave the photo into the story, do not just attach it.
  The moon post is the template: the image is the hook and the metaphor, not decoration.

---

## SECTION 4: THE PROFILE (context, not per post)

Content is judged alongside the profile, not alone. A clean photo, cover image and
about section make people comfortable engaging in public and clicking through. Keep
it polished. Not a per post task, but the reason a strong post can still underperform.

---

## THE CHECKLIST (run before any LinkedIn post ships)

1. Does the first line, 12 words or fewer, force the see more click? (contrarian,
   results, vulnerable, or the honest middle)
2. Is it a story with short lines and air, not a wall of text?
3. Does it end on a real question a stranger can answer?
4. Zero external links in the body. (Link in first comment if essential.)
5. No engagement bait, no pod behaviour, on niche.
6. A real photo of a person where possible, woven into the story, not a generic graphic.
7. No dashes. Justin's voice. Hidden thread and 1 in 10 rule still applied.

This runs on top of hidden-thread.md, never instead of it. The hook earns the read,
the hidden thread decides what the read is for.
