The last screen of setup, and the one that decides the sale

Sign up, then four questions, then this page, then the two doors where we ask for money. Whatever a parent believes when they hit that screen was built here.

What is there now

A celebration, seven cards explaining how the day works, and the reminder ask. It is clear and it is calm. It also never once mentions the thing the parent typed one screen earlier.

Three things I would change

One. Use their answers. They have just picked their worries from a list. Handing those three back with what happens tonight, this term and by sixteen turns a product tour into their plan.

Two. The page tells a brand new parent there are sixty scripts. There are 335, and 90 of them are free. It is the worst place in the whole product to undersell, because the price comes next.

Three. The question before it is too narrow. Six tiles, and asking for a phone, online safety and starting the conversation were folded into something else at some point. The biggest worries you named cannot be answered back, because we stopped asking about them. I would take it to nine.

My recommendation: Direction A

Their three worries, each answered in the same shape: the words for tonight, the habit for this term, what it looks like by sixteen. Then the three that come for every family whether they picked them or not, the phone, social media and AI chatbots. Then how the day works, why the advice can be checked, her own side, and what is open for four days.

A tour tells them what the app has. This tells them what Friday looks like. Only one of those is worth paying for.

Direction B, and its honest trade off

One screen, no scrolling, three numbered promises. It is the calmer, more confident page and it respects a tired parent at the end of setup.

It shows far less of what they are about to be asked to pay for. If the two doors are the next screen, showing less right before them is a real risk.

What I left as your call

Whether to widen the challenge question to nine tiles. It is a small change to setup but it is the thing that makes this page personal, and without it we are guessing at two thirds of what a parent came for.

Whether the button says start with tonight's script or something plainer. It sends them onward and the two doors catch them on the way.

The child in the mockups is called Olga and the worries are the three I picked as a likely set. The real page reads both from what they entered.
