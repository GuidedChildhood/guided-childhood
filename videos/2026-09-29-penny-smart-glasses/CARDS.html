Smart glasses reply · for VotesforSchools · 29 September 2026

How to use these cards

Eight cards, your eight parts, your order. Print them, hold one at a time, read it, stop, next card. Landscape, phone at eye level, window light in front of you. Say the first line of card 1 twice; we keep the better take. Mistakes do not matter, say the line again and carry on.

Bold gold lines in brackets are cues for you. The box at the bottom of each card is the motion graphic that will land on those words, so you know where to leave a beat. Point at the space beside you when the cue says so.

Both Meta claims were checked against Meta Connect on 23 September 2026. The 59,000 figure is VotesforSchools' own count and is confirmed with Penny before this goes out.

Card 1 of 8 · Introduce yourself and thank them
[Look at the camera. Warm. Say this first line twice.]

Hi, I'm Justin Phillips, founder of Guided Digital Childhood.

We give parents, schools and children the tools for screens and everything digital: keeping the good, learning the risks, so nobody hits a cliff edge at sixteen.

Thank you to all fifty nine thousand of you who took part in the smart glasses discussion.

Graphic: on "parents, schools and children" three small cards pop in a row; on "cliff edge at sixteen" a pathway line draws left to right, rises gently, and where a cliff would drop it keeps going, with "16" in mono at the end. Then on "fifty nine thousand" a counter races from 0 to 59,000 in huge ink type beside you and breaks into a scatter of small voice bubbles that settle into the shape of the number. The DiGi star lands as the full stop. Mono eyebrow: THANK YOU.
Card 2 of 8 · Recognise their concerns
[Pause after "possibilities". Point left on "concerns".]

You highlighted both the possibilities and the concerns.

Especially what happens when someone is filmed without knowing.

And when enough people speak up, companies do change things. This summer Meta made its glasses stop recording if someone covers the light.

That is a start, not the finish. A light is not permission. It needs to go further.

Graphic: two white cards tilt in from opposite sides, "Possibilities" and "Concerns", then the concerns card pushes forward. On "filmed without knowing" a drawn outline of glasses appears with a tiny recording light that blinks, and a person silhouette beside it who does not turn round. On "covers the light" a hand slides over the light and the camera icon greys out with a cross; mono caption: AUGUST 2026 · COVERED LIGHT, NO CAMERA. On "a start, not the finish" a progress bar fills a third of the way and stops. Caption in mono: A LIGHT IS NOT PERMISSION.
Card 3 of 8 · Recognise the benefits
[Softer. Point right on "read text aloud".]

Without losing the good, because there is real good here.

Glasses can read text aloud for people with sight loss.

Meta has also announced hearing support for some adults with hearing loss.

Graphic: the glasses outline on a person's face; a page of text in front of them turns into a sound wave that travels to their ear. Then the wave changes to a hearing icon with the words "some adults" underlined by a butter marker sweep. Everything drawn in ink line, nothing photographic, no Meta branding.
Card 4 of 8 · Explain what is coming
[Faster. Point up on "Muse".]

And things are moving quickly.

Meta plans to bring its new AI agent, Muse, to glasses in the next few months.

That is technology you can speak to and ask to carry out tasks.

Graphic: the word "Muse" types on with a block cursor, then a speech bubble from you turns into a to do list that ticks itself off (book, find, buy). A small calendar flips "in the next few months". We use type and drawn icons, never Meta's own Muse avatar, because it is theirs.
Card 5 of 8 · Make your main point
[Slow down. This is the line.]

So digital education cannot stop at screens.

We need to understand technology we can wear and talk to, too.

Graphic: a phone, then a tablet, then a laptop line up beside you; the camera pulls back and the row keeps going: a watch, earbuds, glasses. The word "screens" gets a butter strike through and "wear and talk to" lands big. Held for two seconds. This is the hero shot of the video.

We want to prepare you as this technology develops.

Adults and technology companies have responsibilities here, too.
