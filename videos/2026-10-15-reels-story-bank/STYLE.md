# The Kitchen Cut: our talking head reel style

Justin, 15 October 2026: "I love Snatch editing, where it flips scenes,
shutting door slam, moving to plane then arrive... analysing IT guy method
but own style. Build a style."

So this is his skeleton, our register, and Guy Ritchie's cutting. It sits on
top of `talking-head-video` (the pipeline) and `silent-ugc` (the rules for any
vertical). Where they disagree, `silent-ugc` wins on what may be shown.

## 1. What we take from the Family IT Guy method

From the catalogue in `content/packs/2026-10-14-reels-story-bank/research/`:

| His move | Why it works | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| A title card fixed at the top for the whole video | The scroller knows the topic in half a second | Same, Nunito 900, cream panel, one gold underline |
| A real person to camera | Trust, and the algorithm favours faces | Justin, filmed on his phone. Never a made person |
| One credential line early | Why listen to him | One true scene from the GREEN list in `founder-context.md` |
| One named fix, usable tonight | His saves come from these | One fix, in the app's own words |
| Big word by word captions | Most watch on mute | Same, our fonts, the spoken word lit in gold |
| Fear as the hook (his reach) | Shares | **Never.** The parent's own moment is our hook |
| Ends on his product | Funnel | Ends on the stage check, identical every time |

## 2. What we add: the cutting

Snatch works because the edit has an opinion. Every cut lands on a beat, the
camera never sits still for long, and time is squeezed when nothing happens
and stretched when something matters. Seven moves, used the same way every time
so the style becomes recognisable.

1. **Two angles, swap every line.** Angle A: front, chest up, eye level.
   Angle B: tighter, 45 degrees to one side, slightly lower. Film the whole
   script in A, then the whole script again in B. The edit alternates line by
   line, and never holds one angle for more than two lines.
2. **The freeze frame name card.** On the first line the frame freezes, tints
   cream, and a mono label slides in: JUSTIN · DAD · BUILT GUIDED CHILDHOOD.
   Half a second, then it runs on. This is the Snatch character intro.
3. **The smash cut insert.** A 0.3 to 0.6 second close up of a real object,
   with one hard sound: a bedroom door shut, a phone put face down, a toggle
   clicked off. It punctuates the line before it. One or two per reel.
4. **The time jump.** Three or four inserts at 0.3 seconds each, speed ramped,
   for "an hour later" or "every night this week": tea poured, tea cold, lamp
   on, clock. This is the Snatch travel montage, done with a kitchen.
5. **The gold line jump.** Our version of the plane on the map. A gold dotted
   line (the pathway from `StageRoad.tsx`) draws across the frame from Justin
   to where the app screen arrives, with a whoosh. Every jump to the product
   uses it, so the brand's signature becomes the transition.
6. **The number pop.** When a script counts (one, two, three), a big gold
   numeral punches in beside him on the beat.
7. **The still ending.** No cutting on the last line. Angle A, still, to
   camera: "What stage is your child? Three questions, no sign up." Hold one
   second, then the end card.

## 3. Sound

Muted first: the captions carry everything. With sound on, a music bed with
a clear beat, cuts landing on it, and one sound per insert (door, click,
kettle, whoosh). No sound effect without a picture that makes it.

## 4. What it never does

- No synthetic person, ever. Justin, or hands and objects.
- No child's face, no child's name, no real child's screen.
- No fear, no statistic as the hook, no platform as the villain.
- No more than one fix per reel.
- No dashes on screen, in captions or in the caption text.
- Never longer than 45 seconds.

## 5. The shoot, every time

1. **Setup A**, then **setup B**, reading the whole sheet in each.
2. **The insert list** in the read sheet, filmed once and reused across reels.
   Hands and objects only. Two seconds each, steady, good light.
3. Send everything. The edit does the rest.
